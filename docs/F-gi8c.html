Readings and comparison details

F-gi8c: Missal, Dominica II Adventus (end of the Epistle Rom 15:13, gradual Ex Sion species) and the Gospel Mark 1:6-7

A narrow parchment binding strip at Stams is the foot of a leaf from a plenary missal. A plenary missal is a Mass book that writes out the readings and the sung texts together with the prayers. Only the last two lines of the text block survive on each face. One face has the end of the Epistle of the Second Sunday of Advent, Romans 15:4-13, ending "...pace in credendo ut habundetis in spe et uirtu[te]". It is followed directly by the gradual Ex Sion species decoris eius Deus manifeste veniet with its verse Congregate illi. The other face has Mark 1:6-7, "[...]re edebat et predicabat dicens. Veniet fora / [...] me cuius non sum dig(n)us procumbens solvere corri[giam]", where the Vulgate has "fortior". That is part of the Gospel pericope Mark 1:(2/4)-8, which medieval missals read on a weekday in Advent.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 17: parchment, one fragment item, 245 x 80 mm (measured as 126/245 x 20/80 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck). It has the title "x", the catalogue date "1200-1399; 1400-1599", no linked description, and two images (Stams_STA_Frg_017_01r and _01v). The record does not name the text. Its physical description fits the images.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the face with the Advent II Mass. The basis is the run epistle-end, gradual, then verse in the order of the Mass of the Second Sunday of Advent. That run is read on a page image of the York Missal and agrees with the Cantus Index standard text of the gradual (g00496). The other face is the text of Mark 1:6-7. Which Mass this book read it at is not established. The York Missal reads Mark 1:4-8 on the Friday after the Second Sunday of Advent and Mark 1:2-8 on the Monday after the First, so both a slot after and a slot before Advent II are attested. The chant has no musical notation and the lessons are written in full, so the book is a missal, not a notated gradual. Differences from the Vulgate: "Veniet" for "Venit", "fora" where "fortior" stands (both Mark 1:7; the letter after "dig-" in "dignus" is uncertain, read as n or as a t-like stroke); spellings "habundetis" (abundetis) and "syon" (Sion). The staff with hollow notes in the lower margin of the gradual face is a later addition in a different, lighter ink, not part of the book.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers all four lines. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's reread of all four lines at reading size. A second research reader reread the same four lines. The letters before "edebat" and the first word of the second line on canvas 0 are uncertain (cut and curled corner). Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the four lines with the Vulgate (Mark 1:6-7 and Rom 15:13, from an online Vulgate text, not a page image). The reader also compared them with the Cantus Index text of the gradual. An independent research reader then reread the four lines on the images and compared the Advent II face with page images of the York Missal, ed. Henderson (1874), pp. 2-5: the epistle given by incipit and explicit, followed by "Gradale. Ex Sion species decoris ejus: Deus manifeste veniet. V. Congregate illi". That reader found the same order and checked the placement of Mark 1 in that edition. The coordinator read canvas 0, line 2 (native box 2550,1930,3650,2250, "[post] me cuius non sum dig(n)us", Mark 1:7) on the image, reading the letter after g as n where two other readers saw a t-like stroke; the comparison with the York page images rests on the independent checker. Gaps: the York edition prints the Epistle by incipit and explicit only, so the epistle wording rests on the Vulgate text. No missal of the Cistercian or the Brixen/Salzburg use was compared, and the day of the Mark Gospel in this book is open.

The differences are "Veniet" for "Venit", "fora" where the Vulgate has "fortior" (perhaps an abbreviation whose mark is lost at the cut edge), and the spellings "habundetis" and "syon". They show only that this copy has these readings. They do not bear on the identification, and they are not evidence of the book's use.

Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

  • Both faces show the last two lines of the page above a blank, folded-over lower margin, so the strip is the foot of one leaf. Which face is the recto depends on the day this book read Mark 1. If after Advent II (as the York Friday), the gradual face comes first. If before (as the York Monday after Advent I), the Gospel face comes first.
  • The later music in the lower margin of canvas 1 was not studied. A later blue-ink note on canvas 0 was not read.
  • Not claimed: the date of the leaf (the catalogue gives "1200-1399; 1400-1599", and the second range may reflect the marginal music, but that was not examined), its origin, the use of the missal (Cistercian, diocesan or other), or any relation to the York Missal beyond the shared common formulary.
