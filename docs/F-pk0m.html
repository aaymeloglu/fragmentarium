Readings and comparison details

F-pk0m: Augustine, De civitate Dei, XVI.43 - XVII.1

This parchment leaf in Stams is from a copy of Augustine's De civitate Dei (The City of God). It is written in two columns on each side. The recto carries book XVI, chapter 43, from Moses leading Israel out of Egypt to the Judges; the verso carries the end of XVI.43 and the opening of XVII.1, on the age of the prophets. In Bernhard Dombart's Teubner edition (vol. 2, 1877) this is pp. 194-197. A modern note in blue ink in the margin of the recto names a printed book, the Officia propria Brixinensia (Brixen 1673), which is the volume the leaf was taken from as a binding, not the text on the leaf.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 77: parchment, one fragment item, 210 x 180 mm (measured as 178/210 x 143/180 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description, with two images labelled 077_01r and 077_01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: one leaf, used as a wrapper, with the head folded over and the foot turned under.

  • Identification: identified, high. The basis is continuous agreement in wording and order with Dombart's text in five passages, covering all four columns, across the end of book XVI and the opening of book XVII, with a red running head "XVI" over the verso. The opening of XVII.1 ("Quoniam ergo superioris libri usque ad regnum David factus est finis") is Augustine's own transition between books, so the leaf is a copy of the work and not a quotation of it; no alternative needed testing. The copy differs from Dombart in transmission variants: "spiritus" omitted before "Dei", "tunc enim per eum", "rana et" omitted from the list of plagues, "mors pecori" for "mors pecorum", the clause "quando tabernaculum testimonii nuncupatum est, ubi Deus sacrificiis futura praenuntiantibus colebatur; cum" absent between "actus est" and "scilicet" (probably by eye-skip, the scribe's eye passing from one place to a similar one further on), "nondum gentibus omnibus" for "nondum de omnibus gentibus", "et capram", "generare posse" for "posse generare", "generationum series" for "generationum", "fuisse(?)" for "fuit", "incipit homo" for "homo incipit", "prima lingua" with Dombart's later manuscripts and early editions, "indicauit" with his manuscript A, "possimus" with the early editions, and "reuersus(?) israhelitis" for "reversis Israelitis". No claim is made about the manuscript's place in the tradition.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading of 31 October 2026 and the source-assisted reading. A research reader reread on the native images the whole of recto col. a and col. b, the whole of verso col. b and the lower half of verso col. a, about 95 lines. An independent checker read the upper half of verso col. a (15 lines), which is heavily stained and covered by mirror-image printed offset; several of its readings there were settled only with the edition open and are marked probable. The running heads over the recto rest on the first reading. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the lines listed above with Dombart's text, on page images of pp. 195-197 from archive.org; for p. 194 (recto col. a) no page image was kept with the comparison, which may rest on the archive.org OCR text. An independent checker read canvas 1, verso col. a, lines 1-15, native box 3300,1850,1000,1100, on the image before opening the edition, and compared them with Dombart p. 195, lines 30-33, and p. 196, lines 1-6, on the page images (p. 195, p. 196). Every legible word falls in sequence there ("ab Abraham scilicet usque ad David ... ante hunc ergo velut pueritia ... et ideo prima lingua inventa est, id est Hebraea"), running on into the research reader's transcription of the foot of the column with about two lines between, and the checker found the four new variants listed above. The checker had read the report's first-reading note "ideo prima lingua [...] hebrea" for this column before reading, and the checker's line-by-line readings outside that line were often wrong before the edition was opened; agreement was established on the page image. The checker also confirmed the red running head "XVI" over verso col. b. The modern note was not rechecked. Dombart-Kalb (Corpus Christianorum, Series Latina 47-48), the standard modern edition, was not consulted. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

Leaf order, other items, and limits

The text runs from recto col. a to col. b, and from recto col. b on to verso col. a and col. b, so the two blocks on each side are the two columns of one page and the leaf is single, not a bifolium. Comparing the column ends with the edition, about 17 to 25 lines are lost from each column, split between the turned-under foot and text lost or hidden under the folded head.

Other items: the modern note in blue ink, written vertically in the right margin of the recto inside round brackets (canvas 0, region 4894,1866,478,1916, image), reads "~~Manuale~~ / Officia propria Brixinensis / Brixen 1673", with "Manuale" struck through. It names the host book, a proper office of the diocese of Brixen (Bressanone) printed at Brixen in 1673, not the text of the leaf. A pasted slip at the head of recto col. b carries the remnant "[..]op[r]iu[m] / Brixinen[se] / ...", apparently part of the host book's own label, which supports the note; the mirror-image printed offset over verso col. a fits a printed host book. Dark brown letters beside the note, perhaps "ACIA", are probably transfer or label residue and are unread. The host copy was not checked.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the manuscript (the script looks twelfth to thirteenth century, but this has not been assessed), its place among the manuscripts of De civitate Dei, the reading of the running heads over the recto, and whether other Stams fragments come from the same manuscript.
