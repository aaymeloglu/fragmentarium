Cod. 3560 (F-xra7)

Wien, Österreichische Nationalbibliothek · catalogued as Homilia (?), 1401 – 1450 CE

"Guillermus" (Guillelmus Parisiensis or Johannes Herolt), Postilla super epistolas et evangelia, Gospel postil for Dominica XIX post octavas Pentecostes (Mt 9:1-8)

The front pastedown and flyleaf of Cod. 3560 are one leaf of the Postilla super epistolas et evangelia ('Postilla Guillermi'), the Gospel postil for the nineteenth Sunday after the octave of Pentecost (Mt 9:1-8), opening with the collection's dated formula 'Hoc signum fecit dominus Ihesus anno eius 31 ...'. A research reader collated three passages, one per visible face, with page images of the Ulm edition of about 1478 (GW 11940, Munich copy, scans 296-298); the coordinator read the close of the Gospel gloss on Cod3560_Ir against scan 297. The leaf is shorter than the print: it lacks a long passage on the causes of illness, the source sigla and several glosses, and gives the three kinds of blasphemy at 'hic blasphemat' where the print has them later; whether it is an abridgement or another form is open, and no manuscript of the work was compared. No blind check was made for this record. The edition is anonymous; GW and ISTC name Guillelmus Parisiensis, BSB-Ink and the Bodleian Johannes Herolt.

Readings and comparison details

F-xra7: "Guillermus" (attributed to Guillelmus Parisiensis or to Johannes Herolt), Postilla super epistolas et evangelia, Gospel postil for the nineteenth Sunday after the octave of Pentecost (Mt 9:1-8)

The paper leaf used as front pastedown and flyleaf of Vienna, Cod. 3560, carries the postil (a running exposition of the Sunday Gospel, glossing it phrase by phrase) on the healing of the paralytic, Matthew 9:1-8, for the nineteenth Sunday after the octave of Pentecost, in the collection printed in the fifteenth century as the Postilla super epistolas et evangelia and later as "Postilla Guillermi". The leaf begins with the collection's dated opening formula, "Hoc signum fecit dominus Ihesus anno eius 31, 12 kal. sep., feria v, luna 24(?), ind. iiij", and a summary of Mark 5, continues with the glossed Gospel, and ends in a note against despair, breaking off at "unde Bernhardus Mentiris Caym maior [est dei pie]tas quam quevis ini[quitas]". Its text is shorter than the printed one in places and arranges one note differently.

The Fragmentarium record describes Wien, Österreichische Nationalbibliothek, Cod. 3560, front pastedown and flyleaf, as part of one trimmed paper leaf, 205 x 140 mm, Latin, in a bastarda, from Germany or Austria, dated 1401-1450, with the title "Homilia (?)". Its description (by Ivana Dobcheva) quotes the opening, recognises the Gospel passages (Mark 5:1-13 and Matthew 9:4-8), quotes "boni medici qui primo removent causam morbi", "maior est iniquitas mea" and "unde Bernardus mentiris cayn maior", and notes that the lower half of the leaf is pasted to the front board and the upper half served as a flyleaf. That description fits the leaf; it names no work or author. The host volume is a fifteenth-century volume of sermons, "Sermones casuales de festis et de sanctis", from Mondsee.

  • Identification: identified, high confidence for the work and the locus. Three passages, one on each visible face, agree in wording and in order with the Ulm edition of about 1478: the dated opening formula (year of Christ's life, calendar day, weekday, age of the moon, indiction) with the summary of Mark 5 that introduces the pericope; the glosses woven into the Gospel text; and the note on God's mercy to sinners, with Peter, Mary Magdalene, Paul, the thief on the cross, Judas and Cain and their citations (John 21, Luke 7, Acts 9, Luke 23, the Decretum, Genesis 4, Bernard), in the same sequence. The "boni medici" gloss is Nicholas of Lyra's and also stands, in Google Books excerpts, in Ludolph of Saxony's Vita Christi; Lyra or Ludolph would account for that gloss, but not for the dated opening and the note in this order, and Ludolph was found to have neither in the excerpts searched. Hits for "Judas magis peccavit desperando" in Herolt's Sermones Discipuli and for "Mentiris Cayn" in the Postillae maiores and Petrus de Palude's Thesaurus novus were commonplaces in excerpts and were not compared further. A Magdeburg manuscript described in U. Winter's catalogue (seen in excerpts) has postils that open "Ante initium praesentis evangelii scribit sanctus ...", with the leaf's "presentis" where the print has "illius"; how that catalogue names the text was not seen, so whether it carries this postil in the leaf's form, or a related collection, is untested. Differences from the print: the print's passage of about a page on the causes of bodily illness, after "intendit curationi", is absent from the leaf; the leaf gives a three-part definition of blasphemy at "hic blasphemat", where the print has a one-clause definition and gives the three ways of blaspheming later, after the Cain passage; the leaf has none of the print's source sigla ("Ly.", "Cris.", "Gor.") and lacks several of its glosses; and there are differences of wording (listed below).
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading was made before any source was known. The source-assisted reading was made by a research reader who reread all three visible faces at native size against the first reading and, once the candidate was found, against the print. The small numerals of the opening formula ("31", "12", "24", "iiij") were read with the print open; "luna 24" could not be settled from the leaf alone (the first reading had "2e"). Words under the two shelfmark labels and a glued strip, and at the torn fold, are not read; the first two lines of the pastedown give only isolated, uncertain words. "Magdalena", written with an interlinear addition, was read as a word, not letter by letter. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader collated the three passages with page images of the Ulm edition (Munich copy, scans 296-298). The coordinator read the close of the Gospel gloss, from "et vade in domum tuam" to "id est ad salutem", on canvas 1 (Cod3560_Ir), native box 320,500,980,3420, against the page image of scan 297 of the Munich copy: the leaf follows the print in wording and order, apart from spelling, but has "et surrexit" where the print has "Qui statim surrexit ad imperium Ihesu Christi", has no "Ly." sigla, and lacks the print's gloss "id est Christum qui est virtus dei patris"; the face breaks off at the fold after "ad salutem". No blind check was made for this record: no crops were set to fresh readers. Gaps: no manuscript of the Postilla was compared; the half-face pasted to the board is hidden; the scholarship behind the two attributions was not read. No human palaeographic review is claimed.

Differences. After "intendit curacioni" the print has a passage of about a page on why the illness was inflicted and on the five causes for which God inflicts bodily illness; the leaf goes straight on to "Et ecce quidam de scribis". At "hic blasphemat" the leaf defines blasphemy in three members ("deo aliquid attribuere quod sibi non convenit [aut] removere quod sibi convenit aut adt[ribuere ...] quod deo proprium est"); the print here has the single clause "Blasphemare est creature attribuere illud quod est proprium dei" and gives the three ways of blaspheming after the Cain passage ("notandum quod tribus modis blasphematur deus"). The print's source sigla and several of its glosses ("id est Christum qui est virtus dei patris", "ut deus non ut purus homo") are not on the leaf. Wording differs in small points: presentis / illius, venienti / veniente, multos habens / habens multos, poterat domare / potuit eum domare, interrogavit / interrogabat; "et surrexit" / "Qui statim surrexit ad imperium Ihesu Christi"; Mary Magdalene "que multa peccata commisit" / "cum multis fornicata est"; the leaf lacks "verecunda" and "apostolus"; "Luce 7mo" / "Lu. 7. fides tua te salvam fecit"; "23 q. iij(?) Si non licet" / "23. q. 3. Sed non licet". Between "ad salutem" and "cum iuramento negavit" the print has the transition "Unde circa presens evangelium ... Notandum quod Hugo de Prato dicit quod deus misericorditer recipit peccatores. De hoc habemus exemplum in multis. Nam petrus"; on the leaf this falls on the damaged first lines of the pastedown, where "ex(emplum) i(n) mult[is]" and perhaps "recipit" can be made out. These differences show that the leaf's text differs from the printed text; they do not show whether the leaf's shorter form is an abridgement, an earlier form or a parallel one.

In the print each Gospel postil opens with such a dated formula and "Ante initium illius evangelii scribit sanctus ...".

Attribution. The Ulm edition names no author: its text opens (scan 7) "Epistolarum et evangeliorum de tempore et sanctis liber incipit", and no prologue was seen in this copy. GW 11940 and ISTC attribute the work to Guillelmus Parisiensis; BSB-Ink H-139 files the edition under Johannes Herolt, and the Bodleian's incunable catalogue gives "Guillermus [pseud., i.e. Johannes Herolt]". Later editions carry the title "Postilla Guillermi super epistolas et evangelia" (Google Books excerpts of editions of 1511, 1516 and 1518, with the same dated openings). Both names are given here; the scholarship behind them was not read.

The order of the text, other items in the images, and what is not claimed

The leaf was cut across: its upper half is the flyleaf, its lower half is pasted to the board. The flyleaf verso (canvas 2) is the upper half of one side, whose lower half is hidden under the paste. The flyleaf recto (canvas 1) and the pastedown (canvas 0) are the upper and lower halves of the other side: canvas 1 ends "id est ad salutem" at the fold, and canvas 0 continues with the note that follows those words in the print. The text therefore runs canvas 2, the hidden half, canvas 1, canvas 0, and the visible pair, which the catalogue calls the recto, is by the text the later side of the leaf. The hidden half should hold the end of the Mark 5 summary and the Gospel up to "Confide fili, remittuntur tibi peccata tua".

The other canvases show the binding and the first page of the host volume (canvases 5-8 and 12), a rear flyleaf and pastedown in a different, more cursive hand (canvases 9-11), and parchment binding strips with a few letters of a large textualis (canvases 13-16). None of these was read, and no claim is made about them.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the copy, which are the catalogue's (1401-1450, Germany or Austria); a date for the compilation of the Postilla is given in the literature but was not verified and is not used here; the author of the Postilla; whether the leaf's shorter text is an abridgement of the printed form, an earlier form or a parallel one; that the leaf was written at Mondsee, which is the host volume's provenance; and whether this copy is otherwise known to scholarship.
