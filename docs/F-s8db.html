F-s8db — Guido de Baysio, Rosarium super Decreto

Re-adjudicated 22 September 2026.

  • Identification: identified, high confidence. The selected commentary on Gratian C.10 q.1 c.13 (Quia sacerdotes) agrees with Guido de Baysio's Rosarium super Decreto. The connected account of Roman laymen and Damasus consulting Jerome is diagnostic, beyond the canon's lemma.
  • Transcription: selected source-assisted reading. The isolated reader recovered little reliable text from the faded crop. The coordinator subsequently inspected manuscript and print images with the earlier proposal available. No full transcription or accuracy rate is certified; there was no human palaeographic review.
  • Verification: manuscript and printed images. The opening lower-cover passage was compared with the 1481 Rosarium, Freiburg image 0415. A further lower-cover passage was inspected but proved too faded to confirm the wider endpoints previously proposed.

Evidence

The preserved image-only response has six physical rows, the last clipped, and substantial errors and gaps. Its conjectural animalis? is not independent support for the assisted [D]amasus. The raw failure remains visible.

Selected assisted readings, crop lines 2–5:

l(ai)cos qui sua auct(oritat)e p(re)sumunt accipe(re) […] / eccl(es)iis q(uo)d viciu(m) p(re)cipue inficiebat oli(m) ro(manos) […] / [D]amasus (con)suluit Jeronimu(m) q(ui)d de talib(us) […] / cui ip(s)e Jero. […] ut seq(ui)t(ur) […]

Slashes indicate physical line changes. Parentheses are proposed expansions; brackets mark missing or uncertain material. The bracketed D is an assisted completion of the divided name. Hidden right-hand endings are not supplied as visible text.

The 1481 edition, Freiburg image 0415, printed folio 206a, lower left continuing upper right, has the same progression: laymen presuming to take church offerings, the vice formerly affecting Romans, Damasus consulting Jerome, and Jerome's ensuing response. The longer printed wording supplies oblationes de ecclesiis and quid de talibus esset faciendum. Those completions clarify the comparison; they are not a recovered manuscript reading. Orthography and abbreviation vary, and the faded response verb is left unresolved here.

An additional lower-cover crop reaches a red initial and the apparent sequence Sanctorum … Salvatoris. It is compatible with the next canon but does not yield enough of the commentary to verify the old extension through q.2. The upper cover is not newly collated. This additional check was candidate-aware and inconclusive, not held-out confirmation.

Result and remaining scope

The opening supports the Rosarium identification. Retain that attribution while restricting the verified locus to the sampled c.13 commentary. The old report's c.14–q.2 c.2 endpoints, siglum expansions and full wrapper reconstruction remain unconfirmed. No-hit searches against other glosses do not establish their exclusion, and the running-commentary format alone would not identify Guido. Better images of the faded material would be needed to settle the wider span. The saved before version preserves the earlier transcription and reconstruction.
