Readings and comparison details

F-ju8m: Jacobus de Voragine, Legenda aurea, All Saints (the four orders of saints), and the end of All Souls, the Four Crowned Martyrs, Theodore and the opening of Martin

This bifolium in Stams is from a copy of the Legenda aurea of Jacobus de Voragine, a collection of saints' lives and feasts arranged by the church year. One leaf carries part of the chapter on All Saints, the section on the dignity of the four orders of saints: apostles, martyrs, confessors and virgins. The other leaf carries the section on suffrages for the dead from the chapter on All Souls, ending with the story of the knight whose horse was not sold for the poor (told on the authority of Turpin in this copy), then the whole chapters on the Four Crowned Martyrs and on Theodore, and the etymology that opens the chapter on Martin. In the chapter numbering of Graesse's edition (2nd ed., 1850) these are cap. 162, pp. 723-726, and caps. 163-166, pp. 738-741, the book's sequence for 1 to 11 November.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 241: parchment, one fragment item, 399 x 325 mm (measured as 372/399 x 300/325 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no date and no linked description. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images, which show one bifolium photographed open on both faces.

  • Identification: identified, high. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and sequence with Graesse's text and with Maggioni's critical text over five passages, one on each of three pages and two on the fourth, and the Legenda aurea's own sequence of All Saints, All Souls, Four Crowned, Theodore and Martin, with the expository All Saints and All Souls chapters divided "primo ... quarto" and the rubricated "De nomine" etymology before Martin. These treatise chapters are the Legenda aurea's own, not passages quoted inside another compilation; no other containing work fits the sequence, and Juan Gil de Zamora's Legende sanctorum, which reuses some of its saints' lives, was not tested on these chapters. The copy has small differences of its own: it adds "ut ait turpinus" to the knight's story, reads "cc.lxxvii" for the year of the Four Crowned where Graesse and Maggioni have CCLXXXVII, and several times agrees with Maggioni ("statutum fuit", "Marmaritanorum") or with the first printed edition Graesse reports as Ed. Pr. against Graesse's text. None of these bears on the identification.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading of 30 October 2026 and the source-assisted reading. The first reader saw the pencil shelfmarks on the photographs ("Stams, STA, Frg. 241r", "241v") and had a standing note that mentioned a "Stams pilot" project, without any proposed work or author; the rubrics it quotes ("De quatuor coronatis", "De sancto theodoro", "De nomine sci martini") are read from the leaf. A research reader (Claude) reread three passages on the native images with the editions at hand (Theodore, the confessors, the Turpin story), and an independent research reader read two more (the Four Crowned and the apostles; see Verification). Every other line rests on the first reading, checked against the overview photographs and the edition. Canvas 0 is darkened and abraded; its right page and the faded right third of its left page were not otherwise reread. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader (Claude) compared the three reread passages with Graesse 1850, p. 739 on the page image and the other pages in the archive.org OCR text, and with Maggioni's text as paragraphs in Corpus Corporum (not on page images). An independent research reader then read, before seeing that comparison, the opening of the Four Crowned on canvas 0, left page, native regions 1400,680,2300,540 and 1400,1120,2300,520, and compared it with Graesse pp. 739-740 on page images fetched from archive.org and with Maggioni in Corpus Corporum: sustained agreement with small variants. The same reader read the apostles section on the abraded canvas 0, right page, region 4150,1380,2250,520, and matched it with Graesse cap. 162 in the OCR text, so the most damaged page is matched in its wording, not only in outline. That reader also confirmed the Graesse chapters and pages against the running heads and the leaf order from the text running from canvas 1, right page, onto canvas 0, left page. Coverage: all four pages carry collated text; the later lines in the lower margin of canvas 0, left page, were not read. No human review.

The differences are of four kinds: word order; small additions and omissions, such as the added "ut ait turpinus" and, in the first reading of the suffrages section, the lack of Graesse's "hujus secundae partis", which his Ed. Pr. also omits; corruptions such as "at clamantes", "intra" for "in terra", and "flammea" for "framea" in the first reading; and agreements with Maggioni or with Graesse's Ed. Pr. against Graesse's text. The year "cc.lxxvii" has one X fewer than both editions. Elsewhere, as far as the first reading and the overview photographs show, Leaf A follows Graesse cap. 162 in order: the apostles' four excellences, the martyrs, the bloodless martyrdom with Bernard and Gregory, the confessors "corde, ore, opere" with Chrysostom's four reasons, then the virgins. Leaf B follows the suffrages section of cap. 163 and caps. 164-166, through Theodore's dialogue with the governor and Martin's etymology ("quasi Martem tenens ... martirum unus ... irritans uel prouocans").

Edition used: Th. Graesse (ed.), Jacobi a Voragine Legenda aurea vulgo Historia Lombardica dicta, 2nd ed. (1850), archive.org bub_gb_h2rkGCFC1G0C: the end of All Souls and the Four Crowned on p. 739, Theodore from p. 740. The critical text, G. P. Maggioni (ed.), Legenda aurea (Florence: SISMEL, 1998), was read as text in Corpus Corporum, where the Four Crowned is cap. CLX. Graesse's title page and Maggioni's edition both attribute the work to Jacobus de Voragine; the attribution is standard.

Leaf order and limits

The order follows from the text. Leaf A has canvas 0, right page, as recto (apostles, then martyrs), ending "Hoc enim pacto cuncti pugnauerunt. hac [conditione...]", and canvas 1, left page, as verso, beginning "hac milicia triumphauit" (martyrs, confessors, virgins). Leaf B has canvas 1, right page, as recto (All Souls suffrages and the knight's horse), ending "clamor uelut leonu(m). ursor(um) (et) lupor(um)", and canvas 0, left page, as verso, opening "[ip]sum ex medio abstulerunt" and continuing with the Four Crowned, Theodore and Martin. The rest of All Saints and the first part of All Souls are lost between the two leaves, so this is not the central bifolium of its gathering.

Other items in the images: pencil shelfmarks, a purple mark, and two lines of later script in the lower margin of canvas 0, left page, which were not read and do not look like a note on the text.

Not claimed: date, origin, script dating, or the copy's place in the textual tradition of the Legenda aurea, including its repeated agreement with readings Graesse labels Ed. Pr. and the Turpin attribution, which was not checked against Maggioni. Maggioni was not seen on page images.
