Readings and comparison details

F-yslj: Chrysostomus Henriquez, Fasciculus Sanctorum Ordinis Cisterciensis, probable source of excerpt notes (St Bernard's miracles in Germany; Life of St Robert of Newminster)

A paper wrapper from Stams carries Latin working notes that agree with, and were probably taken from, Chrysostomus Henriquez, Fasciculus Sanctorum Ordinis Cisterciensis. This is a collection of lives of Cistercian saints, first printed at Brussels in 1623 and again at Cologne in 1631. The notes share two things with it:

  • St Bernard. Henriquez's tally of the miracles Bernard of Clairvaux worked on his journey through Germany in 1146-1147. The tally runs 173 blind, 158 lame, 15 crippled ("contracti"), 53 mute, 65 deaf, 11 paralysed and 55 with withered hands. All seven categories and numbers agree with Henriquez, in the same order.
  • St Robert. A string of short notes on the life of St Robert, abbot of Newminster in Northumberland (died 1159). These follow Henriquez's chapter and his margin headings, in order.

The other face of the wrapper carries a German administrative entry made before an abbot of Stams, which is only partly read.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 174: paper, two fragment items, 318 x 215 mm (measured on Frg. 174_1 as 310/318 x 172/215 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), dated 1600-1699, online since 18 September 2021. It has the title "x", no linked description, and two images (174_01r02r and 174_01v02v). The record does not name any text, and its date range fits. Modern pencil notes on the leaves say the sheet was taken from the binding of Stams Cod. XIII n. 1, and give the date "1621 Aug. 29". That date belongs to the German entry and is wrong on the month (see below).

  • Identification: identified, high, for the notes on the right panel of piece 174-2r. The two parts rest on different evidence.
    • The tally. The heading "S. Bern. in peregrinatio(n)e germania fecit miracula" is followed by seven categories with numbers. These agree in order and in every number with Henriquez's summary of the German journey: "Centum enim & septuaginta & tres lumine oculorum orbati ... illuminati sunt", and so on. The note's "Aliaque innumerabilia" answers Henriquez's "innumerabilia pene miracula".
    • The Robert notes. They follow Henriquez's life of Robert of Newminster (lib. I, distinctio 11) in sequence and key words, not in continuous wording:
    • his abstinence
    • all his monks predestined except two lay brothers
    • "alligat[us]"
    • a figure "in the form of a peasant" seen in the choir
    • a false accusation
    • his death at the same hour as Editha
    • Why Henriquez and not an earlier print. The Robert life itself was in print before Henriquez: in John of Tynemouth and Capgrave's Nova Legenda Anglie (1516), and in a restyled text in Laurentius Surius, De probatis sanctorum historiis III (Cologne 1572), under 7 June. Henriquez reprints Surius almost word for word. What points to Henriquez is that two of the note's items reproduce his margin headings, "Omnes Monachi eius sunt praedestinati" and "Falso accusatur", which the 1572 Surius does not have. These headings are short phrases, so they make Henriquez the probable source, not a proven one: a lost or unsearched intermediary with the same headings and tally cannot be ruled out. The double l of "[a]lligat" agrees with Surius and Henriquez ("alligatus"), not with Capgrave ("ligatus").
    • Robert of Molesme. Robert of Molesme, the founder of Cîteaux, was considered and set aside. Only the motif of two brothers sent on his behalf resembles his life. The sequence above, ending with Editha, belongs to Robert of Newminster.
    • No earlier tally found. No printed tally of Bernard's miracles before 1623 was found in full-text searches, which reach the Vita prima and later works that follow Henriquez. This is a negative search result, not proof that none exists.
    • Unidentified material. The devotional tags at the head of the panel are not identified: "Addere calcar currenti non opus est", "regione umbra mortis ... Regio mortis, carcer opacus, civitasque obsessa sine vic[tu]?" and "Nativitas tua Dei genitrix Virgo". Neither are a few German pen lines.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading and the source-assisted reading.
    • A research reader reread the whole notes panel of 174-2r on native crops, and the German entry on 174-1v.
    • A second research reader reread the five Robert lines and the dateline of the German entry before opening any source.
    • Single words of the German entry were reread from neutral crops by fresh readers, and decided on the image.
    • The numbers were reread on a separate crop. The lame count is 158, as in Henriquez.

Accuracy has not been measured.

  • Verification:
    • A research reader compared the notes with page images of the 1631 Cologne edition of Henriquez (p. 100 for the tally, pp. 251-253 for Robert).
    • A second research reader compared the Robert lines with page images of the 1623 Brussels edition (pp. 210-211), of the 1631 edition, and of Surius 1572. That reader also confirmed the tally on the 1623 page image. Capgrave was read only in Horstman's 1901 edition text, not on a page image.

The coordinator read the heading and tally on canvas 0 (2300 x 750 at 2380,4230) on the image ("S. Gaps in coverage:

  • Surius's margins were checked only in the 1572 edition; later Surius printings were not.
  • Henriquez's Menologium Cisterciense (1630) was not checked.
  • The head of the notes panel was not compared with any source.

Differences. The tally agrees in all seven numbers and in their order. "Ceci sanati" is the note-taker's own heading. "Multa et alia signa fecit, quae non sunt scrip[ta]", which follows the list, is John 20:30 and is not evidence for the source. The Robert notes are a digest: they agree with Henriquez in sequence, in key words and in his margin headings, not in running text. "Subd[itis]" is the note-taker's word; Henriquez says "Fratribus quibusdam". The 1623 edition has the same tally (Brussels 1623, scan 91, checked on the page image) and the same Robert margins (pp. 210-211, checked on the page images), so the notes cannot show which edition was used.

Edition used. The edition is Chrysostomus Henriquez, Fasciculus Sanctorum Ordinis Cisterciensis:

  • Cologne: Cholinus, 1631 (p. 100, p. 251, p. 252, p. 253; Munich, BSB, VD17 12:119096F);
  • Brussels, 1623 (tally, Robert).

The author and title are given on the title pages of both editions. For the earlier Robert texts, the comparison used:

  • Surius, De probatis sanctorum historiis III (Cologne 1572), 7 June, on page images (Internet Archive);
  • Nova Legenda Anglie, ed. C. Horstman (1901), II, pp. 340-343, as text only.

Other items and what is not claimed

  • The German entry (174-1v), partial. The upper right of piece 174-1v carries a German administrative entry. It reads "Actum den [?]29 Tag Monatt Mart[ij]? / Vor Ihrer Ehrw(ürden) Herrn Thoma[n/s] Abbt zu St[...]". It is made in the presence of named persons of Mötz?, Untermieming and other places, among them Oßwaldt Schw[...], Martin Rud[...], Philipp Kirch[...] and Christoff [W/Cr]öber(?). It concerns a reckoning ("Raitung") and annual interest ("Jarszinß"), and ends with a sum "4 f[l.]".
    • The legal action is not fully read. No year is legible in the dateline.
    • The modern pencil date "1621 Aug. 29" disagrees with the month written on the leaf, and its year cannot be checked.
    • Abbot Thomas Lugga ruled Stams 1616-1631, but the leaf does not give his surname.
    • A three-line note at the upper left mentions a priest "zu Undermiming" (Untermieming).
    • No outside record of the entry was found or sought in the Stams charter series.
  • Mirror offsets. Piece 174-2v and the lower part of 174-1v carry mirror-image offsets of other German writing, readable in single words only.
  • Piece 174-1r. It is blank apart from pencil notes.
  • Date. The notes date from 1623 or later only if Henriquez is their source, which is probable but not proven (see above). No order between the notes and the German entry is claimed, because the entry's year is not legible. The note-taker, the date of the notes, and their use (sermon preparation is possible) are not claimed.
