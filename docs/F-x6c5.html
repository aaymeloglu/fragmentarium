MdS 58 Ba 6/F2-6 (F-x6c5)

Orselina-Locarno, Biblioteca Madonna del Sasso · catalogued as Unidentified religious text (?), 1251 – 1400 CE

Psalter text, Psalm 49:5-10 (Gallican or Roman wording) on the strip in the "ant" images; the second strip probably Psalm 49:15-20. Kind of book open (psalter, breviary or Bible)

On the strip shown in the "ant" images seven consecutive line fragments fall on Psalm 49:5-10 in order at one-line intervals of 33 to 40 letters, with coloured initials on the verse openings Et, Audi and Non. The strip in the "post" images is only probably Psalm 49:15-20, on "]ero od[", "]um S" one line later and a red stroke where Peccatori would stand. A research reader reread every line on the native images and compared them with an online transcription of the Stuttgart Vulgate, not a page image. The readings iumenta and holocau- exclude the version from the Hebrew; Gallican and Roman wording cannot be separated. The kind of book (psalter, breviary or Bible) is open, and the hidden sides and the two covered strips were not seen. Even if the second strip's proposed text is correct, placement of the two strips in one column is compatible with the text sequence but unproved.

Readings and comparison details

F-x6c5: Psalter text, Psalm 49:5-10 (with probable Psalm 49:15-20)

The strip shown in the two images labelled "ant" carries Psalm 49:5-10 (Vulgate numbering; Psalm 50 in the Hebrew numbering): seven consecutive line fragments fall on the psalm in order, one line of text apart, with coloured initials on the verse openings "Et adnuntiabunt", "Audi populus meus" and "Non accipiam". The second strip, shown in the two "post" images, is rubbed and is only probably from the same psalm, verses 15-20. The text is the plain psalm with one-line verse initials, not a commentary. The kind of book is open: a psalter, a breviary (the book of the daily office, which contains a psalter) or a Bible.

The Fragmentarium record describes Orselina-Locarno, Biblioteca Madonna del Sasso, MdS 58 Ba 6/F2-6: four parchment strips used as spine reinforcements in a copy of Pelbartus of Temesvár, Sermones Pomerii de tempore (Hagenau 1503), two of them covered by the spine lining and two visible, of 42 x 61 mm and 34 x 64 mm; Latin, textualis (the formal Gothic book hand), dated to the end of the thirteenth or the fourteenth century (1251-1400); title "Unidentified religious text (?)". The description fits what the images show, and it records the coloured initials: a red "U" in line 1 and a red and blue "N" in line 5 on one strip, a red "S" in line 5 on the other. It names no text. The initial it gives as "U" is read here as an uncial E, the E of "Et adnuntiabunt". The catalogue's numbering of the strips could not be matched with certainty to the image labels, so this report goes by the image labels.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the "ant" strip as Psalm 49:5-10; probable only for the "post" strip as Psalm 49:15-20. On the "ant" strip seven line fragments ("]icia. Et a[", "]dex est [.]ud[", "] d[..]tuus", "]te holocau[", "]per. Non [", "]gibus tuis [", "]menta [") occur in the psalm in that order with 33 to 40 letters between one and the next, which is one line each time, and the three coloured initials stand on verse openings. Other psalm passages with "holoca-" were considered (Psalms 50:18-21, 39:7, 65:13-15 and 19:4); none has "semper. Non", "gregibus tuis" and "iumenta" in the three lines that follow. On wording, "iumenta" and "holocau[sta]" exclude Jerome's version from the Hebrew, which has "pecudes" and "holocaustomata". The surviving letters do not separate the Gallican psalter (Jerome's revision from the Greek, the psalter of the medieval Latin Bible and of most of the liturgy) from the Roman psalter (the older Latin version long used at Rome), so the version is left as Gallican or Roman. For the "post" strip Psalms 44:3-8 and 50:4-5 were tried and fit less well; the strip is too rubbed to exclude another psalm absolutely.
  • Transcription: image-only first reading and a source-assisted reading. A research reader reread every line of both strips on enlargements of the native images. The assisted reading keeps "holoca[" and "]ment[" from the first reading and replaces its other letter groups. On the "ant" strip the first group of line 3 is uncertain, "tuus" in line 3 and "tuis" in line 6 are read from minims (the short upright strokes of i, u, n and m) and are probable, and line 8 is unread. On the "post" strip only lines 4 and 5 give letters that can be placed, and those are probable, not secure. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the seven lines of the "ant" strip, and lines 2, 4, 5 and 7 of the "post" strip, with Psalm 49 in an online transcription of the Stuttgart Vulgate, which gives the Gallican version beside Jerome's version from the Hebrew. The source was a transcription, not a page image of an edition or of a medieval psalter. For a text as fixed as the psalter that is enough to identify the passage; it shows nothing about which psalter this was. The coordinator read "-icia. Et a-", "-dex e(st)", "holoca-", "-p(er). Non", "-gib(us)" and "-menta" on an enlargement of the "ant" strip (canvas 0, native box 4760,2500,5300,3750) against the same transcription of Psalm 49:5-10; line 3 and the second strip were not checked, and no page image of an edition was used. The hidden sides of the two visible strips and the two covered strips were not seen.

Textual evidence

Each strip appears in two images of the same face. The "ant" strip: canvas 0, mds_58Ba06_rinforzi_ant_cont, native-pixel box 4760,2500,5300,3750, and canvas 1, mds_58Ba06_rinforzi_ant_phy, box 224,4252,813,5375, where the strip is upside down. The "post" strip: canvas 3, mds_58Ba06_rinforzi_post_phy, box 4930,2100,5290,3350, and canvas 2, mds_58Ba06_rinforzi_post_cont, box 308,4492,785,5896, upside down. All four images are 5610 x 8023 pixels and the boxes are approximate. Each strip is a narrow vertical slice of a leaf with 6 to 10 letters of each line, cut at both ends. This is an online transcription, not a page image. The text is biblical and no question of authorship arises.

The strips and what is not claimed

If the placing of the "post" strip is right, the text is compatible with the two strips coming from one column: about six lines of text (verses 10-15, 243 letters) would fall between the last line of the "ant" strip and the first of the "post" strip. That reconstruction would require a column of at least 22 lines. The text sequence alone does not establish that the strips came from the same column; no physical join has been demonstrated. The parchment cover of the same host volume is a separate catalogue record and is not treated here.

Not claimed: the kind of book, which stays open between psalter, breviary and Bible, since the visible faces show no antiphon, rubric or psalm division, and Psalm 49 has no division initial of its own in the liturgical distribution; the version beyond Gallican or Roman; the date and script, which are the catalogue's; the origin; the use. The hidden sides and the two covered strips might settle the kind of book.
