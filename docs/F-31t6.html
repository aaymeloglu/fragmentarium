Frg. 81 (F-31t6)

Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Two manuscripts. Items 81_1-2 (hand 1): anonymous Tractatus passionis 'Scitis quia post biduum' (ed. Ferber 1935), on the arrest, the bloody sweat, the crucifixion outside the gate, Christ's age and the vinegar and gall. Items 81_9-10 (hand 2): Antonius Rampegolus, Figurae Bibliae, chapters Religiosi (end of A and B, Expoliavi me tunica mea) and Perseverantia (B, figura of Apoc. 1, to Exibit homo); items 81_3, 81_4-8 and 81_11 not identified

Hand 1: five passages on both faces of items 81_1 and 81_2 agree in argument, order and authorities with the commentary in Clm 2941 on page images, four collated by a research reader and 81_2r col. B by an independent checker who read it on the image before opening the source; the Stams copy has wording variants and two places of different content (a Bernard quotation on 81_2v, a note on 'Quem queritis' on 81_2r), a different redaction or copy, and Ferber's edition was not seen. Hand 2: 81_9v was collated by a research reader with the Paris 1510 print (scans 345-346, Religiosi), and an independent checker read 81_10r on the image before opening any source and collated it with scan 326 (Perseverantia), finding figura, moralisation, authorities and the next lemma in the same order. Items 81_4-8 and 81_11 are in the same hand but were not collated: the 81_8r lemma 'Docet manus meas ad prelium' occurs in the print only as a quotation (scan 328), and 81_11 ('Sinite parvulos') was not found in the print. Item 81_3 is unread, the material (paper per the catalogue) was not checked, and there is no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-31t6: Anonymous, Tractatus passionis "Scitis quia post biduum" (items 81_1-2), and Antonius Rampegolus, Figurae Bibliae, chapters Perseverantia and Religiosi (items 81_9-10)

This record holds pieces of two different manuscripts, which the catalogue already separates by hand. The pieces in the first hand, items 81_1 and 81_2, are from an anonymous Latin commentary on the Passion narrative of the four gospels, beginning Scitis quia post biduum Pascha fiet, edited by M. Reinhildis Ferber (1935) as the Latin source of the German Kreuziger of Johannes von Frankenstein. They cover the arrest of Christ, the bloody sweat, the crucifixion outside the gate, the age of Christ at his death, and the vinegar and gall. Two of the pieces in the second hand, items 81_9 and 81_10, are from the Figurae Bibliae of Antonius Rampegolus (died 1423), an alphabetical handbook of moral topics for preachers: item 81_9 has part of the chapter Religiosi and item 81_10 part of the chapter Perseverantia. The other pieces in the second hand, items 81_4-8 and 81_11, are not identified.

The Fragmentarium record describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 81: paper, eleven items, 205 x 168 mm, title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description, with a reconstruction summary by Claudia Sojer assigning items 81_1-3 to one hand and 81_4-11 to another. It names no text. The six images each show several pieces mounted together: canvases 0 and 1 items 1-6 (fronts and backs), canvases 2 and 3 items 7-8, canvases 4 and 5 items 9-11. The division of hands fits the images. Items 81_1 and 81_2 are wide strips from a two-column book in gothic textura, each showing the outer column of one page, the fold, and the two columns of the facing page, so each is probably part of a bifolium (a folded sheet); item 81_3 is a small holed scrap. Items 81_4-11 are in a small, fast, heavily abbreviated cursive with red initials. The catalogue says paper; the first reading took the pieces for parchment; the material was not checked.

  • Identification: identified, high, for items 81_1-2 (hand 1) and items 81_9-10 (hand 2). Items 81_3, 81_4-8 and 81_11 are not identified.
    • Hand 1: five passages, on both faces of item 81_1 and both faces of item 81_2, agree with the commentary in Munich, BSB, Clm 2941 in argument, order of questions, authorities (Hebrews, Chrysostom, Isaiah 53:7, "dominus Albertus", Theophilus) and transitions, between red-framed lemmata (quoted gospel phrases) such as "Sequitur. Dormite" and "Sequitur. In littera". The fragment carries the commentary itself, not a quotation from it. The Stams copy differs from Clm 2941 in wording ("intimus" for "intrinsecus", "naturalem" for "supernaturalem", "universo mundo" for "toti et universo orbi") and twice in content: on 81_2v a boxed quotation of Bernard (O Maria cur filius ...) follows the bloody sweat where Clm passes to Adhuc eo loquente, and on 81_2r a note on "Quem queritis" follows the Isaiah quotation where Clm has Augustine on the falling back. This tract circulates in several versions; these are differences of redaction or copy, not of work. Thomas Aquinas, Summa theologiae III q. 46 a. 10, shares the phrase "ut veritas responderet figurae", but the surrounding text is not his.
    • Hand 2: on item 81_9 the end of section A of Religiosi (the three strong men at the cistern of Bethlehem, 2 Sam 23) and section B (Expoliavi me tunica mea, Song of Songs 5, with the figura, the Old Testament type, of Jonathan giving his tunic to David, 1 Sam 18) agree with the Paris 1510 print in sequence of lemma, moralisation and figura. On item 81_10 section B of Perseverantia (the figura of Apocalypse 1, the Son of Man vestitum podere) runs into the opening of the next section, Exibit homo ad opus suum, as in the print. Differences are of wording: "veste propria se exuens" for "vestem propriam extrahens", "attingens" for "pertingens", "tui certaminis obtinere" for "certaminis tui habere". Perseverantia falls before Religiosi in the alphabetical order, which is consistent with one codex.
    • Not identified: items 81_4-8 and 81_11 are in the same hand, with lemmata under red initials and "Fig(ura)" sections, but were not collated. The lemma read on 81_8r, "Docet manus meas ad prelium", occurs in the 1510 print only as a quotation inside the closing figura of Perseverantia (scan 328), not as a lemma, and no entry with the lemma of 81_11v, Sinite parvulos venire ad me, was found in the print. They may come from the same work, but that is not shown.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading and the source-assisted reading. The first reading did not cover canvases 2, 3 and 5. A research reader reread on the native images 81_1r col. B (lower) and col. C (upper), 81_1v col. B, 81_2v col. B (upper) two bands of 81_9v, and parts of 81_7r and 81_8r, all with the source in hand. An independent checker read 81_2r col. B, lines 1-21, and the upper part of 81_10r before opening the sources, and the lower part of 81_10r after (see Verification); its lines are given in the tables below, not in the assisted file. The reading of 81_9r rests on the first reading. The hand-2 cursive is hard to read, and the readings of 81_9v and 81_10r are reliable mainly in the phrases compared. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared four hand-1 passages (81_1r cols. B and C, 81_1v col. B, 81_2v col. B) with page images of Clm 2941, images 397, 416 and 417, and the hand-2 passages of 81_9v with page images of the Paris 1510 print, scans 345-346. An independent checker read 81_2r col. B, lines 1-21 (canvas 0, native box 5050,2580,950,1370), a face not collated by the research reader, on the image before opening the source, and compared it with the page image of Clm 2941, image 397, col. b: lines 1-17 follow Clm argument by argument, with the same authorities and the same two causes, and the text diverges after line 17. For hand 2 it read the upper part of 81_10r (canvas 4, native boxes 5000,2700,1720,750 and 5000,3400,1720,750) on the image before opening any source, found the page through the MDZ full text of the print on its anchor phrases ("podere", "talos", "fidelis"), and compared it with the page image of the Paris 1510 print, scan 326, chapter Perseverantia: the figura, the moralisation, the authorities and the next lemma agree in order, with the variants given below. It also looked for the lemmata of 81_8r and 81_11 in the print and did not find them as lemmata. Gaps: Ferber's edition was seen only through a search inside its Google Books volume, so the hand-1 text was compared with one manuscript, not the edition; items 81_4-7 were not examined against the print, 81_11 only briefly, and 81_3 not at all; 81_2r after line 17 and the lower part of 81_10r were read only with the source in hand. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

Other items and limits

Items 81_4-8 and 81_11 (hand 2) are not identified. Lemmata and figurae read on them, mostly in the first reading: 81_6v Psalm 106:26 Ascendunt usque ad celos; 81_7r a figura of Joseph and Benjamin; 81_7v a section on kin ("cognatio") under a red C; 81_8r Docet manus meas ad prelium and Notas mihi fecisti vias vite, with a figura from Exodus; 81_10v Sagitte potentis acute; 81_11r a numbered list ("6o vestit vasa ad sacrificium ...", "palleum"); 81_11v Sinite parvulos venire ad me (Mt 19). Searches of the 1510 print's full text did not find these as lemmata, but its OCR is unreliable and that is not a negative result. Item 81_3 is assigned to hand 1 by the catalogue only and is unread. Item 81_2r beyond the checker's lines, with Papias and a figura of Saul, was not collated.

Not claimed: that items 81_4-8 and 81_11 are from the Figurae Bibliae; that 81_9 and 81_10 come from one codex; which redaction of the Passion commentary the hand-1 pieces follow; the material, date or origin of either manuscript; or the arrangement of the bifolia.
