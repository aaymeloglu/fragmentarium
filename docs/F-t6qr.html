Justinian, Codex with the Accursian Glossa ordinaria: C. 4.13 Auth. Habita (main text and gloss); gloss on C. 4.21.21 to C. 4.22.1

Two binding strips from two leaves of a glossed Codex. Strip 254-1 has the framed main text of the Authentica Habita under the rubric 'l. Ex patroni. Nou[a constitutio]' with Accursius' gloss (Speciali, Optione). Strip 254-2 has Accursius' gloss on C. 4.21.21 (Dolo, the gloss on 'subtili reuerentia', Per solitam, Sopita with its allegation chain) and the C. 4.22 rubric. A research reader compared the line openings with page images of the 1574 Accursian Codex (cols. 763-765, 831-832), and line openings below Plus valere (transactio, precio, arbi. tu. l. fi., usuf. legato, non constabat aliud) follow the printed In contractibus gloss in order on the same source. Only line openings survive, so the match rests on short openings in sequence; lemmata are sometimes shorter than in print, and the redaction of the gloss, date and origin are not established; versos unread.

Readings and comparison details

F-t6qr: Justinian, Codex with the Accursian Glossa ordinaria, C. 4.13 (Auth. Habita) and C. 4.21.21-4.22.1

Stams, Frg. 254 consists of two parchment binding strips from two different leaves of a glossed manuscript of Justinian's Codex. Each strip preserves only the left-hand edge of a column, so every line is cut after its first 8 to 15 characters.

  • Strip 254-1: the main text of C. 4.13 with the Authentica Habita of Frederick I. This is the privilege for students placed after C. 4.13.5 Ex patroni, here under the rubric "l. Ex patroni. Nou[a constitutio]" and enclosed in a red and blue ruled frame. Below it is the ordinary gloss of Accursius on Habita.
  • Strip 254-2: Accursius' gloss on C. 4.21.21 (Cum quidam instrumentum), the rubric of C. 4.22 (Plus valere quod agitur quam quod simulate concipitur), and, a few lines below it, the allegation chain of the Accursian gloss on C. 4.22.1 (In contractibus).

The catalogue record is at fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-t6qr: Stams, Stams (Stealth Published), Frg. 254, parchment, two fragment items, 449 x 45 mm (item 254_1 444/449 x 40/45 mm), online since 2021-09-18. Its title is "x" and it links no description. The catalogue therefore identified no text. Its count of two items and its dimensions fit the two strips in the photographs.

  • Identification: identified, confidence high.
    • Strip 254-1. The line openings of the framed main text follow the Habita text in sequence over some twenty lines, from "[H]abita su[p(er)]" to "[...] iustissim[a]". The rubric names the Codex law after which the constitution stands, and the gloss block below the frame has the lemma "[E]x patroni." (on C. 4.13.5) before the gloss on Habita.
    • Strip 254-2. The gloss lemmata, which are the quoted words a gloss comments on, run Dolo, a lemma read "Re?ictam" whose gloss matches the printed gloss on subtili reuerentia, Ei, Per solitam, Sopita, in the order of these words in C. 4.21.21. They are followed by the Plus valere rubric. The allegation chain of the Sopita gloss (its string of references to other laws) agrees with the Accursian gloss as printed: "fr(atr)is ... inst. de p[oe.] ... de ag[ri.] ... post s(ente)ntiam ... instru(m). l. i. ... iudex. ibi sic ... fuit d[...]". Below the rubric, line openings "[...]t(ra)nsact[io]", "[...]precio.", "arbi. tu. l. fi.", "[usu]f. legato" and "[non] constabat a[liud]" follow the allegation chain of the printed gloss on C. 4.22.1 In contractibus in its order.
    • Alternatives. I considered the Libri feudorum and the tenth collatio of the Volumen, where constitutions of Frederick also circulate. They are not the containing work: strip 254-2 glosses C. 4.21.21 of the Codex itself, and the gloss on strip 254-1 matches the Codex gloss on Habita. A canon-law gloss, suggested by a first impression of the strips, is not supported.
    • Differences from the 1574 print. Lemmata differ in two places ("Sopita" against "Et sopita"; "Re?ictam", a word not in C. 4.21.21, where the print has "Subtili reuerentia"). The Pagina gloss appears to end after about five lines with "s. e(o). l. ii.", but only line openings could be compared. One Optione allegation cites the same Digest law by another incipit: "l. si q(ui)s a[...]" against "l. si quis intentione".
    • Which version of the Accursian gloss the manuscript carries is not established.
  • Transcription: I made the source-assisted reading, rereading the rotated recto of both strips in bands of 10 to 15 lines. The Habita main text, the Speciali and Optione glosses, and the left-strip glosses from Pagina to Plus valere were reread on the image. The first reading, made from the images alone, had taken the framed main text for gloss; it was corrected against the source. Accuracy has not been measured. Readings:
    • image-only first reading
    • source-assisted reading
  • Verification: a research reader compared the strips with page images of the 1574 Accursian Codex: Habita in cols. 763-765 and C. 4.21.21-4.22 in cols. 831-832. Because only line openings survive, the match rests on short line openings in sequence, not on complete lines.

The left strip below the Plus valere rubric (canvas 0, native box 1590,3000-2400,4060, read rotated 180 degrees) was read on the image and compared with the 1574 page image, col. 832 (archive.org n542):

  • Directly below "[P]lus ual[ere] / agitur" stand three lines, "[s]ic paran[...]", "[.]uo fi?at[...]", "[...]m. it(em)? a[...]". They do not follow the printed title summary "Quia per scripturam ..." and are not matched.
  • A red two-line initial follows (I or T; its top bar runs both ways from the stem), then about eight faded lines, among them "[...]stitut[...]" and "[...]ns. §. i. (et) de".
  • The next openings, "[...]re donat. m[...]", "[...]ue. (et) circa.", "[...]t(ra)nsact[io]", "l. ii. emp(t). l. ii[...]", "[...]precio.", "arbi. tu. l. fi. h[...]", "[usu]f. legato" and "[non] constabat a[liud]", fall in the order of the printed gloss on In contractibus ("... de dona. inter ui. & ux. l. si sponsus. §. circa ... de transactio. l. cum ea ... de contrah. empt. l. 3 ... si ex precio ... infra arbi. tu. l. fi. ... ff. de usuf. le. ... uel ibi non constabat aliud actu"). Only line openings survive.

An independent checker also read the rubric and opening lines of the framed Habita on separate crops.

Not covered:

  • most of the gloss block on strip 254-1 between the frame and the Habita gloss (gloss on C. 4.13; the lemma "[E]x patroni." stands near its end);
  • the versos of both strips, which are abraded and carry later notes.

Differences:

  • The manuscript writes "i(n)ferre", "irrog[ata]" and "carea(n)t" where the 1574 text has "inferat", "irroganda" and "se carituros".
  • The frame closes at "iustissim[a]". Whether "a tali conamine cadat" followed outside the frame or is lost cannot be seen. The two words before "iustissim[a]" are not resolved.
  • The display capitals before the two-line H of Habita (Q or C, O, N) are not explained.
  • Gloss lemmata differ from the print, as noted above. Its gloss signes restart at "a" for Sopita, where 1574 has "g".
  • The Pagina gloss and an Ei gloss reading "p(re)sumit(?)" do not follow the printed wording. These lines are uncertain and were compared only by their openings.
  • The three lines directly below the Plus valere rubric do not follow the printed title summary "Quia per scripturam ...".

Edition compared: Codicis DN. Iustiniani ... repetitae praelectionis, libri 12. Accursii commentariis ... illustrati, 1574. I read page images, not OCR:

  • cols. 763-764
  • col. 765
  • cols. 831-832

The gloss is attributed to Accursius by the title page of that edition. The wording of Habita and the lemmata of C. 4.21.21 were also checked against Krüger's text of the Codex at droitromain, CJ 4.

Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

The two strips come from two different leaves. In the 1574 print their passages are about 68 columns apart, so they are not parts of one leaf. Their order and distance in the manuscript are not known.

The versos (canvas 1) are heavily abraded. The left verso strip has a red and blue frame with faint line ends, and the right verso strip has a later cursive note of about eighteen lines; neither was read. Faint later marginal notes on the recto were not read either. The block of gloss on strip 254-1 between the frame and the Habita gloss was not collated. It is gloss on C. 4.13: a two-line uncial E opens the lemma "[E]x patroni." (C. 4.13.5), which is followed by a blue-paraph lemma and the two-colour H of the Habita gloss; a line above reads "[...] posse.".

Not claimed:

  • the date and origin of the manuscript;
  • which redaction of the Accursian gloss it carries;
  • any relation between this copy and the 1574 print or other witnesses.

The date 1574 is that of the edition compared, not of the fragment.
