Nicholas Trevet OP, commentary on Augustine, De civitate Dei, III.17-30

The quire is not a chronicle but a commentary on De civitate Dei book III: the head 'L. 3' and the marginal chapter numbers are Augustine's, and the text numbers the events he alludes to and supplies them from Livy, Orosius, Valerius and Seneca. A research reader (Claude) collated 1r lines 1-11 and 1v lines 10-21 with Paris, BnF, Latin 2075, ff. 125v-126r, on the page images, and compared four more passages (2r, 3r, 6r-6v, 8v) with the CoMMA machine-read text of that manuscript only; the wording agrees apart from small variants (duumvirorum/duorum virorum, ita quod ... traderent/Itaque ... redderent, fuisse/habuisse), and the printed commentary on book III (Thomas Waleys, GW 2883) is a different text. The attribution to Trevet rests on the BnF notice (seen through a summary), a later humanistic note on f. 1r of Latin 2075 and Ridewall's citation of the commentator's remark on Marcus Drusus as reported by Kaeppeli; no medieval rubric and no second Trevet manuscript were seen, and Kaeppeli's locus III.20 against the fragment's 'C. 26' is unexplained. Year numerals, cross-reference numbers and some proper names were read with the source at hand and not confirmed by readers without it.

Readings and comparison details

F-4tsf: Nicholas Trevet, commentary on Augustine, De civitate Dei, III.17-30

This parchment quire in Bern carries part of the commentary on Augustine's De civitate Dei by the English Dominican Nicholas Trevet (died about 1334): book III, from the fourth of the numbered points of chapter 17 to chapter 30, where it breaks off in the account of Caesar at Ariminum. It is not a chronicle. The running head "L. 3" and the marginal chapter numbers are those of Augustine's book III; the text takes the events of Roman history to which Augustine alludes one by one ("Quartum quod hic tangit ...", "the fourth thing he touches on here"), supplies each from Livy, Orosius, Valerius Maximus or Seneca, and closes each with Augustine's own words as a lemma, the phrase being commented on ("unde dicit: ubi erant, scilicet dii eorum, quando ..."). Two passages agree in wording and sequence with a manuscript of Trevet's commentary, Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, Latin 2075, ff. 125v-126r, read on the page images, and four more agree with a machine-read text of the same manuscript.

The Fragmentarium record is Bern, Burgerbibliothek, Cod. A 94.24, title "Compilation of Roman History", parchment, four bifolia forming one quire, 225 x 160 mm, France, 14th century, from the property of Jacques Bongars, online since 6 September 2021, with 17 images. Its description (Florian Mittenhuber, 2021, from e-codices) reads the head as "Liber 3us", counts chapters 17-30 with chapter 17 divided into sections 4-15, gives the incipit "... fame etc. Quartum quod hic tangit, contigit anno ab urbe condita 353, de quo scribit Titus Livius 5 dicens, quod tristem hyemem gravis pestilensque estas excepit" and the explicit "... Cesar ut Ariminum venit, quinque cohortes, quas tunc solas habuit", and takes the text for part of the third book of an unidentified (world?) chronicle compiled chiefly from Livy and Orosius, "probably unedited". The physical description and the incipit and explicit fit. The description of the work does not: the book and chapters are Augustine's, and the record does not mention Augustine or name an author.

  • Identification: identified, high for the text. The agreement is in the commentator's own prose, not in the historians he quotes: the numbering of the points of III.17, the lemma formula, cross-references to his own earlier exposition ("patet supra li. 2 c. ..."), and first-person source notes ("non tangitur in Orosio nec in Eutropio, sed puto ...", "non legi", "nil inveni in Orosio"). The long extracts from Livy and Orosius are quotations inside it, so those authors are sources, not the containing work. The other commentary on these chapters that was compared, Thomas Waleys on books I-X as printed at Strasbourg about 1468 (GW 2883), was read on the page for III.17-19: it treats the same chapters lemma by lemma ("Et infra ...") in different words and has none of the numbered points, though it shares sources and some citations (both cite Seneca's Declamationes on Metellus). John Ridewall's commentary was not compared. The attribution to Trevet rests on three things, none of them a medieval rubric: the BnF notice of Latin 2075 (known here only through a summary of the Biblissima record of it); a note in a later, humanistic hand at the head of f. 1r of that manuscript, read on the image, "Post has tabulas est libellus f. Nicolai de Treveth enarrans locos obscuriores qui in libris de civitate dei reperiuntur"; and John Ridewall's report, quoted by Kaeppeli (1959, p. 203), that an earlier commentator on the work held that Orosius does not mention Marcus Drusus, a remark the fragment contains and which Kaeppeli, following Smalley, takes as aimed at Trevet. Kaeppeli gives Ridewall's locus as III, 20; in the fragment the remark stands under the marginal "C. 26". That difference is unexplained. The fragment and Latin 2075 differ in small points of wording and probably in some numerals (listed below).
  • Transcription: source-assisted, selected passages. The image-only first reading was made from the images alone and covers parts of ff. 1v-4r. A research reader (Claude) reread on the native images f. 1r lines 1-11, f. 1v lines 10-21, f. 2r lines 6-9, f. 3r about lines 13-19, the last lines of f. 6r, f. 6v lines 1-9 and the last nine lines of f. 8v; that revision is the source-assisted reading. Only f. 1r lines 1-6, f. 2r and f. 3r were read before the text of Latin 2075 was in view. Two further research readers read crops of f. 1r and the foot of f. 6r without the source: they confirmed the content (the lectisternium for Apollo, Latona, Diana, Mercury and Neptune, the ten-year siege of Veii, Camillus, "de Druso ... Valerius li. ix") but could not resolve the year numerals or the book and chapter numbers of the cross-references on f. 1r, and differed over the proper names on f. 6r. Those numerals and names are therefore marked uncertain below and are not offered as read from the leaf. Folios 2v, 3v, 4r-5v, 7v and 8r were looked at only reduced. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader (Claude) compared f. 1r lines 1-11 and f. 1v lines 10-21 with Latin 2075, ff. 125v and 126r, on the BnF page images (black-and-white microfilm), and compared the passages on ff. 2r, 3r, 6r-6v and 8v with the CoMMA machine-read text of the same manuscript only (automatic recognition with roughly one character in ten wrong, not checked on the image for those passages). The same reader read Waleys in the Munich copy of GW 2883 on the page images. COORDINATOR CHECK: the coordinator (Claude) read f. 1v lines 13-21 (canvas 1, native box 1100,2300,5900,3900) on the image against BnF Latin 2075, f. 126r col. b: from "miserunt legatos ad Samnites" through "in Caudinas furculas etc. Decimo tangit" the leaf agrees with Latin 2075 word for word and line for line. Two further readers, working from shuffled crops without the source, confirmed the content of 1r and 6r (the lectisternium with its six gods, the ten-year siege of Veii, Camillus, "de Druso ... Valerius li. ix") but not the year numerals or the proper names on 6r, which stay uncertain. Gaps: the two manuscripts were not collated throughout; ff. 126v-131 of Latin 2075 were not seen as images; the opening of the commentary at f. 113 of Latin 2075 was not viewed; no second manuscript of Trevet's commentary was compared; Kaeppeli's repertory of Dominican writers was not consulted.

Manuscript and print compared, and the attribution

Paris, BnF, Latin 2075: Gallica; page images f. 125r, with the heading "Capitulum xvii", f. 125v (not foliated on the image; it lies between the rectos numbered 125 and 126), f. 126r, f. 1r with the note naming Trevet. The BnF notice (Archives et manuscrits) is reported as giving "F. 113, Nicolaus Trevethus, Commentarius in libros de Civitate Dei, 'Gloriosa dicta sunt ...'", after Robert Kilwardby's tables to the same work; it was consulted only through a summary of the Biblissima record.

Thomas Kaeppeli, "Une critique du commentaire de Nicolas Trevet sur le De civitate Dei", Archivum Fratrum Praedicatorum 29 (1959), 200-205 (PDF): p. 201 on the commentaries of Trevet, Waleys, Ridewall and Baconthorpe, and on the manuscripts and editions joining Waleys on books I-X to Trevet on XI-XXII; p. 203 for Ridewall's words, from Oxford, Corpus Christi College 186, f. 148: "de Marco Druso, de quo tamen non facit Orosius mentionem, sicut videtur uni doctori valenti, qui scripsit super istud volumen beati Augustini".

Augustine, De civitate Dei, with the commentaries of Thomas Waleys and Nicholas Trevet, Strasbourg, Johann Mentelin, not after 1468 (GW 2883), Munich copy: scan 558, on III.17, scan 560, on III.18-19. That the commentary on book III in this edition is Waleys's follows from the Gesamtkatalog der Wiegendrucke entry and Kaeppeli's account, not from a heading read on these pages.

Leaf order and what is not claimed

The eight leaves follow one another in the order of the modern foliation as far as the passages read show: the points of chapter 17 on ff. 1r-2r, chapter 19 on f. 3r, chapter 26 on f. 6r, Caesar and Pompey at the foot of f. 8v. The catalogue's chapter range 17-30 was not checked face by face, and whether the quire runs without a gap was not tested. Later cursive notes in the margins were not read.

Not claimed: a date or origin for the fragment beyond the catalogue's; any relation between this copy and Latin 2075; that the fragment is unknown to the literature on Trevet (one web search found no mention; Kaeppeli's list of manuscripts was not seen); that Trevet's commentary on these chapters is unprinted (Kaeppeli says the editions give Waleys for books I-X, and no edition of Trevet on book III was found, but the early prints were not surveyed). The date given for ff. 113-161 of Latin 2075 in the summary of the BnF notice was not verified and is not used.
