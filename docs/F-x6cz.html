Frg. 134 (F-x6cz)

Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, 1400 – 1499 CE

Noted hymnal, probably Cistercian: Common of Saints (end of Deus tuorum militum, Iesu corona virginum, Christe cunctorum) and hymns of an Office of the Passion, probably of the Crown of Thorns (end of Bonaventure's Christum ducem; Beata Christi passio with Qui pressura in the arrangement of Mone's MS K; Iesu velatus facie at Terce); Mone I nos. 85-87

The hymns were read on all four pages and compared with Mone, Lateinische Hymnen I nos. 84-91 (archive.org OCR text) and with Waddell's Primitive Cistercian Breviary (Google Books snippets); an independent research reader read canvas 1 left against Waddell and Cantus and found agreement apart from 'adque' and 'mentibus' (Waddell 'atque', 'sensibus'). Qui pressura agrees with every feature Mone reports for his Karlsruhe MS K, headed for the feast of the Crown of Thorns, and Iesu velatus facie is listed under De corona spinea in Clm 2541 (at Terce) and Heidelberg Cod. Sal. X,7 (Salem) in MMMO; the leaf itself has no feast heading, so the occasion is probable. The Deus tuorum militum ending, the division of Iesu corona virginum and the Gloria tibi domine doxology make Cistercian use probable. The marginal rubric on canvas 1 right is unread, the leaf order is not settled, and the date and origin were not examined.

Readings and comparison details

F-x6cz: Noted hymnal, probably Cistercian, hymns of the Common of Saints and of an Office of the Passion (Bonaventure, Christum ducem and Qui pressura; Iesu velatus facie)

This parchment bifolium at Stams comes from a noted hymnal, a choir book of Office hymns with every strophe written under its melody. Its two leaves are not consecutive. One leaf carries hymns of the Common of Saints: the end of the hymn for one martyr, Deus tuorum militum, in the form ending "letemur in perpetuum iuncti polorum atriis"; under "De virginibus" the hymn Iesu corona virginum (Cantus ID 008330) with its second part, beginning Quocumque pergis, marked off by a red "ymnus"; and under "In dedicatione" the first strophe of Christe cunctorum dominator alme (008275.1). The other leaf carries hymns of an Office of the Passion: the end of Christum ducem, the Lauds hymn of Bonaventure's Officium de passione Domini, with the doxology "Gloria tibi domine, gloria unigenito"; then the strophe Beata Christi passio followed by Bonaventure's Qui pressura mortis dura in a rearranged form; then, under "ad terciam ymnus", Iesu velatus facie fuisti sol iusticie. The rearranged Qui pressura agrees with a manuscript that Mone reports for the feast of the Crown of Thorns, and Iesu velatus facie stands under that feast in two other books, so a Crown of Thorns office is probable, though the leaf itself has no feast heading. The forms of the Common hymns point to Cistercian use.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 134: parchment, one fragment item, 354 x 230 mm (measured 313/354 x 187/230 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), dated 1400-1499, title "x", no linked description, with two images labelled 01r and 01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images, a single bifolium with one column of eight notated lines per page.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the hymns and their order; the occasion of the Passion hymns and the Cistercian use are probable, not shown. The basis is the hymn texts read on all four pages, compared with Mone's edition and with Waddell's edition of the Cistercian breviary. Qui pressura on the leaf has every feature Mone reports for his Karlsruhe manuscript K (headed there "ad tertiam in festo de spinea corona", for the feast of the Crown of Thorns): the strophe Beata Christi passio placed before it, the strophe In flagellis left out, the strophes In amara and Sanguis Christi before Nostre genti, the reading "tue corone" for "tue mortis", "da venire" for "et venire", and the closing strophe Laus honor virtus. On the leaf that strophe begins "Tue genti" and reads "gloria" where K has "victoria". The hour of Beata Christi passio and Qui pressura on the leaf is not settled: the red rubric in the margin may be "ad (com)pl(etorium)" or "ad pri(mam)" and was not read with confidence; K has them at Terce. Mone prints the Terce hymn as Tu qui velatus facie, at Prime; the leaf begins "[I]esu velatus" and puts it at Terce, as in the Crown of Thorns office of Munich, BSB Clm 2541, f. 145v. The Common hymns follow the Cistercian forms: the Deus tuorum militum ending "letemur in perpetuum iuncti polorum atriis" (which Blume found only in Milanese and Cistercian books), Iesu corona virginum divided at Quocumque pergis with the doxology "Gloria tibi domine", and the full doxology "Gloria tibi domine, gloria unigenito, una cum sancto spiritu" on the Passion leaf. This rests on snippets of Waddell's edition. Christe cunctorum for the Dedication is not diagnostic on its own. Differences from Waddell: "adque" for "atque" and "adauge mentibus" for "adauge sensibus" in Iesu corona virginum.
  • Transcription: source-assisted. The image-only first reading covers every text line; the source-assisted reading is a research reader's reread on the native images of both pages of canvas 0 and the right page of canvas 1, made with Mone and Waddell at hand; an independent research reader read the left page of canvas 1. It corrects the first reading's "con[...] letemur [...] p(e)c(c)atum macte polo" to "[l]etemur in perpetuum iu(n)cti polo[rum a]triis" and confirms "tue co/rone". Unresolved: the abraded opening of the first line of canvas 0 right, the word erased after "Beata", the marginal rubric beside it, and the faint "co/rone". The neumes were not transcribed. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the Passion hymns with Mone's printed text (archive.org OCR text, not page images) and the Common hymns with Waddell's edition in Google Books snippets (page images not seen). An independent research reader read the left page of canvas 1, region 1750,1150,2050,2650, before comparison and found word-for-word agreement with Waddell's text of Iesu corona virginum strophes III-V and with the Cantus standard text of Christe cunctorum strophe I, apart from "adque" and "mentibus". The same reader found Jesu velatus facie (Cantus a03058) in two inventories of Musica medii aevi (MMMO), both under De corona spinea: Munich, BSB Clm 2541, f. 145v, at Terce, and Heidelberg, Cod. Sal. X,7 (from the Cistercian abbey of Salem), f. 146r. Passage checked independently: canvas 1, left page, region 1750,1150,2050,2650, "adque laudibus post te canentes cursitant" to "vota pariterque ymnum cerne benignus", read on the image against Waddell, The Primitive Cistercian Breviary, p. 630 (Google Books), and Cantus Index 008275.1 (Cantus Index). Gaps: Mone and Waddell were not seen as page images; Analecta Hymnica 50 (Bonaventure's office) and Mone's manuscript K were not seen; the top line of each page is damaged.

Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

  • Leaf order: each leaf reads in its own order (leaf A: canvas 0 right, then canvas 1 left; leaf B: canvas 1 right, then canvas 0 left), and the text does not run across the fold, so leaves are missing between them. Whether leaf A or leaf B came first in the book is not settled: the bifolium could be folded either way.
  • Notation: leaf A has larger rhombic notes; leaf B has small tailed notes. Over "Xpe cunctorum" and "patris eterni" on canvas 1 left, a second, lighter layer of small stemmed notes stands beside the rhombic notes. Whether these differences mean different notators or later additions was not examined.
  • Other marks: modern pencil on canvas 0, "Aus IV/77", "1663/84" and a further note, and the shelfmarks "Stams, STA, Frg. 134r" and "134v".
  • Not claimed: the date (the catalogue's 1400-1499 was not tested); that the book was made or used at Stams; the feast of the Passion hymns, which the leaf does not name; the hour of Beata Christi passio and Qui pressura; and any relation to Mone's manuscript K or to Clm 2541 and Cod. Sal. X,7 beyond the shared texts.
