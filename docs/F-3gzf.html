Justinian, Digesta (Digestum vetus) 15.1.4-11, De peculio, with the Glossa ordinaria of Accursius

The main text of all four faces agrees in wording and sequence with D. 15.1.4.1-5.1, 7.1-8, 9.4-9.6 and 10-11.3, and the gloss of canvas 2 agrees gloss by gloss with the Accursian ordinary gloss in the Paris 1566 glossed Digestum vetus (machine text, page image for glosses e-i). On canvases 0 and 3 single glosses were matched with the same print in machine text, from the first reading; the canvas 1 gloss 'non voluit' was matched only with a search snippet of a Lyon 1627 edition. A research reader compared the main text with a transcription of Mommsen's text; the coordinator read canvas 2, main column, lines 1-12 on the image against the transcription of D. 15.1.10-11. The leaf has small differences from Mommsen's text (obtulit, dedit, exegit, subtractam, captus sit; the Greek word of D. 15.1.7.2 not written), and several glosses are shorter than the print. The ac. sigla stand. Two blind readers did not confirm the siglum az. after the gloss 'sed non abstulisset' (the other possible az., after 'domino faciente deductionem', was not checked) or the word Depositi after the inscription of D. 15.1.5, which are left uncertain. The pieces are the upper part of one two-column leaf; the labelled versos are the true recto.

Readings and comparison details

F-3gzf: Justinian, Digesta (Digestum vetus) 15.1 De peculio, with the Glossa ordinaria of Accursius

The two parchment pieces are the upper part of one leaf of a glossed Digestum vetus, the first of the three medieval volumes of Justinian's Digest. The main text, in a large hand, is from book 15, title 1, De peculio, on the actions against a master or father up to the value of the fund (peculium) held by a slave or son: D. 15.1.4.1 to 15.1.5.1, 15.1.7.1 to 15.1.8, 15.1.9.4 to 15.1.9.6 and 15.1.10 to 15.1.11.3, with gaps between these stretches where the lower part of each column is lost. Round it, in a small hand, stands the ordinary gloss (the standard marginal commentary) of Accursius on the same laws, most of its entries ending with the siglum ac. (an abbreviated signature)

The Fragmentarium record describes Innsbruck, Universitäts- und Landesbibliothek Tirol, Frg. C30: two parchment particles, 135/149 x 91/103 mm, Latin, dated 1400-1450, with the title "Legal text" and red initials and paragraph marks; it names no work, author or passage. That description fits, as far as it goes. The date is the catalogue's statement and was not examined here.

  • Identification: identified, high confidence, for both layers. The main text agrees with D. 15.1 in four passages, one on each face, in wording and in the sequence of the fragments of the title. On canvas 2 some fifteen glosses agree, gloss by gloss and allegation by allegation, with the Accursian gloss as printed at Paris in 1566, and single glosses on canvases 0 and 3 agree with the same print (machine text, from the first reading); the gloss "non voluit" on canvas 1 was matched only with a search snippet of a Lyon 1627 edition. An earlier apparatus to the Digest, by Azo or Hugolinus, was considered for the gloss because a sign read az. stands after one or two entries (after "sed non abstulisset" it was not confirmed by the blind readers; after "domino faciente deductionem" it was not checked); the glosses compared are those of the Accursian apparatus, where the print itself signs one of them "Azo", and most end with ac. A later commentary built on Accursius is not the layer here, at least on canvas 2, where the gloss was read in full: the entries are the Accursian glosses themselves, set round the text they gloss and keyed to it by reference signs, not a discussion of them. Some glosses are shorter than the print and two stand in the reverse order; the main text differs from Mommsen's edition in the small ways listed under Textual evidence.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading and the source-assisted reading. A research reader reread on the images, with the Digest and the printed gloss at hand: all 24 main-text lines and the whole gloss of canvas 2; the lower twelve or thirteen lines of the main column on canvases 0 and 3; the upper twelve lines on canvas 1. The gloss of canvases 0, 1 and 3 was checked only at the phrases quoted below, from the first reading and the overview images. The other lines, including the first lines of canvases 0 and 3 and the last of canvas 1, rest on the first reading. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the main text of all four faces with a transcription of Mommsen's text of the Digest (no page image of the edition was consulted), and the gloss with the machine text of the 1566 print, and with its page image for the glosses e to i of column 1520. The coordinator (Claude) read canvas 2, main column, lines 1-12 ("haberi in posteriorem condempnationem" to "si manumisso eo agatur") on the image against the transcription of D. 15.1.10-11 at The Latin Library, a transcription and not a page image: the leaf follows the Digest in sequence with the variants listed below. The coordinator did not recheck the gloss. Two fresh readers, given two neutral crops of this record without transcription or source, did not confirm the siglum az. after the gloss "sed non abstulisset" on canvas 2: both read a d with a z-shaped sign, neither az. nor ac. They did not confirm the word read Depositi after the inscription on canvas 3: both read "-posita" with an initial of uncertain form. Both read the book number of that inscription as "l. xx" followed by a tall stroke and three minims, not "xxix", and both read "de paulo" in the next line where the research reading has "de peculio". These readings are marked uncertain below, and the inscription is not used for the identification. Gaps: the sign after the gloss "domino faciente deductionem" (print h), read az., was not checked; the gloss of canvases 0, 1 and 3 was not reread line by line; the variant readings were not placed in the medieval transmission of the Digest; no palaeographer or legal historian has reviewed the reading or the match.

Differences. The manuscript has dominus conventus for conventus dominus; obtulit, dedit, exegit for obtulerit, dederit, exegerit; debitor domini for debitor domino (the 1566 print also has domini); debeatur twice for debeat, puta for ut puta, and no id est before id solum (canvas 0); subtractam for subreptam, peculiari for peculiaria and captus sit for captus sum (canvas 3); debetur for deberetur (canvas 1). The Greek word of D. 15.1.7.2 (παχυμερέστερον) is not written: the line passes from "debet" to "et in hanc". Julian's book is cited as "iul. l. xii" throughout. On canvas 3 the abraded inscription of D. 15.1.5 (the heading naming the jurist and book quoted) is partly legible as "l. xx[..]"; the red-initialled word after it, read "[.]eposit[.](?)", may be the Digest's Depositi, and the next line has "dum(taxat) de pec(ulio)(?) conveniuntur". These are differences from Mommsen's critical text; they were not collated with the medieval vulgate (the text current in the law schools) or its apparatus.

Differences in the gloss. The manuscript gives the glosses without the printed lemmata, keyed to the text by signs. Several are shorter than the print: f lacks "ex fideiussione servus", l stops at "domino", p omits the middle of the second reading and the third. Glosses e and f stand in the reverse order. The print's casus (summaries of the case) and the additions of later jurists are absent from the leaf.

Editions used: for the main text, Digesta Iustiniani Augusti, ed. Th. Mommsen and P. Krüger, D. 15.1.4-11, consulted in the transcription at The Latin Library; the title, the sequence of fragments and their wording identify the passages. For the gloss, Pandecta iuris civilis, 1: Digestum vetus, Paris, Merlin, 1566, cols. 1511-1520, Bavarian State Library copy scanned at the Internet Archive, leaf n865 (leaves n861 to n865); the print gives these glosses as the ordinary gloss and signs them "Accursius", "Ac." and "Azo". The edition details come from the Internet Archive metadata; the title page was not viewed.

Leaf order and limits

By the text, the order of the faces is canvas 3 (02_v), canvas 1 (01_v), canvas 0 (01_r), canvas 2 (02_r). Canvases 3 and 1 carry columns a and b of the true recto, canvases 0 and 2 columns a and b of the true verso, so the labelled versos are the true recto. Each piece has one column of main text (24 to 26 lines survive) and one column of gloss, with gloss lines in the upper margin, which shows that the top of the leaf is preserved. The four surviving stretches of Digest text hold about 199, 196, 209 and 185 words, and the gaps between them about 180, 214 and 222; on that count alone each column lost about as much as it keeps, which suggests a two-column leaf of about fifty lines cut vertically between the columns, of which the upper half survives. The pieces do not join line to line. One blue initial (canvas 3, at the start of D. 15.1.5) and red paragraph initials mark the text; a later pale-brown hand added a few interlinear words introduced by s. (scilicet), such as "s. cum debebat" on canvas 2.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the copy, which are the catalogue's (the gloss text does not date it); the place of its variant readings in the medieval text of the Digest or of the gloss; that the gloss of canvases 0, 1 and 3 contains nothing beyond Accursius, which was only spot-checked; the readings of the doubtful sigla after the glosses h, i and o on canvas 2.
