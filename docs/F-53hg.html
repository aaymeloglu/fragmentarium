Galen, Ad Glauconem de medendi methodo, old Latin translation, book II: end of the chapter on soft swellings and the chapter on scirrhus, with the subsection 'de uirtutibus adipum' (Greek II.5-6, Kühn XI 101-105; Venice 1522, tract. II cap. x-xi; Vendôme, BM, Ms. 109, f. 28v-29r)

A research reader reread verso lines 2-12 and 14-24 and the foot of the recto on the native images and compared them with the page image of the Venice 1522 Latin Galen (tract. II cap. x-xi) and, for the verso, with Vendôme, BM, Ms. 109, f. 28v-29r: the wording and order agree from 'Fit autem sirosis id est durus tu[mor] ex glutinoso quodam' through the remedies called malactica to the ranking of fats ('ualde melius est leopardinum quam leoninum & melius est ursinum quam taurinum'), and the leaf sides with the Vendôme manuscript against the print in several readings. Gariopontus's Passionarius, which takes over the chapter, is not the text of the leaf: the Basel 1531 edition and Paris, BnF, NAL 695 lack the ranking of fats and word three sentences differently. The red chapter number xxxiiii beside 'Incipit ... uirtutibus adipum' is unexplained, since Vendôme 109 numbers the section xiii and Paris, BnF, lat. 6837 numbers it xv. Most of the abraded recto was not compared, three recto lines were read only with the source as a guide, and no critical edition of the translation was used. The verso lay face to face with a seventeenth-century German account leaf, Stams, Frg. 175, leaf 9r (F-pmci): each carries the other's offset.

Readings and comparison details

F-53hg: Galen, Ad Glauconem de medendi methodo (old Latin translation), book II, on soft swellings and on scirrhus

This parchment leaf in Stams is from a copy of Galen's Ad Glauconem de medendi methodo ("Therapeutics to Glaucon") in the old Latin translation, the free Latin version that circulated in the early Middle Ages. It has the end of the chapter on soft swellings and the chapter on scirrhus (a stone-hard swelling), which correspond to book II, chapters 5-6 of the Greek text (Kühn's edition, vol. XI, pp. 101-105). The verso has a rubricated subsection "de uirtutibus adipum" (on the powers of fats), numbered xxxiiii in red in the margin. That number is unexplained: the manuscripts and editions compared count this section differently.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 120: parchment, one fragment item, 241 x 178 mm (measured as 194/241 x 134/178 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description, with two images labelled 120_01r and 120_01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: one leaf in a single column, photographed on both sides.

  • Identification: identified, high. The basis is agreement in wording and order on both sides of the leaf with the text printed as "Tractatus secundus" of Galen ad Glauconem in the Venice 1522 collected Latin Galen (capitula x-xi), and, on the verso, with a manuscript of the same work, Vendôme, Bibliothèque municipale, Ms. 109, f. 28v-29r. The recto ends "Fit autem sirosis id est durus tu[mor] / ex glutinoso quodam sed spisso atque", and the verso goes on with the misuse of drying remedies by untaught physicians, the remedies called malactica (softeners), the ranking of marrows and suets, and the ranking of animal fats (lion, leopard, bear, bull, deer, goat, then goose among birds). Alternatives considered: (1) Gariopontus, Passionarius, an 11th-century compilation that takes over this chapter. It is not the text of the leaf: in the Basel 1531 edition (cap. xlii "De sclirosi et cura"), in Paris, BnF, NAL 695 (read on the page) and in Paris, BnF, lat. 6951 (machine transcription only) the chapter ends "... deinde taurinum, uel uitulinum, aut aliquid aliud" (Basel 1531; NAL 695 has "sic et uitulinum. Aut aliquod aliud") and passes to "De apostematibus" with no ranking of fats, and it words three earlier sentences differently from the leaf (table below). (2) Galen, Methodus medendi XIV.4-5, and the pseudo-Galenic De simplicibus medicamentis ad Paternianum, entry "De sepo", have the same teaching and, in the second case, a similar list of fats, but not this sequence of sentences; they are parallels. (3) Another compilation that copied Ad Glauconem II unchanged cannot be excluded from a single leaf; nothing on the leaf points to one. Differences from the witnesses compared are stated under Textual evidence: the leaf goes with the manuscripts against the 1522 print in several readings, has two words ("quod sit malagma") found in none of them, and numbers the fats section 34 where Vendôme 109 has 13 and Paris, BnF, lat. 6837 has 15.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading of 30 October 2026 and the source-assisted reading. A research reader reread verso lines 2-12 and 14-24 and the last two lines of the recto on the native images. The first reading, made without any source, already had the verso's lower block nearly as given here ("quod sit malagma proinde", "dicantur malactica", "sebum deinde taurinum", "De adipibus", "inter quadrupedes", "ualde melius est leopardinum quam leoninum", "& melius est ursinum quam taurinum", "uero spissius & siccius & magis hir-", "obtimum est adipe anserin-") and the recto's last line ("quodam sed spisso"). Verso lines 2-12 were corrected with the source open ("indoctis medicis qui non intellegendo", "diaforeticis curant medicaminibus", "feculenta quasi in lapide constringitur"), and the three lines from the middle of the recto were read only with the source as a guide. Verso lines 1 and 13 and the rest of the recto, which is abraded, were not read. A second, fainter script in large looped strokes lies between and across the verso's lines: it is the mirror-image offset of the German account entries on Stams, Frg. 175, leaf 9r (F-pmci), which in turn carries the reddish offset of this verso line for line, so the two leaves lay face to face in a later binding or bundle. Accuracy has not been measured.

Differences from the 1522 print: the leaf has a heading and number before "De adipibus" where the print runs on; "sed spisso" for "et spisso"; "quadrupedes" for "quadrupedia"; "spissius" for "spissum est"; "adipe anserin-" for "anserinum"; "dicantur" for "dicuntur"; "in lapide" for "in lapidem"; "sic uitulinum" for "et sic vitulinum"; and the words "quod sit malagma" in line 15, which the print lacks. In line 2 the leaf's letters before "possit" do not fit the print's "nullo modo ipse tumor exinde" and were read, with the manuscript below in hand, as "inde modo aut uix".

Vendôme 109 (f. 28v-29r, page image) has the same text with numbered red chapter headings: "xii De scirosin ..." at "Fit autem scirosin id est durus ut lapis tumor", and "xiii De uirtute adipum" before "De adipibus uero optimum est leoninum inter quadrupedes dumtaxat et ualde melius est leopardinum quam leoninum ...". It agrees with the leaf against the print in "nullo inde modo aut uix possit auelli", "quadrupedes", "spissius et siccius", "sicut uitulinum" and "optimum est anserinum adipe", and it lacks "quod sit malagma". Its list of chapters for book II (f. 24r) runs from i to xxviii and names "De uirtute adipum" as xiii.

The numbering. The red numeral beside the heading on the leaf is xxxiiii (four minims after xxx). Vendôme 109 numbers the same section xiii within book II and leaves the chapters of book I unnumbered on the pages looked at; Paris, BnF, lat. 6837, f. 84r, has "De adipibus. xv"; Poitiers 184 has the rubric "De adipibus" without a number; the 1522 print has no division there. No witness read gives 34 or a number near it. Whether the Stams copy divided book II more finely or counted on from book I is not known.

Leaf order and what is not claimed

The text runs from the recto to the verso, so the catalogue's 01r and 01v are in the right order. About twenty words of the text lie between the recto's last word and the verso's second line; they must stand in the verso's first line and under the fold at its head, which were not read.

On the recto "Fit autem" follows "[sa]nitatem." in mid-line, with no heading visible before it; the end of that line is in the fold, where two of the manuscripts compared place their heading, so a chapter heading there is not excluded.

No date or place of origin is claimed; the script was not studied beyond noting that it is Caroline minuscule. Nothing is claimed about the relation of this copy to Vendôme 109, to the other manuscripts or to the printed text beyond the readings listed. The meaning of the number xxxiiii, the form of the book the leaf came from (the work alone or a medical collection containing it), and the second script on the verso are open. Whether the fragment is already recorded in the literature on the Latin Galen was not checked.
