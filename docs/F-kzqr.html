Readings and comparison details

F-kzqr: John Buridan, Summulae de dialectica, tract VII De fallaciis, 7.4.3 (on the fallacy of ignorantia elenchi)

This paper leaf at Ghent carries part of the Summulae de dialectica of John Buridan (Paris arts master, died about 1360), from the seventh treatise, on fallacies: chapter 4, part 3, on the fallacy called ignorantia elenchi (an argument that seems to refute the respondent but does not truly contradict what he said). The text is Buridan's own commentary on his short textbook clauses. The recto opens in the middle of a quotation of Aristotle, "... quod erat proprium elenchi, alii autem secundum syllogismi definitionem. Puto ergo quod Aristoteles istas duas auctoritates ponit ad remotionem dubii", and the verso is legible as far as the example of the piece of silver hammered from three cubits to four, "... non esse maius secundum totalem corporeitatem et opponens ...". The source compared is Hubert Hubien's electronic transcription of the Summulae, not a page image of an edition and not a manuscript.

The Fragmentarium record is Gent, Universiteitsbibliotheek, HS.2582/308, title "Philosophical treatise", paper, one trimmed leaf, 183 x 150 mm, cursiva, Latin, dated by the catalogue 1401-1450, tagged "Topic: Philosophy", online since 23 October 2025, with two images. The title fits as far as it goes; the record names no author, work or subject and has no linked description.

  • Identification: identified, high. Five passages, three on the recto and two on the verso, agree in wording and in order with section 7.4.3 of Hubien's text: Buridan's explanation of why Aristotle lists this fallacy among those "outside speech", his reply to the objection that the division of fallacies into those in speech and those outside speech would then fail, the summary of the fourth to seventh clauses, and the first, second and third "modes" of the fallacy with their examples (the chimera that is not white; "of every contradiction one part is true"). The matched text is the commentary layer, not Buridan's numbered base clauses for 7.4.3, which the leaf does not contain, and not Aristotle's Sophistici elenchi, which it quotes. A commentary on Peter of Spain's treatise on fallacies and a commentary on the Sophistici elenchi were considered from the vocabulary before the match was found; neither was read. John Dorp's commentary on Buridan's base text, which circulated in place of Buridan's own, was not compared; the wording on the leaf is that of Buridan's commentary as Hubien gives it. A few small differences of wording from Hubien are listed below; two of them are uncertain readings.
  • Transcription: source-assisted, selected passages. The image-only first reading was made from the images alone. A research reader (Claude) reread on the native images, with Hubien's text at hand, recto lines 1-7, 14-20 and 32-37, verso lines 1-6 and scattered words of verso lines 20-28; that is the source-assisted reading. Recto lines 8-13 and 21-31 and verso lines 7-19 rest on the first reading and were checked against the source only for sequence. The last recto line and verso lines 29-37 are not read. A blind check by two readers without the source supports "alii secundum syllogismi", "duas auctoritates ... ad remotionem dubii", "loca sophistica seu argumenta sophistica" and "chymera non alba"; it did not settle "peccas contra" or "per", nor the word read "aliam". Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader (Claude) compared the reread lines with Hubien's electronic transcription of Summulae de dialectica 7.4.3 (plain text; no page image of an edition and no other manuscript was used). COORDINATOR CHECK: the coordinator (Claude) read the recto band beginning "Deinde etiam tu dicis quod tunc illa divisio fallaciarum" (canvas 0, third text band) on the image and compared it with Hubien's transcription of 7.4.3: the wording and order agree, with "illa"/"ista" and "Dico"/"Ego dico" as listed below. Two further readers, working from shuffled crops without the source, independently read "alii secundum syllogismi", "duas auctoritates ... ad remotionem dubii", "loca sophistica seu argumenta sophistica" and "chymera ... non alba". Gaps: about half the recto and most of the verso were not reread word by word; the stained foot of the verso is unread, so the point where the leaf's text ends is not fixed; Dorp's commentary and the manuscript tradition of the Summulae were not consulted.

Differences, fragment first: "loca sophistica" for "loci sophistici"; "illa divisio" for "ista diuisio"; "Dico" for "Ego dico"; "in dictione" for "secundum dictionem" (recto 20); "volendo" for "uolens". These five are the research reader's readings of a difficult cursive and were not among the words put to the blind check. Two further differences are uncertain readings and are not offered as variants: "peccas contra(?)" where Hubien has "peccas per", and "aliam(?)" where Hubien has "priorem, scilicet". In recto lines 2-3 the wording of the sentence beginning "Quia enim hanc fallaciam enumerauit" could not be read with confidence. No sentence present in Hubien was found missing in the lines reread, and none added. Spelling (y/i, e/ae, ci/ti) is not counted.

Text used and attribution

Iohannes Buridanus, Summulae de dialectica, section 7.4.3 (headed "SDD 7.4.3"), in the electronic transcription prepared by Hubert Hubien and distributed as a plain-text file by Peter King; the same text is in the Corpus Corporum database. This is a transcription without apparatus; the manuscripts behind it were not checked, and no printed critical edition of the seventh treatise was consulted. The Summulae with its commentary is Buridan's standard and securely attributed logic textbook; the attribution was not examined beyond that.

Leaf order and what is not claimed

The recto precedes the verso: the last recto line read ends in the sentence on the nature of negation ("de ratione negationis ...") and the verso opens later in the same sentence group ("... oportet observare non solum quod una sit affirmativa"); the text between them in Hubien is about one manuscript line long and corresponds to the damaged last line of the recto, which was not read. Nothing else is in the images except a library stamp and the scale.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the copy (the catalogue's 1401-1450 is its own estimate from the script and was not assessed); whether the leaf comes from a complete Summulae or from a separate copy of the treatise on fallacies; where exactly the verso ends; any relation of this copy to other manuscripts or to Hubien's text beyond the differences listed; novelty.
