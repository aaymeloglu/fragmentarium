Status: IDENTIFIED

Johannes Balbus (Giovanni Balbi of Genoa, OP, d. 1298), Catholicon (1286), part 5 (the alphabetical dictionary), letter C. Leaf 1 = entries Cain (end) – Cathedra … Catinus; leaf 2 = Cibus … Ciliarcha/Cilix. Two non-consecutive leaves from the same manuscript copy. Verified against the OCR of the Strasbourg (Adolf Rusch) c. 1475–77 print, Bibliothèque Sainte-Geneviève OEXV 3 RES, and Google Books snippet of the 1510 print.

Notes

  • Date/script are consistent: the Catholicon (1286) was copied in huge numbers in the 14th–15th c.; a two-column textualis copy of c. 1400–1450 in Bruges fits. Nothing in the text contradicts the catalogue. The running "C" headers and alternating red/blue initials are standard Catholicon layout.
  • The fragment's readings have minor variants against the 1475 print (e.g. "Catapuera docta puella" matches; "cataposis" in the fragment where the print reads "catatiposis"; fragment "Cicendelum" vs print "Cicendulum"). These are ordinary manuscript variants, not a different text.
  • The 1460 Mainz Catholicon (archive.org OEXV2, Bibliothèque Sainte-Geneviève OEXV 2 RES) also exists in OCR but the OCR is unusable for these leaves; the Rusch print was used instead.

Ruled out

  • Isidore, Etymologiae (not alphabetical; entries here cite Hugutio, Papias, Pantheon, Theodolus, Graecismus, which post-date Isidore).
  • Papias, Elementarium; Hugutio, Derivationes; Osbern; Brito; Vocabularius Ex quo; Gerardus de Schueren: excluded because the fragment itself cites "secundum Hugutionem", "secundum Papiam", "in Pantheon" and "in iiii. parte dixi" (the Catholicon's own cross-reference to its fourth part, on tropes and rhetorical colours) and "de isto libro sicut dixi in principio" under Catholicon; and because the wording is verbatim Balbus.
