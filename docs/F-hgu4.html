Two paper bifolia in one fifteenth-century hand, taken from the binding of Stams Hs 308 in 1964: bifolium 1 has the Regensburg oration on one leaf and the Frankfurt oration on the other, bifolium 2 has De liberorum educatione from the end of the analogy and derivation section through barbarism and solecism to the opening of the defence of poets. A research reader reread selected lines on all leaves except the right page of canvas 1 and compared them with Wolkan III and Wolkan II on Internet Archive OCR and with Müller 1713 on the MDZ page image (p. 480) and OCR (p. 479). An independent research reader read seven lines of canvas 3 right (box 4350,2180-6650,2620) on the image and found them in agreement with Wolkan II p. 131 on the page image, and checked the locus of canvas 3 at pp. 129, 130, 138 and 140. Variants are minor (e.g. vitanda? / vindicanda, nostro / vestro, Viscella / vistula, omission of nobilissimum Ladislaum, dicemus / dicamus). The right page of canvas 1 and most of the left pages of canvases 2 and 3 rest on the image-only first reading, which was exposed to a pencil note naming Aeneas Piccolomini and the Regensburg diet on canvases 0-1; no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-hgu4: Enea Silvio Piccolomini, orations Quamvis omnibus (Regensburg, 1454) and Constantinopolitana clades (Frankfurt, 1454), and De liberorum educatione (grammar section and opening of the defence of poets)

These two paper bifolia in the archive of the Cistercian abbey of Stams carry three works by Enea Silvio Piccolomini (Aeneas Silvius, later Pope Pius II), in one fifteenth-century hand. The first bifolium has, on one leaf, part of the oration Quamvis omnibus that Enea gave at the Regensburg Diet in 1454, and on the other leaf part of the oration Constantinopolitana clades that he gave at the Frankfurt Diet in October 1454, both urging war against the Turks after the fall of Constantinople. The second bifolium has part of De liberorum educatione, his treatise on the education of King Ladislaus Postumus (Wiener Neustadt, February 1450): the end of the section on analogy and derivation, barbarism and solecism, etymology, vetustas, auctoritas and consuetudo, "Concatenate sunt inter se discipline", and the opening of the defence of reading the poets.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 121, two paper fragment items, 410 x 305 mm, with the title "x", no date and no description. The images themselves carry earlier notes: a modern pencil note in the lower margin of the right-hand page of canvas 0 reads "Aeneas Piccolomini / Reichstag z. Regensburg / 1454 23/4 / (cf. ... S. 461)", the left margin of canvas 1 has "1455/53 (? 28.10.1456?)", and both bifolia carry the shelf note "bis 1964 als (or im) Einband zu(r) Hs 308", so they came out of the binding of Stams Hs 308 in 1964. The pencil note fits one leaf of the first bifolium. It does not separate the Frankfurt oration on the other leaf, and nothing is written on the second bifolium.

  • Identification: identified, high, for all three texts. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and sequence with the printed texts over several passages on each leaf: R. Wolkan, Der Briefwechsel des Eneas Silvius Piccolomini III/1 (Fontes rerum Austriacarum II/68, 1918), Nr. 291, pp. 542-546, for the Regensburg oration; J. J. Müller, Reichstags-Theatrum unter Keyser Friedrich V., vol. 1 (1713), pp. 479-480, for the Frankfurt oration; Wolkan II (FRA II/67, 1912), Nr. 40, pp. 129-140, for De liberorum educatione. The first leaf and the second leaf of bifolium 1 share phrases, but collation shows two distinct orations, the Frankfurt speech reusing Enea's own Regensburg wording; they are not one text repeated. The texts are Enea's own works copied as such, not quotations inside another work. The Regensburg oration is printed inside Enea's narrative of the diet, the letter to Johann Vitéz; whether this copy was taken from the oration on its own or from that narrative cannot be told from the surviving text, which is all speech. The differences of wording are minor and are listed under Textual evidence.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading is exposed for canvases 0 and 1: its reader saw the pencil note naming Aeneas Piccolomini and the Regensburg diet before reading those pages, and says so. No identification is written on canvases 2 and 3, which that reader read blind. The source-assisted reading is selected lines. A research reader (Claude) reread on the native images lines 1-11 of the right page of canvas 0; lines 1-13 and a band of 16 lines lower on the left page of canvas 0; 13 lines of the left page of canvas 1; and two bands of 12 and 11 lines on the right page of canvas 2. An independent research reader (Claude) read seven lines of the right page of canvas 3. Everything else, including the right page of canvas 1, the left page of canvas 2 and the left page of canvas 3, rests on the first reading. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader (Claude) compared the reread lines with the editions. Müller was read on the MDZ page image for p. 480 and on OCR for p. 479; Wolkan II and III on Internet Archive OCR. An independent research reader read the right page of canvas 3, a page the first comparison had not covered, on the native image, box 4350,2180-6650,2620 (seven lines through the display heading "Soloecismum"), before seeing the comparison, then compared it with Wolkan II p. 131 on the Internet Archive page image (leaf n154). Wording and sequence agree throughout, including the Quintilian examples (venite, abi, ego, peculiolum); the differences are "q(ui)s?" against the edition's "hunc", "dicas?" against "dixeris", and a possible "hinc" before "soloecismus", all doubtful readings or trivial. The same reader checked the top of the right page of canvas 3 (p. 129), the "Barbarismus quidem" band (p. 130) and the first and last lines of the left page of canvas 3 (pp. 138 and 140) for locus only, which fixes the extent and page order of bifolium 2. Not compared line by line: the right page of canvas 1 (the start of the Frankfurt text on that leaf) and most of the left pages of canvases 2 and 3. Wolkan III was not seen on page images. No human review.

Differences

Regensburg: "de vitanda? iniuria" against the edition's "de vindicanda injuria" (the manuscript reading is unclear and may be a slip); "que[cumque]?" against "que"; "consulit" against "consulet"; "hoc iam?" against "hoc ipsum"; "suum" interlinear; and in the first line "noverunt" and "e[ius]", the latter beside a hole, where the edition has "norunt" and "sua". Frankfurt: silentes / silemus, concalcant / conculcant, fiet / fiat, "Sarracenos turcos aut tartaros" / "Saracenos, Thurcos & Tartaros", "querit Inter nos" / "querit. Quid inter nos", the first-person "nostra", "nostro solo", "nostro imperio", "nostri sanguinis" against Müller's "vestra", "vestro", "vestri", "vistula" / "Viscella", and the manuscript lacks "nobilissimum Ladislaum" after "sanguinis". De liberorum educatione: ducat / ducet, imbuatur / imbuetur, quam plures / complures, maxime / maximi, putavit / putabat, dicemus / dicamus, "neque semel legentur" / "neque enim semel legentur", and on canvas 3 "q(ui)s?" / "hunc" and "dicas?" / "dixeris". None of these bears on the identification, and the relation of this copy to the manuscripts behind the editions is left open.

Editions and attribution

  • Regensburg: Wolkan III/1 (FRA II/68), Nr. 291, Enea's letter to Johann Vitéz narrating the diet, oration at pp. 538-547, introduced "in hunc modum Eneas oravit" and closed "Hanc orationem cum verbis Latinis pronuntiasset Eneas" (archive.org). Wolkan's base manuscript is Vat. lat. 3888, with corrections in Enea's hand.
  • Frankfurt: Müller, Reichstags-Theatrum unter Keyser Friedrich V., vol. 1 (1713), pp. 478-481, headed "Oratio Aeneae Sylvii in conventu Franckfordiensi de Constantinopolitana clade et bello contra Turcos congregando" (MDZ bsb10492351). The oration is also in the Basel 1551 Opera (seen in Google Books snippets only).
  • De liberorum educatione: Wolkan II (FRA II/67), Nr. 40, Bishop Eneas to King Ladislaus, edited from Melk, Cod. 13, with Enea's autograph corrections (archive.org); also in the Basel 1551 Opera, pp. 965-992.
  • Not consulted: Cotta-Schønberg's modern editions of the orations and Deutsche Reichstagsakten 19.

Leaf order, other notes, and what is not claimed

  • Bifolium 1: canvas 0 is the outer side (left page = leaf B verso, right page = leaf A recto), canvas 1 the inner side (left = A verso, right = B recto). Leaf A runs from "ad insequendum commoditates qui noverunt hominem" (Wolkan III p. 542) to "vosque tanti mali causam" (p. 546); leaf B from "[quod si certissimi essetis nunquam] illos ad vos esse venturos" to "Augustus Octavianus ... de quo illa sunt metra". The text breaks between A verso and B recto, so leaves are lost between them and this bifolium was not the centre of its quire.
  • Bifolium 2: the reading order is canvas 3 right, canvas 2 left, canvas 2 right, canvas 3 left. The text runs from "...oportebit ne mireris scabellum ex scamno ... in duodecim tabulis pagunt" (Wolkan II p. 129) at the top of canvas 3 right to "...eruditissimis viris poetarum lectionem nedum non vitabimus" (p. 140) at the foot of canvas 3 left. It runs on from canvas 2 left to canvas 2 right, so canvas 2 is the inner side, probably the centre of a quire.
  • Same hand, same size and the same host volume make it likely that both bifolia come from one composite volume of Enea's works; this is not proved.
  • The pencil dates on canvas 1 ("1455/53", "28.10.1456?") were not examined. No date, origin, recension or relation to other copies of these works is claimed.
