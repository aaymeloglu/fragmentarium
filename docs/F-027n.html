Readings and comparison details

F-027n: Mathias Heckhen of Mils, second guardianship account for Ursula, daughter of the late Oswald Geissler, wife of Martin Vischler of Absam, Herrschaft Thaur, from 24 August 1637, closing clause dated 1 July [16]39

Stams, Frg. 231 holds the opening and closing pages of a guardianship account (Gerhabschafft Raittung), the statement a court-appointed guardian (Gerhab) rendered of a ward's property. Mathias Heckhen of Mils, in the Herrschaft (lordship) of Thaur, the court-appointed and confirmed guardian of Ursula, legitimate surviving daughter of the late Oswald Geissler of Mils and his late wife Ursula Lindtnerin(?), renders his second account ("Andere Gerhabschafft Raittung"). Ursula is now married to Martin Vischler of Absam, in the same Herrschaft. The account concerns her paternal and maternal inheritances and runs on from the first account, of 24 August 1637. The closing records that Vischler, as her husband (Ehevogt), asked for a copy of the account, which was made out under a noble seal on behalf of the authority ("von obrigkhait wegen"), in the presence of Urban Aichner of Absam and Hanns Rotlass(?) of Thaur as Vormund and Beistand, on "den ersten tag Monats Juli Anno Neununddreissig". The century is not written in the closing; it is supplied from the 1637 date in the account's own text, which gives 1 July 1639. The leaves between the opening and the closing, with the body of the account, are lost.

The Fragmentarium record describes Stams, Frg. 231 as paper, three fragment items, 335 x 214 mm (measured on Frg. 231_1, 169/335 x 117/214 mm), with four images. It gives the title "x", no date and no description, so it says nothing about the content and there is nothing in it to correct.

  • Identification: identified, high confidence, as an archival document: the regest above. The guardian, the ward, her parents, her husband, the action (the second guardianship account and the copy issued to the husband), the places and both dates are read on the image. The closing year is read only as "Neununddreissig", without a century; "1639" depends on the opening's "Sechzehenhundert Sibenunddreissigisten" (1637) and is labelled supplied in the regest. Uncertain: the mother's surname, Lindtnerin(?); the second witness's surname, read Rotlass(?) and by another reader [V/R]oßl..(?); the first letter of the first witness's surname (A, possibly K). The bifolium is a fair copy in a chancery hand with Fraktur display words. Since the closing records that a copy was requested and issued, it may be that copy, but no seal survives on these leaves and this is not established. No outside record of the act was found; none is expected for a private act of this kind.
  • Transcription: an image-only first reading and an assisted reading. There is no edition: "assisted" here means reread at native size by a research reader with the first reading in hand. The research reader read the opening (1r), the closing (2v), the start of the account (1v) and the end of the balance and the inheritance clause (2r). An independent reader reread 2v and 1v. The pen trials on the third leaf and the name entries on the lower piece of canvas 0 were not fully read. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: no outside record was found, so the record link is the Fragmentarium record itself. The checks are against the image.
    • An independent reader read the closing on 2v (15 lines, canvas 0, native box 380,700 to 2250,2650) and the start of the account on 1v (canvas 1, 500,700 to 2600,2750) on the image before seeing the research transcription, and agreed on the substance, the witnesses as far as they can be read, the 1637 start date and the closing date. That reader then checked the parties on 1r (canvas 0, 2850,1250 to 4550,2250) with the research transcription known, and read the husband's first name as "Mart-/in", split across a line break.
    • Two blind readers, given single-word crops without the research reading, answered six questions (closing year, start year, start day and month, guardian, deceased father, husband), and a third reader adjudicated against the image. "Neununddreissig", "Vierundzwainzigisten", "Heckhen zu Mils", "Oswalden Geisslers" and "Vischler" held; the guardian's forename is written "Mathieas"; "Sechzehenhundert" held against one blind reader's "Funffzehenhundert"; the adjudicator confirmed "Mart-/in Vischler" on a wider crop. The month (Augusti) is cut at the edge of its crop and rests on the research and independent readings.
    • The coordinator read no passage of this fragment beyond the word check and the independent reader's reread of 2v and 1v.

Gaps in coverage: 2r (the balance of 21 fl 17 kr and the inheritance clause) was read only once; the authority whose seal was used is not named in the lines read securely; nobody outside this research has reviewed the reading; the court records of Thaur, where a record of the act would be, were not consulted.

The dating clauses are plain calendar dates, with no feast or weekday to convert: "Vierundzwainzigisten Augusti Sechzehenhundert Sibenunddreissigisten Jars", 24 August 1637, on 1v, and "den ersten tag Monats Juli Anno Neununddreissig", 1 July [16]39, on 2v, the century supplied from 1v. Tyrol used the Gregorian calendar from 1583, so both are Gregorian.

In the closing, the independent reader reads a further phrase, "mit Vorgemelts Herrn Haubtman vnd Pflegers ... Ferttigung(?)", which would refer back to a captain and Pfleger (local administrator) named on the lost leaves. The research reader did not read these words, so whose seal was used is left open.

What is not claimed

  • That the surviving bifolium is the copy issued to Vischler.
  • The authority or official whose seal was used, or that the act was done before the court of Thaur rather than another office.
  • For whom Aichner and Rotlass(?) acted as Vormund and Beistand; the text gives the roles, not the person.
  • The mother's surname, the second witness's surname and the first letter of the first witness's surname, all uncertain.
  • The relation of Michael(?) Geissler to Ursula, which the text read does not state.
  • Any reading of the pen-trial leaf beyond the repeated name, or of the name entries on the lower piece.
