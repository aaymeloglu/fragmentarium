Frg. 223 (F-rnlr)

Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Hartmann Pistoris, Quaestionum iuris libri secundi pars posterior (Leipzig: Steinman, 1584), q. 36, pp. 459-462 and 467-470; Friedrich Martini (praes.) and Heinrich Abraham von Einsiedel (resp.), Theses de iurisdictione secularium iudicum (Ingolstadt: Eder, 1584), pp. 19-22; with an incomplete German letter and a chancery endorsement (partly read)

Two printed pieces (223-1, 223-3) are leaves of Pistoris's q. 36 on female succession to fiefs, and 223-4 is the inner bifolium C2-C3 of Martini's Ingolstadt theses. Research readers compared Pistoris pp. 467, 469 and 461 and Martini pp. 19-21 with page images of the BSB copies: line breaks, marginal numbers and signatures agree, Martini's including the same damaged type. On p. 469 the marginal 29, 'primò' and unligatured head exclude the 1593/1596 Lantzenberger setting, and p. 467 excludes the 1598 setting (VD16 P 3000, P 3003). The pieces also hold an incomplete German letter about a settlement involving Margareta Schillbrig[...] (surname partly read), her daughter's noble kin and 200 fl., and an endorsement 'Commissio Serenissimi Domini Archiducis in consilio' signed H. Pa[n/r]storffer; they are undated, their writer is not named, and no outside record was found. The other pieces are blank or carry offsets; no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-rnlr: Hartmann Pistoris, Quaestionum iuris libri secundi pars posterior, quaestio 36 (Leipzig 1584), and Friedrich Martini, Theses de iurisdictione secularium iudicum (Ingolstadt 1584), with an incomplete German letter

This record is a bundle of paper binding waste. Its printed pieces come from two books of 1584.

  • Pieces 223-1 and 223-3 are leaves of Hartmann Pistoris's Quaestionum iuris tam Romani quam Saxonici libri secundi pars posterior, from quaestio 36, which concerns whether daughters excluded by a son can later succeed to a fief. They belong to the edition printed at Leipzig by Steinman in 1584.
  • Piece 223-4 is the inner bifolium of quire C of the university theses De iurisdictione secularium iudicum, defended at Ingolstadt in 1584 under Friedrich Martini by Heinrich Abraham von Einsiedel. A bifolium is a sheet folded once into two leaves.
  • Pieces 223-2 and 223-7 carry an incomplete German letter about a settlement involving a Margareta Schillbrig[...], and a separate Latin chancery endorsement. They are reported below as partly read.
  • The remaining pieces are blank or carry only offsets, that is, mirror images of ink or print transferred from neighbouring sheets.

The Fragmentarium record describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv (Stealth Published), Frg. 223: paper, 18 fragment items, 320 x 218 mm (measured on Frg. 223_2 by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), online since 18 September 2021, with the title "x". It gives no description, date or identification. Its twelve images show the pieces two to eight at a time, each with a pencil label "Stams, StA, Frg. 223-n r/v".

  • Identification: identified, high, for the printed pieces.
    • Pistoris. Pages 467-470 and 460-461 agree with the Leipzig 1584 edition word for word and line for line, including the marginal paragraph numbers and signatures. On p. 469 the fragment and the 1584 copy both place the marginal number 29 against "nus ipsi succedere queat" and print "primò" and an unligatured "QVAESTIO". The Leipzig edition dated 1596 on its title (colophon 1593, printed by Michael Lantzenberger) has the same pagination but a different setting. It places 29 one line lower, has "primo" and the Æ ligature, and so is excluded. The 1598 Lantzenberger edition is also a different setting on p. 467. The Heidelberg 1603 edition is paginated differently.
    • Martini. Pages 19-22 agree line for line with the Ingolstadt 1584 print, including the same damaged pieces of type ("li-m itibus" and the "m" of "minùs" on p. 20). That makes this the same setting, not a page-for-page reprint.
    • Letter and endorsement. These are not identified as an act. The letter's date, writer and end are lost, and no outside record was found.
  • Transcription: image-only first reading and source-assisted reading.
    • The printed text was read nearly in full by the first reader. Research readers reread on the image the top of p. 469, the middle of p. 467, p. 20 (theses LXI-LXII), p. 21 and the foot of p. 19.
    • The letter was read by a research reader. Its key words were reread on neutral crops by two further readers, and disagreements were settled on the image. Many words of the letter remain uncertain.
    • Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification:
    • A research reader compared the fragment's p. 469 (top) and p. 21 with page images of the BSB copies of the two 1584 books, and p. 461 (lower half) with the first reading.
    • A second research reader read Pistoris p. 467 and Martini p. 20 before opening the sources. They then compared them with the same copies and with the 1598 edition (Berlin, SBB), and checked the editions of the pars posterior in VD16, the union catalogue of sixteenth-century German imprints.
    • The coordinator compared Pistoris p. 469 on canvas 1 (Stams_STA_Frg_223_01v), native box 4150,1150-5850,2050 ("Ponamus enim, aliquem duos post se reliq[uisse] filios ... ex hoc ipso refellitur ... ut docent Romanus, Alex. Ias. & DD. in l. 1.") with BSB bsb11924582, image 00485, side by side: the running head, line breaks and marginal 29 agree.
    • Not compared on page images: pp. 468 and 470, the badly damaged face 223-3v, and pp. 21-22 of the theses beyond their foot lines. No outside record of the letter was found; the Tyrolean archive's finding aids were not searched.

The pieces

  • 223-1: a bifolium of Pistoris. One face has pp. 470 and 467 (signature Nnn 2), the other pp. 468 and 469 (Nnn 3). It is the inner sheet of gathering Nnn.
  • 223-3: a damaged piece of the same book, mounted sideways, with pp. 460 and 461 (signature Mmm 3) on one face. The other face, with a signature fragment "mm 2", was read only in part.
  • 223-4: the Martini theses, pp. 20-21 on one face and pp. 22 and 19 on the other: the inner bifolium C2-C3 of the quarto.
  • Blank pieces: 223-5, 223-6 (with a watermark not identified) and the strips 223-8 to 223-18 are blank or carry only offsets. 223-6v holds a mirror image of the letter on 223-2r; 223-7v and 223-13r hold mirror images of print.

223-2, letter (partial). A single paper leaf in a German cursive of the period, written on both sides. The text breaks off at the foot of the reverse side, so its continuation, date and signature are lost.

  • It is addressed to "Wolgeborn, Edl, Gestreng, Hochgelert, Vesst, gnedig vnnd gebietund Herren", a plural address to a group of lords.
  • It concerns a commission set up at the instance of "der Edlen vnnd Tugentsamen Frawen Margareta Schillbrig[...] zu H[?]dern" (surname ending lost or uncertain, place uncertain). The appointed commissioner was instructed to give the parties a due ruling, apparently if an amicable settlement failed ("im fahl die guete verschlag(?), gebürlich zuverabschiden"; partly uncertain).
  • According to the letter, the settlement now waits on the daughter's demand that her noble relatives ("Adelich freundtschafft") ratify it. This causes delay and cost, while a sum of 200 fl. in cash is at stake.
  • The writer asks the lords for an order so that the matter can be dealt with "mit dem mann allain" (reading probable) and the 200 fl. paid in cash.
  • The writer is not named on the surviving leaf. Whether the writer is the commissioner, who is mentioned in the third person, is not established.

223-7, endorsement (partial). A torn paper sheet carrying the Latin chancery formula "Commissio Ser(enissi)mi D(omi)ni Archiducis in consilio" ("by commission of the Most Serene Lord Archduke, in council"). Below it, in a smaller hand, is a case line, "L[...] contra(?) Schilbr[...](?) von(?) H[?]dern / Camerl(?)-Relat(ion)(?)", then the signature "H. Pa[n/r]storffer" with a paraph (n or r undecided). The case line suggests it belongs with the letter, but that is not proven. Which archduke is meant is not stated on the sheet.

Not claimed: a date for the letter or the endorsement, the identity of the lords addressed, the writer, or the signer. The 1584 imprints date only the printed waste, not the manuscript pieces or the binding.
