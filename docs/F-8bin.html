F-8bin — unidentified didactic sacramental poem

  • Identification: not identified; confidence none. The inspected passages support a Latin didactic poem concerned with the Eucharist and other theological material. No author or work match was established. Genre and subject are not an attribution.
  • Transcription: selected assisted image readings. An isolated reader covered the top of fol.90r; the coordinator then inspected its larger band, the lower part of that page and the end of fol.91v. The coordinator had access to candidate readings. The selected readings remain uncertain, with no full metrical or palaeographic certification.
  • Verification: manuscript images and bounded retrieval. Three phrase searches found no sustained matching text. No candidate edition was available for line-by-line comparison. Common theological imagery in search results does not identify the poem.

Catalogue: Paris, BnF, Latin9377, ff.90–93, paper guard leaves catalogued as eastern France, 1451–1500. Manifest; fol.90r, Gallica f191. Cached local crops were taken from downloaded full images; exact crop coordinates/scale have not been recovered. No additional enhancement was applied.

Selected readings

The preserved image-only response has five readable rows plus clipped strokes of a sixth. It recovered portions such as passurus, Traderet and victus amore, but not a reliable continuous passage. Its mari? differs from mori in the coordinator's assisted reading; uncertain beginnings and endings are not filled by metre.

Fol.90r upper band, physical rows 3–6 in the larger crop:

Atq(ue) mori voluit s(ed) nec diffusio tanta / Sufficiebat ei nisi se passurus in esca(m) / Traderet et potu(m) [uncertain word] victus amore / Sub sacramenti miro velamine tectu(m).

Parentheses are proposed expansions, the uncertain word before victus is left unresolved, and the final words cross damaged areas. The sixth line was effectively unavailable in the shorter isolated packet, so it is an additional assisted image reading.

The first complete row of the lower fol.90r crop reads:

Quo mirabilit(er) elephas natat ambulat agn(us).

This familiar elephant/lamb image occurs in many forms in Christian writing. The wording is a useful search anchor but insufficient to establish Gregory or any later compiler as the poem's author.

A separate fol.91v image, f194, bottom band, gives an apparent division formula: … disiungere fessos / Ut se venture reparent(?) pausando diete. The preceding oxen word, partly clipped at the crop's top, remains uncertain. After a red O initial the text turns to bonum diffusivum … sui. This supports a sectional transition; it does not establish the entire poem's organization or identify a source for the following section. The marginal reference and several words remain unresolved.

Retrieval and result

Web searches for sub sacramenti miro velamine, elephas natat with ambulat agnus, and tauros disiungere fessos with sacramentis yielded no sustained match to the inspected poem. The last query tests a reading that has not been fully checked against the image. Results for the elephant/lamb language included prose and later uses of the common image; none established this poem. This is a limited search result, not proof of an unprinted text.

Adam of Aldersbach's Summula remains an untested candidate. The cached file labelled as its OCR contains a catalogue of canon-law manuscripts, so it cannot support comparison with the poem or its exclusion.

A more useful next step is a direct comparison with a complete candidate poem or a repertory entry matching several consecutive verses. The remaining folios, complete metre, marginal references, original leaf sequence, composition date and provenance have not been established. The catalogue's copying date is retained as catalogue information. No human palaeographer reviewed these excerpts and no transcription accuracy rate was measured.
