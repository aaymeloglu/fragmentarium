Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Missal (probably Cistercian): end of the Ordo missae from the Libera nos embolism to Meritis et precibus; a Mass of the Visitation(?) added in a later hand

The catalogue's r/v labels are reversed: the face pencilled 69v is the recto. A research reader read the main text on the images and collated it with page images of the Cistercian missal Fribourg BCU L 305 (ff. 144r-v) and the Missale Romanum, Milan 1474 (pp. 210-211): the commixtio after the Agnus in the 'promerendam et capessendam' form, 'libera me queso', 'custodiat me' straight to the Placeat, and 'Meritis et precibus' agree with L 305 and not with the Roman print, with small differences ('Et cum spiritu tuo', 'dominus'). The use is probably Cistercian (medium): a 'Meritis et intercessionibus' blessing and 'custodiat me' also occur in Salzburg-province and other German diocesan books (Seckau, Brenner's codex), so the diagnostic points are the 'precibus' wording, Corpus/Sanguis straight to the Placeat, and the commixtio after the Agnus; Augsburg is excluded on page images, but no Brixen, Salzburg, Freising or Benedictine missal was checked and the Carthusian use is untested. An independent checker read the added small-hand Mass on the image and compared it with the Visitation Mass of the 1474 print (vol. 2, p. 208) on its page image: the collect ('elizabet inspirasti', probable reading) and the Song of Songs lesson fit, so a Visitation Mass added in a later hand is likely, but the feast word is illegible.

Readings and comparison details

F-v82x: Missal (probably Cistercian), end of the Ordo missae, with a Mass of the Visitation(?) added in a later hand

This parchment leaf at Stams comes from a missal. Its main hand gives the end of the Ordo missae (the fixed prayers of the Mass) from the end of the Canon to the end of Mass. The library's labels run against the text: the face pencilled "69v" is the recto and comes first.

  • Recto (pencilled 69v): the end of the embolism "Libera nos" (the prayer after the Lord's Prayer), "Per omnia secula seculorum. Amen", "Pax domini sit semper vobiscum. Et cum spiritu tuo" with red crosses, "Agnus dei" as a cue only, then the commixtio (the prayer said as the host is put into the chalice) "Hec sacrosancta commixtio corporis et sanguinis domini nostri Ihesu Christi fiat michi et omnibus sumentibus salus mentis et corporis et ad vitam eternam promerendam et capessendam preparatio salutaris. Amen", and "Domine Ihesu Christe fili dei vivi ... libera me queso per hoc sacrosanctum corpus et sanguinem tuum".
  • Verso (pencilled 69r): "[custodiat me in] vitam eternam. amen", the prayer "Placeat tibi sancta trinitas", and the blessing "Meritis et precibus istorum et omnium sanctorum suorum misereatur nostri omnipotens dominus. amen".
  • Added Mass: below that, a smaller and later-looking hand added a Marian Mass: introit Gaudeamus ... sub honore [beat]e Ma[rie] virgi[nis] de cu[ius ...] with the verse Eructavit, a collect "Collecta ... deus qui ex [abundancia ...] ... elizabet inspirasti(?) presta quesumus ut per eius ...", a lesson "lectio libri sapiencie" from the Song of Songs 2:8-14, and the gradual Benedicta et venerabilis. This is most likely the Mass of the Visitation, but the word naming the feast is not legible.

The main text was matched step by step on page images with a Cistercian manuscript missal (Fribourg, BCU, Ms. L 305) and contrasted with the Roman Missal printed at Milan in 1474. The use of the book is probably Cistercian, an inference of medium confidence.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 69: parchment, 247 x 207 mm (measured 186/247 x 140/207 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), no date, with the title "x", no linked description, and two images labelled 01r and 01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: a single leaf, trimmed and folded in on all four sides as a wrapper or binding lining, with sewing holes, one column of large textualis with red initials.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the content.
    • Ordo missae: the leaf runs Pax → Agnus Dei → "Hec sacrosancta commixtio ... promerendam et capessendam" → "Domine Ihesu Christe fili dei vivi ... libera me queso" → (Corpus and Sanguis, lost between the faces) "custodiat me in vitam eternam" → Placeat → "Meritis et precibus". L 305, ff. 144r-144v, has the same sequence and every formula that separates it from the Roman print. The 1474 Roman Missal (pp. 210-211) has "Fiat commixtio et consecratio" before the Agnus, "Domine Ihesu Christe qui dixisti", "libera me per hoc sacrificium" without "queso", "custodiat animam meam", Quod ore sumpsimus and Corpus tuum before the Placeat, and no Meritis. The commixtio and Meritis also agree word for word with the Cistercian Ordo printed in Der Katholik 1882.
    • Differences from L 305: "Et cum spiritu tuo" written out; "Amen" after the commixtio where L 305 has "Per eundem"; the Agnus as a cue; "omnipotens dominus" (L 305 "deus"; the 1882 text also "Dominus"); "simus semper" (L 305 "semper simus"). So it is not a copy of L 305.
    • Use (inference, medium): probably Cistercian. A blessing of the "Meritis et intercessionibus istorum et omnium sanctorum" type after the Placeat, and the formula "custodiat me", also occur in diocesan books of the Salzburg province and elsewhere in Germany (Seckau, and a German codex printed by Brenner), and a commixtio after the Agnus also occurs in Sarum and in an Italian order. So none of these is Cistercian by itself. The diagnostic points are the wording "precibus ... misereatur nostri", the run from Corpus and Sanguis straight to the Placeat with no ablution prayers (the prayers said while the vessels are cleansed), and the commixtio after the Agnus in the "promerendam et capessendam" form. The Augsburg order, read on page images of its printed missals of 1510 and 1555, differs on each point (commixtio before the Agnus, "ad vitam eternam capessendam", "qui dixisti", ablution prayers, no Meritis), so Augsburg use is excluded. No missal of Brixen (the diocese of Stams), Salzburg or Freising and no Benedictine missal was checked, and the Carthusian order of Mass was not tested.
    • Added Mass (likely Visitation): introit, verse, collect and gradual agree with the Visitation Mass of the 1474 Roman Missal (vol. 2, p. 208), whose collect reads "... ad salutationem beate elizabeth inspirasti: presta quesumus: ut per eius visitationem donis celestibus repleamur", and whose lesson is "Lectio libri sapientie" from the Song of Songs ending "tua decora". The Assumption normally has the gradual Propter veritatem and a lesson from Ecclesiasticus, so it does not fit; that point rests on general knowledge and was not checked in a source. The feast word after "de cuius" falls at the fold and is not read.
  • Transcription: source-assisted. The image-only first reading covers both faces, with the small-hand zone mostly unread. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's reading of the whole recto (17 lines), the main-hand lines of the verso and the five lines on the folded bottom flap, with lines 1-8 of the small hand read by an independent checker. The rest of the line after "Agnus dei" on the recto is ghost-faint and unread; the verso's line 2 is a faded ghost line; "unus deus" after "trinitas" does not seem to fit the gap but the line is faded; "elizabet inspirasti" is a probable reading, not a certain one. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the main text with page images of L 305 (ff. 144r-144v), of the 1474 Roman Missal (pp. 210-211) and of the Cistercian Ordo in Der Katholik 1882 (pp. 326-327). The use claim was tested against Seckau, Brenner's codex and the Augsburg printed order on page images of nineteenth-century editions or descriptions; the Cistercian missals Dijon 114 and Pontarlier 8, which have the same sequence, and the Italian order were seen only in OCR text of printed descriptions. An independent checker read lines 1-8 of the small hand on the verso (canvas 0, native boxes 2850,2950,4100,3600 and 4000,2950,5300,3600, with blue-channel crops) before opening the source, and compared them with the Visitation Mass of the 1474 Roman Missal, vol. 2, p. 208 (page image). Introit, verse, collect and gradual agree as far as legible, apart from "Collecta" for "Oratio", "exh[abundancia]" and "elizabet"; the lesson lines read ("transiliens colles ... en ipse stat post parietem nostrum ... [can]cellos") fall within the Song of Songs lesson that the print gives by incipit and explicit. The feast word remained unread. The checker had seen the research reader's partial reading of the small hand first, and also confirmed the reversed labels from the matching notches on the two images and the run of the text. Gaps: no person has reviewed the reading. No Brixen, Salzburg, Freising, Benedictine or Carthusian missal was compared, and no second Cistercian missal from the German-speaking area. The Visitation lesson was compared only by incipit and explicit.

The differences from L 305 are listed under Identification. Against the 1882 Cistercian Ordo the leaf has "sit tibi" for "tibi sit", and probably lacks "unus Deus" after "trinitas". The commixtio wording "ad vitam eternam promerendam et capessendam" differs from the Dominican form "ad vitam capessendam aeternam" and from the Augsburg "ad vitam eternam capessendam". In the added Mass the small hand writes "Collecta", "exh-" and "elizabet"; nothing else legible differs from the 1474 print. The embolism is the common text.

Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

  • Recto and verso: the two images are the two faces of one leaf; a notch on the left edge of canvas 1 mirrors one on the right edge of canvas 0. The face carrying the earlier part of the Ordo (canvas 1, pencilled 69v) is the recto. About six or seven lines, the end of the "Domine Ihesu Christe" prayer and the Corpus and Sanguis formulas, are lost or folded under at the foot of the recto.
  • The added Mass: written in a smaller, later-looking hand in the space below the end of the Ordo, much faded except on the folded flap. If it is the Visitation, it is later than the institution of that feast in 1389; when the Cistercians took up the feast was not established.
  • Other marks: pencil "VI,c Jk 17" and the pencil shelfmarks "Stams, STA, Frg. 69r" and "69v"; a later pen scribble over the line after "Agnus dei".
  • Not claimed: the date, the origin, the use of the book (Cistercian is probable, not established), that it was made or used at Stams, the feast of the added Mass (likely, not read), and any relation to F-l7tu (Frg. 212), which shares the Meritis ending but has a larger script (line pitch about 155 against about 113 pixels here).
