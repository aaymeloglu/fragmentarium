Frg. 226 (F-mgy1)

Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Deed of sale: Abbot [Bernhard] and the convent of Stams sell Hans V[eh]lin and Anton Welser of [Memmingen] a redeemable yearly rent of 50 Malter rye from the abbey's great corn tithe at Kettershausen (Augsburg diocese) for 1100 Rhenish gulden; Friday after St George, probably [14]92 (27 April 1492); cancelled original in two strips

The two strips are one charter, each line running from item 1 on into item 2. Issuer, buyers, object, place, 50 Malter (25 each), price 'ailffhundert' gulden, payment between Martinmas and Christmas, redemption, sealing and the dating clause 'In dem Zwa[y]vnd ne[w]ntzigisten Jare An freytag nach sant Jörgen' are read; the abbot's name and the hundreds of the year are lost at the left edge and supplied, and the unit 'Zwa[y]' is uncertain. Hans Vöhlin died on 18 March 1496 (snippets), which excludes 1496 and fixes 27 April 1492; Abbot Bernhard of Stams is attested in 1492 (monasterium.net regests). A fresh reader given neutral crops confirmed the issuer, Anton Welser, 50 Malter, Augsburg diocese on the Günz, Kettershausen, the seal clause, the St George clause and the endorsement; it lost the year's tens under holes, could not read the sums and read the first buyer's faded surname otherwise; the coordinator reread the year as above, the sums as 'ailffhundert' and the surname as V[eh]lin, and the third person's forename, [Er?]hard or Bernhard Vöhlin of Memmingen, keeps its first letters lost. No outside record of the sale was found; no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-mgy1: Abbot [Bernhard] and the convent of Stams sell Hans Vöhlin and Anton Welser of Memmingen a redeemable rent of 50 Malter rye from the tithe at Kettershausen, Friday after St George, probably [14]92 (27 April 1492)

These two parchment strips in the archive of the Cistercian abbey of Stams are adjacent parts of one cancelled deed of sale. The abbot and convent of "Gotzhauß zu sand Johans ze Stams des Ordens von Citel [Bri]xner Bistumbs" (the house of St John at Stams, Cistercian order, diocese of Brixen) act after deliberation in chapter, to avert growing damage to their house. They sell to "den Ersamen und weysen Hansen V[eh]lin und Anthonien Welser" and their heirs a yearly rent of fifty Malter (a grain measure) of rye, twenty-five to each buyer. The rent is drawn from the abbey's great corn tithe at "Kättrißhausen" (Kettershausen) "in Augspurg Bistumb an der Guntz gelegen", in the diocese of Augsburg on the river Günz. The price is "ailffhundert" (eleven hundred) good Rhenish gulden, paid in cash. The rent is due every year between St Martin's day and Christmas. If the harvest fails, the abbey delivers other grain of the same quality or pays the Memmingen price ("Memmingn werunge") for each Malter. The vicar of the church keeps his fixed portion of forty Malter of rye from the tithe, which the abbey promises not to change; in that clause "[Er?]harden Vehlin auch burg[er] zu Memm[ingen]" is named, in a role not established. The deed gives warranty and damages, lets the buyers distrain (seize) the abbey's people and goods with or without court, renounces all privileges and exceptions, and reserves the abbey's right to buy the rent back for 1100 gulden. Abbot and convent announce their seals. The dating clause reads "Der geben ist Nach Cristi vnsers [lieben herrn geburt ...] [dar]nach In dem Zwa[y]vnd ne[w]ntzigisten Jare An freytag nach sant Jörgen des hailigen Ritters tag": Friday after St George in the [..]-ninety-[second] year, 27 April 1492. The endorsement marks the deed "Cassirt", cancelled. The strips were later cut into a comb shape and used in a binding.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 226, two parchment items, 437 x 189 mm (item 1 measured 431/437 x 183/189 mm), with the title "x" and no date, type or description. Modern additions are the pencil shelfmarks "Stams, STA, Frg. 226-1r/1v/2r/2v". The record has nothing to correct beyond the missing regest; it can add that the two items are one charter, item 1 the left and item 2 the right strip.

  • Identification: identified, high. One document. Every line runs from the first strip straight on into the second: line 3 "Sonder auch dam[it]" continues "[d]it wir uns und unsers Gotzhaus wachsenden schaden furkomen"; the corroboration formula "zu warem vestem v[nd] o[ffen-]" continues "[-]em urkund"; the date "An freytag nach sant J[ö-]" continues "[-]rgen des hailigen Ritters tag". The left end of every line is lost on a third strip that does not survive. The regest is read on the image: issuer, buyers, object, place, quantity, price, payment term, redemption, sealing, and the day and decade of the date. The first buyer's surname is faded and read "V[eh]lin"; the endorsement independently names "Hannsen Vehlin". Supplied, not read: the abbot's name [Bernhard], lost with the left end of line 1; the hundreds of the year, [14], lost in the same way; and the buyers' town [Memmingen], given in the text only for the third person and by the Memmingen measure. The unit of the year is damaged by holes and read "Zwa[y]" (two), uncertain; the other unit that fits the traces is "Sechs" (six). The parties decide: Hans Vöhlin, head of the Memmingen Vöhlin company and father-in-law of Anton Welser, died on the Friday after Laetare 1496, 18 March 1496 (his gravestone in St Martin's, Memmingen, quoted in a description of that church, 1846; R. Eirich, Memmingens Wirtschaft und Patriziat von 1347 bis 1551, 1971; Google Books snippets). A buyer of April 1496 is therefore impossible, and Anton Welser moved to Augsburg that year. The hand and the parties (Anton Welser the elder, 1451-1518) give the fifteenth century. Bernhard Wälsch was abbot of Stams 1484-1501, and the Stams charter regests on monasterium.net name "Abt Bernard" on 25 February and 29 September 1492 (AT-StiAStams/Urkunden O_XX_2, M_XI_12). The incorporation of Kettershausen church into Stams was confirmed by Pope Innocent VIII on 26 July 1492 (Eines Fürsten Traum, 1995, snippet), which fits a deed of April 1492 that fixes the vicar's portion. No independent record of the sale was found.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers both text faces, the endorsement and the set-off on the backs. Its reader had no proposed identification in context. It could not read the year and gave its decade as "[?]vndzwaintzigisten" (twenty). The source-assisted reading is a research reader's (Claude) reread of both text faces at native resolution in bands of about eight lines, with enhanced crops (autocontrast, equalised blue channel) of the year words, the sums, the names and the rotated endorsement. It reads the decade as "ne[w]ntzigisten" (ninety), the sums as "ailffhundert", the third person's town as "Memm[ingen]", and aligns the two strips line by line. Line ends in the cut-away gaps of both strips are physically lost. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: there is no outside text of the act to compare. A fresh reader who had not seen the reading was given neutral crops of the words the regest depends on and asked what each says: the last lines of both strips with the year and the feast, an enlargement of the year word, the first line, the buyers' names, the place and diocese, the third person, the two sums and the endorsement. The fresh reader confirmed "Anthonien Welser" with "funfftzig Malt[er]", "Augspurg Bistumb ... an der Guntz", "Kittrißhausen", "Gotzhauß zu Sand [Jo]hans ... Ordens von Citel", the sealing clause "Abbt und Conuent ... hencken an disen brieff. Der geben ist Nach Cristi ...", "[J]örgen des hailigen Ritters tag" and the endorsement ("Sannt Johanns ... Stams ... funfftzig malter Roggen"). It lost the tens of the year under the holes but saw "...unzigist..."; the coordinator reread the last line as "in dem Zwa[y]vnd ne[w]nzigisten Jare An freitag nach sant [Jörgen]". The fresh reader could not read the number word of the sums; the coordinator read "ailffhundert" in both places. For the first buyer the fresh reader read "Hansen Gebeln(?)" with low confidence; the coordinator saw a faded "V[eh]lin", so the surname is marked. For the third person the fresh reader read "[..]rnharden", the coordinator "[..]rharden"; the forename is Erhard or Bernhard, its first letters lost. The research reader checked Hans Vöhlin's death and the abbot's dates in book snippets and monasterium.net regests, not page images. Not checked: Lebersorg's Stams chronicle, the Stams archive's own inventories beyond monasterium.net, the Memmingen town archive and the Welser and Vöhlin papers. No human review.

No edition or regest of this sale was found, so there is no source column. The date is converted in the Julian calendar: St George (23 April) 1492 was a Monday, and the Friday after it 27 April 1492; the result is the same if St George is kept on 24 April. Related records show the setting, not the act: Stams received the patronage of St Michael's at Kettershausen ("Kathrishausen") from the lords of Freiberg in 1403 and sold the great tithe there in 1546 (Stams regests G_XX_1, G_XXII_1, N_XV_1 on monasterium.net); a notarial instrument of 15 July 1499 at Memmingen names the proctor of Abbot Bernhard of Stams and the vicar of Kettershausen (Brno, Diecézní archiv, on monasterium.net).

Physical notes and limits

  • Two long strips of one charter, each with a continuous spine on the left and four or five tabs on the right separated by cut-away gaps, the shape of binding strips. About 60 lines in a German cursive book hand of the later fifteenth century. The left end of every line is lost, and line ends are lost in the gaps of both strips.
  • Sealing is announced, but the foot of the sheet with any fold and seal tags is cut away. The endorsement on the back of the first strip (canvas 1) ends "Cassirt": the rent was redeemed and the deed returned and cancelled, which explains how it came back to the abbey.
  • The backs carry mirror-image set-off of another written leaf from the strips' use in a binding (canvas 1 and 3, tabs); it was not read. The host volume is not named.
  • Not claimed: the abbot's name, the hundreds and the unit of the year as read (both supplied or uncertain, see above), the place of issue, the role of the third person, and the date of redemption.
