F-nei7 — Gent, Universiteitsbibliotheek, HS.2582/164

Identification: Partial, with low identification confidence. The leaf contains an anonymous scholastic discussion of divine Word and Love, creation, and the Father's innascibility, probably from a commentary or question sequence on Sentences I. Its references to distinctions and to book II support that context. No author, exact distinction, or specific edition has been identified. The primary texts compared below offer related arguments, not a sustained textual match.

Transcription: The preserved image-only reading covers the recto's opening six complete lines and a cropped seventh. It contains substantial unresolved abbreviations and several mistaken tentative readings. The separate source-assisted revision, made on 22 September 2026, corrects and extends selected passages after exposure to the preceding report and external texts. Neither record is a verified full-leaf transcription.

Verification: A Codex reader inspected both full-side photographs and selected original-detail cached crops, comparing recto 23–26 and 40–48 with primary-text transcriptions in LombardPress. This is model image review, not human palaeographic verification. No candidate print image was successfully inspected. The comparisons establish shared subject matter and argumentative parallels; they do not establish authorship or certify the transcription.

Catalogue and image coverage

The Fragmentarium catalogue and IIIF manifest identify one parchment leaf, 315 × 198 mm, in Latin, with the title “Unidentified text on metaphysics,” a catalogue date of 1301–1350, and the script classification Northern Textualis. The date and script description are catalogue statements, not new conclusions of this review.

The inspected images are the manifest's canvases labelled fol. [1]r and fol. [1]v. The recto has a single column of 48 visible text lines. The verso has ten visible lines near the top, with a worn lower part of the text block and an underlined final line. Much of the remaining surface carries no legible text in the photograph. These images do not establish whether that area was erased, why the text stops, or whether the leaf preserves the original page edges.

The frozen packet is the cached recto top band, approximately 2000 × 455 pixels. This review also inspected cached full-side images and recto bands 2, 4, 7, and 8, verso bands 1 and 2, and a detail of verso line 5. The bands overlap: repeated rows are not extra manuscript lines. They are reduced derivatives, and exact native-image crop coordinates have not been recovered. Line references below count from the top text row on each side and can be located in the public viewer. No new enhancement was performed.

The counting construction in v.3–4 revises the preceding report's quia ille tres / quia ille. The abbreviated participle remains tentative, but cum predictis and the counts are visible. The text distinguishes what innascibility names negatively from what it allows one to understand positively. This requires more care than assigning the author to a school from “negation-only” vocabulary.

The internal references include supra d.18, infra d.26, and, at r.39, “Sed hoc magis spectat ad 2m librum.” These support a Sentences context. They do not securely locate this leaf in a particular distinction: references may anticipate or recall discussions without mapping the present passage to a narrow numbered range. The earlier preference for distinction 20 is therefore not retained as a finding. Verso 6–9 have not been newly transcribed; only “Circa 2m principale” is retained from the underlined v.10.

Targeted web retrieval used the connected phrase about philosophers who ignored the emanation of the divine persons, the innascibilitas / catholice / sustineri cluster, and relegandum a divinis. It returned no sustained candidate passage. That result neither excludes an author nor proves the work unedited. Earlier no-hit searches and vocabulary-based exclusions are not carried forward as positive evidence.

Result and remaining limits

The supported result is an anonymous theological discussion in a probable Sentences I context, Partial / low. Identification confidence applies to that limited contextual claim; it does not grade transcription accuracy. The assisted review improves particular readings and supplies checkable comparisons, while leaving the author, compiling work, exact locus, and many abbreviations unresolved.

The next discriminating check would be a legible image of Meyronnes's relevant Quodlibet passage or another candidate carrying the manuscript's connected Word/Love discussion and philosophers corollary. A title or isolated doctrinal resemblance will not suffice. A higher-quality image of the worn verso could recover further argument or cross-references. No claim of literary dependence, witness novelty, erased lower text, or refined date is made.
