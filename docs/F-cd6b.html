Guillelmus Redonensis (ascribed; printed 1603 as glosses of Johannes de Friburgo), Apparatus in Summam de casibus Raymundi, book III: dispensations; res iudicata and the opening of excommunication

All four surviving column portions agree closely in wording and order of lemmata with the gloss columns of the Rome 1603 edition, pp. 348-350 and 384-386, compared by a research reader on page images; the coordinator read the leaf 2 verso glosses Querelam, Engeltrudam and Si quem (canvas 0, box 180,1120,1560,1440) against the page image of p. 386. Two blind readers transcribed five crops: they supported 'de quaestionibus', 'falsi' (one reader) and the frame of the lemma 'engel..dam', but not 'presbyteros', 'nisi' before 'a xx', or the extra lemmata 'evidenter' and '[de] utraque', which are given as uncertain. The leaf differs from the print in several allegations and has notes the print lacks; nothing is inferred about the state of the apparatus. The ascription to William of Rennes rests on Pennington 1971 and a Bodleian catalogue entry seen as a search result; the 1603 title page, read in OCR only, names Johannes de Friburgo; the copy's date is the catalogue's.

Readings and comparison details

F-cd6b: Guillelmus Redonensis (ascribed), Apparatus in Summam de casibus Raymundi, book III, glosses on dispensations and on sentences

This trimmed bifolium carries the apparatus of glosses on the Summa de casibus (also called Summa de paenitentia) of Raymond of Peñafort, book III, written out on its own without Raymond's text. Each note opens with a word or phrase of Raymond's (a lemma), underlined in red, and often goes on "ibi dicitur" to summarise the canon or decretal that Raymond cites. Leaf 1 has the glosses on the title on dispensations, from the gloss Tutum to Praecipiunt. Leaf 2 has the glosses on Res iudicata and Quibusdam casibus in the title on sentences and the opening glosses of the section on excommunication, Querelam, Engeltrudam and Si quem, with a red rubric "De sententia excom[municationis]". Scholarship ascribes the apparatus to Guillelmus Redonensis (William of Rennes). The Rome 1603 edition, which prints it in the margins of Raymond's text, calls it the glosses of Johannes de Friburgo.

The Fragmentarium record describes Antwerpen, Erfgoedbibliotheek Hendrik Conscience, Fragm. 55: one trimmed and restored parchment bifolium, 157 x 119 mm, Northern Textualis, red decoration, dated 1251-1300, with the title "Gloss on the Decretals" and the tags "Canon Law" and "Commentary". The subject tags fit. The title does not name the work: the text is a gloss on Raymond's Summa, and the decretals and canons it summarises are the ones Raymond cites. The record names no author or work. Its date and script are the catalogue's statements and were not examined here.

  • Identification: identified, confidence high. All four surviving column portions agree closely in wording and in the order of lemmata with the gloss columns of the Rome 1603 edition, pp. 348-350 and 384-386; the allegations differ in several places, the leaf has some notes the print lacks, and smaller variants are listed under Textual evidence. Two alternatives were tested. Raymond's Summa itself is the text commented on, not the text on the leaf: the shared phrases ("querela falsi non tollitur", "a fraterna societate separat", "pro veritate accipitur", "redire ad gradus pristinos") stand in the gloss columns of the 1603 print, not in Raymond's text, and the passages that look like questions ("Sed numquid ... R(espondeo) ...") are part of the printed gloss. A gloss on the Decretals, the catalogue's title, does not fit, because the notes follow the order of Raymond's citations, not a decretal title. Later works that might reuse this apparatus were not compared, nor were manuscripts of the apparatus. The attribution is recorded both ways: Guillelmus Redonensis in the scholarship, Johannes de Friburgo on the 1603 title page.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers both faces; the source-assisted reading gives the lines relied on. A research reader reread on the images, with the edition at hand: leaf 1 recto lines 1-10 and its last 8 lines, leaf 1 verso lines 1-8 and its last 9 lines, leaf 2 recto lines 1-8 and its last 6 lines, and leaf 2 verso lines 1-7. The rereading corrects the first reading in several places that bear on the match, for example "engel[tru]dam(?)" for its "cum quedam", "de q(uesti)o(n)ib(us)" for "de quibus" and "f(a)l(s)i" for "fil(ii)". The rest of the bifolium rests on the first reading. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the reread passages with page images of the Rome 1603 edition, pp. 348, 349, 350, 384, 385 and 386; the edition's machine text was used only to find the pages. The coordinator (Claude) read the leaf 2 verso passage from "obici potest" to "In c. si quem dicitur quod" (canvas 0, native box 180,1120,1560,1440) on the image against the page image of p. 386 of the 1603 edition: the glosses Querelam, Engeltrudam and Si quem follow the print in wording and in sequence. Two fresh readers, given five neutral crops of this record without the transcription or the source, transcribed them. They supported "de q(uesti)o(n)ib(us)" (one reader's letters), "f(a)l(s)i" (one reader), "ibi d(icitu)r" and the red rubric "De sententia", and the frame of the lemma "engel..dam" with a mark at the l, though neither wrote "-tru-". They did not support four readings, which are therefore printed below as uncertain: the word after "plures" on leaf 1 verso (both read a word like "p(ro)b(at)os", not "p(res)b(yte)ros"); a "n(isi)" before "a xx annorum" (both saw no word there); the text-line words "de s(ente)n(t)ia excom(m)unicatio(n)is" (both read "de sua exequato(r)is" or similar); and the two underlined lemmata "euide(n)t(er)" and "[de] utraq(ue)" (read as "eudent/eade(m)" and "dutq(ue)"). Both also read the abbreviation before "minor" as "eg°", printed here as "ex(communicati)o(?)". Gaps: the upper part of leaf 2 verso is lost; the middle lines of each column portion rest on the first reading; no manuscript of the apparatus and no modern edition of Raymond's Summa was compared; no palaeographer or canonist has reviewed the reading or the match.

Differences. The leaf and the print differ in the allegations (the citations of laws and canons) in several places: "§ meminisse" for "§ si ex causa", "l. si pens(?) (et) l. ult." for "l. 1 in fi. & l. 2 & 3", "ii q. vi § quippe" for "3. q. 6. haec quippe", "§ Item quorum" for "§ sunt etiam quorum", "§ Si(?) quis posset" for "§ sed quis posset". The leaf writes "In l. querela dicitur" where the print has the lemma Querelam. Smaller variants: "celebrat ... committens" for "celebrans ... committit", "de manu" for "de manibus", "diuidere duos" without "inter", "laicis uel monachis" for "ex monachis, vel laicis", "in episcopis" for "in Episcopos", "quis presens fuit" for "aliquis praesens fuerit", "resci(n)d(it)(?)" for "recidit". The leaf has notes the print lacks at these places: "c. cum non ignores. pa(ri)(?) fac(it)(?)" on leaf 1 verso, where the print has only Et capitulum; after "denuntiasse § ult." on leaf 2 recto, "sic notauit(?) Jo. ... Inter dilectos ... res inter alios etc." and an underlined lemma read "contenti(osum)(?)", followed by the content of the print's gloss L. 2 as a note on "c. quod ad consultationem"; and, on leaf 2 verso, two underlined lemmata read "euide(n)t(er)(?)" and "[de] utraq(ue)(?)" (glossed "maiori siue minori"), both uncertain on the blind check. Whether the 1603 editor revised the allegations was not tested. Nothing is inferred from these differences about the relation of this copy to the printed text or about the state of the apparatus.

The gloss stands in the side columns, keyed by letters. The title page, with the name of Johannes de Friburgo, was read in the scan's machine text only, not on the page image. The ascription to Guillelmus Redonensis rests on Kenneth Pennington, "Summae on Raymond de Pennafort's Summa de casibus in the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Munich", Traditio 27 (1971), 471-480, notes 3 and 17, which says that Raymond's Summa "with the apparatus of Guillaume de Rennes was printed in a Rome 1603 edition" and dates the apparatus to about 1240-1245; and on the Bodleian medieval catalogue's work entry "Guillaume de Rennes, Apparatus in Summam sancti Raymundi", seen only as a search result. The date of about 1240-1245 is that of the apparatus, not of this copy. The attribution was not checked further.

Leaf order and limits

The text fixes the arrangement: a single-column bifolium seen opened flat. On canvas 0 (the outside of the bifolium) the left block is the lower part of leaf 2 verso (print p. 386) and the right block, an upright piece above a horizontal strip, is the whole column of leaf 1 recto (pp. 348-349). On canvas 1 (the inside) the left block is the whole column of leaf 1 verso (pp. 349-350) and the right block is the lower part of leaf 2 recto (pp. 384-385). Leaf 1 recto ends "i(bi) p(ro)hi(betur) neq(ui)s" and leaf 1 verso goes on "[...] eccl(esi)am diuide(re) duos", as the print runs on "ne quis praesumat ecclesiam diuidere inter duos". The print's gloss between the end of leaf 2 recto and the surviving part of leaf 2 verso is about the length of the lost upper part of leaf 2 verso. The gloss of pp. 350-384 is missing between the two leaves, so further leaves stood inside this bifolium. The red rubric "De sententia excom[municationis]" stands where the print begins § 9, Sequitur de sententia excommunicationis.

Red marks in the upper margins, "iii" over leaf 1 recto and a stroke like "I" or "L" over leaf 1 verso, may be a running title "Li(ber) iii" across the opening; that is not established. A small word in the lower margin of canvas 0 under leaf 2 verso is unread.

Not claimed: a date or origin for the copy beyond the catalogue's; any relation between this copy and the 1603 print or other manuscripts of the apparatus; that the notes the print lacks belong to an earlier or later state of the apparatus; a decision between the two attributions; the title numbering in Raymond's book III, which was not checked against the modern edition of the Summa.
