  • Transcription: selected assisted image readings. The isolated upper-left crop produced almost no continuous text. Reading a useful question and its response

The preserved image-only response is explicitly very incomplete. Its conjectural alteratio and ratione cannot sustain a source search. The subsequent selection of more legible passages was answer-aware, not another independent reading.

Canvas2, column b, crop1280,370,1180,600, begins with an apparent question. Selected assisted opening, first two rows:

Dubitatur hic … utrum s(cilicet) magis vivant calida / et humida quam temperata …

The intervening words at the beginning and the following objection remain uncertain. The crop's sixth row shows a crossed-out word and a supralinear replacement appearing to be magis; the cancelled form is not reconstructed. A smooth expansion would conceal this intervention.

Canvas3, column a, crop520,900,1100,580, provides a separate response. Selected rows6–10:

… Ad istam questionem est intelligendum / q(uod) vita stat p(er) calidum et humidum et ex defectu ipsius calidi / mors … Si autem calidum posset stare sine humido … / … calidum non potest stare sine humido / quia ipsum humidum pabulum est ipsius calidi …

Parentheses mark proposed expansions; omissions and unresolved words remain visible. This connected passage supports the topic and question-commentary form. It is not an exact match to a named author's text.

An additional canvas1 column-b crop1770,900,1108,720 includes in libro de morte et / vita … marcedo et extinctio, followed by the distinction between corruption from itself and from another. The relevant words occur in the upper part of the band; several line beginnings are cut. This gives a textual cross-reference beyond a general inference from heat/moisture vocabulary. It does not settle which Parva naturalia work supplies the running base text.

Candidate comparison

The Lokert collection containing Buridan's questions, Paris1518, IA ARes25204, OCR near the question whether natural heat can corrupt itself, has the same Aristotelian opposition marcedo/extinctio and candle/wood examples. It then enumerates five doubts, beginning with whether a thing can corrupt itself and whether things sharing a subject can corrupt one another. That is not a demonstrated match to the selected question/response above. The available OCR is corrupt, so absence of a phrase cannot exclude another reading, recension or related compilation.

There is also a bibliographical correction to the old search account: the relevant OCR pages around the self-corruption discussion carry the running heading De iuventute et senectute. The old description treating the entire searched range as De longitudine was too broad. Exact foliation and edition layout were not newly checked on images.

Searches on22September2026 for vita stat per calidum et humidum with pabulum, utrum magis vivant with temperata, and the inherited tres gradus calidi with humidi did not identify this sequence. The arithmetic passage was not fully reread in this pass; its query is expressly inherited evidence. Generic heat/moisture parallels do not identify Buridan, Jandun, Burley, or another commentator.

Result

Retain a subject/genre refinement only: anonymous scholastic questions in the Parva naturalia tradition. Exact authorship and the base work remain unresolved. The earlier broad exclusions based on format or no-hits, detailed degrees/years reconstruction, continuous leaf order and central-bifolium claim are not newly established. The saved before version retains those earlier readings and proposals. A useful next comparison must match the question, its connecting argument and response over a sustained span, preferably on actual witness images. No specialist reviewed these new readings and no accuracy rate was measured.
