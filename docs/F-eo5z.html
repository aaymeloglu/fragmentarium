Richard of Middleton, Quaestio disputata de gradu formarum; leprous-parent and asses arguments

F-eo5z — Richard of Middleton, De gradu formarum

  • Identification: identified; high confidence. Richard of Middleton, Quaestio disputata de gradu formarum. The leprosy argument followed by the ass/intellective-soul argument agrees in distinctive wording and sequence with the excerpts in Hocedez, Richard de Middleton (1925), p. 392. The familiar leprosy maxim alone does not establish this attribution. The comparison identifies the text but does not independently establish authorship of the underlying work.
  • Transcription: selected, source-assisted readings; accuracy unmeasured. Two isolated readers transcribed separate crops, and CATMuS recognized the first crop. All raw outputs were preserved before comparison. They contain substantial errors and gaps. The selected readings below are the coordinator's source-assisted image readings with candidate transcriptions and Hocedez available. No specialist has checked these readings.
  • Verification: manuscript crops compared with a scanned printed source. The coordinator inspected native crops of fol. [2]v column b and fol. [1]r column a, and the image of Hocedez p. 392. Hocedez supplies selected Latin quotations and French discussion, not a full edition of the fragment. Zavalloni's complete edition and apparatus remain unchecked. The remaining columns, proposed leaf order, and witness novelty remain unverified.

A marginal abbreviation beside line 5 is provisionally expanded p(er)f(ec)te(?). The image-only reader independently noticed both the cancellation and the marginal writing but could not resolve the latter.

Crop D: manifest canvas 0, fol. [1]r column a; native region 4650,1450,1950,1500, no rotation. Selected lines 2–8:

2 introduceretur forma asini [abbreviated words unresolved here]
3 anima intellectiva infunditur a deo m(ater)ie ipsius hominis
4 in illo eodem [instanti?] m(ater)ia ipsius asini equaliter esset
5 apta ad recipiendum animam intellectivam sicut
6 m(ater)ia ipsius hominis. Quia dispositio ipsius m(ater)ie ad hoc
7 q(uod) sit nata recipere formam est denudatio ab omni alia
8 forma. …

These connected clauses provide better evidence than the common leprosy maxim. They agree with Hocedez's quoted argument about an ass's matter being equally ready to receive an intellective soul once stripped of every other form. A material difference remains: the manuscript appears to have equaliter, where Hocedez prints sequeretur. This must stay a reported variant, not be silently harmonized. The isolated reader's denudata on line 7 differs from the assisted reading denudatio.

The crop also shows the transition to another impossibile at lines 16–18, consistent with Hocedez's sequence, but its last line is clipped. No complete transcription of that next argument is claimed. The isolated reader had no candidate source, while the coordinator inspected the passage with source knowledge.
