Status

IDENTIFIED. Robert Holcot OP (d. 1349), Super Sapientiam Salomonis (Postilla / lectiones on the Book of Wisdom, Oxford c. 1333–1336), Lectio II (end) and Lectio III (beginning) on Wisdom 1:1–2, in the numbering of the Hagenau 1494 edition. Verified line by line against the page images and OCR of Hagenau: Heinrich Gran, 1494 (Biblioteca de la Universidad de Sevilla copy, archive.org A336075, viewer leaves n40–n41), and against the API snippets of the Paris 1509 edition (Google Books LBQi1LuRX9IC, ObnT1yi7gmkC, HvhJAAAAcAAJ). The visible text: col. a = end of Lectio II ("Non solum autem invenitur ... Apparet autem eis qui fidem habent in illum ... Dubium hic utrum preter virtutes morales acquisitas necesse sit ponere theologicas" + objections) and start of Lectio III ("Ad istud dubium ... superaddantur virtutes theologice ... In simplicitate cordis querite illum: Rectitudo / Gratitudo / Sollicitudo / Altitudo"); col. b = Lectio III continued (Jacob's simplicity, Rom 16:19, Mt 6:22 with Augustine De sermone Domini II, Gregory Hom. in Ezech. I.7, the three simplicities, "cor ... liberaliter, integraliter, perpetualiter", mutuant / vendunt / donant).

Ordinary manuscript-level variation; the fragment is a good, possibly better, witness than the 1494 print. Secondary confirmation: Heiko A. Oberman, Spätscholastik und Reformation (1965), quotes the same Holcot passage ("superaddantur virtutes theologice, que ideo theologice dicuntur quia a solo deo infunduntur et sola divina revelatione in sacra scriptura nobis traduntur"; Google Books qvPYAAAAMAAJ).

Ruled out / checked

  • Not Bonaventure, In Sapientiam (the rhymed divisio Rectitudo / Gratitudo / Sollicitudo / Altitudo is quoted in Rouse, Preachers, Florilegia and Sermons, and Rivers, Preaching the Memory of Virtue and Vice, as an example of rhymed division; the dubium with Aristotle's Ethics and the "mutuant / vendunt / donant" development are Holcot's, and the whole run matches the Holcot print, so Bonaventure was not collated).
  • Not Nicholas of Lyra or Hugh of St Cher on Wisdom (no dubia with responses "Ad primum ... Ad tertium"); not checked line by line because the Holcot match is complete.

Notes / queries

  • Catalogue title "Unidentified theological treatise" can be replaced by: Robertus Holcot OP, Super Sapientiam Salomonis (Postilla in librum Sapientiae), lect. II–III (on Sap. 1:1–2), ed. Hagenau 1494 leaves n40–n41. Stegmüller, Repertorium Biblicum no. 7416. Date 1350–1400 is consistent (work composed c. 1333–1336; very widely copied in Central Europe: Kraków BJ holds a 1445 copy, archive.org jbc.bj.uj.edu.pl.NDIGORP061674, and a c. 1380 miscellany with Holcot, jbc.bj.uj.edu.pl.NDIGORP061554).
  • The leaf is the beginning of the Wisdom commentary proper (chapter 1, second and third lectiones); the lost inner face carries the preceding or following lectio. The turn-in strips visible on the pastedowns (_65A9192_1, _65A9191_1) show the inner face is written in the same hand.
  • Other Toruń covers in this set (F-1exy Lyra; F-3yl2 Aquinas compendium) come from different manuscripts; nothing links this leaf to them beyond the binding workshop.
