Missal, sanctorale: end of the Mass of St Lucy (13 Dec) and Mass of St Thomas the Apostle (21 Dec); use open

A binding strip from one column of a missal: recto with the end of the Lucy Mass (Mt 13:49-52; Off. Offerentur; secret Accepta tibi sit; Co. Diffusa; PC Satiasti) and the opening of the Thomas Mass (In. Mihi autem; Gr. In omnem terram; All. Nimis honorati; Jn 20:24), verso with Jn 20:24-29 and Off. Constitues eos principes. A research reader compared it with Lippe's edition of the Milan missal of 1474, pp. 303-305, on page images, and the chants with Cantus Index; a second reader independently reread verso lines 11-24 against the same pages. The prayers agree with Milan apart from small variants (tibi sit, semper, a longer offertory); the Thomas gradual, Alleluia and offertory differ from Milan's, so the book does not follow the Roman/Milan chant set here, but its use is not established. Verso lines 18-23 are largely illegible and unidentified.

Readings and comparison details

F-fns2: Missal, sanctorale, Masses of St Lucy (end) and St Thomas the Apostle, 13 and 21 December

A narrow vertical binding strip at Stams comes from one column of a missal, the book of the Mass texts. The strip covers the sanctorale, the part of the book with the Masses of saints' days. The recto has the end of the Mass of St Lucy (13 December): the end of the Gospel (Mt 13:49-52), the offertory Offerentur regi, the secret (prayer over the gifts) Accepta tibi sit, the communion Diffusa est gratia and the postcommunion Satiasti domine familiam. It then has the Mass of St Thomas the Apostle (21 December) under a red heading "Thome ap[ostoli]": the introit Mihi autem nimis honorati with Ps. Domine probasti, the gradual In omnem terram with V. Caeli enarrant, the Alleluia V. Nimis honorati, and the start of the Gospel Jn 20:24. The verso continues the Gospel to Jn 20:29 and gives the offertory Constitues eos principes. A cue follows that ends "suppl(iciter)" and a red communion cue. The last lines of the verso are too abraded to read. The prayers agree with the Lucy and Thomas Masses printed in the Milan Missale Romanum of 1474. The chants of the Thomas Mass differ from that book.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv (STA), Frg. 211. It describes the strip as parchment, one fragment item, 325 x 57 mm (measured as 322/325 x 55/57 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no date and no linked description, with two images, Stams_STA_Frg_211_01r and _01v. The record does not name the text. Its physical description fits the images.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the two Masses. The basis is the sequence and wording of the prayers, chants and readings. The Lucy prayers and the order Lucy, then Thomas, agree with the Missale Romanum Mediolani 1474 (ed. Lippe, vol. I, p. 304), read on page images. The chant incipits are confirmed in Cantus Index (g00083, g00302, g00005, g00444, g00473, g00261). The Gospels are Vulgate. The use of the containing book is open. Its Thomas chants (gradual In omnem terram, Alleluia Nimis honorati, offertory Constitues) differ from Milan's (Nimis honorati V. Dinumerabo, Gaudete iusti, In omnem terram). This shows only that the book does not follow the Roman/Milan chant set for this feast. No Salzburg-province or Cistercian missal was available to test the set against. Differences from Milan in the prayers: the secret reads Accepta tibi sit (Milan Accepta sit tibi). The postcommunion has eius [quesumus] semper interventione (Milan lacks semper). The offertory continues in laetitia et exsultatione adducentur, where Milan stops at offerentur tibi. In the Gospel the strip has cum e[is] and Dicit ei ihesus, the Vulgate readings, where Milan has cum illis and Dixit.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers both faces. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's reread at reading size of all 24 recto lines and all 24 verso lines, the verso also in contrast-enhanced crops. A second research reader reread verso lines 11-24. Verso lines 17-24 remain largely unread: lines 18-23 show only fragments ("omnip(oten)s d(eu)s ut beat[i]", "[..]andi ubi d[..]", "ap(osto)li tui · P(er)"), which match neither the Milan postcommunion Adesto nobis nor the following Milan collect. The words struck through in red on recto line 19 are read with doubt. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the recto and the Gospel on the verso with the Lucy and Thomas Masses in Lippe's edition of the Milan missal of 1474, pp. 303-305, on archive.org page images, and the chant incipits with Cantus Index records. A second research reader independently reread verso lines 11-24 (the end of the Gospel, the offertory and the secret/communion cue) and compared them with the same Milan pages on page images and the offertory with Cantus Index g00261. That check confirmed the identification and corrected verso line 13 to Dicit ei ihesus. The Vulgate wording of the Gospels was checked from knowledge, not on a page image. A Sarum and an Augsburg missal were seen only as Google Books snippets.

The coordinator compared verso lines 12-14 (canvas 1, native box 2080,3120,3020,3480, "[De]inde dicit thome ... [qui] non vide[runt]", Jn 20:27-29) with Lippe's Milan 1474, p. 305, on the page image (archive.org n338): the legible words agree; the strip is dark and only a few words per line were read.

The differences from the Milan text are those listed under Identification. The chant differences (gradual, Alleluia and offertory of St Thomas) set the containing book apart from the Milan book. The prayer differences are small variants of the kind found between copies of these Masses. Recto line 19, struck through in red, appears to be a cross-reference ("quere in ...", "look for it in ..."), the counterpart of Milan's "Require in uigilia sancte thome". The Thomas collect does not appear in the legible text. The verso cue ending "suppl(iciter)" fits either the secret Debitum ... suppliciter exorantes given as an incipit or the preface cue Et te domine suppliciter.

Source: Missale Romanum Mediolani, 1474, ed. Robert Lippe, vol. I (Henry Bradshaw Society 17, London 1899), pp. 303-305, archive.org missaleromanumme01cath: p. 304, p. 305. Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

  • The recto ends with the opening of Jn 20:24 ("unus ex duodecim") and the verso continues it, so the recto/verso labels are in reading order.
  • A single column with red rubrics and red-touched initials; Gothic textualis. Fold lines and sewing holes at the top and bottom show use in a binding. Faint writing runs at right angles in the upper blank part of the recto. It is unread and may be offset from another leaf.
  • Not claimed: the date of the strip, its use (diocesan, monastic or Cistercian), its origin, the identity of the prayers on verso lines 18-23, or any relation to another manuscript or print.
