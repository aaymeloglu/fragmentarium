Reading and comparison

The preserved image-only response precedes source comparison. Its physical lines 3–5 read, with uncertainty, In generacōe … virtutes? activa et passiva and enim hominis virtus activa est in viro Passiva vero. The abbreviation and singular/plural construction must not be settled solely from a fluent expansion.

The numbered rows preserve physical divisions; ellipses omit unverified words; ? marks an uncertain reading or expansion. The normalized singular requiritur … virtus remains uncertain against the isolated reader's requiruntur? virtutes?; it is not presented as a settled manuscript variant. The preserved notation generacōe with proposed expansion (generacione) is retained in the linked image-only response. Other abbreviation boundaries have not been systematically resolved, so the normalized comparison must not be reused as a diplomatic transcription. The isolated reader flagged crowded strokes around the subsequent adiutorium et adiutor passage. No definite scribal correction is established there.

The 1507 edition, n15, lower right column, has In generatione enim requiritur virtus activa et passiva correspondens active quasi contra eam divisa. It continues at the top left of n16 through the woman/man comparison and shared species. The connecting explanation supports the identification; Genesis 2:18 alone would not.

An additional check in the larger manuscript crop reaches Tercio capitulo … Tu autem insidiaberis calcaneo eius, agreeing with the transition at n16. This supports the local sequence; the full extent of the text on the pastedowns is unverified.
