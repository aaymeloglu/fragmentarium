Readings and comparison details

F-13e8: Stams abbey, register of reckonings (Raittungen) with servants, tenants and officials, 1538, four leaves

Stams, Frg. 178 is four paper leaves from an account book of the Cistercian abbey of Stams in Tyrol. Each entry is a reckoning (Raittung), a settlement of accounts between the abbey ("wir", "vnns") and a named person, with debts and credits set out in two columns and a balance. Six reckonings survive, all in one German Kurrent hand of 1538, with a few later additions in lighter ink. They settle with master Petter the sawyer (Sagmaister), who served from 2 May to 14 September 1538 and was paid off on 15 September; with a ferryman (Ferg) on 12 September, year not on the leaf, and with Michl Ferg the ferryman on 7 November 1538, probably the same man; with Michl Salgeber, the abbey's Amtmann (local steward) at Fliess, for grain owed from the 1537 yield, on 7 November 1538; with Sabina, widow of Frantz Vischmayster, who had been Marstaller (master of the stable) of "my gracious lord of Stams", on 8 October 1538; and with one other man whose heading is lost (2r). The leaf with the 12 September reckoning carries the number 92 at its head, probably a folio number, so the leaves come from a foliated book rather than loose bills.

The Fragmentarium record describes Stams, Frg. 178 as paper, four fragment items, 331 x 184 mm (measured on Frg. 178_1, 157/331 x 90/184 mm), with four images. It gives the title "x", no date, no description and no host volume, so it says nothing about the content and there is nothing in it to correct. A modern pencil note on the leaves, "Einband zu II/G 34 (anno 1586)", names the volume they were used to bind as Stams archive II/G 34, of 1586. That is a modern archival note, not part of the catalogue record, and the year 1586 dates the host volume, not these entries.

  • Identification: identified, high confidence, as an archival document: leaves of a Stams abbey account book of reckonings, 1538. Every reckoning's party, office, action and date is read on the image, and the sums were recomputed and agree (Tyrolean reckoning, 1 fl = 60 kr, 1 kr = 5 Vierer, 1 lb = 12 kr, 1 Mark = 10 lb). The abbey is named in the text: "meins gnedigen herrn von Stambs" on 3v and "Paul von Stams" on 4v. The year 1538 is written on 3r, 4v and 3v and is supported by the text's own references to the "1537 Jarnutz" (yield of 1537), a reckoning of 26 November 1537 and "zins vom 38 Jar" (rent for 1538). Elements not read or uncertain: the year of the 12 September reckoning on 1r, which is not on the surviving part; the heading and the man's name on 2r (Henysl?); two names on 3v; and the middle digit of one grain total on 4v. The account entries are originals in a working book (running totals, insertion marks, deletions, later additions), not copies; there are no seals or signatures. No outside record of these entries is known, and none is expected for an abbey's internal accounts.
  • Transcription: an image-only first reading and an assisted reading, made by a research reader at native size from crops, with the first reading in hand. There is no edition, so "assisted" here means reread with the earlier reading and the arithmetic of the sums, not with a source text. The research reader reread the heads of all six reckonings: 3r, 4r, the upper part of 1r and 2r, the heading of 4v and 3v. A second, independent reader read the lower half of 4v, which until then rested on the first reading. The bottom of 2r and 1r, the left margin note of 3v and the short vertical note on 1v rest on the first reading only. 2v and 1v are otherwise blank. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: no outside record exists, so the record link is the Fragmentarium record itself. The checks are against the image and against the account's own arithmetic.
    • An independent reader read the lower half of 4v on the image (canvas 3, native box 1000,1900 to 3850,4800) knowing the research summary of it but not a transcription, recomputed the sums on 3r, 4r, 1r and 3v, and checked the dates on 4v and 3v and the pencil note on 3v. The 3v wage calculation (11 weeks of a yearly 10 fl, 6 fl and 5 lb, which is 1 Mark 7 lb 11.8 kr) agrees with the written "1 m(ark) 7 lb 11 [k]" and supports both the reading and the units.
    • Two blind readers, given single-word crops without the research reading, answered the name and heading questions, and a third reader adjudicated against the image: "Petter Sagm[aister]" (3r), "Michl Ferg" (4r), "Michl Salgeber Ambtman" and "zu Flies" (4v, where both blind readers had read a P for the F), "am 8 tag Octobris" and "Franntzen Vischmay[sters]" (3v; the forename is written with four minims after the a). All held.
    • The coordinator reread the year on three word-check crops side by side: the dating line of 3r (canvas 2, within native box 4200,300 to 6900,1250), the date line of 4v (canvas 3, within 1000,380 to 3850,1850) and the date line of 3v (canvas 3, within 4250,300 to 6950,1800), with the research reading known. The third digit has a flat top and a bowl curling back to the left, the z-shaped 3 of this period's hands, and differs from the 7. The blind readers had leaned to 1578: both read 1578 on the 4v and 3v crops, and on the 3r crop one read 1578 (alternatively 1538) and the other 1538. The coordinator's reading 1538 agrees with the account's own references to the 1537 yield, the reckoning of 26 November 1537 and the rent for 1538. Page images: canvas 2, canvas 3.

Gaps in coverage: the lower parts of 2r and 1r and the 3v margin note were read only once; the rye side of the 4v account does not reconcile with what is legible; nobody outside this research has reviewed the reading; Stams's holdings at Fliess and the salt carriage to Hall were not checked against a source.

All dates are plain calendar dates, with no feast or weekday to convert, and are Julian (Tyrol changed calendar in 1583): 2 May, 14 and 15 September, 8 October and 7 November 1538; 26 November 1537; 12 September, year not surviving. From 2 May to 14 September 1538 is 136 days, about 117 after Sundays, so 106 working days fits a year with the usual feast days off.

Leaves, other items, and what is not claimed

Leaves 1 and 2 have clipped corners and turned-in margins from reuse as a binding, and their versos (2v, 1v) are mostly covered; 1v has a short vertical note in the margin, not read with confidence. Leaves 3 and 4 are whole, with a horizontal fold. Leaf order within the book is not established beyond the number 92 on 1r. Modern pencil notes on the leaves give the shelfmark ("Stams, STA, Frg. 178") and the host volume ("Einband zu II/G 34 (anno 1586)").

Not claimed:

  • the year of the 12 September reckoning on 1r, or that its ferryman is certainly Michl Ferg of 4r;
  • the name of the man on 2r, the person named after "wittib" on 3v and the surnames of the two witnesses there;
  • the middle digit of the rye total on 4v (7 or 3), the amount lost in the hole above it, or a reconciliation of the rye account;
  • who the abbey's officials named only by forename (Herr Bastian, Paul, Barthl) were;
  • that any other leaves of this account book survive, at Stams or elsewhere.
