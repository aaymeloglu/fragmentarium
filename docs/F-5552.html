Readings and comparison details

F-5552: Johannes de Imola, Commentaria in tertium librum Decretalium, X 3.17.6 (Cum causa)

These two paper pieces are the halves of one leaf from the commentary of Johannes de Imola (Giovanni da Imola, died 1436) on the third book of the Decretals of Gregory IX, in the title on sale (De emptione et venditione, X 3.17), at the chapter Cum causa. The passage discusses which action lies when a seller has been cheated of more than half the just price under the Roman rule of Code 4.44.2, and whether a minor who has sworn not to challenge a sale loses that remedy and the right to be restored to his former position (restitutio in integrum).

The Fragmentarium record describes Gent, Universiteitsbibliotheek, HS.2582/236: paper, 309 x 142 mm, a "physical reconstruction" of two strips, Latin, cursiva, fifteenth century, with the title "Roman law". The subject label fits the citations, which are almost all to the Digest and Code, but not the work, which is a commentary on canon law. The record names no author or text. Its date and script are the catalogue's statements and were not examined here.

  • Identification: identified, high confidence. Three passages from three of the four columns agree with the printed commentary in wording, in the sequence of opinions reported and in the citations, and the leaf has the running title "de emptor[e] et vendi[tore]", the Decretals title. A commentary or lecture on Code 4.44.2 itself (Bartolus, Baldus, Salicetus) was considered, since that law is the subject; it is not the containing work, because those jurists are cited in the third person, "hic" refers to the decretal and its gloss, and the running title is the canon-law one. Other canonists on the same chapter were not collated; a later work reproducing Imola at length is excluded only by the sustained agreement over four columns, including his first-person cross-references ("ut dixi", "per ea que superius dixi"). The manuscript has a clause that the 1575 print lacks: "de officio iudicis deserviente actioni ex eo [...] non autem de officio iudicis competente loco actionis", where the print has only "de officio iudicis competente loco actionis".
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers all four faces; the source-assisted reading gives the lines relied on. A research reader reread on the images: recto column a lines 1-22, recto column b lines 1-8 with the running title, and the right part of verso column a lines 1-10. Two further readers, given the image only, read the running title. All other lines rest on the first reading. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the first passage with a page image of the Venice 1575 edition, fol. 200r column a, except its last line, and compared that line and the other two passages with the uncorrected machine text of the same scan. Verso column b and the faded left part of verso column a were placed by sequence from the first reading and not collated. Differences. In the first passage the manuscript distinguishes the judge's office "serving the action" from the judge's office "standing in place of an action" and says the cited laws concern the former, not the latter; the 1575 text has only the second phrase. The print itself draws that distinction a column earlier (fol. 199v, "officium iudicis nobile quod succedit loco actionis, an vero officium iudicis deserviens actioni"). The two texts differ here; nothing is inferred about how either came to its wording. The manuscript has "xiii li. ff. veteris" where the print has "xii et xiii libro digesti veteris", with a hole just before. In the second passage the manuscript has extra words in line 2 ("dicta l. ii. ut ... et etiam Bal. ibi") that were not fully read. In the third it spells out "beneficium Velleianum" where the print abbreviates.

Edition used: Ioannis ab Imola in primum [-tertium] Decretalium commentaria, Venice 1575, Internet Archive, running head "Ioan. ab Imo. super Tertio Decre." and "De emp. et vend.". The attribution rests on that title page and running head. Google Books also returns the first passage from an edition it dates 1500 under the same author and title (snippet only). The chapter is taken to be Cum causa because the commentary here refers back to "c. cum dilecti supra eodem" and its summary opens with the alternative claim that chapter allows; the lemma itself was not read on a page image. Modern scholarship on the attribution was not consulted.

Leaf order and limits

The catalogue's two "strips" with four faces are read here as one leaf cut vertically in two, each half trimmed to a long octagon and slit down the middle. Canvas 0 has recto column a and the first letters of column b at its right edge; canvas 2 has the rest of column b, the running title and the outer margin; canvas 3 has verso column a; canvas 1 has the line ends of verso column a and verso column b. In that order (canvas 0, 2, 3, 1) the text runs as in the print: the last lines of canvas 0 lead into the first line of canvas 2. The join was inferred from layout and text sequence; the letters on either side of the cut were not matched. The pencil note "Class. 91" on two faces is modern.

By the first reading set against the edition's machine text, the rest of recto column a reports Petrus de Bellapertica, Salicetus and Angelus on the minor's unqualified oath; verso column a continues with notaries informing women and minors of their legal protections and Bartolus's contrary opinion; verso column b weighs the opinions of Dinus and Bartolus. These placements were not collated, and the left part of verso column a has lost most of its ink.

Not claimed: a date or origin for the copy beyond the catalogue's; any relation between this copy and the printed editions or other manuscripts of the commentary; that the manuscript's fuller clause is the author's wording; that the leaf is unrecorded in the literature on Johannes de Imola.
