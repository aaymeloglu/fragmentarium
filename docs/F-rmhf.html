Wien, Österreichische Nationalbibliothek · catalogued as Theological / biblical commentary (unidentified), 1201 – 1300 CE

Peter of Reims OP (Petrus de Remis, d. 1247; attributed), Sermones de tempore (Sunday sermons on the epistles and gospels), Schneyer IV nos. 85 (Alter alterius onera portate, Gal 6:2; front pastedown) and 93-94 (Renovamini spiritu mentis vestrae, Eph 4:23, and the opening of Surge tolle grabatum; flyleaf and back pastedown)

Lead from a CoMMA manuscript-text search (tools/comma.py), checked on page images. A research reader (Claude) read all four text images of the fragment at reading size and collated them on page images of Troyes, Médiathèque du Grand Troyes, Ms. 303 (rubric fol. 135v 'Incipiunt capitula magistri Petri provincialis super epistolas et evangelia', image-checked; sermon lxxxv on fol. 183r col. b, sermon xciii on fol. 187v col. a, its end and sermon xciiii on fol. 188r col. a) and Amiens, BM, Ms. 268 (IIIF canvases 346 and 351). The front pastedown (22 lines on bearing burdensome people: Mt 17, Jer 23, Num 11, Hos 11, Eccli 22, Prov 21, Acts 13) and the flyleaf recto (22 lines on renewal by baptism and the stag) agree with both witnesses in wording and sequence; the flyleaf verso (the phoenix, the explicit 'tanto resurgent gloriosius quanto hic vixerunt humilius', then 'Surge tolle grabatum tuum et ambula') and the back pastedown (Judas Maccabeus cleansing the sanctuary, lines 1-12) were not used in discovery and also agree. The fragment shares several variants with Amiens 268 against Troyes 303 (honus, illorum excipias, vel tedio, portans eos, quam hominem imprudentem, desidia; reformavit, homo without peccator, veneno prave persuasionis) and has a few readings of its own (humoribus? for in moribus; a shorter text on the back pastedown). Attribution: Schneyer, Repertorium IV, Petrus de Remis, Sermones de tempore, no. 85 incipit 'Sunt onera culpae, poenae, morum incompositorum', no. 93 incipit 'Bene dicit apostolus spiritu non carne' and explicit 'quanto hic vixerunt humilius', followed by no. 94 Surge tolle grabatum (archive.org full-text search, snippets only; page images not seen); Schneyer's list of further manuscripts of this series names Amiens 268, Lille 88 and Troyes 303, and his vol. I notes that Peter of Reims is often called only 'provincialis'. The Clairvaux catalogue (Rothschild and Heid 2021, P 71) gives Troyes 303 fol. 135v-216v as Petrus Provincialis Remensis, Sermones in epistolis, in evangeliis, citing Kaeppeli, Scriptores I 3326, and an edition printed under the name of Antony of Padua (Pedeponti 1739); Kaeppeli and that print were not consulted. Lille 88 was compared only in machine HTR. Leaf order, the codicological relation of the four pieces, dating and recension are not established. Transcription is source-assisted and not human-reviewed.

Readings and comparison details

F-rmhf - Peter of Reims, Sunday sermons on the epistles and gospels

  • Identification: identified; high confidence. The four text pieces in the binding of Wien, ÖNB, Cod. 2248 carry two sermons of the Sunday cycle on the epistles and gospels attributed to the Dominican Peter of Reims (Petrus de Remis, provincial of France, bishop of Agen, d. 1247): Schneyer IV, Petrus de Remis, Sermones de tempore, no. 85, Alter alterius onera portate (Gal 6:2), on the front pastedown; and no. 93, Renovamini spiritu mentis vestrae (Eph 4:23), with the opening of no. 94, Surge tolle grabatum, on the flyleaf and back pastedown. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and sequence with two manuscripts of the cycle on page images, over four passages, two of them not used in discovery, and the attribution of those manuscripts in Schneyer and in the Clairvaux catalogue. The attribution to Peter of Reims rests on those repertories and a rubric naming "magister Petrus provincialis"; the cycle was also printed in 1739 under the name of Antony of Padua (not consulted).
  • Transcription: source-assisted; accuracy unmeasured. The readings below are a research reader's (Claude) revision of the preserved isolated image-only reading, made with the witnesses at hand. They cover the visible lines of the front pastedown and flyleaf recto, the flyleaf verso, and lines 1-12 of the back pastedown. Lines lose letters at the cut edges; a few words remain uncertain and are marked. No human has reviewed them against the images.
  • Verification: page images of the fragment and two witnesses. A research reader (Claude) collated the fragment crops listed below with Troyes, Médiathèque du Grand Troyes, Ms. 303, fols. 183r, 187v and 188r, and Amiens, Bibliothèques d'Amiens Métropole, Ms. 268 (IIIF canvases 346 and 351). Lille, Médiathèque Jean Lévy, Ms. 88, a third witness, was compared only in its machine HTR. Schneyer was searched in archive.org's full text (snippets, not page images). Leaf order, the relation between the four pieces, dating and recension are not claimed.

The fragment

The manifest has four images with text: the front pastedown (Cod2248_VDS, 2166 × 2752 pixels), the recto and verso of the front flyleaf (Cod2248_Ir, 2284 × 2945; Cod2248_Iv, 2166 × 2752) and the back pastedown (Cod2248_HDS, 2166 × 2752). Each shows one column of 22 lines in a small textualis with red touches, cut at one side. The flyleaf recto has a faint heading above the column in another script, perhaps Renouat(ur) ho(mo) quot modis, and later marginal notes; these were not read further.

The text of the flyleaf recto runs on, after about one line, into the back pastedown; the flyleaf verso carries text from the end of the same sermon, roughly a hundred of the fragment's lines later by the measure of Troyes; the front pastedown comes from sermon 85, earlier in the cycle. That the pieces come from one manuscript is likely from the hand and layout but has not been checked palaeographically.

Witnesses and attribution

Troyes 303 (13th century; from Clairvaux) contains Peraldus's Summa de vitiis, then, after the explicit, the rubric Incipiunt capitula magistri Petri prouincial(is) sup(er) ep(isto)las (et) eu(an)g(e)lia and a numbered list of themes beginning Hora est iam nos de sompno surgere, Ite in castellum (rubric, fol. 135v, checked on the image). The list numbers Alter alterius onera portate lxxxv, Renovamini spiritu mentis vestre xciii and Surge tolle grabatum xciiii (read in the HTR of the list; the sermons themselves carry rubricated numbers, partly visible on the images). The manifest labels the foliation of this opening as "f. (134v-135v) - 136"; folio numbers below are those written on the pages.

Schneyer, Repertorium der lateinischen Sermones des Mittelalters, IV (1972), under Petrus de Remis (dispositio: 1. Sermones de tempore, 2. de sanctis, 3. de communi sanctorum, 4. de sanctis, series 2, 5. Themata de tempore), lists the de tempore series from Paris, BnF, lat. 12417, fols. 1-26, as nos. 1-103. Found by full-text search of the archive.org scan, snippets only:

  • no. 85, T55, Alter alterius onera portate (Gal 6:2), incipit Sunt onera culpae, poenae, morum incompositorum ..., explicit ... onus sollicitudinum, quia curam eius egit, followed by no. 86 Nemo potest duobus dominis servire (the OCR prints the number before Alter alterius as 95; its successor is 86, and Troyes numbers the sermon lxxxv);
  • no. 93, T59, Renovamini spiritu mentis vestrae (Eph 4:23), incipit Bene dicit apostolus spiritu non carne, in cuius renovatione multi frustra laborant ..., explicit ... tanto resurgunt gloriosius, quanto hic vixerunt humilius, followed by no. 94 Surge, tolle grabatum.

The list of further manuscripts of this series that follows no. 103 includes Amiens 268, Lille 88 and Troyes 303. In vol. I, Schneyer notes that the Dominican Peter of Reims is often introduced only as "provincialis". The Clairvaux catalogue, J.-P. Rothschild and C. Heid, La Bibliothèque de l'abbaye de Clairvaux du XIIe au XVIIIe siècle II (2021), P 71, describes Troyes 303 fols. 135v-216v as Petrus Provincialis Remensis, Sermones in epistolis, in evangeliis, de sanctis, citing Kaeppeli, Scriptores Ordinis Praedicatorum I, no. 3326, and an edition printed at Pedeponti in 1739 under the name of Antony of Padua (archive.org full-text snippets). Kaeppeli and the 1739 print were not consulted.

Amiens 268 (catalogued in ARCA as Peraldus, Summa de vitiis et virtutibus, 13th century, second quarter) also contains the cycle (sermons 85 and 93 seen on its images); its rubric to sermon 93 reads sermo de epistola. Lille 88 (ARCA: Peraldus, Summa, etc.) contains the cycle according to its HTR, which includes the Renovamini sermon; its images were not consulted.

Result and limits

The fragment's two sermons are identified with nos. 85 and 93-94 of the Sunday cycle that Troyes 303, Amiens 268 and Lille 88 transmit and Schneyer and the Clairvaux catalogue assign to Peter of Reims. The fragment shares a group of readings with Amiens 268 against Troyes 303 on both leaves, but three witnesses do not establish a recension or the fragment's place among the many manuscripts Schneyer lists. Humoribus (line 13 of the front pastedown) and the shorter text on the back pastedown are the fragment's own readings or reading errors of this transcription.

Not checked: Schneyer's page images and the exact page of nos. 85 and 93; Kaeppeli no. 3326; the 1739 print under the name of Antony of Padua; Lille 88 on its images; the lower half of the back pastedown; the marginal notes and faint heading of the flyleaf. The ledger's earlier note of a reworded parallel in a fifteenth-century Hungarian Sermones dominicales was not reexamined; the fragment agrees with the Peter of Reims text, not with a reworking.
