Fragm. 101 a-v (F-ziuq)

Antwerpen, Erfgoedbibliotheek Hendrik Conscience · catalogued as Unidentified text on Roman law, 1501 – 1600 CE

Henricus de Piro, Super Institutiones, on Inst. 1.7-1.8 and 1.21-1.24

Six passages on five of the 22 faces (fragm. a-b recto and verso, q-r recto, s-t recto last paragraph, u-v recto) agree in wording, order and legal citations with the Cologne c. 1481 edition (GW 12256), read by a research reader from page images of the Munich copy; the coordinator checked fragm. u-v recto lines 1-10 on the image. Differences are minor (sint/fuerint, an added videre, Continuatio/Continuatur, usque ad/in, singular or plural of ultima voluntas), and the manuscript leaves the title headings blank. The other 17 faces were only surveyed, the lettering of the leaves does not follow the text order, and only one of three incunable editions was compared. Whether the leaves were copied from a print or are an independent manuscript witness is not determined. No human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-ziuq: Henricus de Piro, Super Institutiones, on Institutes 1.7-1.8 and 1.21-1.24

The paper leaves are identified with high confidence as part of the lectura (lecture commentary) on Justinian's Institutes by Henricus de Piro (Henricus Brunonis de Piro, died 1473), printed in the fifteenth century as Super Institutiones. Five of the 22 faces were compared with a printed edition and carry the commentary on Book 1, titles 7-8 and 21-24, in the edition's wording and order.

The Fragmentarium record describes Antwerpen, Erfgoedbibliotheek Hendrik Conscience, Fragm. 101 a-v: eleven partial paper leaves, 166 x 224 mm, in a cursive hand, dated 1501-1600, catalogued as an "Unidentified text on Roman law", with no further description. The subject is as catalogued; the record names neither the author nor the text commented on. The catalogue date has not been reassessed.

  • Identification: identified, high. Six passages on five faces agree with the edition in the commentary's own connecting prose: the "continuatio" (the link from one title to the next), the division of the title into parts, the summary, and the notes on the Accursian gloss, each with the same legal citations in the same order. The matched layer is therefore the commentary itself, not a source it quotes. Nicolaus Everardi's Loci argumentorum legales treats the argument from contracts to last wills with the same doctrine but different wording and has nothing corresponding to the other passages, so it is not the text. Small differences of wording from the edition are listed below. Whether these leaves are an independent manuscript witness or were copied from a printed edition is not determined.
  • Transcription: source-assisted selected reading of the passages collated. The image-only first reading covers only the first four faces (fragm. a-b and c-d) and left much of the heavily abbreviated cursive unresolved; a research reader reread the quoted lines on the images with the edition at hand and expanded the abbreviations from it, so the reading of fragm. q-r, s-t and u-v has no independent first reading. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the manuscript images with page images of the Cologne edition of about 1481 for five of the 22 faces (fragm. a-b recto and verso, q-r recto, the last paragraph of s-t recto, u-v recto). The coordinator read fragm. u-v recto lines 1-10 on the image against the research reader's transcription of the print. The other 17 faces were looked at in overview only and were not collated; fragm. c-d is very pale and its verso appears blank. The relation of this copy to the printed editions is not determined, and only one of the three fifteenth-century editions was compared. No human review.

Differences. The manuscript has ultimis voluntatibus where the print has the singular in the first passage; sint for fuerint (Inst. 1.24); an added videre and Continuatio for Continuatur (Inst. 1.22); usque ad for usque in and, as read, an opening "Continuatio patet quia" that the print lacks at Inst. 1.23, where the manuscript words after "alieno" were not read securely. The manuscript omits the printed title headings, leaving blank space, and where it gives the lemma (the opening words of the Institutes passage) it writes it in the line. In the lower half of fragm. a-b verso some citations between "notatur" and "sic dominus" were not read. None of these differences shows by itself whether the leaves depend on a print.

The edition compared is Henricus de Piro, Super Institutiones [Cologne: Konrad Winters, about 1481], Gesamtkatalog der Wiegendrucke 12256, ISTC ip00651000, in the copy of the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Munich: fol. 15r, 15v, 16r, 37v, 38r, 38v, 39v, read as page images. The attribution to Henricus de Piro rests on the edition's printed title and on the GW and ISTC entries; the scholarly literature on the author was not consulted. Two further editions exist, [Cologne: Johann Koelhoff the Elder, about 1482] (GW 12257) and Louvain: Egidius van der Heerstraten, 1486/88 (GW 12258); neither was compared.

Order of the leaves and limits

The faces collated show that the lettering of the bundle does not follow the order of the text throughout: fragm. s-t carries the end of one title and the opening of Institutes 1.22, fragm. q-r the opening of 1.23, fragm. u-v the opening of 1.24, and fragm. a-b the end of 1.7 and title 1.8. Other faces open with further "Continuatio patet" paragraphs and were not placed. Narrow stubs at the left edge of some faces carry cut line ends of other leaves and were not read.

No claim is made about the date or origin of the leaves, about how much of Book 1 they contained, or about whether this copy has any value as a witness independent of the printed editions.
