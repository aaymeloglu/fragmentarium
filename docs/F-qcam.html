Frg. 89 (F-qcam)

Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Easter table of 28-year lines beginning in 1353 (solar-cycle rows xxij-xxviij), with its canon: Peter Philomena of Dacia, Canon supra kalendarium ('Quere inter numeros in superiori parte istius tabule'), adapted to the Easter table (example 1380, letter e); with a golden-number rota dated 1357 and a Tabula paschalis

The ten canon lines agree in wording and order with Peter of Dacia's canon on the page image of Clm 3512, fol. 289ra, adapted to this table: the example is 1380 with letter e (Clm: 1311, t), 'littera primacionis' probably becomes 'littera annualis/annalis', and Clm's sentence on second-tier years is absent. The grid is identified by its numbers: in line k, row r the year is 1352 + 28(k-1) + r and the letter codes that year's Julian Easter (a-v from 21 March, punctus right; a-q from 10 April, punctus left); about 90% of some 110 legible cells agree, and line 1, row 28 'e' is Easter 1380, the canon's example. An independent checker read canon lines 5-10 on the image before reopening Clm 3512 (not blind, having read the canon transcription) and read the grid blind before testing it against computed Easter dates. Pedersen's edition was seen only in snippets and no other copy of this adapted canon or table was found; no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-qcam: Peter Philomena of Dacia, Canon supra kalendarium, adapted to an Easter table of 28-year lines from 1353

This parchment leaf holds a computus table, that is, a table for reckoning the church calendar, with its instructions. The upper part is an Easter table: each column ("linea") covers 28 years and each cell gives one year's Easter date as a letter. The first line runs from 1353 to 1380. Below it, the instructions for using the table (the canon) are Peter Philomena of Dacia's Canon supra kalendarium, written for his table of new moons (1292), here adapted to the Easter table: its worked example is the year 1380 with the letter e, and where Peter's text speaks of the "littera primacionis" (the new-moon letter) the leaf probably reads "littera annualis" or "annalis". Beside the canon are two rotae (circular tables): one of golden numbers, epacts and claves, with a year line read as 1357, and a standard Tabula paschalis of the Easter full moons.

The Fragmentarium record describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 89: parchment, 358 x 235 mm, with a reconstruction summary by Claudia Sojer giving dimensions only, no catalogue date, title "x" and no linked description. It identifies no text. Its description fits the images: one leaf, landscape and cut on all sides, written on one face (canvas 0); the other face (canvas 1) has only show-through, a brown offset mark and a pencil shelfmark.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the canon and for the table it explains.
    • Canon: its wording and order agree with Peter of Dacia's canon in Munich, BSB, Clm 3512, fol. 289ra, through all ten lines, from the incipit "Quere inter numeros in superiori parte istius tabule positos unum numerum ex illis qui maxime similis est annis domini" to the worked example.
    • Adaptation: the leaf's example is "m ccc lxxx" with "e", where Clm 3512 has 1311 with "t" and Peter's printed versions have 1311, 1296 or 1422; the new-moon letter is replaced, probably, by "littera annualis/annalis" at both places; the leaf adds "sive in[tegr]aliter(?)" and "in eadem linea", and lacks Clm's sentence on the years of the second tier (1368, 1444 ...).
    • Table: it is identified by its numbers. The rows are years 22-28 of the 28-year solar cycle, each with its concurrent (the weekday number of 24 March), a leap-year counter and its dominical letter(s) (the letter of the year's Sundays). In line k, row r, the year is 1352 + 28(k-1) + r, and the letter is that year's Julian Easter date: 21 March to 9 April are lettered a-v with the punctus (dot) to the right, 10 to 25 April a-q with the punctus to the left. Line 1, row 28 reads "e·", which is 25 March, Easter 1380, the canon's "littera ultima linee prime scilicet e". About 90% of some 110 legible cells agree with the computed dates; the rest are faded or show letter confusions. Sixteen lines survive; a full table of 19 lines would run to 1884, but that is an inference. No other copy of this table was found.
    • Alternatives: the grid is not Peter's own Kalendarium of new moons, which in Clm 3512 (image 582) has rows of days with four columns of new-moon letters and hours, and uses its puncta for quarter-hours. No other work with this canon's incipit was found.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading and the source-assisted reading. A research reader reread on the native image all ten canon lines, the centre of the left rota, all nineteen sectors of the right rota, the left columns of the grid and rows 22-28 of the letter grid. An independent checker read canon lines 5-10, the whole grid and the year line of the left rota (see Verification). The outer rings of the left rota rest on the first reading. Line 6 ("sive in[..]liter") and the words "annualis" and "annalis" in lines 5 and 7 are probable, not secure; line 10 is largely unread. The second face was checked on the overview only. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader collated the canon with the page image of Clm 3512, fol. 289ra, and saw F. S. Pedersen's edition only as search snippets. An independent checker read canon lines 5-10 on the image (canvas 0, native box 1860,3150,1680,800) before reopening the source, finding "a(n)nual(is)" in line 5, "a(n)nal(is)" in line 7, two words after "precise" in line 6, and "e" in line 9; it had read the research reader's transcription of the canon first, so this reading is not blind. It then compared them with the page image of Clm 3512, image 581 and found agreement in "versus sinistram", "computando a sinistra", "in capite secunde linee" and "littera ultima linee prime", with the leaf's "annualis/annalis" standing at both places where Clm has "littera primacionis". The checker also read every legible cell of the grid (canvas 0, 1880,780,2420,1870 and 4200,780,2250,1870) before testing any hypothesis, then tested the cells against computed Julian Easter dates: row 22 agrees in all 15 lines, and line 1, row 28 gives Easter 1380. Gaps: the right rota was checked by computation only; Pedersen's edition was not seen in full; Leipzig, UB, Rep. II 170, another copy of the canon, was unavailable; no manuscript of the canon adapted to an Easter table was found for comparison. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

The differences are the adaptation described above (the example year and letter, "annualis/annalis" for "primacionis", the second-tier sentence absent) and smaller ones: the leaf lacks Clm's "primacionis" and "inter alios numeros" in line 1, as do Pedersen's base text and Delisle's printed incipit; it has "minor est" for "est", adds "in eadem linea", and has "Si autem" where Clm has "Et si" and Pedersen's text "Si autem". The leaf shares "versus sinistram" with Clm where Pedersen's printed versions read "versus dextram"; one shared reading makes no claim about the canon's transmission.

The red capitals stand in cells with ordinary letter values; what else they or the red quarter-circle marks signify is not established.

The right rota, headed in red "In hac tabula quilibet aureus nu(meru)s ostendit pascha iudeorum ... pascha xp(ist)ianorum", gives for each of the nineteen golden numbers the standard Dionysian date of the Easter full moon (for example i, Non. apr., 5 April; xvi, xij kl. apr., 21 March). The left rota's year line reads "Anno d(omi)ni · m° · ccc° · l·vij", 1357; "l·iiij", 1354, cannot be wholly excluded.

Dating, other items, and limits

The table's first line runs from 1353 to 1380, and the rota is dated 1357 (or 1354), so the leaf was written in or after 1353, probably between about 1357 and 1380. The choice of 1380 as the example suggests a copy made around that year; that is an inference. The leaf is incomplete: the canon runs on past the cut ("Inuenta itaque"), and the table is cut at the right after sixteen lines and at the top above row xxij.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the leaf beyond the table's own years, the length of the complete table, the meaning of the red capitals and corner marks, or that another manuscript carries the canon in this adapted form.
