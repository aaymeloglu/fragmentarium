The column is the opening of the Demonstratio de algorismo ascribed to Jordanus de Nemore, from 'Figure numerorum sunt' to 'Equidistantes numeri sunt inter quos continue', compared in full by a research reader with page images of Eneström's print (pp. 26-27); the coordinator read defs. 9-13 on the leaf against Eneström p. 27, word for word except 'fiunt' for 'fiant'. The Opus numerorum is set aside on Busard's statement, seen in an excerpt, that it omits his defs. 3, 5 and 8, which the leaf has. The two lines above are matched to the close of Euclid's De speculis from excerpts only; two blind readers read 'ascendit' there, against the edition's 'accenditur'. The pasted-down face and the marginal drawings are unexamined, and the copy's date is the catalogue's.

Readings and comparison details

F-t2ia: Jordanus de Nemore (ascribed), Demonstratio de algorismo, the opening definitions (Eneström defs. 1-13, breaking off in def. 13)

The prose column on this leaf is the beginning of the Demonstratio de algorismo, a demonstrative treatise on algorism (arithmetic with the Hindu-Arabic numerals) ascribed in its manuscripts to Jordanus de Nemore. It opens "[F]igure numerorum sunt .1.2.3.4.5.6.7.8.9. et est prima unitatis, secunda binarii et sic deinceps" and runs through the definitions of the work, numbered on the leaf, to the definition of equidistantes numeri, where it breaks off at "Equidistantes numeri sunt inter quos continue". In Eneström's printed text these are definitions 1 to 13; the leaf lacks his definition 4. The two lines above the column, in the same hand, are probably the close of Euclid's De speculis (the medieval Latin Catoptrica, on mirrors), but that match rests on quotations seen in search excerpts only.

The Fragmentarium record describes Cambridge, Magdalene College, Pepys Library, PL 2981, p. 9, no. 17, as one parchment leaf, 192 x 146 mm, in Gothic script, Latin, dated 1201-1300, with the title "Mathematical treatise on cubes and circles", and cites McKitterick and Whalley, Catalogue of the Pepys Library IV (1989), p. 6, which was not seen. The linked description is an empty form. The leaf is pasted as item 17 on page 9 of Samuel Pepys's calligraphical album, where Humfrey Wanley's caption calls it "a Fragment of Geometry ... shewing the then common Form of our Arithmetical Figures in present use"; that is a period description, not an identification. "Mathematical" fits. "Cubes and circles" seems to describe the two drawings in the left margin, not the text. The record names no author or work.

  • Identification: identified, high confidence, as the opening definitions of the Demonstratio de algorismo ascribed to Jordanus de Nemore. The basis is agreement in wording and in order through the whole column with Eneström's printed text (definitions 1-13, without his definition 4), and the leaf's own item numbers, which run without a gap to 14 and match the numbering of Busard's critical edition. The other algorism ascribed to Jordanus, the Opus numerorum (inc. "Communis et consuetus"), takes the Demonstratio's definitions into its long introduction; according to Busard, in an excerpt of his edition seen through Google Books, it leaves out his definitions 3, 5 and 8 (and 14-16). The leaf has all three, each with its own number, in an unbroken series that begins directly after the end of another text, with no introduction. This exclusion rests on Busard's statement; the introduction of the Opus numerorum was not collated. The Algorismus demonstratus and Sacrobosco's Algorismus vulgaris were not collated. Differences from Eneström's print: no "novem" after "sunt"; "Omnia loca primo" and "a primo" for his "prime differencie" and "a prima" (Busard's text, in an excerpt, reads as the leaf does); no definition of articulus (Eneström supplies it from a Dresden manuscript because his Berlin base manuscript lacks it); "dicuntur numeri eiusdem differencie" for "dicuntur eiusdem numeri differencie"; "fiunt" for "fiant"; the spelling "scifula vel cifula" for "sciffula vel scifula".
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading was made before any source was known. The source-assisted reading was made by a research reader who reread every line of the column on native-size crops against the first reading and then against Eneström's print. The two lines above the column were understood only after the source was found, and three words of them ("radi(us/is) calefactis circa") were read with the source in hand; they are marked uncertain. No line rests on the first reading alone. The labels of the marginal drawings were read in part ("columpna"). Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the whole column with page images of Eneström's print (pp. 26-27). Busard's critical edition (2000) was seen only in Google Books excerpts, and the De speculis close only in excerpts of Takahashi's edition and of manuscript descriptions. The coordinator read definitions 9-13, from "usque ad denarium. Omnis figura per se considerata aliq(uam) simplicium numerorum prime differencie representat" to "Equidistantes numeri sunt inter quos continue", on canvas 0, native box 1450,2750,3170,3780, against the page image of Eneström p. 27: the passage agrees word for word with definitions 9-13 (the leaf writes the numeral ".9." where the print has "IX"), except that the leaf has "fiunt" where the print has "fiant", which the coordinator confirmed on the leaf. Two fresh readers transcribed one neutral crop of this record (the two lines above the column) without any transcription or source. Both read "ascendit" where the research reading had "accenditur", so the reading is corrected to "ascendit(ur)"; both read "radius" (the same minims as "radiis"); "stupa posita accendetur" was given by one reader only, the other reading "supra po(s)ita ... attenderet", and those words are marked uncertain. Gaps: the pasted-down face is not imaged; Busard's edition and apparatus were not read in full, so the leaf is not placed among his witnesses; the De speculis close was not read on a page of any edition; the printed Pepys catalogue was not seen. No human palaeographic review is claimed.

The remaining definitions (Eneström 8 and 9, leaf items 9 and 10, ll. 14-17) agree with the print apart from spelling ("aliq(uam)" stands in the print as "aliquam"). The term differencia is the algorists' word for the place or rank of a digit.

Differences. The leaf lacks "novem" in the first sentence and the definition of articulus. It reads "Omnia loca primo ... a primo" where Eneström prints "prime differencie ... a prima", and "dicuntur numeri eiusdem differencie" where he prints "dicuntur eiusdem numeri differencie"; in both places the excerpts of Busard's text read as the leaf does. It has "fiunt" for "fiant" and spells the word for the zero sign "scifula vel cifula" (f and long s cannot be told apart here). Eneström counts the first three items as his definition 1 and numbers on from there; the leaf leaves its first item unnumbered, numbers the rest from 2 and, without articulus, reaches 14 at Equidistantes numeri, as Busard's numbering does. Eneström states that his text is not critical: he prints the Berlin manuscript and supplies definition 4 from Dresden. These differences show that the leaf's text is not Eneström's printed text; they do not place the leaf among the manuscripts of the work. In the two top lines the leaf has "ascendit(ur)" where the edition excerpt and the quoted explicits have "accenditur", and it has no "Explicit" line.

Attribution. The leaf names no author. The Berlin manuscript is headed "Demonstratio magistri Jordani de algorismo" (Eneström, p. 25), and Cambridge, Pepys 2329 has "Algorismus demonstratus per Jordanum ut creditur" (Busard, excerpt, p. 92). Eneström leaves the authorship open; Busard (excerpt, p. 104) concludes that the work should be accepted as Jordanus's. The author is therefore given as Jordanus de Nemore, ascribed.

The leaf, other items in the images, and what is not claimed

Only one face of the leaf can be seen; the other is pasted to the album page and was not imaged. Show-through of running prose in the same script is visible between the lines. If the hidden face continued the optical text, the visible face would be the later page of the leaf, but this was not established. The left margin holds two drawings that were not identified with any text: a table of numerals (each of nine rows one numeral repeated, from 9 down to 1, with a row 9 to 1 and nested arcs) and a rectangle with a median and two circles, labelled in part "columpna". Canvas 1 shows the whole album page, with items 16, 18 and 19, which are other records, and Wanley's captions.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the copy, which are the catalogue's (13th century) and were not examined; the place of the leaf's text among the manuscripts of the Demonstratio; a firm identification of the two top lines with Euclid's De speculis, which rests on excerpts; the meaning of the marginal drawings; and whether the printed Pepys catalogue or other scholarship already identifies this leaf.
