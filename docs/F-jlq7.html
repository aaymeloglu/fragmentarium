Readings and comparison details

F-jlq7: Copy sheet of three documents of the 1446-1451 settlement in Tyrol: Frederick III's pardon of the Tyrolean estates (Vienna, 2 April 1446), Albrecht VI's parallel pardon (1446), and Duke Sigmund's confirmation of the Tyrolean liberties (Innsbruck, 17 August 1451)

This parchment sheet from the archive of the Cistercian abbey of Stams is not a single deed but a copy sheet. Three documents follow one another in one fifteenth-century cursive hand, each opening with a large initial, with no seals, slits or signatures. All three belong to the settlement that ended King Frederick III's guardianship over his cousin Duke Sigmund of Austria and Tyrol. In the first, Frederick pardons the estates (Landschaft) of the county of Tyrol for everything they did against him on Sigmund's behalf, as agreed through Margrave Albrecht of Brandenburg and other princes. In the second, Frederick's brother Duke Albrecht VI issues a parallel pardon. In the third, Duke Sigmund, after the homage of his subjects, confirms all their graces, rights, letters and liberties and adds provisions on paid procurators, forestalling purchases (Fürkauf) by foreigners, and daughters who marry without consent.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 191, catalogued as "Deed" (Urkunde), German, second half of the 15th century, host volume STB IV/4.

  • Identification: identified, high. Each document's regest (issuer, beneficiary, legal action, place, date) is read on the image, with the elements the image does not give supplied from a named source and labelled, or stated as lost. Document 1 agrees in wording and order with the full text printed by Chmel from the royal register (Regesta chronologico-diplomatica Friderici III., Anhang no. 65) across eight passages; document 3 agrees phrase for phrase with the 1451 charter printed by Chmel (Materialien I/2, no. CLXXIV, pp. 354-355). Document 2 is recorded by Schober (no. 17) and Regesta Imperii XIV/4, which agree with the sheet on issuer and substance; no text of it was found for a wording comparison. Supplied from Chmel: in document 1 the feast (Judica), the year (1446) and the mediator's name; in document 3 the year (1451). Lost: document 2's issuer's name (under modern tape; Albrecht VI from his calling the Roman king "Bruder") and its whole dating clause (burn-hole). The outside records date document 2 to 2 April 1446 (Schober) or 3 April 1446 (RI XIV/4).

Every disagreement among them was re-read at native resolution and either confirmed, corrected or marked uncertain. Line ends are cut throughout by the trimmed right edge. The issuer's personal name lies under the tape at the join with the left strip; only the titles of duke of Austria, Styria, Carinthia and Carniola and count of Tyrol are read. He calls the Roman king "lieben herrn und Bruder", which identifies him as Albrecht VI, Frederick III's brother. The text ends in the burn-hole after the opening words of the sealing clause; place, day and year are lost. Schober (no. 17) and RI XIV/4 record Albrecht's pardon of the Tyrolean estates for having acted for Duke Sigmund, dated Vienna, 2 April 1446 (Schober) or 3 April 1446 (RI XIV/4). The surviving text agrees with those regests on issuer, beneficiary and action; neither prints a text, so no wording comparison was possible.

Dating

  • Document 1. Read: "Geben ze Wyenn an Sampstag vor dem Suntag", Vienna, Saturday before a Sunday whose name is lost at the edge. Supplied from Chmel's register text ("sabbato ante Judica anno etc. xlvj"): the feast and the year. Easter 1446 fell on 17 April (Julian), so Judica Sunday was 3 April and the Saturday before was 2 April 1446, the date Schober gives for the original.
  • Document 2. Lost in the burn-hole. The outside records give Vienna, 2 April 1446 (Schober) or 3 April 1446 (RI XIV/4); the sheet cannot decide between them.
  • Document 3. Read: "ze Insprugk an Eritag nach unnser lieben frawen tag Assumpcionis", Innsbruck, Tuesday after the Assumption (15 August); the year is cut off. Tuesday after the Assumption fell on 16 August 1446, 22 August 1447 and 17 August 1451 (Julian). Sigmund issued a plain confirmation of the old liberties in 1447 and the confirmation with added provisions in 1451; the sheet carries the 1451 provisions word for word, so the year 1451 is supplied from Chmel, giving 17 August 1451.

The modern pencil years on the face ("1446" under documents 1 and 2, "1451" under document 3) agree with these dates but were not used as evidence.

Physical notes and proposed catalogue corrections

  • The sheet carries three copied documents, not one deed. There are no seals, sealing slits or signatures, and the three texts run on in one hand. The copy is no earlier than August 1451. The catalogue's "2nd half 15th century" fits the hand.
  • The text face is the image labelled 01v; 01r is the back. The archive's own pencil note on the face reads "Stams, StA, Frg. 191v".
  • Other modern pencil notes on the face: "Landts Freyheiten Confirmationes mit Vertrag" and a reference to a Stams copy book, "(Hs 84) S XVII n. 73 [or 72], fol. 120, 122, 127", which was not consulted.
  • The back served as the outside of the host volume's cover. It has a sixteenth-century heading "Registru(m) expensaru(m) ...", the stamp "Stiftsarchiv Stams", an oval label "IV/4", and on the turn-in "[...] Wein Urbar beider Ambter [...] anno 1585 Jar", which dates the host Urbar to 1585.
  • The pencil note "Palimpsest" on the back is mistaken: the faint Gothic letters at mid-left of the back (about x 1300-3500, y 2900-3600 on 01r) are mirror-reversed ink offset from a facing page, not erased writing.
