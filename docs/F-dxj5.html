Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Office of the Holy Trinity, Matins, first nocturn: lessons from the creed Credimus sanctam trinitatem, R1 Benedicat nos deus, R2 Benedictus dominus deus Israel (piece 33-1)

Piece 33-1 of five gives the end of lesson 1 (Credimus sanctam trinitatem), R1 Benedicat nos deus with neumes, lesson 2 (Pater plenus deus in se), R2 Benedictus dominus deus Israel with V Replebitur maiestate, and a new lesson opening with a red Q at Qui totus ubique, continued on face B (accidens esse potest ... nature nichil addi). The order and wording were compared with the Sarum Breviary (ed. Procter-Wordsworth, cols. mxlviii-mxlix) on its page images, and an independent checker confirmed the verse and lesson division on the lowest lines of face A. The manuscript lacks unius essentie, a quo omnia... and non tamen duo lumina..., adds Credimus patrem and equaliter, and divides the lessons differently from Sarum. Its lessons also differ from those of the 1496 Breviarium Cisterciense, but no Salzburg-province breviary was reached, so the use is undetermined. Pieces 33-2 to 33-5 and most of face B are unidentified.

Readings and comparison details

F-dxj5: Office of the Holy Trinity, Matins, first nocturn (piece 33-1)

The largest of the five pieces in this record, labelled 33-1, is identified with high confidence. It comes from a noted Office book: chants are written with neumes, the staffless signs that record a melody. The piece holds part of Matins for the feast of the Holy Trinity, from the first nocturn (the first group of readings and chants):

  • the end of the first lesson, the creed Credimus sanctam trinitatem;
  • the first responsory, Benedicat nos deus deus noster;
  • the second lesson, Pater plenus deus in se;
  • the second responsory, Benedictus dominus deus Israel, with its verse Replebitur maiestate eius omnis terra;
  • the opening of a third lesson, Qui totus ubique est, which continues on the other side of the piece.

The other four pieces are not identified.

The Fragmentarium record describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 33: five parchment pieces, measuring 122 x 72 mm (taken from piece 33-1, 120/122 x 71/72 mm). The title is given as "x"; there is no description and no date. The record's reconstruction note says that 33-1 and 33-2 belong together, that 33-3 to 33-5 differ from them in script and content, that 33-3 and 33-4 belong together, and that all five come from the same host volume. The catalogue does not name any text.

  • Identification: identified, high, for piece 33-1 only.
    • The lessons are a Trinitarian creed. The order lesson, R1 (first responsory) Benedicat nos deus, lesson, R2 Benedictus dominus deus Israel, V (verse) Replebitur maiestate, lesson is that of Trinity Matins as printed in the Sarum Breviary. The wording of the lessons agrees with it across seven connected lines of lesson 1 and four lines of lesson 2.
    • The responsory pair is the common core of the Trinity office: of 135 chant inventories in the Cantus Index network that have either chant, 103 have both. It places the feast and the service, not the use (the liturgical custom of a particular church or order).
    • The manuscript's lesson text differs from Sarum's:
    • it lacks unius essentie, a quo omnia per quem omnia in quo omnia and non tamen duo lumina sed unum lumen;
    • it has Credimus patrem where Sarum has Patrem, and equaliter procedentem where Sarum has procedentem;
    • it reads potest for Sarum's poterit;
    • it divides the lessons differently: lesson 2 ends at incommutabilem, and a red initial Q opens a new lesson at Qui totus ubique est, words that Sarum keeps inside lesson 2.
    • The 1496 Breviarium Cisterciense, the Cistercian order's printed breviary, has the same two responsories but different first-nocturn lessons, a sermon beginning Cum ad intelligendam dignitatem summae trinitatis. So the manuscript does not follow that print's lesson series.
    • No Salzburg or Brixen breviary for summer could be reached. The use of the book is not determined.
    • A search of the CoMMA full-text corpus with phrases from the lessons found only other copies of the creed.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers all five pieces. The source-assisted reading covers piece 33-1: a research reader reread face A line by line from crops, and two lines of face B.
    • The neumed chant lines are faint. The R2 incipit rests on a few words ("dominus deus ... solus").
    • The rest of face B is abraded and partly overwritten by a later hand, and is not read.
    • Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared face A and the first two lines of face B with the Sarum Breviary on its page images. The same reader read the Cistercian print of 1496 on its page images.
    • An independent checker compared a passage the identification had not used: the lowest lines of face A (R2, the verse Replebitur maiestate eius omnis terra, and the red Q opening Qui totus ubique). The checker compared them with Sarum cols. mxlviii-mxlix on the page images and confirmed the verse and the lesson division.
    • The checker also tried the unread lines of face B. They were too damaged to test the identification either way.
    • The coordinator read piece 33-1 face A, lesson 2 opening "pater plenus deus in se ... a patre et filio" on the image; the comparison with the Sarum page images rests on the research reader and the independent checker.
    • Gaps: lessons 1 and 2 were compared with one printed breviary only. Most of face B is unread. Pieces 33-2 to 33-5 were not identified.

The differences are listed under Identification. The omissions do not show which form is earlier, or whether either depends on the other. Several of the manuscript's readings, Credimus patrem and aequaliter among them, occur in other printings of the creed outside the breviary. On face B the text runs in the same order as Sarum's lesson 2, with about two to three lines lost where the piece is cut. Below these two lines, a neumed chant line seems to read magnificentiam(?), a word not in Sarum's third responsory Quis Deus magnus. A large red H stands lower down. These lines are not identified, and they leave open whether the responsory series continues as in Sarum.

The breviary compared is Breviarium ad usum insignis ecclesiae Sarum, edited by F. Procter and C. Wordsworth, fasc. 1 (Cambridge 1882), cols. mxlviii-mxlix. It reprints the Paris edition of 1531 printed for Chevallon and Regnault, as the title page states. Page images: n581 and n582. The comparison of uses drew on the Breviarium Cisterciense of 1496 (archive.org, leaves n341 and n344). The creed itself has been printed under different names: as the Symbolum orthodoxae fidei of Pope Leo III in the works of Anastasius Bibliothecarius (PL 129), among the spurious works of Hilary, and as Alcuin's (PL 101). These attributions were seen in search snippets only. The identification here is liturgical, a feast, service and set of lessons, and makes no claim about the creed's author.

Other pieces and what is not claimed

  • 33-2, face A: in a similar hand to 33-1, with lesson and neumed chant lines, R and V signs and a red "Laudes". The words read ("ante dominum", "a patre paratus et qui", "non audiet", "in iudicio", "lapis") do not match the end of Trinity Matins or Lauds in Sarum or in the Cistercian print.
  • 33-2, face B: written in a different Gothic hand. Its readings include "[I]n illo t[empore] ... ego mitto uos sic(ut) o[ues]" (Matthew 10:16), with neumed chant lines. Not identified. Whether this face belongs with 33-1 is open.
  • 33-3, 33-4 and 33-5: not legible at word level on the published images.
  • What is not claimed: no date, origin or use for the book. No claim that it was or was not made for Stams. No claim about the relation of its lesson text to the printed breviaries or to other copies of the creed.
