Readings and comparison details

F-yxvr: Papias, Elementarium doctrinae rudimentum, letter F, Facio-Favilla

This trimmed leaf, preserved in two pieces, is from the Elementarium doctrinae rudimentum of Papias, the Latin dictionary also printed as Vocabularium. It carries part of letter F in three columns a side: the recto runs from Facio to Farina, the verso from Farmacon to Favilla. The entries are Papias's: short definitions in alphabetical order, with his grammatical notes ("Far ris solu(m) geminavit r causa differentie", "Farracior fructuosior") and longer articles.

The Fragmentarium record describes Gent, Universiteitsbibliotheek, HS.2582/286: one trimmed parchment leaf in two fragments, 349 x 294 mm, Northern Textualis, red and blue initials, dated 1251-1300, with the title "Latin glossary", the subtitle "lemmata 'Facio'-'Fauilla'" and the tag "Topic: Liber glossarum". The alphabetical range is right. The record names no author or work; the tag names the Liber glossarum, a glossary that is Papias's main source, not the text on the leaf. The record's date and script are the catalogue's statements and were not examined here.

  • Identification: identified, confidence high. Passages A and B agree in wording and in the order of entries with the Papias manuscript Paris, BnF, lat. 7609 (13th century) and, where it has the entries, with the Venice 1485 print; passage C (Famuli to Fana, across the join) was compared with the Venice 1485 print only; the rest of the leaf follows the same order by the first reading. The Liber glossarum was tested by searching its online edition by headword and comparing two shared entries: for Fantasia/Fantasma and for Fatuus it has the full Isidorian articles, where the leaf has the shorter Papias wording, and it has no headword Farratior. A headword search is weak evidence for absence; the positive evidence is the wording of the shared entries. Isidore's Etymologiae is not the text either: the Isidorian entries compared (Fantasma and Fatuus) stand in shorter form inside Papias's alphabetical sequence, with his grammatical notes around them. The long definition of fatum comes ultimately from Boethius; it is the entry order and the grammatical notes around it that carry the attribution to Papias. Later dictionaries that reuse Papias were not compared. Differences of wording from lat. 7609 and the print are listed under Textual evidence.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers all six images; the source-assisted reading gives the lines relied on. A research reader reread on the images: fragment A recto, column 3 (23 lines) and its first line; fragment A verso, column 3 (14 lines); fragment B recto, the lower half of column 2 (6 lines). The rereading corrects the first reading at one point that bears on the work: the word at the end of the Fatuus entry, which the first reading gave as "pap·" (as if a Papias source mark), is "pan." The rest of the leaf rests on the first reading, used only to follow the order of entries. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared passages A and B with page images of BnF lat. 7609 and of the 1485 print, and passage C with the print only; the Liber glossarum was checked by headword search of its online edition. The coordinator (Claude) read the top of column 3 of fragment A verso, from "[fort]unam vocabant. Fatum vero quod appositum est" to "alterum tamen pendet ex alto" (canvas 1, about native box 5350,350,7619,2800), on the image against BnF lat. 7609, Gallica f160: the leaf follows the manuscript in wording and order, with its "in motum" and "ex alto". Two fresh readers, given two neutral crops of this record without the transcription or the source, transcribed them. They supported "tantus" (not "taurus") after "hebraice" and "pan" (not "pap") at the end of the Fatuus entry; both read the blue initial of the lemma "Far" as H, so that letter is given here as uncertain. Gaps: two to three lines are lost at the tear in each column; recto column 1 is heavily abraded and was not reread; passage C was not compared with lat. 7609; no modern edition of letter F was consulted; no palaeographer has reviewed the reading or the match.

Differences from lat. 7609 (and the print, where it has the entry): "tantus" for "taurus"; "inanimate rei" for "rei inanimate"; "Far ris ... for aris" for "Far farris ... for faris"; "nondum" for "necdum" in Farrago; "quosdam" for "quidam"; "Fatuus" without "est"; "fatuus et fatuellus vocatur pan" run on with the Fatuus entry instead of set as its own entry; "Far. frumentum" run on after Farratus where lat. 7609 gives it its own initial; and spellings "cybus", "cybos", "sopno", "Farracior". Against the print only, the leaf lacks "servitium" after "obedientia". In passage B the leaf agrees with lat. 7609 against the print in "in motum" (print "in modum") and "ex alto" (print "ex altero"). The Fantasia/Fantasma group stands at this place in lat. 7609 and on the leaf but not in the print. By the first reading, checked for order only, verso column 1 runs Farmacon, Farmacopotas, Farmatia, Farmacopola, Farmacentria, Farmacos, Farmati, Faros, the order of lat. 7609 (f159, foot of column a into column b), where the print has only Farmaceutria, Farmacos, Farmati, Faros. These agreements and differences describe the texts compared; they are not evidence that the leaf descends from either.

Leaf order and limits

In each column the text runs from fragment A down into fragment B, then to the top of the next column on fragment A. Two joins show this: on the recto, column 2 of fragment B ends "Nam fania grece apparitio dicitur vel a fando." and column 3 of fragment A begins "[vel] a greco phone id est vox"; on the verso, column 2 of fragment B ends "Quod fortuitu venit nulla causa palam" (first reading) and column 3 of fragment A begins "[fort]unam vocabant". Comparison with lat. 7609 shows that two to three lines are lost at the tear in each column. The recto (Facio-Farina) precedes the verso (Farmacon-Favilla), as the catalogue has it.

Red marks in the margin stand beside many entries: "hy" (very often), "Ser", "sol", "pla", "p". Lat. 7609 has marks of the same kind ("hy", "ser", "bo"). The mark beside Favisse (canvas 3, 6200,4900,8200,5900) reads "pla", not a Papias mark. The marks were not compared with those of lat. 7609 entry by entry, and at "Fatum est dispositio" lat. 7609 has "bo" where the leaf's mark was not read closely. Their meaning is not interpreted here.

Not claimed: a date or origin for the copy beyond the catalogue's; any relation between this copy and lat. 7609, the 1485 print or other Papias manuscripts; whether Gent HS.3799.7 (F-ls2f), catalogued as Papias, is another leaf of the same manuscript, which was not compared.
