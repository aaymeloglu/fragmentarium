Readings and comparison details

F-wbkk: Breviary, Visitation office Accedunt laudes virginis, Matins, end of the first nocturn and the second nocturn

Three small parchment slips at Stams (a fourth is blank) come from a breviary or a book of Matins, the night Office. They carry part of Matins of the rhymed office for the Visitation of the Virgin, Accedunt laudes virginis, the office issued for the feast after its institution by Urban VI in 1389 and usually attributed to Cardinal Adam Easton. The text that survives runs:

  • the end of the first nocturn (one of the night Office's sections of psalms, lessons and responsories): the verse En felix salutatio of the responsory Elisabeth congratulans (Cantus 600773a);
  • the rubric In ii nocturno, and the antiphons Non fuit Christus oneri (203299), Transivit in itinere (204950) and Longam viam pertransiit (202933);
  • a prose lesson, Abiit igitur Maria cum festinatione ... sed abiit festinanter, then Tu autem and the responsory Maria parens filios plangens (601372).

The chants were matched to the Cantus Database and the whole run to a printed Roman breviary of about 1481. The use of the book is not identified.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 260: parchment, 82 x 65 mm, no date, with the title "x", no linked description, and two images labelled 01r04r and 01v04v. Its Reconstruction Summary carries "Bill's notes": "260: feast of the visitation (this is 04v): antiphon 204950: Transivit in itinere ... Antiphon: 202933: Longam viam pertransiit Maria montes circuit hilaris la[borando ...]". So the catalogue already named the feast and the two antiphons on one face, with their Cantus numbers and the readings "prospere", "temptavit" and "colloqui". The pencil label on that slip reads "260_1v", and slip 260_4 is blank on both faces, so "04v" is probably the end of the image name "01v04v". The catalogue did not name the office or identify the other faces: the responsory verse, nocturn rubric and first antiphon, the lesson and the responsory Maria parens. Its description fits the images: four slips of about 80 x 60 mm, photographed side by side, in gothic textualis with red rubrics and initials.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the texts and the office. The basis is every inked line read on the images and collated with the Breviarium Romanum of about 1481 (Visitation, Matins, nocturns I-II), with the chants also checked in the Cantus Database inventory of Utrecht, Universiteitsbibliotheek, Ms. 406.
    • Office: every chant read belongs to the Matins series of Accedunt laudes virginis, in the same order as in the print and in Utrecht 406. The other early Visitation office, Jan of Jenštejn's Exurgens autem Maria, does not contain them. Eight inventories in Cantus hold the three chants Maria parens, Transivit and Longam viam; that places the office, not a use.
    • Lesson text: the lesson is a widely copied Visitation legend, also found in late Legenda aurea prints and diocesan breviaries (in OCR text only). The leaf's lessons are divided and assigned differently from the c. 1481 print. In the print "Abiit igitur" opens lesson v, which ends at "cupiebat" before the responsory Rosa de spinis; "Noluit ibi" opens lesson vi; and Maria parens follows lesson iv, Cum igitur angelus. On the leaf "cupiebat · Noluit" runs on with only a red-touched capital, and the lesson ends at "festinanter" with Tu autem and Maria parens. If this lesson is the first of the second nocturn, as the space after the third antiphon suggests, Maria parens is the fourth responsory in both books and only the lesson text differs; that is not tested.
    • Other differences: the leaf seems to omit "collaudabat Ierusalem" (a skip from one "Ierusalem" to the next); "prospere" for the print's "propere" (as in Utrecht 406); "temptauit" where the print and Cantus have "evitavit", though "euitauit" is not excluded; "colloquiis?" for "colloquia"; "dabant(ur)" (as in Utrecht 406) for "dabatur"; "in sophie" (Utrecht "vi sophie"). The readings "prospere" and "temptavit" agree with the catalogue note, so they are not independent confirmations of it.
    • Use (not identified): a Cistercian use is consistent with the evidence but not shown. Analecta hymnica 24 lists among the sources of the office a fourteenth-century Cistercian breviary, Prague XIII A 5 b, to which the office was added in the fifteenth century; so at least one Cistercian book carried it. That breviary's lessons were not compared, and no other Cistercian breviary was found.
  • Transcription: source-assisted. The image-only first reading covers every face. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's reading of every inked line at native size, the faded slip 1r on a blue-channel crop; an independent checker reread 1r and 2v on the image. Slip 1r is the least secure face: its lines 1 and 3-4 remain mostly illegible. Also uncertain: "temp(t)auit", "colloquiis", the reading of 2v line 4, and a possible extra abbreviation "n'" before "in publico". Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared all six faces with the page image of the Breviarium Romanum c. 1481 (scan 114, both columns), the chants with the Utrecht 406 inventory (database records, not images), and the office with Analecta hymnica 24 on page images. An independent checker read slip 1r (canvas 0, native box 5350,1950,6650,2950, blue channel equalised) and slip 2v (canvas 1, 4150,1950,5350,2950) on the image and compared them with the Breviarium Romanum c. 1481 (page image, scan 114). The text is the same throughout: 1r is too fragmentary to compare word by word but does not conflict with the print, and shows "abrup-" at the end of line 5 ("inflammatione: per abrupta"); 2v lacks the print's "collaudabat hierusalem" and runs "cupiebat · Noluit" on without the print's responsory and "Lectio sexta"; "suavitate" for "suavitatem". The checker had seen the research transcription of both faces first. Gaps: no person has reviewed the reading. Prague XIII A 5 b and the Constance and Augsburg breviaries (seen in OCR snippets only) were not compared on images. The physical arrangement of the slips was not checked on the object.

Slip arrangement, other items, and what is not claimed

  • Text runs: one run goes 2r → 3v (one line: "matris digne · S[ed]", continuing 2r line 9) → 1v. The other goes 1r (the lesson opening, with a two-line initial A) → 2v → 3r (the lesson end, Tu autem and Maria parens, with a large red M whose tail runs into a blank lower margin). Slip 1's two faces fall in the wrong order relative to slip 2 for a single column, so the slips cannot come from one column of one leaf.
  • A two-column leaf is uncertain: a reconstruction with slip 1 behind the top of one column and slips 2 and 3 behind the other is strained. The single line on 3v lies at the height of 3r's first line, while 3r has three more written lines below it, so 3v and 3r cannot both be column feet. The text missing between 1r and 2v (about 140 characters in the print) would force columns of about 13 lines, an unusual page for a breviary. The slips may come from two leaves or two books, and the pencil r/v labels may not give the reading side consistently.
  • Show-through: lines 2-3 of 3v are show-through of 3r's "Maria parens fi- / lios plangens", not text on 3v.
  • Not claimed: the date (if this is the office issued after 1389, the book is later), the origin, the use of the book, that it was made or used at Stams, the authorship of the lessons, and the physical layout of the slips.
