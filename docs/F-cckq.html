Readings and comparison details

F-cckq: Tabula utriusque iuris attributed to Johannes de Erfordia, article "Appellatio"

The leaf is identified with high confidence as part of the article "Appellatio" in the Tabula utriusque iuris, an alphabetical repertory of canon and civil law attributed to the Franciscan Johannes de Erfordia. Three passages, two of them consecutive, agree with a fourteenth-century manuscript of the work at the same point of the article, on how an appeal is tacitly renounced. The leaf does not carry the same form of the text as that manuscript: wording, citations and the surrounding material differ.

The Fragmentarium record describes part of a parchment leaf, Leuven, Rijksarchief, 248 × 175 mm, dated 1250–1300, catalogued as an unidentified canon law text. The work is usually dated to about 1285 or later, which sits at or after the end of the catalogue's range; the date of the leaf has not been reassessed here.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the work and article; the recension is open. The matched passages are the compiler's own digest, with his cases, his chain of citations and his source sigla ("hoc b.", "Jo.", "In."), not a quotation from the Decretals or their gloss. Both witnesses have the same layout of bracketed distinctions and the same style of cross-reference ("Require in vocabulo electio" on the leaf). Alternatives opened on page images and excluded: Monaldus's Summa (Vatican, Pal. lat. 695) and the short "Appellatio" of Vatican, Pal. lat. 804, a manuscript catalogued under the same author and title whose article is nine lines of maxims. That manuscript shows that texts under this name differ widely, so the attribution should be read as the catalogues' label for this family of text.
  • Transcription: image-only first reading by an isolated reader and a source-assisted selected reading. The left column of the recto is torn and was read with low confidence throughout. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader read ff. 27r–31v of the Bremen manuscript from its page images and compared three passages; the coordinator compared the first passage, about fourteen lines, on the fragment image and the Bremen page image. The start of the article in the Bremen manuscript (f. 26) was not read, and the upper part of the fragment's recto was not located in it. The work has no edition. No human review.

The third passage precedes the first in the Bremen manuscript, as the recto precedes the verso on the leaf, so the order of the two faces agrees with the order of the article.

Differences between the two witnesses

The leaf is not a copy of the Bremen text. Where Bremen has "appellavit ab episcopo et postea redit ad episcopum" the leaf has "ad papam ... ad ordinarium", which changes the case put; it has videtur renunciasse for intelligitur renuntiare; it adds two citations ("e. ut debitus", and, as the research reader read it, "ex. de testi. cum venissent"); and it marks opinions with the sigla "Jo." and "In." where Bremen writes "Nota secundum In." and "hoc In. et Ht.". After the second passage the two diverge: Bremen continues "Utrum autem excommunicatus ...", the leaf "Aut appellatur super causis maioribus ...". The leaf's recto also carries material before the renunciation section (that the appellee need not prosecute; "Qui in electionibus appellat ... Require in vocabulo electio") that was not found at the corresponding place in Bremen. The literature is reported to distinguish an earlier and an enlarged redaction of the Tabula; which form either witness has was not established.

The work and its attribution

The witness is Bremen, Staats- und Universitätsbibliothek, msa 0137, fourteenth century, from the Bremen Franciscans, catalogued as Johannes de Erfordia, Tabula iuris utriusque. The attribution here is that catalogue's; no ascription was looked for in the manuscript and no study of the work was read. No claim is made about the leaf's date or origin beyond the note above.
