Stams, Stams (Stealth Published) · catalogued as x, not given

Lehenbrief: Abbot Paul and the convent of Stams grant the Walthauserin, Johann's son and daughter (Johannes and Petronella) and other Stiftsfreie at Tartsch fields and a meadow at Mals in Erblehen; [16]33 (century supplied), place, day and month lost

The regest is read on the image: issuer Abbot Paul and the convent of Stams, beneficiaries, grant in Erb- und Baurecht, plots at Mals near St Martin's church, rent of rye and barley to the Zinskasten at Mals and 1 lb Berner; the endorsement names Johannes, Petronella and Wilhalm. Only 'Unnd Im Dreiunddreissigisten Jare' survives of the date; the century is supplied from the abbot list (Paul I Käsinger 1540-1542, Paul II Gay 1631-1638; Pelagius Bauer to 1540 excludes 1533), giving 1633. The host volume, Hs. 119, is titled 'Mitt[er]kellner Ambts urbar / Ad Annum 1578', which conflicts with 1633; the conflict is unexplained, and a later rebinding is possible but unchecked. An independent research reader reread lines 7-11, the intitulatio and the dating lines on the image (reading holds; adds 'Jacoben Mitterhouers erben' and 'Mittag' as a bound direction) and checked the abbot dates against Austria-Forum and the Oberdeutsche Personendatenbank. The 'n.' placeholder leaves open original, engrossment or copy; place, day and month are lost at the cut edge; no outside record found; no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-gtpi: Abbot Paul and the convent of Stams grant the Walthauserin, Johann's son and daughter and other Stiftsfreie at Tartsch acres and a meadow at Mals as a hereditary tenancy (Erblehen), [16]33

This parchment sheet in the archive of the Cistercian abbey of Stams carries one tenancy grant (Lehenbrief). Paul, abbot, and the convent of Stams, as lords of the land ("Recht grunt unnd Lehenherren"), grant in perpetual hereditary tenure ("Recht bestannden Erblehen", "erb unnd Paurecht") to the Walthauserin and to the son and daughter left by Johann [...], for themselves and for other Stiftsfreie (free tenants of the abbey), now at Tartsch in the Vinschgau, a set of plots at and around Mals: three Mattmal (a measure of land) of field "hindter Jann[...]", one and a half Mattmal of field below St Martin's church, land "under Prauradunt, auch Mallser pirt gelegen", and a meadow of two old Mannmad (another land measure) "in Stelnig(?)". The holding had partly fallen back to the abbey. The tenants must offer the Baurecht to the abbey first if they sell, with a month for the abbey to buy, may not sell to powerful clergy or similar people, must keep the land in good repair, and owe a yearly rent of grain, one and a half Mut of rye and one Mut of barley by the Clausen measure, to the abbey's Zinskasten at Mals, with mention of its grain store at Mais outside the town of Meran and a money rent of one pound Berner. The abbot announces his own seal. Only "Unnd Im Dreiunddreissigisten Jare" survives of the date; the century is supplied below. The sheet was later cut down to serve as the cover of a Stams register.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 198, one parchment item, 368 x 283 mm (measured 335/368 x 246/283 mm), with the title "x" and no date, type or description. Modern additions on the sheet are a pencil "Hs. 119", "IV/13" in ink, the stamp "Stiftsarchiv Stams" and the pencil shelfmarks "Frg. 198r/v". The record has nothing to confirm or correct beyond the missing regest.

  • Identification: identified, high. The regest is read on the image: issuer (Abbot Paul and the convent of Stams), beneficiaries (the Walthauserin and Johann's son and daughter, with other Stiftsfreie at Tartsch; the endorsement names the children Johannes and Petronella and also names Wilhalm), legal action (grant in Erblehen, Erb- und Baurecht), object (fields and a meadow at Mals) and rent. Lost in the cut right edge: the place of issue, the day and month, the saint's day of the rent, and the thousands and hundreds of the year, so no dating clause can be converted. The year is given as [16]33: "Dreiunddreissigisten" (thirty-third) is read, the century is supplied, not read. Stams had only two abbots named Paul, Paul I Käsinger (1540-1542) and Paul II Gay (1631-1638); Pelagius Bauer was abbot 1525-1540, which excludes 1533, so a thirty-third year under an Abbot Paul is 1633. This conflicts with the host volume: the back of the sheet carries the register title "Des Gottshaus Stambs Mitt[er?]kellner Ambts urbar / Ad Annum 1578" and the pencil "Hs. 119", so the host volume is titled for 1578, and a cover made in 1578 could not be a deed of 1633. The conflict is unexplained. A later rebinding of the register is possible but has not been checked, and the research reader's impression that the hand looks earlier than 1633 has not been tested palaeographically. The "n." in "Wir paulus Abbt unnd n. das Conuennt", a blank for a name, and the absence of any surviving plica, seal slit or tag leave open whether the sheet is a sealed original, an unsealed engrossment or draft, or a chancery copy. No independent record of the act was found.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers the whole text face, the dorsal title and the endorsement. Its reader had no proposed identification or reading of the document in context; it reports that its background context mentioned a "Stams pilot" and that the sheet itself shows the shelfmark "Frg. 198r/198v", the stamp "Stiftsarchiv Stams" and the dorsal title "Ad Annum 1578", none of which names the act. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's (Claude) reread of the whole text face at native resolution in eight half-width crops, with the endorsement and the dorsal title. It corrects the first reading in "zu Mayss ausser der Stat" (not "Maysshauser der Stat"), "Tartsch" (not "Tautsch") and "Mitt[er?]kellner". An independent research reader (Claude) read lines 7-11, the intitulatio and the sealing and dating lines afresh on the image. Personal and place names are read letter by letter and some remain uncertain. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: there is no outside text of the act to compare. An independent research reader read on the native image of canvas 1 the land description, lines 7-11 (boxes 1050,1580-2800,2100 and 2700,1580-4420,2100), the intitulatio (box 1000,1020-4420,1240) and the sealing and dating lines (box 1050,4700-4420,4900), as a fresh reading set against this report, and found that the reading holds. That reader added the first name in "Jacoben Mitterhouers erben" and read "Mittag" (south) before "Claus Ph[...]" as a direction in the bounds, not part of a name. The same reader checked the abbot dates on which the supplied century rests against two secondary online lists, Austria-Forum, Stift Stams (Thomas Lugga 1616-1631, Paul II Gay 1631-1638, Bernhard Gemelich 1638-1660) and the Oberdeutsche Personendatenbank, Stams (Pelagius Bauer, abbot to 1540; "Paul Gei", abbot, died 1638). Paul I Käsinger's dates rest on a Google Books snippet of Siebmacher only. The host volume, Hs. 119, was not examined, and monasterium.net, the Stiftsarchiv Stams inventory and the Südtiroler Landesarchiv were not searched. No human review.

Physical notes

  • One document of about 38 lines in a German chancery hand, with flourished line fillers. The right edge is cut away, losing a word or more in each line, and the lower right corner is cut off; the lower third of the text face is blank.
  • No plica, seal slits or seal tag survive in the part that remains.
  • The sheet was cut down to wrap a register. The back carries its title in large script, box 4784,1129-6440,2087 on canvas 0 (image): "Des Gottshaus / Stambs Mitt[er?]kellner / Ambts urbar / Ad Annum 1578", the last digit written heavily. The first reader noted that the 8 may be written over another digit; this was not examined further.
  • A three-line endorsement in a sixteenth- or seventeenth-century hand, box 2440,560-2820,2050 on canvas 0, read rotated (image): "W[...] ... Johannes und Petronella / der Walth[...] und Wilhalmen Stifftsrecht auch / [...] Stifftsfreyen Lehenbrief". It names the grantees independently of the text.

Unresolved

  • Whether the sheet is a sealed original, an engrossment or draft, or a copy.
