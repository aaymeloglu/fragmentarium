Frg. 206 (F-xvty)

Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Missal, Proprium de tempore, Paschaltide: Dominica II post Pascha (from Victimae paschali) with its Wednesday and Friday masses and the Dominica III introit (leaf 1); Wednesday and Friday after Dominica IV and Dominica V post Pascha (leaf 2)

Two non-consecutive missal leaves, read on the images. Leaf 1 runs from the end of Victimae paschali through Jn 10:11-16, the Offertory, Secret, Communion and Postcommunion of Dominica II, then the Wednesday mass (1 Pt 1:18; Lc 24:1) and Friday mass (Rom 15:14 Certus sum; Mt 9:14), to the Dominica III introit Iubilate; the catalogue's 01r is the leaf's verso. Leaf 2 has Jn 17:11-26 (Wednesday), 1 Thess 5:1 and Jn 13:33 (Friday) and the Dominica V introit, collect and lesson. A second research reader compared 24 lines of 01v with the Missale Pataviense 1498, fol. 85v, on page images, and they agree word for word apart from spelling and a doubled Communion alleluia; the fragment also reads 'venerunt mulieres' in Lc 24:1, as Passau does, and Passau fol. 86r has the same Friday mass. The two Friday lessons agree with a group of 17 uses in Usuarium's tables, from Salzburg, Passau and Chur to Lübeck, Schwerin and Trondheim; this places the book in that group by inference, with no single use claimed, and Brixen differs only in Usuarium's one Brixen witness (the 1493 Brixen print was not checked). No human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-xvty: Missal, Proprium de tempore, Dominica II to Dominica V post Pascha with the Wednesday and Friday masses

Two parchment leaves at Stams, used in a binding, come from a missal: the book holding the chants, prayers and readings of the Mass. Both leaves are from the temporale, the cycle of Sundays and seasons, in the weeks after Easter. Neither leaf is complete, and the two are not consecutive.

  • Leaf 1 begins in the mass of the second Sunday after Easter (Dominica II post Pascha):
    • the end of the sequence Victimae paschali ("...[gr]atia quid vidisti in via ... tu nobis victor rex miserere")
    • the Gospel John 10:11-16 Ego sum pastor bonus
    • the Offertory Deus deus meus ad te de luce vigilo
    • the Secret Benedictionem nobis domine
    • the Communion Ego sum pastor bonus
    • the Postcommunion Praesta nobis omnipotens deus ut vivificationis tuae gratiam consequentes
    • the Wednesday mass that follows, with the lesson 1 Peter 1:18-25 Scientes quod non corruptibilibus and the Gospel Luke 24:1-12 Una sabbati valde diluculo
    • the Friday mass, with the lesson Romans 15:14-16 Certus sum and the Gospel Matthew 9:14-16 Quare nos et pharisaei
    • the opening of the next Sunday's introit, Iubilate deo omnis terra (Dominica III). An introit is the entrance chant of the Mass.
  • Leaf 2 belongs to the week after the fourth Sunday:
    • the Wednesday Gospel John 17:11-26, beginning "Respiciens ihesus in celum discipulis suis dixit. Pater sancte serva eos"
    • the Friday mass, with the lesson 1 Thessalonians 5:1-5 De temporibus et momentis and the Gospel John 13:33-36 Filioli adhuc modicum
    • the fifth Sunday after Easter (Dominica V), with the introit Vocem iocunditatis, the collect Deus a quo cuncta bona procedunt and the lesson James 1:22-27 Estote factores verbi

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 206: parchment, two fragment items, 374 x 227 mm (measured on Frg. 206_1 as 300/374 x 182/227 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), online since 18 September 2021. It has the title "x", no linked description, and four images (206_01r, 01v, 02r, 02v). The record does not name the text. Its physical description fits the images. Modern pencil notes on the leaves read "Dom. II p. Pascha" and "Dom V. Post Pascha". Both are correct.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the content: the masses and their parts named above.
    • Basis. Each part is read on the images, and the red rubrics on the leaves name the days ("D(omi)nica v", "f(er)ia iiij", "f(er)ia vi. Ad thessalonicenses", "S(ecundu)m Johannem").
    • The readings. The lessons and Gospels agree with the Vulgate in the lines read. The chants and prayers are the standard formularies of these Sundays.
    • Wednesday and Friday masses. The weekday masses use readings that are recorded for these days in the Usuarium database of missals.
    • Bible or lectionary? The leaves were taken at first for a Bible or lectionary. They are neither, because chants and prayers stand between the readings.
    • Wording against the Vulgate and Passau. Luke 24:1 reads "venerunt mulieres ad monumentum", a liturgical addition not in the Vulgate. The Communion Ego sum pastor bonus ends "all(elui)a all(elui)a", where the Missale Pataviense of 1498 has one alleluia. The John 17 Gospel opens with a liturgical incipit ("Respiciens ihesus in celum ...") in place of the Vulgate's "Haec locutus est Iesus et sublevatis oculis".
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers leaf 1 only. The source-assisted reading covers the following:
    • A research reader reread 02v col. a in full, the top of 02r col. a and the top and foot of 02r col. b, the foot of 01r col. a and the middle of 01r col. b.
    • A second research reader reread 24 lines of 01v (John 10:12 to the Wednesday rubric) and the top of 01r col. a.
    • The rest of 01v rests on the first reading.
    • The middle of 02r col. b lies under a dark stain and is partly supplied from the Vulgate, in brackets.
    • Much of 01r is faded and is not read.

Accuracy has not been measured.

  • Verification: a research reader compared the readings with the Vulgate and the weekday assignments with Usuarium's tables. A second research reader made an independent comparison. They wrote down 24 lines of 01v before opening the source, then compared them with page images of the Missale Pataviense (Augsburg: Erhard Ratdolt, 2 December 1498), fol. 85v. They also checked that the Friday mass on fol. 86r has the fragment's sequence: Romans 15 Certus sum, Matthew 9 Quare nos et pharisei, then Iubilate. That reader also confirmed the leaf order. The coordinator read canvas 3, col. a (1450,1150 onwards, the end of John 13:33-36, the rubric Dominica V and the introit Vocem iocunditatis with Psalm 65) on the image and compared it with the Vulgate and the introit text; it agrees.

Gaps in coverage:

  • Only one printed missal was compared on page images.
  • The leaf 2 Gospels were collated with the Vulgate only.
  • The red folio numeral on 01v ("l" followed by x's and minims, perhaps "lxxxiiij") is not read with certainty.

No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

The differences from the 1498 Passau print are as follows:

  • spellings (Benediccionem, oblacio)
  • the rubric forms ("Co." for "Cōmu."; the Postcommunion is headed "Complenda" in both)
  • the doubled alleluia at the end of the Communion
  • the Luke 24:1 addition "venerunt mulieres" (mulieres is not in the Vulgate), which the fragment shares with Passau 1498

The Passau missal was read on page images in the Munich Digital Library (fol. 85v, fol. 86r). Its title and use are those of the printed edition (GW M24621). It is a witness of the shared formulary, not of this book's use.

Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

  • Leaf 1 order. The image labelled 01r is textually the verso of leaf 1, and 01v is the recto.
    • The last line of 01v col. b reads "Sec(undu)m lucam. In illo t(empore). Una sabbati valde".
    • 01r col. a opens "diluculo venerunt mulieres ad monumentum".
    • A red folio numeral stands in the upper margin of 01v.
  • Leaf 2 order. The order 02r, then 02v is right: 02r ends with John 13:33 and 02v opens with John 13:33-36.
  • Gap between the leaves. Dominica III and IV are missing between the two leaves.
  • Use: an inference, not an attribution. Both Friday lessons agree with the minority reading of a group of 17 uses in Usuarium's tables (listed below), among them Salzburg, Passau, Augsburg and Chur but also Lübeck, Schwerin, Kamień Pomorski and Trondheim. For Passau this is confirmed on the page image of the 1498 print. For the other dioceses it rests only on Usuarium's tables. Two shared lessons place the book in that group, not in any one diocese of it.
  • Brixen. Brixen (Bressanone), Stams's diocese, has other Friday lessons in Usuarium's single Brixen witness. The printed Missale Brixinense of 1493 (GW M24292) could not be opened and was not compared, so Brixen is not excluded.
  • Cistercian use. Cistercian sources in Usuarium show no proper readings for these weekdays. This makes a Cistercian missal unlikely, but no Cistercian missal was compared on images.
  • Not claimed: the date, origin or use of the leaves, the binding they came from, and any relation to the Passau print beyond the shared text.
