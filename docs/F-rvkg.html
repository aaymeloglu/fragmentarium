Frg. 131 (F-rvkg)

Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, 1000 – 1099 CE

Noted breviary, probably of secular cursus (Office with neumes; probably the central bifolium of a gathering): 1 January (Matins end with Bede, Hom. evang. I.11 = PL 94 col. 54; Lauds, Hours, Vespers), ferial chapters before the Epiphany, Epiphany first Vespers, Compline and Matins nocturn I (Isa 55:1-2+55:12-56:2, 60:8-15, 61:10)

A research reader reread all eight columns on the native images. The 1 January lessons agree word for word with Bede, Hom. I.11, in PL 94 col. 54B-D (page image), over two passages in different columns. The Isaiah lessons were compared with the Vulgate in archive.org OCR text; a second research reader independently collated Isa 60:8-15 on a page image of the Clementine Vulgate (Hetzenauer 1914) and found 60:12 'quod servierit tibi peribit' without 'non' and 60:15 'qui te pertransiret' (word order), both confirmed on the image. The chants were compared with Cantus Index standard texts and inventories. The three-antiphon first nocturn (19 Cantus inventories go straight to the versicle, 52 add a fourth antiphon) suggests a secular cursus; the rare None antiphon Circumcisus est dominus (Cantus 001807, 11 south German, Swiss, Austrian and Bohemian entries, ten at Matins) has its one secular witness, an Augsburg Cathedral antiphoner-breviary of c. 1580, at None as here. Use, region and the central-bifolium leaf order are inferences; the Hereford Breviary comparison was OCR only, no person has reviewed the reading, and the catalogue date 1000-1099 was not tested.

Readings and comparison details

F-rvkg: Noted breviary (probably secular) for 1 January and the Epiphany, with Matins lessons from Bede, Homiliae evangelii I.11, and Isaiah 55, 60 and 61

This parchment bifolium at Stams comes from a breviary, a book holding the whole Office (lessons, chapters, prayers and chants), with its chants written under staffless German-type neumes. The two leaves appear to be the central pair of a gathering, so the text runs continuously across all four pages:

  • 1 January: the end of Matins, with a lesson from Bede's homily for the Circumcision ("Scire etenim debet vestra fraternitas ... Iustus autem ex fide vivit") between the responsory cues Sancta et immaculata and Verbum caro; then Lauds, Prime, Terce, Sext, None and Vespers.
  • The weekdays before the Epiphany: chapters (short Bible readings) and collects.
  • 5 January: first Vespers and Compline of the Epiphany.
  • 6 January: Matins of the Epiphany, with the invitatory, the first nocturn (one of the night Office's sections of psalms, lessons and responsories) and its three lessons from Isaiah.

Each item was matched to its source: the Bede passage to the Patrologia Latina, the Bible texts to the Vulgate, and the chants to Cantus Index. The first nocturn has three antiphons before its versicle, the pattern of the secular Office of cathedral and collegiate clergy rather than the monastic one, but the use of the book is inferred, not established.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 131: parchment, one fragment item, 517 x 276 mm (measured 509/517 x 256/276 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), dated 1000-1099, with the title "x", no linked description, and two images labelled 01r and 01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images, a single bifolium with two columns of 30 surviving lines on each page.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the offices and their texts. The basis is all eight columns read on the images.
    • Bede: the 1 January lessons agree word for word, over two passages in different columns, with Bede's homily for the Circumcision, Hom. I.11 (PL 94, Hom. X, col. 54B-D). The last lesson stops at "Iustus autem ex fide vivit", short of the end of the homily.
    • Isaiah: the Epiphany lessons are Isaiah 55:1-2 joined to 55:12-56:2a, then 60:8-15, then 61:10 onward. The join in the first lesson is inferred from the space available (see the closing section).
    • Chants: every chant is a standard text in Cantus Index: Lauds antiphons O admirabile commercium, Quando natus es, Rubum quem viderat, Germinavit (end), Ecce Maria genuit; Benedictus antiphon Mirabile mysterium; None antiphon Circumcisus est dominus octavo die secundum legem Moysi (Cantus 001807); Magnificat antiphon Qui de terra est (004464); Compline antiphon Salutis nostrae auctorem (004685); invitatory Christus apparuit nobis; antiphons Afferte domino, Fluminis impetus, Psallite deo nostro; responsories Hodie in Iordane with verse Caeli aperti sunt (006849) and Omnes de Saba with verse Reges Tharsis (007314). The hymns are given as cues: A solis ortus, and at Compline "Ymnus Ihc refulsit", that is, Iesus refulsit omnium.
    • Collects: Deus qui salutis aeternae is in the Gregorian sacramentary, Omnipotens sempiterne Deus qui unigenito tuo novam creaturam in the Gelasian. The others are standard Christmas-season prayers, not located by number.
    • Differences: from the Bede text, only spelling ("cetu" for "coetu"). From the Vulgate: "laudes" (55:12), "appendit" for "adprehendit" (56:2, a slip), "Me etenim" (60:9), "glorificabit" (60:9), "reges eorum" (60:11), "quod servierit tibi peribit" without "non" (60:12, a slip that reverses the sense), "et abies" (60:13), and "qui te pertransiret" for "qui per te transiret" (60:15, a change of word order). In chant 001807 the fragment reads "octauo die" for "octava die".
    • Use (inference): after Psallite deo nostro the fragment goes straight to the versicle and the first lesson. In Cantus Index, 19 inventories do the same and 52 continue with a fourth antiphon, Omnis terra, as monastic books do. A secular cursus is therefore likely. Two features point to southern Germany, Austria or Switzerland: the invitatory on the feast itself, which the Roman curial use omits, and the rare None antiphon Circumcisus est dominus. That antiphon has only 11 entries in Cantus Index, from southern Germany, Switzerland, Austria and Bohemia (three Prague books); ten are at Matins, mostly in monastic books. The eleventh is the one secular witness, an antiphoner-breviary made for Augsburg Cathedral about 1580 (Copenhagen, Det Kongelige Bibliotek, Gl. Kgl. S. 3449 8°, vol. II, f. 130r), and it places the antiphon at None, as this fragment does. These are leads, not a determination of use or place.
  • Transcription: source-assisted. The image-only first reading covers all eight columns. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's reread of every column on the native images, in bands of about fifteen lines, with close crops of the Compline rubric and the following collect. It corrects the first reading's "(et)" to "uel" (twice) in the Bede passage; reads the abraded gutter lines as "Qui enim nunc per evangelium suum terribiliter ac salubriter clamat", "masculus cuius praeputii" and "peribit de ce[tu]"; reads "Unus Deus qui iustificavit" for "Unur[..]s qui iu-", "Y(mnus) Ihc refulsit" for "I hic refulsit", and "Corda nostra. Ut in mat(utinis)" for "Corda nostra. Vt imma[..]". The first letters of the gutter-side lines of canvas 0, column 3, lines 1-2 and 22-30, remain uncertain, and the neumes were not transcribed. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the Bede lessons with PL 94 on page images. The Isaiah lessons were compared with the Vulgate in archive.org OCR text only; Isaiah 60:8-15 was then collated independently by a second research reader on a page image of the Clementine Vulgate (Hetzenauer, 1914), which found the omission of "non" in 60:12, confirmed on the image. The chants were compared with Cantus Index standard texts and placed with its inventories (database records, not manuscript images). The secular Epiphany pattern was compared with the Hereford Breviary in archive.org OCR text only. Passage checked independently: Isaiah 60:8-15, canvas 0, column 1 lower part and column 2 lines 1-18, native boxes 600,2380,1950,3850, 1960,120,3360,1250 and 1960,1150,3360,2300, read on the image and compared with Hetzenauer pp. 719-720 (page image, page image). Apart from the differences listed above it agrees word for word; "die ac nocte" (60:11) is also the Clementine reading. The report had no transcription of the Isaiah lessons, but the reader knew its OCR-based variant list before reading. Gaps: no person has reviewed the reading. No secular breviary or antiphoner of the region was compared to test the use. The later cursive note between columns 3 and 4 of canvas 1 was not read.

Bede: the passages agree in wording and sequence. "ce[tu]" (canvas 0, column 3, line 30) is the fragment's spelling of PL "coetu". The word after "paradyso" on line 28 is too worn to confirm PL "hominibus". The fragment's lesson ends at PL col. 55A, "ex fide vivit". Isaiah: the differences are listed under Identification. The black "H" after the first and second lessons probably cues the formula "Haec dicit Dominus Deus: Convertimini ad me et salvi eritis", with which the Hereford Breviary ends its Epiphany Isaiah lessons; that is an interpretation. The chapter at Compline, "Populus qui sedebat in tenebris", is Isaiah 9:2; the chapter given for the hour of None is close to, but not identical with, Philippians 3:3.

Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

  • Leaf order: leaf X is canvas 0 right page (recto) and canvas 1 left page (verso). Leaf Y is canvas 1 right page (recto) and canvas 0 left page (verso). The text runs X recto, X verso, Y recto, Y verso.
  • Lines lost at the trimmed edges: where text runs across a column break it can be measured. Isaiah 60:10 between canvas 0 columns 1 and 2, and the Bede passage between canvas 0 columns 3 and 4, each lose about 90-100 characters, three or four lines.
  • The central pair of the gathering: the end of the 1 January Magnificat antiphon and the beginning of the chapter Romans 3:19 fit those few lost lines between X verso and Y recto. So the leaves are probably consecutive, the central bifolium of a gathering.
  • The first Epiphany lesson: only the same few lines lie between Isaiah 55:2 (end of canvas 1, column 4) and 55:12 (top of canvas 0, column 1). So the first lesson must leave out 55:3-11. That is an inference from measurement.
  • The 1 January Matins lessons: the Bede homily before the surviving lesson is about 1.7 times its length, which fits a three-lesson nocturn better than a four-lesson monastic one. Lesson lengths vary, so this is weak evidence.
  • Other marks: modern pencil "Stams, STA, Frg. 131r" and "131v"; later pen marks in the outer margins; a later cursive note, partly numerals, between columns 3 and 4 of canvas 1 (not read). The O of Omnes sitientes contains a drawn face.
  • Not claimed: the date (the catalogue's 1000-1099 was not tested), the origin, the use of the book (the secular cursus and the regional connection are inferences), that it was made or used at Stams, and any relation to the manuscripts that share the antiphon Circumcisus est dominus.
