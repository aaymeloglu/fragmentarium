(Pseudo-)Hugh of St Victor, Expositio in regulam sancti Augustini, Middle Dutch translation with the lemmata of the Rule, chapters 6-7 (PL 176, cols 899-904)

On all eight pages the Dutch follows the Latin of the Expositio sentence by sentence, lemma and commentary alike: the growth of sin and the psalm verse (col. 900), secret and public correction with 'pater des cloesters' for 'patri monasterii' (col. 903), and Gehazi and Naaman (col. 904). A research reader compared the images with PL 176, as page images for cols 901-904 and machine-read text for cols 899-900, rereading three of the eight pages; the coordinator read fol. [3]r on the image. The wording agrees closely with the Rooklooster translation edited by I. M. Kramp from Antwerp, Plantin-Moretus M 380, in five passages seen only as Google Books search snippets, with Flemish spelling and single-word differences ('ghenouchten' for 'gewoenten', 'pater' for 'uader', 'wreedelike rechten' for 'wreetliker daer ouer ordeelen'); the translation version is left open. The text sequence shows leaves [2] and [5] are consecutive and both precede [3] and [4], which does not fit the catalogue's numbering within one quire; this was not checked on the paper.

Readings and comparison details

F-n8nv: (Pseudo-)Hugh of St Victor, Expositio in regulam sancti Augustini in Middle Dutch, chapters 6-7 (PL 176, cols 899-904)

The two bifolia (folded sheets of two leaves each) are identified with high confidence as part of a Middle Dutch translation of the Expositio in regulam sancti Augustini, the twelfth-century commentary on the Rule of St Augustine that is printed among the works of Hugh of St Victor and is now generally treated as not his. The eight pages give the Rule's own sentences as lemmata (the passages of a base text quoted for comment), each followed by the commentary, from chapters 6 and 7 of the Latin as printed in the Patrologia Latina: the eye fixed on a woman and the loss of chastity, God as the witness whom nothing escapes, the secret and then public correction of a brother, and the brother who secretly receives letters or gifts, with the example of Gehazi and Naaman. The catalogue calls it an unidentified spiritual treatise.

The Fragmentarium record describes Leuven, KU Leuven Bibliotheken Bijzondere Collecties, Ms. 1317: two paper bifolia, "part of the same quire", leaf 135 x 85 mm, Middle Dutch, hybrid script with pen and red decoration, restored, dated 1451-1500, under the title "Unidentified spritiual treatise" and with no description. The four images are labelled fol. [5]v-[2]r, [2v]-[5]r, [4]v-[3]r and [3]v-[4]r. The physical description fits; the record names no work. The catalogue date has not been reassessed.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the work and the place in it: a Middle Dutch translation of the Expositio, chapters 6 (De habitu exteriori et interiori) and 7 (De correctione fratrum) in the numbering of PL 176, cols 899-904. On all eight pages the Dutch follows the Latin sentence by sentence, lemma and commentary alike; four passages are set out below. No other work was a serious candidate: a search of the Corpus Corporum Latin library for the Gehazi passage returned this commentary alone. Which Middle Dutch translation this is has not been settled. Several exist. In five passages the wording agrees closely with the one that I. M. Kramp edits as the "Rooklooster translation", from Antwerp, Museum Plantin-Moretus, M 380, and it differs from the wording of two other Dutch translations in the same volume; but that edition was seen only as short search snippets, not read on the page, and the bifolia differ from the Antwerp text in spelling (western, Flemish forms) and in single words (listed below). The version is therefore left open.
  • Transcription: image-only first reading and source-assisted reading. The first reading covers all eight pages. A research reader reread three pages on the images with the Latin at hand, [2]v, [4]v and [3]r, and found the first reading sound there, with small corrections. The passages quoted from [5]v, [3]v and [4]r rest on the first reading. Lines under repair tissue at the foot of the first two images are largely unread. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the manuscript images with the Latin of PL 176, read as page images for cols 901-904 and as uncorrected machine-read text for cols 899-900, and with Google Books search snippets of Kramp's edition for the Dutch. The coordinating model read the upper sixteen lines of fol. [3]r on the manuscript image (canvas 2, right-hand page, native box 2300,250,4600,2500: "Als men eene mesdaet heymelic doet ... pater des cloesters ... met ghetughen es verwonnen zo salmene wreedelike rechten") and compared them with the research reader's quotation of PL 176, col. 903A, "Culpa cum occulte committitur ... patri monasterii debet indicari ... ut convictus districtius iudicetur", not with the page image. Gaps: five of the eight pages were not reread; no page of any edition or manuscript of a Dutch translation was read; the Latin was compared in the Patrologia print only. No human review.

Differences. Against the Latin the Dutch is a close translation, with the freedoms of one: "immanitatem sceleris" becomes "anxtelike grootheit der sonden", "passione leprae" becomes "metter plaghen der besiectheit", and "patri monasterii" becomes "den pater des cloesters". Against the Antwerp text in the snippets, sentence structure, word order and the characteristic renderings ("recht als met eenen bande der noet", "anxtelike grootheit der sonden", "harde dwaes", "hemelsche scoonheit") agree, and the following words differ: "ghenouchten" for "gewoenten" on [2]v, where the Latin has "consuetudinem", so that the bifolia are in error or follow a variant; "want die psalme seit" for "wat seit de psalmiste", where the Antwerp wording is the closer to the Latin question; "gherechtighe" for "rechtuaerdeghe"; "hauwen ende slaen" for "te houwen"; "helich man" for "heilech mensche"; "pater" for "uader"; "wreedelike rechten" for "wreetliker daer ouer ordeelen"; "verzuimt" for "uerroukeloset"; "pinicheit" (uncertain) for "sericheit"; and the bifolia lack "in den lichame". The same sentences in two other Dutch translations printed in the same volume read differently in the snippets (p. 392, "si maket onmenschelicheit der boesheit"; p. 398, "swaerre correctien weerdich"; pp. 476-482, a later version). Whether the bifolia belong to the Rooklooster translation, to a reworking of it, or to one of its known copies cannot be decided from snippets.

Leaf order, other items in the images, and limits

Each page has one column of 18 to 21 lines. Long passages are underlined in red. The underlining marks the Rule's lemmata in places but also runs over commentary (most of fol. [3]r), so it does not reliably separate the two layers.

The sequence of the text gives the order of the pages. Fol. [2]r, [2]v, [5]r and [5]v follow one another without a break, from the lemma ending "fugit ipsa castitas de moribus" to "Abominatio est Domino defigens oculum" (cols 899-900). Fol. [3]r and [3]v are continuous (col. 903A-B), and so are [4]r and [4]v (col. 904A-C). Leaves [2] and [5] are thus consecutive, which makes that sheet the innermost of its quire, and both leaves of the other sheet come after it: about five leaves' worth of Latin separates [5]v from [3]r, and about two leaves' worth separates [3]v from [4]r. On this evidence the second sheet did not lie between the leaves of the first, as the numbering [2] to [5] in one quire implies; it would more naturally come from the following quire, with one sheet lost from inside it. This is an observation from the text sequence, with gap sizes estimated from the length of the Latin; the paper and sewing were not examined.

The lower lines of both pages on canvases 0 and 1 are under repair tissue. Canvas 3 carries a heavy offset (ink transferred from another leaf) of a larger script at right angles to the text, with a grey-blue offset along the fold; it was not read. Pencil numbers at the head of canvases 1 and 2 are modern.

No claim is made about the date or origin of the bifolia, about which Middle Dutch translation or which branch of it they carry, about whether they are already recorded among its witnesses, or about the book from which they come.
