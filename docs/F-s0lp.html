Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Lehenbrief of King Ferdinand I as count of Tyrol: Hans Veit von Annenberg, for himself and as Lehenträger for Anthoni, Sigmund and Jacob, after the death of [...]ristan von Annenberg, receives the Tyrolean fiefs (castle at Latsch with orchard, sawmill, tithes, meadows, farms), with confirmation of a grace of King Henry; [1527-1564] by title, dating clause lost

The regest is read on the image: the royal style ('...Hungern, Behaim, Dalmatien, Croatien etc. Künig, Infant', which in a grant of Tyrolean fiefs fits only Ferdinand I), Hans Veit von Annenberg as petitioner for himself and as Lehenträger for Anthoni, Sigmund and Jacob, the grant of the fiefs of the County of Tyrol after the death of '[...]ristan von Annenberg', the confirmation of King Henry's grace, and the fief list beginning with the castle at Latsch. The left part of every line and the dating clause are cut away; the date is [1527-1564] from the title alone. A fresh reader given neutral crops confirmed the style, the names, 'Künig Hainrichs', 'Burg zu L(a/o)tsch' and the back title '15 Stambs 64', and with the coordinator read '[...]ristan' in line 4, correcting a proposed '[Ch]ristof' and withdrawing a narrower date. The surname's minims allow Ammenberg; Annenberg rests on the fief group, which matches the 1510 Annenberg Lehensrevers quoted in the Hofnamen im Landgericht Schlanders (snippets) in items, order and wording. No record of this act was found; sealing cannot be checked; no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-s0lp: King Ferdinand I grants Hans Veit von Annenberg, for himself and for Anthoni, Sigmund and Jacob, the Tyrolean fiefs of the Annenberg family with the castle at Latsch, [1527-1564]

This parchment sheet in the archive of the Cistercian abbey of Stams carries the upper right part of one fief letter (Lehenbrief, a lord's grant of land held in fief) issued in the name of King Ferdinand I as lord of the County of Tyrol. Of his title only the end survives, in a display line: "[... H]ungern, Behaim, Dalmatien, Croatien etc. Künig, Infant [...]". The text states that "Unnser Getrewer lieber Hanns Veit von Annenberg" has come to the king after the death ("nach absterben seines [...]") of "[...]ristan von Annenberg". He comes for himself and "anstat als Lehentrager" (as the vassal who holds the fief on behalf of others) of the "[...]gelassner Süne, mit Namen Anthonj, Sigmundt, unnd Jacob" (sons left behind). He seeks the "Vessten, Stuckh, unnd Guetter" (castles, pieces and goods) held in fief of the County of Tyrol ("Graveschafft Tyrol Lehenschafft"), and the king grants them "als Lehens unnd Lanndtsrecht ist". The king also confirms ("Wir haben Inen also bestättet") a grace of "[...] Künig Hainrichs, Löblicher gedechtnus" (King Henry of Bohemia, count of Tyrol, who died in 1335), as if it were written in the letter "[von wort] zu Wort". The grace concerns the succession of sons and the power to dispose of the fiefs ("Das Sy die vorgenanten Lehen geschaffen mugen"). The list of fiefs follows, each item introduced by "Item":

  • "[die] Burg zu L(a/o)tsch [Latsch in the Vinschgau] unnd ainen Paumgarten daselbst" (the castle and an orchard);
  • a sawmill ("Wassersag");
  • tithes;
  • meadows;
  • farms and a homestead that had belonged to "her Perchtolds ... von Meran";
  • lands "auf Latsarer(?) Veld".

The text breaks off at the cut lower edge, so the dating clause is lost. The sheet was later reused as the wrapper of a Stams register.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 49, one parchment item, 258 x 217 mm (measured 256/258 x 214/217 mm), with the title "x" and no date, type or description. The image labelled 01r is the text face. Modern additions on the back are a blue "IV/8", a pencil "Hs. 12[5/6]", the stamp "Stiftsarchiv Stams" and the pencil shelfmarks "Stams, STA, Fag. 49r/v". The record has nothing to confirm or correct beyond the missing regest.

  • Identification: identified, high. The regest is read on the image: the royal issuer (by title), the beneficiaries (Hans Veit von Annenberg for himself and as Lehenträger for Anthoni, Sigmund and Jacob), the legal action (grant of the Tyrolean fiefs after a death, with confirmation of King Henry's grace) and the main object (the castle at Latsch with an orchard, a sawmill, tithes, meadows and farms). The issuer's name is lost with the left part of the sheet. The title, kingdoms of Hungary, Bohemia, Dalmatia and Croatia with "Infant" (prince of Spain), in a grant of fiefs of the County of Tyrol, fits only Ferdinand I. He was king of Hungary and Bohemia from 1526/27 and ruled Tyrol until his death on 25 July 1564. The same words stand after "Croatien" in his royal and his imperial style, so the date is given as [1527-1564], from the title alone. The dating clause, with year, day and place, is lost below the cut edge. Whose death occasioned the grant is not established: only "[...]ristan" survives of the name, perhaps Cristan. The surname's minims (repeated short vertical strokes) allow Annenberg or Ammenberg. Annenberg rests on the fief group: the castle at Latsch was the Annenbergs' fief, and a 1510 investiture of Siegmund von Annenberg quoted in Die Hofnamen im Landgericht Schlanders (1927) lists "die burg zu Letsch u. ainen paumgarten daselbs" and "ain wassersag u. ainen zehennden aus ainem ..." in the same order and wording as lines 18-19. It rests also on printed snippets naming a Hans Veit von Annenberg of Dornsberg in the 1560s. Many place names in the list are uncertain. Display script, cadels and a folded blank margin show a formal chancery engrossment. The seal place is lost, so whether the sheet is the sealed original cannot be checked.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers the display line, all 28 text lines and the back. Its reader had no proposed identification or reading in context. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's (Claude) reread of the whole text face at native resolution in four bands of about ten lines, with detail crops of lines 1-5, and of the back title. It agrees with the first reading in the names, the royal style and the fief list. It reads "Graveschafft" and "nicht Töchtern(?)" and leaves the start of line 4 open, now read as "[...]ristan" (see Verification). Place names in lines 21-28 ("Pleonad(?)", "Durspurgl(?)", "Tozan(?)", "Gutrann(?)") and words on the stained fold in lines 8-9 remain uncertain. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: there is no outside text of this act to compare. A fresh reader who had not seen the reading was given neutral crops of the words the regest depends on and asked what each says. These were the royal style, the names in lines 1-2, the name before "von A...enberg" in line 4, "Künig Hainrichs" in line 15, the castle in line 18, the last lines and the back title. The fresh reader confirmed the style (Hungern, Beheim, Dalmatien, Croatien, Künig, Infant), "Hanns Veit von A(nn/mm)enberg", "Anthonj, Sigmundt, vnnd Jacob(?)", "Künig Hainrichs Löblicher gedechtnus", "Burg zu L(a/o)tsch ... Paumgarten daselbst" and "15 Stambs 64". In line 4 the fresh reader and the coordinator both read "[...]ristan". That corrects a proposed "[Ch]ristof" and withdraws a narrower dating built on a Christoph von Annenberg genealogy. The research reader compared the fief list with the 1510 Annenberg investiture in Die Hofnamen im Landgericht Schlanders (Google Books snippets only, no page images). Passage checked independently: lines 18-19, canvas 0 (Stams_STA_Frg_049_01r), box 2280,3330-4600,3470, image, against the Hofnamen snippet; no outside page image. Not checked: the Tiroler Landesarchiv fief registers (Lehenbücher), where Ferdinand's Tyrolean investitures are recorded, and the host volume. No human review.

The two lists agree in items, order and wording as far as the sheet goes. The 1510 text is a different act and corroborates only the fief group and the reading Letsch. No edition, regest or register entry of Ferdinand's grant itself was found. The searches covered monasterium.net (the Stams abbey charters, and Annenberg charters in other archives such as Marienberg, where grants of King Henry to Heinrich von Annenberg of 1318 and 1327 are registered), the Austrian State Archives' catalogue (archivinformationssystem.at) and Google Books.

Physical notes and limits

  • One document: a display line in large chancery Fraktur with an enlarged K in "Künig" and large oval cadels in the top margin, then 28 lines of German chancery cursive with flourished capitals and line fillers. The left part of the sheet is cut away, and the cut lower edge removes everything after line 28. A blank right margin strip is folded under, and a stained horizontal fold crosses lines 8-9.
  • The back carries the wrapper title of the host register written along the spine, box 2750,750-3250,2150 on canvas 1, read rotated (image): "15 Stambs 64", probably the year 1564 written around the name. A small paper slip tied with thread reads "15. 6[...] / Coll[...]". The host volume (pencil Hs. 12[5/6]) was not checked.
  • Not claimed: the year, day and place; the identity of "[...]ristan" and his relation to Hans Veit and the three sons; the reading Annenberg rather than Ammenberg beyond the evidence stated; the location of most places in the fief list; and whether the sheet is the sealed original or a spoiled or superseded engrossment.
