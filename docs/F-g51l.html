Liturgical calendar of the use of Auxerre: a name column for 17-23 July (Theodosius ep., Arnulf ep., Arsenius conf., Marinus conf., Praxedis, Mary Magdalene, Apollinaris) and two strips of golden numbers and Sunday letters (probably mid-August) with 'festum duplex'

Three strips of a graded liturgical calendar. The name strip has Theodosius bishop, Arnulf bishop, Arsenius confessor, Marinus confessor, Praxedis, Mary Magdalene and Apollinaris for 17-23 July, the same seven feasts on the same days as the calendar of the Auxerre missal Paris, BnF, Latin 17312 and the printed Auxerre missal dated 1487 (the 1532 Auxerre missal agrees except for 'Martini' on 20 July), compared on page images; the printed calendars of Troyes, Nevers, Autun, Paris and Orléans lack Theodosius on 17 July and Marinus on 20 July. The other two strips have golden numbers, Sunday letters and 'festum duplex' without names, probably for mid-August (23-25 March is not excluded). Calendars of Sens, Langres, Chartres and the religious orders were not compared; the church within the diocese and the date are not established.

Readings and comparison details

F-g51l: Liturgical calendar of the use of Auxerre, 17-23 July and probably mid-August (three strips)

Three small parchment strips at Stams come from a liturgical calendar: the list of feasts, day by day, that stands at the front of a missal, breviary or psalter. One strip keeps the names for 17-23 July. They are the feasts of the diocese of Auxerre in Burgundy: all seven stand on the same days, in the same order, in the calendar of an Auxerre missal, Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, Latin 17312, and in the calendar of a printed Auxerre missal (a later edition differs on one day). The other two strips keep only calendar numerals and letters, probably for mid-August, and the grade "festum duplex" (a feast of double rank).

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 96: parchment, three fragment items, 56 x 11 mm, with the title "x", no date and no linked description. The record does not name the text. The strips are taped to a paper folder that carries a modern German typescript analysing them; it points to Auxerre for two of the saints by way of Grotefend's handbook, without comparing an Auxerre calendar.

  • Identification: identified, high, as a calendar with the sanctoral (the selection of saints) of the diocese of Auxerre.
    • Basis. Strip a reads, under a red "Augusti", Theodosius bishop, Arnulf bishop, Arsenius confessor, Marinus confessor, Praxedis, Mary Magdalene, Apollinaris. The calendar of BnF Latin 17312 has these seven on 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22 and 23 July. So do the printed Missale Autissiodorense (Paris, Jean Du Pré, dated 1487 in the library record) and, except for 20 July, where it prints "Martini", the Auxerre missal of 1532.
    • What is distinctive. Theodosius was bishop of Auxerre. Theodosius on 17 July and Marinus on 20 July are absent from the printed calendars of the neighbouring uses read on the page (Troyes 1489, Nevers 1490, Autun 1493, Paris 1490, Orléans 1519). Grotefend's index enters both as Auxerre saints, but from the Acta Sanctorum, not from an Auxerre calendar, so his index is not a survey of their spread. Arnulf (18 July) and Arsenius (19 July) are found in many dioceses and do not narrow the use.
    • Alternatives. The calendar of Utrecht, the nearest in this run among those Grotefend prints, has Theodosia virgin on 17 July, Frederick on 18 July and Sabinus on 20 July. The list of masses in a missal of Sens (BnF Latin 10502), the metropolitan church of Auxerre, has no mass for any of the four saints of 17-20 July. Calendars of Sens, Langres and Chartres and of religious orders were not compared.
    • Differences. The strip has "Arsenij conf." where Latin 17312 has "Arsenii monachi" (the 1532 print has "Arsenii confessoris"), and "Arnulphi" for "Arnulfi" (as in both prints). The two prints add Victor before Praxedis on 21 July; the strip, like Latin 17312, has Praxedis alone. Latin 17312 gives no grades; "festum duplex" is a grade term of the printed Auxerre calendars, though not peculiar to them (Nevers uses it too).
    • Limits. Three Auxerre calendars were compared (one manuscript, two prints whose dates were not checked against their colophons). No calendar of a religious house in the diocese was compared. A book made elsewhere from an Auxerre model is not excluded.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers the three strips. The source-assisted reading gives strip a as reread by a research reader at native size, with the fifth name completed from the source. The numerals of strip b are faint and are not read with confidence. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader read strip a on the image and compared it with the July page of BnF Latin 17312 on the library's page image, a digitisation from microfilm. The same reader read the entries for the four saints in the text of Grotefend's index and the July section of the list of masses in BnF Latin 10502 on its page image. An independent checker reread strip a, confirmed the seven days on Latin 17312, and read the July calendars of the printed Auxerre missals (1487, 1532) and of Troyes, Nevers, Autun, Paris and Orléans on archive.org page images. The red "Augusti" is the end of the Roman date of 16 July, the seventeenth day before the kalends of August. The right ends of the lines are cut away, so it cannot be seen whether "et confessoris" followed the two bishops as in the missal. Apart from that the differences are the title of Arsenius and the spelling of Arnulf.

BnF Latin 17312 was read on the library's images: the July page (region with 15-25 July) and the August page. The library catalogues the book as "Missale ad usum ecclesiae Autissidorensis" and describes its calendar, which also serves as an obituary, as that of the parish church of Gy-l'Evêque in the diocese of Auxerre. The attribution to Auxerre is the library's.

Grotefend's entries were read in the text of H. Grotefend, Zeitrechnung des deutschen Mittelalters und der Neuzeit, vol. 2 (Hannover 1892-1898), index of saints, under Theodosii, Marini, Arnulfi and Arsenii, and his Utrecht calendar in the same volume. The Sens list of masses is in BnF Latin 10502, catalogued as "Missel de Sens".

The other strips and what is not claimed

  • Strip c. Red golden numbers i, (blank), ix beside the Sunday letters e, f, g. In the usual calendar this run falls on 17-19 August or 23-25 March; the grading of the 1487 Auxerre calendar (17 August a double, then days of the lowest rank) favours August, but the alignment of strips b and c is not established. Golden numbers mark the days of the new moon in each year of the 19-year cycle; Sunday letters run a to g through the year.
  • Strip b. A ruled grid with four faint red numerals, a large red A and "festum duplex." in red to the left of the grid. The numerals are not read with confidence, so the days of this strip and the feast to which "festum duplex" belongs are open.
  • One calendar. That the three strips come from one leaf is likely from the script, ruling and mounting. It is not proven.
  • Not claimed: the date of the calendar; the kind of book it stood in; whether it served the cathedral, a parish or a religious house of the diocese; any relation to BnF Latin 17312 beyond the shared feasts; how the strips came to Stams.
