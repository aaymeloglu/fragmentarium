Guillelmus Durantis, Repertorium aureum iuris canonici (Breviarium aureum), book I: end of De electione, De translatione, De auctoritate et usu pallii, De renunciatione, De supplenda negligentia prelatorum, opening of De temporibus ordinationum (X 1.6-1.11); Paris 1513 edition, fol. xvi verso - xix recto

A research reader reread the recto's right column and the top of the verso's left column on the native images and compared five passages with page images of the Paris 1513 edition (Munich, BSB): they agree in wording and order, among them 'An soli pape liceat compellere aliquem dimittere', 'vide in nostro speculo' and the opening 'An restituendus sit qui dolo aduersarii renunciat'. The five red title headings and the initial under each agree with the edition, including its combined heading 'De temporibus ordinationum et de etate et qualitate'. The leaf is badly abraded: the recto's left column, the lower third of both sides and the body of the verso's right column were not compared, and most words were read with the edition open. Only one printed edition was used; an abridged form of the work is not excluded.

Readings and comparison details

F-0w8s: Guillelmus Durantis, Repertorium aureum iuris canonici, book I, De electione to De temporibus ordinationum

This parchment leaf in Stams is from a copy of the Repertorium aureum iuris canonici (also called Breviarium aureum) of Guillelmus Durantis (d. 1296), known as the Speculator after his Speculum iudiciale. The work goes through the titles of the Decretals of Gregory IX in order and, under each title, lists legal questions ("An ...", "Quid iuris ...", "Qualiter ...") with a reference to where each is treated, often adding "secundum papam" for the commentary of Innocent IV. The leaf has the end of the title De electione and then De translatione, De auctoritate et usu pallii, De renunciatione, De supplenda negligentia prelatorum and the opening of De temporibus ordinationum, which in the Paris edition of 1513 are fol. xvi verso to xix recto.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 71: parchment, one fragment item, 287 x 195 mm (measured as 282/287 x 184/195 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description, with two images labelled 071_01r and 071_01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: one leaf written in two columns, photographed on both sides. Modern pencil notes on the parchment read "Aus VIII/25" and "anno 1616".

  • Identification: identified, high. The basis is agreement in wording and order with the 1513 edition in five passages, three on the recto and two on the verso, together with the leaf's five red title headings and the large initial under each, which agree with the edition's sequence of titles and with the first word of each title (Quid, Quare, An, Quando, Quando). The heading of the last title on the leaf adds "de etate et qualitate" to "De temporibus ordinationum", as the edition's combined heading does and the Decretal title itself does not. The leaf also has the cross-reference "vide in nostro speculo", the compiler's reference to his own Speculum. Works that treat the same titles in continuous prose are excluded by the form of the text: the Summa of Goffredus de Trano opens these titles with other words (read in New Haven, Beinecke Library, Marston MS 36, fol. 10r-11r) and is several times longer, and the Decretal text itself and its ordinary gloss could not fit five titles on one leaf. The Summa aurea of Hostiensis, the Summa of Henricus de Merseburg and the works of Bernard of Parma were not read; they are set aside because the leaf agrees with Durantis's question list and not because their texts were compared. An abridgement or reworking of the Repertorium is not excluded by the passages read. No difference of wording from the edition was seen in the lines that could be read, apart from spelling; most of the leaf is too abraded to say more.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading of 31 October 2026 and the source-assisted reading. A research reader reread on the native images, with contrast enhancement, the recto's right column from the top to the heading De translatione and the first thirty lines or so of the verso's left column, and the five headings. A few phrases were read before the source was known ("possit eligi ... eligatur", "valet electio pauco[rum]", "consentire [..] dissentire electioni", "secundum omnes", "An soli pape", "significasti", "que non possunt"); the other words in the table below were read with the edition open beside the image, which on so abraded a leaf shows that the edition fits the traces and is not an independent reading. The recto's left column, the lower third of both sides and the body of the verso's right column were not reread and rest on the first reading, which is itself very incomplete there. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the passages listed below with page images of the 1513 edition in the Munich copy (scans 36-41). The coordinator read canvas 0, region 3330,2240,1230,380 (recto col. b, "An soli pape ... compellere aliquem dimittere", "An habens plures", "Qualiter formetur", "in nostro speculo", "De hac materia tractatur lx ... lxi"), on the image and against the 1513 page image https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb10188391?page=37 , and found the same sequence. A separate reader given only four crops of recto col. b and verso col. a, with no source, marked most words as uncertain and found nothing usable in two of the crops; in the other two it read "consentire ... elec-", "An soli", "significat-", "que non possunt" and the rubric "De Renu(n)ciacione R.", which agree with the print. Gaps: only one printed edition was used and no manuscript of the work; roughly half the leaf was not compared; the edition's colophon was not read.

Differences: in the lines read, only spelling (-cio for -tio, "sollempnitatibus" for "solennitatibus"). In passage 2 the small word between "consentire" and "dissentire" is an abbreviation that could be "nec", as printed, or "vel". The edition's heading "De translatione episcoporum vel clericorum" cannot be checked beyond its first two words. Because so much of the leaf is illegible, the absence of observed variants says little about how close this copy is to the printed text.

Edition used: Breviarium aureum, Paris, sold by Galliot du Pre, dated 1513 in the catalogue data for the digitised copy, in the copy of the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Munich, bsb10188391, scans 36 to 41 (37, 38, 40, 41), read on the page images. The attribution rests on the edition's title page, which names "domini Guillermi duranti speculatoris" (partly under overprinted red), on the library's catalogue entry under Guilelmus Durantis, and on the text's own references to "nostro speculo iudiciali".

Leaf order and what is not claimed

The text runs from the recto's left column to the verso's right column, so the catalogue's 01r and 01v are in the right order. Brown-ink notes in the left margin of the verso were not read.

No date or place of origin is claimed beyond the general appearance of a 14th-century hand, which was not studied. The pencilled "anno 1616" is a modern note about the volume the leaf came from and does not date the leaf. Nothing is claimed about the relation of this copy to the printed text or to other manuscripts, or about whether it is the full work or an abridged form of it. Whether the fragment is already recorded in the literature on Durantis was not checked.
