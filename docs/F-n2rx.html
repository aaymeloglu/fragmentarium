About half the text rests on the first reading or is unread.

Readings and comparison details

F-n2rx: Jacobus de Voragine, Legenda aurea, chapters from Dominic to Martin (August to November), four bifolia

The four bifolia of Stams Frg. 219 are from a copy of the Legenda aurea of Jacobus de Voragine, a collection of saints' lives and feasts arranged by the church year. They carry parts of the chapters for August to November: Dominic, Donatus, Cyriacus and Laurence on the fourth bifolium; Cosmas and Damian and Michael on the first; Francis and Dionysius on the second; and Eustace, All Saints and Martin on the third. Each chapter opens, as in the Legenda aurea, with an explanation of the saint's name followed by the rubric and the legend. In the chapter numbering of Graesse's edition (2nd ed., 1850) these are caps. 113, 115-117, 143, 145, 149, 153, 161, 162 and 166.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 219: parchment, four fragment items, 442 x 330 mm (measured on Frg. 219_1 as 390/442 x 282/330 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no date and no linked description, with eight images labelled 219_01r to 219_04v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images, which show four bifolia, each photographed open on both faces.

  • Identification: identified, high, for all four bifolia. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and in chapter structure with Graesse's text over six passages, at least one on each bifolium, with the same miracles, the same Gregory excerpt on the nine orders of angels, and the Legenda aurea's layout of etymology, rubric and legend; the chapter sequence is the book's own. Derivative abridgements and sermon collections built on the Legenda aurea were not tested; the sustained wording across separately collated chapters, including the etymologies that such compilations often drop, favours the work itself. The copy differs from Graesse in many small readings, for example "gemini fratres" for "germani fratres" and "theodora", which Graesse notes as a variant of his "Theodoche", in the Cosmas and Damian chapter. In the Laurence chapter the whole passage on Philip as the first Christian emperor and on Decius's revolt is present, but the sentence "Nec te moueat ... dispensauit" stands before "Iste phylippus" instead of after "erogarent", where Graesse and Maggioni have it; the citation "ut ait Sicardus in chronica sua" and the sentence "Vel forte ideo dicuntur thesauri ecclesie ... sicut infra dicetur" are absent at that point; and the copy reads "phylippus iunior uehementer extimuit" with Maggioni against Graesse's "Philippus minor extimuit". These are variants within the same work; which redaction or family of the Legenda aurea they belong to is not determined.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading of 30 October 2026 and the source-assisted reading. The first reader saw the pencil labels on the photographs ("Stams, STA, Frg. 219_1r" and so on, which it followed for face order), knew the packet's directory name ("stams-b1-readers"), and had a standing note that mentioned a "Stams pilot" project, without any proposed work or author; the rubric naming Cosmas and Damian is read from the leaf. It transcribed about half of its 48 native-resolution tiles. A research reader (Claude) reread on the native images, with Graesse at hand, lines 1-20 of the right page of canvas 0, lines 1-9 of its left page and lines 1-6 of the left page of canvas 6; an independent research reader read the further lines of canvas 6 and the passages on canvases 2 and 5 listed below (see Verification). Every other line rests on the first reading, and much of the first reading's coverage is partial: about half of each page, and the abraded left page of canvas 2 (219_2r) hardly at all. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader (Claude) compared the Cosmas and Damian opening with Graesse pp. 636-637 on page images, and the Michael and Laurence lines with Graesse's archive.org OCR text. An independent research reader then read, before seeing that comparison, the continuation of the Laurence chapter on canvas 6, left page, native regions 1450,680,2340,770, 1450,1560,2150,640 and 1450,2880,2150,540, and compared it with Graesse p. 489 on a page image fetched from archive.org and with Maggioni's text in Corpus Corporum (cap. CXIII): continuous agreement through the Philip and Decius passage, with the transposition and omissions stated above. The same reader read the end of Eustace and the opening of All Saints on canvas 5, left page, region 1450,1600,2300,600, and Francis's vision of the seraph and the stigmata on canvas 2, right page, region 4250,820,2650,600, and matched both with Graesse's OCR text, so that every bifolium has at least one collated passage. That reader also confirmed the Graesse chapters and pages against the running heads and the placement of the canvases. Coverage: about half of the text is unread, and the second and third bifolia are matched only against OCR text.

The differences are of word choice and order ("gemini" for "germani", "mortificacione" for "maceratione", "deportauit" for "portavit"), small additions and omissions ("et bibit"; "intellectum ad" supplied in the margin), a few corruptions ("auxilio" for "exsilio"), and, in the Laurence chapter, the transposed "Nec te moueat" sentence and the absent Sicardus citation and "Vel forte" sentence. Elsewhere the wording and sequence agree with Graesse as far as the first reading shows: in Cosmas and Damian the Palladia gift, Cosmas's order about his burial, the vision and Lysias; in Michael "Tertio honorandi sunt, quia ipsi sunt fratres et concives nostri" and Gregory's nine orders; and on canvas 1 the dedication on Monte Gargano, with "Tunc papa hiis auditis" where Graesse has "Tunc summus pontifex his auditis". The first reading also has "Passi sunt autem sub dyocletiano anno domini cclxxxvii" at the end of Cosmas and Damian, where Graesse has "Passi sunt sub Dyocletiano, qui coepit circa annos domini CCLXXXVII".

Edition used: Th. Graesse (ed.), Jacobi a Voragine Legenda aurea vulgo Historia Lombardica dicta, 2nd ed. (Leipzig, 1850), archive.org legendaaureavulg00jacouoft: Laurence on p. 489, Cosmas and Damian from p. 636, Michael's nine orders from p. 650. The critical text, G. P. Maggioni (ed.), Legenda aurea (Florence: SISMEL, 1998), was read only for the Laurence chapter, as text in Corpus Corporum, where it is cap. CXIII; its apparatus was not consulted. Graesse's title page attributes the work to Jacobus de Voragine; the attribution is standard.

Leaf order, other items, and limits

Within each bifolium the text runs across the faces as in the table: Cosmas and Damian begins on the right page of canvas 0 and ends on the left page of canvas 1, and Laurence begins on the right page of canvas 7 and continues on the left page of canvas 6. The order of the bifolia within their gatherings, and the gathering structure, were not determined.

Other items in the images: pencil labels with the shelfmark; later pencil or ink notes "A / N° 1 - 20"(?) at the top of the right page of 219_2r and "A.A. / N° 1 18"(?) at the top of the right page of 219_4r; a later cursive inscription dated 1666 on 219_3r; marginal headings and corrections, among them a brown marginal "De s(an)c(t)o cyriaco" on 219_4v where the scribe left no rubric. None of these names the work, and none was read further.

Not claimed: date, origin, script dating, or which redaction or family of the Legenda aurea this copy belongs to. Comparing its Laurence chapter and readings such as "gemini" and "theodora" with Maggioni's apparatus would bear on that question.
