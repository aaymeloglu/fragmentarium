Fragmentarium: identifying the unidentified

18 identifications of medieval manuscript fragments catalogued on Fragmentarium as “unidentified”. Each report links the manuscript to the matching edition and explains the evidence. Research and comparisons were performed by LLMs. Method.

Archief Sint-Leonarduskapittel te Zoutleeuw, nr. 1132
Leuven, Rijksarchief te Leuven
Catalogued as: Unidentified canon law text, 1250 – 1300 CE
F-cckq		Tabula utriusque iuris attributed to Johannes de Erfordia, article Appellatio (on tacit renunciation of an appeal)
Three passages, two consecutive, agree with Bremen, SuUB, msa 0137, f. 27v (page images; coordinator compared about fourteen lines), and most of the recto was placed at f. 26r by the research reader. Not the same form of the text: the case is reworded (ad papam ... ad ordinarium), citations, sigla and order differ. A second witness, Bruges Grootseminarie 46/57 f. 31v, agrees with Bremen against the leaf and adds Sext citations; recension of the leaf not decided. Unprinted; attribution is the catalogues'. Kurtscheid 1914 not seen. Excluded on page images: Monaldus (Pal. lat. 695) and the short article of Pal. lat. 804.		2026-10-12
HS.0301
Gent, Universiteitsbibliotheek Gent
Catalogued as: Unidentified computus text, 901 – 1000 CE
F-bcte		Bede, De temporum ratione, chapter 43 (fol. F)
Twenty-seven lines of fol. F recto follow Giles vol. 6 pp. 232-233 (OCR text) in order, and the first lines of the verso continue the chapter; coordinator checked lines 10-19 on the image. Variants include calculatorum and discernerent; not compared with Jones's edition. The first image-only reading used canvases that do not show fol. F. The sideways leaf in the same images (Priscian, Institutiones XIV) belongs to record F-fwl6. Stub before fol. D unread.		2026-10-12

Lat. 13
Antwerpen, Ruusbroecgenootschap
Catalogued as: Unidentified spiritual treatise, 1401 – 1425 CE
F-szlk		Servasanctus de Faventia, Antidotarius animae: the castle of the soul, chapters on feast days, on restraining touch and on the sixth gate
The two leaves are consecutive and follow the Louvain print (Johannes de Westfalia, GW M41855), fols. xlv verso to xlvii verso, in order: three stretches and several rubrics compared with page images of the Wolfenbüttel copy by a research reader, the sixth-gate opening by the coordinator. Includes the author's Faenza miracle. Found only after the second leaf was read and Google Books search matched "porta interioris affectus"; the first pass, on one leaf, took it for a Decalogue treatise. Small variants. The exempla come from Martinus Polonus and Gregory.		2026-10-12
