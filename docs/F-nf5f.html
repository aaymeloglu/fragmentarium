Readings and comparison details

F-nf5f: Anonymous (Cistercian), Libellus ordinis, end of prologue, chapter 1 (Advent) and the rules for the Epiphany season

Stams Frg. 67 is a bifolium from the Libellus ordinis. This is an anonymous Cistercian handbook that sets out the order of the divine office through the year. Its prologue begins "Utilitati confratrum meorum fraterna caritate semper proficere cupiens". The bifolium preserves four passages:

  • the last thirteen lines of the prologue;
  • a red heading that leads into "Capitulum primum" (abbreviated and faded);
  • the opening of chapter 1, on Advent;
  • on the second leaf, rules for the Sunday within the octave of Epiphany, the octave day, the feast of St William (of Bourges), St Paul the first hermit, and the weeks up to the Purification and Septuagesima.

The prologue agrees line by line with the text printed by J. Loserth in 1894 from Rome, Biblioteca Nazionale Centrale, Sessorianus 134 (BNC 1193). Loserth knew that copy only as "libellus ordinis", from its own rubric and explicit. The Stams text differs from it at one point: it names the Order's new definitions of 1350 alongside those of 1316.

The catalogue record is Stams, collection "Stams (Stealth Published)", Frg. 67: parchment, 201 x 135 mm, online since 18 September 2021, title "x", reconstruction summary by Claudia Sojer. The record has no description and does not name the text. Its measurements fit the bifolium shown on the two images (Stams_STA_Frg_067_01r and _01v).

  • Identification: identified, high confidence.
    • Basis. Thirteen consecutive lines of the compiler's own prologue match Loserth's transcription in wording and sequence. The leaf then continues with the rubric into "Capitulum primum" and an Advent chapter, as a book ordering the office "per circulum anni" would.
    • The prologue is the containing work, not a source. It states the book's method: material gathered from a "liber [u]suum" (Loserth's copy reads "usuum": the Cistercian Liber usuum, the customary, also called Ecclesiastica officia) and the books of definitions, with general rules "specified" for particular cases. Chapter 1 opens with the first sentence of Ecclesiastica officia cap. 1, as that method leads one to expect.
    • Alternatives. The Cistercian codifications of statutes (the Libellus antiquarum definitionum of 1316, and personalised copies such as the one Les Enluminures sold as TM 1334) are named by the prologue as its sources. They are collections of statutes, not an order of the office, so they are not this work.
    • Difference of version. The Stams leaf reads "ex libro [u]suum et libello diffinitionum anni MCCCXVI quam (et) ex novellis diffinitionibus anno MCCCL in unum libellum redactis ...". The first letter of "[u]suum" is a closed loop, unlike this scribe's pointed v; the rest is s, four minims and a suspension stroke, which fits "suum". Loserth prints only "ex libro usuum et libello diffinicionum anno domini 1316 in unum libellum redactio decrevi". His "tam" has no answering "quam". Whether the Rome copy or the transcription lost the clause, or the Stams text represents a revision, is not decided here.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading and the source-assisted reading.
    • A research reader reread on the images:
    • all thirteen prologue lines;
    • the rubric block, using a red-channel enhancement;
    • lines 1-9 of chapter 1;
    • lines 9-16 of the first page of the second leaf.
    • An independent checker read the second leaf's verso (lines 1-17) and the first six and last eight lines of its recto.
    • Other lines rest on the first reading.
    • The rubric block is mostly illegible on the published images.
    • Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification:
    • Prologue. A research reader compared it with Loserth's page on the Munich digital library page image. An independent checker re-viewed that page and reread the weakest prologue lines; the numeral "m ccc l" of the 1350 clause is clear on the leaf.
    • A passage the research had not collated against an edition. The independent checker compared chapter 1's opening (canvas 0, left page, lines 5-9) with Ecclesiastica officia cap. 1, as reprinted in PL 166, col. 1385, on the archive.org page image.
    • Leaf structure. The checker confirmed that the two leaves are conjugate: their worm holes match when one face is mirrored onto the other.
    • The coordinator compared canvas 1, right page, lines 1-5 of the prologue ("post [prio]ra de ordine divini offi[cii compendia diversis et] variis novitatibus viciata, ea q[ue] ... ex libro [u]suum et libello diffinicionum ... et novellis [diffinicionibus] anno ... redactis") with Loserth 1894, p. 485, on the MDZ page image (bsb11555259_00527): the wording agrees, with the manuscript's added clause on the novellae diffinitiones.
    • Gaps in coverage. Only the prologue has an outside witness of this work; no edition of the body is known. The Advent chapter is compared only with its source. The second leaf is assigned to the work by conjugacy, hand and subject.

The differences are listed below; the order is the same throughout.

  • The 1350 clause (above), read in part through a worm hole.
  • "anni MCCCXVI quam" where Loserth has "anno domini 1316".
  • "[...]tas? copiam" where Loserth has "singularum".
  • "redactis et annalibus uno anno editis" followed by an unclear word and "de [a/c]r[...]" where Loserth has "redactio decrevi". The last word may be "decrevi", but the second letter looks more like a than c.
  • "videntur" (written vi-/dnt with the ur-sign) where Loserth has "videantur".
  • Spellings "diffinitionum" and "hijs".

The manuscript keeps the first sentence of Ecclesiastica officia cap. 1, with a short abbreviated word after "In" (h with a stroke above, possibly "hoc") that the edition lacks. It lacks "per Adventum", where a red virgula (a short stroke) stands. The abbreviated word after "prout" is not clearly "tempus". From the next sentence on, the leaf departs from that chapter. Just above this passage the leaf gives the date limits of Advent ("Adventus domini non ante v kal. nec post tertio nonas decembris"), introduced as an alternative rule ("vel accipe aliam regulam").

Attribution. The title "libellus ordinis" and the incipit are those of Sessorianus 134 as transcribed by Loserth: "Incipit prologus in libellum ordinis"; "Explicit libellus ordinis per manus fratris Ludowici de Tulna, monachi Sancte crucis". The second names the Rome copy's scribe, not the author. Loserth names no author, and the work is anonymous here. Its Cistercian purpose rests on the prologue's sources (the Order's books of definitions of 1316 and 1350) and on chapter 1's use of Ecclesiastica officia. The red rubric below the prologue is mostly illegible; it mentions "eiusdem ordinis" and, on the next line, "nostris generaliter", without naming the Order.

Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

Leaf order. The two leaves are conjugate.

  • Leaf 1 recto is canvas 1, right page (end of prologue, rubric). Its verso is canvas 0, left page (chapter 1).
  • Leaf 2 recto is canvas 0, right page. Its verso is canvas 1, left page (Epiphany octave to Septuagesima).
  • Advent and Epiphany do not follow one another, so text is lost between the leaves and this is not the centre of its gathering.
  • The lower margin of chapter 1's page carries a further note, which looks as if it is in a different ink, on commemorations of St Eligius, St Andrew and St Nicholas in Advent. This rests on the first reading only. It is cut at the left edge.
  • Pencil shelfmarks "Stams, STA, Frg. 67r/67v" are modern.

What is not claimed:

  • Date. The text on the leaf cites definitions of 1350, so this copy is later than 1350. No narrower date is claimed.
  • Origin and author. Not claimed.
  • Relation between the copies. Whether the Stams text is a revision of the work or preserves a clause lost in the Rome copy or its transcription is open.
  • Other witnesses. Heiligenkreuz, Cod. 337 (a "Rituale Cisterciense", written 1467) and Cod. 338 (titled "Libellus Ordinis") are described in B. Gsell's catalogue (1891) without incipits. Whether they carry this work was not checked.
  • Novelty. None is claimed. This report rests on one published transcription of the prologue.
