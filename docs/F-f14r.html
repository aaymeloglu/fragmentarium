Nicholas of Lyra, Responsio ad quendam Iudaeum ex verbis Evangelii secundum Matthaeum: end of the reply on Mt 5:44, the objection on the leper (Mt 8) with the start of its reply; other side, end of that reply, the objection on Mt 11:25 (Confiteor) and the sabbath objection and reply (Mt 12)

Two joined strips of one two-column leaf. About 48 lines of the first side agree in wording and sequence with Lyra's Responsio, collated by a research reader on the page image of the Venice (Giunta) 1603 glossed Bible, t. 6, cols. 1725-1726. An independent research reader compared a passage not used for the identification, the lower main column and line-ends of the second side, with cols. 1727-1728 of the same edition and found the sixth (Confiteor) and seventh (sabbath) objections in order. The fragment follows Lyra's order and readings ('meruerant', 'dixit curato a demone') where the printed Fortalitium fidei of Alfonso de Spina, which reuses the text, differs; no Fortalitium manuscript was compared. The copy introduces the fifth and sixth objections with an unnumbered 'Item accipit' where the prints have 'Quinto' and 'Sexto', and it has minor wording differences. No manuscript of the Responsio was compared, the second side is matched by anchors only, and the copy's date and origin are not established; no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-f14r: Nicholas of Lyra, Responsio ad quendam Iudaeum ex verbis Evangelii secundum Matthaeum, objections 4 (end of reply) to 7 (Mt 5:44 to Mt 12)

Two narrow parchment strips at Stams come from one leaf of Nicholas of Lyra's Responsio ad quendam Iudaeum. The work is a Franciscan reply to Jewish objections drawn from the Gospel of Matthew, taken in Gospel order.

The leaf is written in two columns. The first side runs:

  • the end of the reply to the objection on "Diligite inimicos vestros" (Mt 5:44), with its chain of Old Testament examples (Isaac and the king of Gerar, Joseph, David and Saul, Elisha and the Syrians, Exod 23, Prov 25, Ps 119) and the answer on the peoples of Canaan;
  • the next objection, on the leper of Mt 8. It argues that Jesus "contradicit sibi et est instabilis in voluntate", because he ordered the leper's offering yet said "misericordiam volo et non sacrificium", and ordered silence in one place and publicity in another;
  • the opening of the reply to that objection.

The other side carries:

  • the end of that reply;
  • the objection on "Confiteor tibi pater" (Mt 11:25), in line-ends only;
  • the sabbath objection of Mt 12 and the start of its reply (the "sempiternum" argument, David and Bethsabee, Jericho, Elijah's forty days and nights).

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Frg. 224 (collection "Stams (Stealth Published)"): parchment, two fragment items, 315 x 59 mm (measured on Frg. 224_1 as 314/315 x 54/59 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), online since 18 September 2021. Its title is "x"; it has no date, no linked description and two images (224_01r02r, 224_01v02v). The record does not name the text. Its physical description fits the images. A small paper label across the foot of the first side reads "Annales Silesiae / X/A14". It looks like a label of the volume or shelf the strips were taken from, and it says nothing about this text.

  • Identification: identified, high.
    • Basis. About 48 lines of the first side's complete column agree with the printed Responsio in wording and sequence. The best-preserved stretches are the Isaac / Joseph / David / Elisaeus examples, the answer on the Canaanites ("dicendum quod illi erant ydolatre pessimi propter quod mortem meruerant ... ne propter hoc filii israel traherentur ad vicium ydolatrie"), the leper objection, and the run "mundato a lepra: vade nemini dixeris ... dixit curato a demone: vade ad tuos". The other side's remains fall in the same order as the print.
    • Alternative considered. Alfonso de Spina, Fortalitium fidei (written c. 1458-1464), book III, carries the same objections and replies almost word for word. It was compared on page images of the 1511 edition. It is not the text on this leaf, as far as the printed Fortalitium shows:
    • The Fortalitium puts the sabbath (Mt 12) and "Confiteor" (Mt 11) objections between the Canaanite answer and the leper objection, under "Contendit iudeus ..." headings. The fragment, like Lyra, passes straight from the Canaanite answer to the leper and only then to Mt 11 and Mt 12.
    • The fragment agrees with Lyra against the printed Fortalitium in "meruerant" (Fort. "meruerunt"), in "cum ipsis enim ... erant amicicie contrahende ne propter hoc filii israel traherentur ad vicium ydolatrie" (Fort. shorter), in "dixit curato a demone" (not in the Fort.), and in lacking the Fortalitium's added "prunas congregabis super caput eius".
    • No manuscript of the Fortalitium was compared.
    • Differences from the printed Lyra. The fragment introduces the fifth and sixth objections with an unnumbered "Item accipit iudeus", where the prints have "Quinto accipit" and "Sexto accipit". It also reads:
    • "legitur?" for "habetur";
    • "voluit ... fieret" for "volebat ... fiebat";
    • "sibi et est" for "sibiipsi & sic ... est";
    • "in servitutem" for "servituti";
    • "ne haberent cum eis amicicias" for "ne contraherent cum eis amicitias";
    • "habitatoribus" for "habitatoribus terrae". Spelling differences ("ydolatre", "amicicie") are not listed.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers both sides. The source-assisted reading covers the passages listed below:
    • A research reader reread at reading size the whole complete column of the first side, the line openings of its second column, and the first sixteen line openings of the main column of the second side.
    • A second research reader reread, before seeing the source, about twenty further line openings of that main column and the line-ends of the other column on the second side.
    • The right half of the second side's main column is water-stained and was not read by anyone. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the first side's column with the Responsio on the page image of the 1603 Venice glossed Bible (columns 1725-1726). That reader also compared it with search snippets of the Lyon 1545 and Venice 1588 editions, and with page images of the Fortalitium fidei (1511). An independent research reader then compared a passage the first reader had not used, the lower main column and the left-column line-ends of the second side, with page images of the same 1603 edition (columns 1727-1728). In the same order, they found:
    • "et sic oportuit", "et in libro ... he[braeos]", "noctib[us]" and "[ma]gis est ad" in the sabbath reply;
    • "Item ac[cipit] ... [confit]eor tibi pa[ter]" for the sixth objection. The coordinator did not make a separate comparison for this record. The coordinator read canvas 0, the line opening the fifth objection ("[...] voluntate. ¶ Item accipit iudeus illud quod [di]citur Mat. viii. Cum autem descendisset [Iesus] de monte"), on the image; the comparison with col. 1726 of the 1603 page image (archive.org n868), where the print has "Quinto accipit", rests on the research reader and the independent checker. Gaps:
    • No manuscript of the Responsio was compared. So the unnumbered "Item" cannot yet be set against the manuscript tradition.
    • The second side was matched only by scattered anchors in order, not collated line by line.
    • The Lyon 1545 and Venice 1588 editions were seen only as search snippets.

The differences in these passages are the unnumbered "Item" (twice), "legitur?", "arguitur", "voluit ... fieret", "sibi et est", "fecit", "in servitutem", "ne haberent", the missing "terrae", "Gen. xl[v]" against "Gen. 43", and spelling. The "non" in "cum ipsis enim [non] erant" and the expansion of "p(ropter)" are uncertain on the image. None of these differences touches the sequence of objections or the readings that separate the Responsio from the Fortalitium. They show only that this copy's wording is not identical with the printed text. They do not show how the copy relates to the printed editions or to other manuscripts.

The attribution to Nicholas of Lyra and the date 1334 rest on the manuscript tradition and the literature. Manuscript records title the work "Responsio ... Nicolai de Lyra ad quendam Iudaeum ex verbis Evangelii secundum Matthaeum contra Christum nequiter arguentem", e.g. the Munich BSB Clm catalogue and Brussels KBR 2590-602 (colophon dated 1436). The date 1334 is given in Die Geschichte der Daniel-Auslegung in Judentum, Christentum und Islam (2012), seen as a search snippet. The heading of the section in the 1603 print was not checked.

Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

  • One leaf, two columns. Words run across the joint between the two strips, so the strips are adjacent pieces of one leaf. The side imaged first (224_01r02r) comes first in the text: it has the Mt 5 and Mt 8 material, and the other side has Mt 11 and Mt 12, which is the Responsio's order. The text between the two sides fits one leaf. Red and blue paragraph marks alternate. Gospel lemmata are underlined.
  • The "Annales Silesiae" label is not evidence for this text or its origin, and the host volume was not investigated.
  • Not claimed: the date of this copy, which is not established. Nicholas of Lyra's date of 1334 is the date of the work, and 1603 is the date of the edition compared. Also not claimed: the copy's origin, its relation to any other manuscript or printed edition, or whether the unnumbered "Item" is a feature of a group of manuscripts.
