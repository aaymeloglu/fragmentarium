Frg. 217 (F-6dkg)

Stams, Stams (Stealth Published) · catalogued as x, not given

Jacobus de Voragine, Legenda aurea (bifolium Frg. 217_1 only): end of Martin, Brice, opening of Elizabeth (Graesse caps. 166-168, pp. 750-753) and part of Clement (cap. 170, pp. 783-786); strip Frg. 217_2 not identified

Five passages on all four pages of the bifolium Frg. 217_1 were reread on the native images and agree in wording and sequence with Graesse 1850 (pp. 751 and 784 on page images, other pages on OCR) and with Maggioni's text in Corpus Corporum; the November order Martin-Brice-Elizabeth-(Cecilia)-Clement and the rubricated 'De nomine' prologue, with readings absent from Juan Gil de Zamora's reworking, show it is the Legenda aurea itself. A research reader (Claude) collated four passages; an independent research reader read a fifth (canvas 1 right, Clement, region 4620,2980,2080,580) before seeing the comparison and matched it against Graesse p. 784 and Maggioni. Variants are minor (particles, omissions such as 'visu', corruptions such as 'latiorem et affluentiorem'), and the copy often sides with Maggioni against Graesse ('emulus', 'regionum', 'amen', 'eicite'). The identification covers item 217_1 only: the strip Frg. 217_2 is read only in scraps, not identified, and not shown to come from the same book. Lines outside the five passages rest on the first reading; no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-6dkg: Jacobus de Voragine, Legenda aurea, end of Martin, Brice, opening of Elizabeth, and part of Clement (bifolium Frg. 217_1 only)

The bifolium Frg. 217_1 in Stams is from a copy of the Legenda aurea of Jacobus de Voragine, a collection of saints' lives and feasts arranged by the church year. One leaf carries the end of the chapter on Martin (the blind and lame beggars at the translation of his body, and the eulogy from Ambrose), the whole chapter on Brice, and the etymology and opening of the life of Elizabeth of Hungary, as far as the lot that gives her St John as patron. The other leaf carries the middle of the chapter on Pope Clement, from Faustinianus at Antioch to the finding of Clement's body in a marble shrine under the sea. In the chapter numbering of Graesse's edition (2nd ed., 1850) these are caps. 166-168, pp. 750-753, and cap. 170, pp. 783-786. The second item of the record, the narrow strip Frg. 217_2, is not identified.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stams (Stealth Published), Frg. 217: parchment, two fragment items, 372 x 230 mm (measured on Frg. 217_1 as 319/372 x 181/230 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no date and no linked description. The record gives only these physical details and does not name the text. Its description fits what the images show: one bifolium and one small strip.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the bifolium Frg. 217_1. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and sequence with Graesse's text and with Maggioni's critical text over five passages on all four pages, together with the Legenda aurea's own November order (Martin, Brice, Elizabeth, then Cecilia and Clement) and its etymological prologue to Elizabeth, rubricated "De nomine". Juan Gil de Zamora's Legende sanctorum, which reuses the Legenda aurea's Martin and Brice, was considered and set aside: the fragment has "et sic ipsi forsitan curarentur", "per quam corpus nequaquam duci putabant", "baptisma susceperunt" and "lxxv ecclesias", where Zamora lacks the first two and reads "baptizati sunt" and ".lxx. ecclesias", and Zamora does not carry the "De nomine" prologue. The copy often agrees with Maggioni where Graesse departs from him ("emulus", "toti mundo cognoscibilem fecit", "ad octauam", "mutentur", "Iohannis", "regionum", "amen", "eicite"), and it has small omissions and corruptions of its own; none of these bears on the identification. The strip Frg. 217_2 is not identified and is not shown to come from the same book.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading of 30 October 2026 and the source-assisted reading. The first reader saw the pencil shelfmarks on the photographs ("Stams, STA, Frg. 217_1r", "217-1v", "Frg. 217_2r", "217_2v") and had a standing note that mentioned a "Stams pilot" project, without any proposed work or author; the rubrics quoted in the first reading are read from the leaf. A research reader (Claude) reread four passages on the native images with the editions at hand (listed below), and read the strip at native size; the fifth passage below is the independent reader's (see Verification). Every other line rests on the first reading, checked only against the overview photographs. The stained right edge of the left page of canvas 0 was not reread. The strip's readings are very uncertain. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader (Claude) compared the four reread passages with Graesse 1850, p. 751 on the page image and the other pages in the archive.org OCR text, and with Maggioni's text as paragraphs in Corpus Corporum (not on page images). An independent research reader then read a fifth passage, not covered by those four, before seeing the comparison: canvas 1, right page (Clement: Clement's care for the poor, Domitilla, Theodora and the blinding of Sisinnius), native region 4620,2980,2080,580, and compared it with Graesse p. 784 on a page image fetched from archive.org and with Maggioni in Corpus Corporum. Eight lines agree continuously in wording and sequence, and the leaf order follows from them. That reader also checked the Graesse chapter numbers and pages against the running heads, and did not re-check the Zamora discriminators. Coverage: all four pages of the bifolium carry collated text; the strip has no match. No human review.

The differences are of four kinds: particles and word order ("enim", "ergo", "igitur", "an"/"en", "id est"); omissions ("visu", "Fuit ergo Symon", "id est"); corruptions ("latiorem et affluentiorem" for "laticem affluentem", "recto et dextro"); and readings where Graesse departs from Maggioni or from the first printed edition he reports as Ed. Pr., where the fragment sides with Maggioni or the Ed. Pr. The first reading, not reread, also gives "ex inprouiso ipsis protinus obuiauit" (Graesse "de improviso", Maggioni "ex improuiso"), "Septem tempora mutentur" (Graesse "mutabuntur", Maggioni "mutentur") and "nomen sancti Johannis" in the Elizabeth chapter (Graesse "Petri", Maggioni "Iohannis"). Elsewhere, as far as the first reading and the overview photographs show, the wording and sequence agree with Graesse: Martin's beggars and the Ambrose eulogy; Brice's accusation, the infant's testimony, the coals, the exile, Justinian and Armenius; Elizabeth's childhood; and the Sisinnius, exile and martyrdom narrative of Clement.

Edition used: Th. Graesse (ed.), Jacobi a Voragine Legenda aurea vulgo Historia Lombardica dicta, 2nd ed. (1850), archive.org bub_gb_h2rkGCFC1G0C: Brice on p. 751, Clement on p. 784. The critical text, G. P. Maggioni (ed.), Legenda aurea (Florence: SISMEL, 1998), was read as text in Corpus Corporum, where its chapter numbers differ (Elizabeth is cap. CLXIV, Clement cap. CLXVI). Graesse's title page and Maggioni's edition both attribute the work to Jacobus de Voragine; the attribution is standard.

Leaf order, the strip, and limits

Leaf A has canvas 0, right page, as recto (end of Martin, Brice) and canvas 1, left page, as verso (end of Brice, Elizabeth). Leaf B has canvas 1, right page, as recto (Clement: Faustinianus, Domitilla, Sisinnius blinded and healed) and canvas 0, left page, as verso (Clement continued: the Sisinnius stones, exile to the Chersonese, the lamb and the spring, martyrdom, the marble shrine). The rest of Elizabeth, the chapter on Cecilia (Graesse cap. 169) and the opening of Clement are lost between the two leaves, so this is not the central bifolium of its gathering.

The strip Frg. 217_2, a narrow piece with about ten short lines a side, is visible at canvas 0, region 1300,1700,340,900 (image), and canvas 1, region 780,1700,300,900 (image). Its few legible word groups ("...mortale", "...stitum...", "...solueret...") gave no match in three Corpus Corporum proximity searches. Its line spacing and letter size look close to the bifolium's, but that it comes from the same book is not shown. The strip is not identified.

Not claimed: date, origin, script dating, or where this copy stands in the textual tradition of the Legenda aurea, including its agreements with Maggioni and with Graesse's Ed. Pr. against Graesse's text. Maggioni was not seen on page images.
