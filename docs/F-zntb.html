Readings and comparison details

F-zntb: Noted monastic antiphoner, Matins of the Third Sunday of Lent (Oculi), responsories Memento mei to Dixit Joseph

This parchment leaf at Stams, old foliation LVI, comes from a noted antiphoner (a choir book of the Office chants with their melodies) arranged for the monastic cursus, the order of Office used by monks. It carries Matins of the Third Sunday of Lent, whose chants tell the story of Joseph and his brothers. The text runs from the end of the great responsory Memento mei through Tollite hinc, Iste est frater and Dixit Ruben, each with its verse, then the single nocturn-III antiphon Cum immundus spiritus under the rubric "Ad cc(antica)", then the responsory Merito haec patimur and the opening of Dixit Joseph undecim fratribus. Cantus IDs in leaf order: 007144, 007769, 006999, 006479, 002009, 007146, 006476. The chants, their order and the rubric agree with the Cistercian antiphoner of Morimondo, Paris, BnF, NAL 1411, ff. 71v-72v.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 18, parchment, 320 x 205 mm (measured 248/320 x 131/205 mm by Claudia Sojer), dated 1450-1499, title "x", with two images labelled 01r and 01v. The record has no description or identification. Nothing in it conflicts with the identification; it simply did not name the text.

  • Identification: identified, high. The basis is the run of seven chants of Matins of Oculi, in this order and with their verses, read on both faces, which agrees with Morimondo, BnF NAL 1411, ff. 71v-72v, on its page images. The text is liturgical and appears in many antiphoners; the claim is the office and its chants, not a particular book. Differences from Morimondo: the leaf writes no versicle between Cum immundus and Merito (Morimondo has "Scapulis suis", f. 72v); it reads "Tollite de frugibus", as Morimondo does, where Salzinnes, Lubiąż and the Cantus standard text have fructibus; there is no Gloria Patri after Dixit Ruben, as in Morimondo (Salzinnes and Köln 1161 write one). In the passage Iste est frater both books spell michi and lacrime; tribulacio has not been checked against Morimondo. Use: a Cantus Database sequence search found 82 inventories with all seven chants and 9 with them in the leaf's order. Of the eight of those opened, the leaf's exact arrangement (Dixit Ruben as the fourth responsory of nocturn II, one antiphon Cum immundus for nocturn III, then Merito and Dixit Joseph) is in Morimondo (1174), Rein (A-Wn Cod. 1799\\, 13th century), Lubiąż (PL-WRu I F 401, 1295) and Salzinnes (1554-5), all Cistercian, and in Köln D-KNd 1161 (12th century, house not checked). The non-Cistercian books opened (Utrecht NL-Uu 406, St Florian XI 480, Oxford Canon. Liturg. 202) arrange it differently. This supports Cistercian use, which fits Stams, a Cistercian abbey; it is an inference from chant order and not part of the identification, and it does not show that the book was made or used at Stams.
  • Transcription: source-assisted. The image-only first reading covers every text line of both faces; its reader noted the pencil shelf label "Stams, StA, Frg. 18" at the foot of the leaf, which names the holding and not the text, and saw no proposed identification. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's (Claude) reread of all text lines of both faces in bands at reading size, with contrast-enhanced crops of the faded Cum immundus line, made with Morimondo available. It corrects the first reading's "intro~ / n?iuit" to "in do- / mum" and its "Deri[...]" cue to Merito. The two heavy words at the start of canvas 0 line 2 ("indo mum"?) and the abraded middle of the Cum immundus line remain uncertain. The neumes were not transcribed. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader (Claude) compared all seven chants on the leaf's images with Morimondo on its BnF page images (canvases 78-79, ff. 71v-73r). An independent research reader then read R. Iste est frater on canvas 1 (labelled 01v), native boxes 2700,3000,3000,500 and 2700,3450,3000,550, before seeing the transcription of that band, and compared it with Morimondo f. 71v foot and f. 72r lines 1-4 on the BnF page image (openapi.bnf.fr, btv1b10033588d/f79, full size 6203 x 4503 pixels): the passage agrees word for word. The same reader checked on the image the foliation "LVI" on canvas 1 and, on canvas 0, the red "Ad cc." and Cum immundus followed directly by R. Merito with no versicle. Salzinnes and Lubiąż were seen only as Cantus Database transcriptions and folio lists; the order evidence for the other inventories comes from Cantus data, not page images. No human review.

Leaf, other items, and what is not claimed

  • One leaf, single column of about ten text lines per face under red-lined staves with German Gothic (Hufnagel) neumes and C and F clefs; red responsory and verse signs, red rubrics and filler strokes. The upper corners are cut away, with a punched hole at the top centre, a horizontal fold and the lower margin folded over. The only modern additions are the pencil shelf labels at the foot.
  • Not claimed: the date (the catalogue's 1450-1499 was not tested), the origin or the house that used the book, or any relation to surviving Stams choir books. The two heavy words at the start of canvas 0 line 2 and the abraded middle of Cum immundus need raking-light or UV imaging.
