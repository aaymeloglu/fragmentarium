Readings and comparison details

F-au0s: Bernard of Parma, Casus longi on the Decretals, book III

The fragment is identified with high confidence as the Casus longi super quinque libros Decretalium of Bernard of Parma (Bernardus Bottonius, d. 1266): the casus on X 3.4.11–12 on one face and on X 3.5.5–6 on the other. Selected lines on both faces agree in wording and sequence with the casus printed beside those chapters in a glossed edition of the Decretals. It is a run of casus one after another, without the decretal text or the gloss.

The Fragmentarium record describes a parchment fragment, Antwerpen, Erfgoedbibliotheek Hendrik Conscience, Fragm. 358, 138 × 94 mm, dated 1276–1325, catalogued as an unidentified commentary on the Decretals of Gregory IX. Those catalogue details have not been reassessed.

  • Identification: identified, high. The basis is consecutive casus prose on two faces, including the transitions from one chapter's casus to the next. Material alternative: another work that copies Bernard's casus word for word; none is known to us, and later commentators who quote this casus (Panormitanus on X 3.5.6) surround it with their own commentary, which the fragment lacks.
  • Transcription: source-assisted selected reading, sixteen lines of one face and parts of fifteen of the other, read by a model with the printed text to hand. The image-only reading recovered little and misread the opening lemma. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: the manuscript images were compared with the OCR text of the printed edition by one model; the printed page images were not opened. A standalone printed Casus longi was not collated. No human review.

The file names of the two images (fol_1v, fol_1r) are the reverse of their manifest labels; the text order agrees with the file names.

Edition and attribution

The casus printed with the gloss in the Roman edition of the Decretals are those of Bernard of Parma, the author of the ordinary gloss, whose Casus longi also circulated and was printed separately (Paris 1475, Venice 1477, Strasbourg 1488 and later, as listed in the Catholic Encyclopedia article on Bernard). That standard ascription is the basis for the author's name here. The comparison was made with the casus as printed beside the decretals, not with a separate Casus longi edition or manuscript, so nothing is claimed about recension. The fragment's form, casus following casus with no decretal text, is that of the separate work.

Not examined: the slivers of the neighbouring columns, the marginal notes, and the date or origin of the hand.
