Readings and comparison details

F-rygw: Galen, De regimine sanitatis, book VI

The main text is identified with high confidence as book VI of the medieval Latin De regimine sanitatis (De sanitate tuenda), in the translation attributed to Burgundio of Pisa. Two separate passages on fol. [1]v agree in distinctive wording and sequence with the 1490 Venice edition. The identification concerns the main text; the marginal annotations have not been identified.

The Fragmentarium record describes two parchment leaves, Gent, Bisschoppelijk Archief Gent, fragment nr. 15, dated 1201–1250, in Southern Textualis, 380 × 247 mm. These catalogue descriptions have not been independently reassessed.

The preserved image-only reading contains substantial errors and gaps. The separately preserved selected revision and the excerpts below are source-assisted, obtained by returning to the manuscript after locating the edition. Two Codex agents compared both passages directly with manuscript and edition images. This is model review, not human palaeographic verification or certification of a complete transcription.

Both passages are on the first leaf, verso, image-002 of the preserved reading. Line labels refer to the native, unrotated crops defined in that reading. Parentheses expand abbreviations; ellipses omit text and do not supply lost words.

The first passage supplied the retrieval phrase. The second is an additional verification passage not used for retrieval; it had already been included in the image-only reading. Crop B covers x=24.9–56%, y=48–61% of the full image; crop C covers x=60.2–90%, y=35.4–48%. The second crop cuts portions of the right edge, so the table does not reconstruct full lines there.

The wording is not completely identical. For example, the manuscript places antea before in hoc libro, where the edition places it after; crop C reads vocant against the edition's nominant. Inflectional readings in the opening list, technical Greek-derived terms, and some abbreviated words remain uncertain. No complete collation is claimed.

The two passages occur in the same order in continuous main-column prose, without a quotation boundary or a change of speaker around the selected text. Their agreement includes transitions, self-reference to earlier treatment, explanatory glosses, and the sequence of symptoms. This supports the containing medical text, rather than merely a shared medical commonplace. The separate marginal glosses and the possibility of further compilation outside the surviving leaves remain outside the claim.

Edition and attribution

The comparison edition is Galieni Pergamensis medicorum omnium principis Opera, edited by Diomede Bonardo, Venice: Filippo Pinzi, 27 August 1490, vol. II. Galeno Latino's edition record locates this translation at signatures q3r–r8v. Its translation record identifies De regimine sanitatis with De sanitate tuenda and discusses the attribution of book VI to Burgundio of Pisa, including the distinction between the abridged earlier books and the fuller sixth book. The Wellcome bibliographic record independently records the printer, date, editor, and two-volume edition.

The printed colophon at the end of book VI, directly inspected, says that the work is attributed to Galen but, according to Rhazes in book IX of the Continens, belonged to Rufus: “Explicit liber de regimine sanitatis qui attribuitur galieno licet secundum rasim nono continentis fuerit ruffi.” That historical attribution caveat is retained here. The modern identification of the translation and work rests on the scholarly record separately from the textual match; the matching passages alone do not adjudicate ancient authorship.

All four manuscript canvases were visually surveyed. Detailed source comparison is limited to the two passages on fol. [1]v and their immediate context. The remaining main text and annotations have not been systematically collated. No claim is made about witness novelty, provenance, or recension.
