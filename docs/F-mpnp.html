Readings and comparison details

F-mpnp: Guillelmus Brito, Summa Britonis (Expositiones vocabulorum Biblie), letter C, Convallis illustris to Coturnix

This parchment strip in Stams is from a two-column copy of the Summa of Guillelmus Brito, a Franciscan dictionary of the hard words of the Latin Bible, arranged alphabetically and compiled about 1250-1270. It carries the last eight lines of four columns from the letter C: the entries Convallis illustris and Convenire, the end of Corbanan and the start of Coriandrum, Chorus, and Corrigia and Coturnix. In the edition of Lloyd W. and Bernardine A. Daly (1975) these are vol. 1, pp. 162, 164, 166 and 168.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 149: parchment, one fragment item, 155 x 63 mm (measured as 150/155 x 61/63 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description, with two images labelled 149_01r and 149_01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: a single horizontal strip with two columns on each face and a wide stained blank band below the text, probably the lower margin.

  • Identification: identified, high. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and order with Daly's text on all four columns, in the edition's order of entries, including Brito's own frame around the authorities he quotes ("Et interlinearis dicit ... In Historiis autem super Actus Apostolorum scribitur quod", "Nos vulgo curleium dicimus a currendo"). Peter Comestor's Historia scholastica, Isidore's Etymologiae and Papias are quoted inside the entries by name and are sources, not the containing work: Comestor has the passage on the three chests of the Temple ("Musach erat arca ... Corbona ... Gazophylacium") but not Brito's frame or the following entry. Johannes Balbus's Catholicon and Reuchlin's Breviloquus, which draw on Brito, were not tested. The copy differs from Daly's text in omitting "non" before "sunt aves modice" in Coturnix, with Daly's manuscript C; in a shortened cross-reference, "Vide infra in expo(sitione)? Gazophi[lacii]" for "Vide infra ubi exponitur gazophilacium", run over into the next line; in perhaps omitting "ibi" in Convenire, with Daly's manuscript D (uncertain under the stain); and perhaps in abbreviating the opening of Coturnix, "sicut dicit m(agister)" with "avis est regia" possibly left out (uncertain). Two insertions in red brackets in verso col. b and one at the end of recto col. a line 4 are not in the edition's text and are unread. One shared variant with manuscript C proves nothing about the copy's place in the tradition.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading of 31 October 2026 and the source-assisted reading. A research reader reread on the native images recto col. b, verso col. a and verso col. b line by line, and recto col. a lines 1-3. An independent checker read recto col. a lines 4-8 and the run-over below line 8. Several of the checker's readings there were wrong at the word level before the edition was opened and were settled with it ("uos inq(uam)" for a first "io ing", "iudei" for "audi", "De ult(im)o" for "Sulno"). The red-bracketed insertions and the marginal finding-words are only partly read. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the three columns read line by line with the page images and OCR text of Daly's edition (pp. 164, 166 and 168), and recto col. a lines 1-3 with p. 162. An independent checker read canvas 0, recto col. a, lines 4-8, native box 2680,2440,840,300, which the research reader had left unread, on the image before opening the edition, and compared them with Daly vol. 1, p. 162, Convenire lines 1-7, on the page image (p. 162). The lines follow the edition word for word in sequence, through the quotation "uos inquam conuenio o iudei" to "De ultimo dicitur xx Mathei nonne ex denario conuenisti mecum", with no substantive difference beyond abbreviation and the possible omission of "ibi". The checker also confirmed "non om. C" in the apparatus of p. 168. The checker had read the report's transcription of the other columns, not of these lines. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

Besides the differences listed under Identification, the copy has single-letter and spelling differences ("reponebatur" for "reponebantur", "hystoriis", "ysidorus"). The first reading's "consentium" is the edition's "transeuntium" on the image. Edition used: Summa Britonis sive Guillelmi Britonis Expositiones vocabulorum Biblie, ed. Lloyd W. Daly and Bernardine A. Daly, Thesaurus Mundi 15 (Padua: Antenore, 1975), vol. 1, archive.org brito-gulielmus-expositiones-vocabulorum-biblie-1: p. 162, p. 164, p. 166 and p. 168. The edition prints the text as Brito's Summa; the attribution is standard.

Leaf order, other items, and limits

About two pages of Daly's text separate the end of each column from the end of the next (pp. 162, 164, 166, 168), which fits a column of roughly two edition pages and the order recto col. a, col. b, then verso col. a, col. b given by the pencil labels. That these are the last eight lines of each column is inferred from the blank band below the text; recto col. a ends mid-entry, at "mecum", with only a run-over below it.

Other items: finding-words in the margins in a later or contemporary hand, among them "Asphaltum" beside Convallis illustris, "O[z]usat/Musac" beside recto col. b line 4, "Corrigiola", "Coredula(?)", and on the verso "Co[..]", "minu(?)" and "kurkebo[..](?)", perhaps vernacular; "Corrigiola" and "kurkebo[..]" are not in the edition. The red-bracketed insertions (verso col. b lines 1-2, and the end of recto col. a line 4) and faint writing in the left margin of the recto are unread. The traces at the end of recto col. a line 4 would be consistent with "in sermone Augustini", the reading of Daly's manuscript C for "ibi" in Convenire, but this is a possibility suggested with the edition open, not a reading.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the copy, and its place in the tradition of Brito's Summa.
