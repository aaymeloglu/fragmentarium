Readings and comparison details

F-szlk: Servasanctus de Faventia, Antidotarius animae

The bifolium is identified with high confidence as part of the Antidotarius animae of the Franciscan Servasanctus de Faventia (Servasanto da Faenza, d. about 1300), from the tractate that describes the soul as a royal castle whose gates must be shut. Its two leaves are consecutive and carry the end of a chapter on lawful killing under the gate of touch, the chapter on keeping feast days, the chapter on restraining touch, and the opening of the chapter on the sixth gate, the inner affection. The text agrees with the first printed edition in order across all eight columns.

The Fragmentarium record describes a parchment bifolium, Antwerpen, Ruusbroecgenootschap, Lat. 13, 166 × 206 mm, dated 1401–1425, catalogued as an unidentified spiritual treatise. The catalogue date has not been reassessed.

  • Identification: identified, high. Three stretches of ten to twenty lines and several chapter rubrics correspond to the print, including the author's own matter: a miracle located at Faenza in the church of St Peter, the allegory's chapter headings, and the transitions between exempla. The exempla themselves (Theoderic's deacon, the Arian bishop Olimpius, Pope Gregory's body at the church doors) are borrowed from chronicles and would not identify the work alone. Decalogue treatises were tested first and excluded (Herolt, Geilhoven's Gnotosolitos, on page images).
  • Transcription: image-only first reading of leaf X by an isolated reader, and a source-assisted selected reading. Leaf Z was not in the first reader's packet. Crumpled bands and most of one column of leaf X are unread. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared three stretches with page images of the Wolfenbüttel copy of the print; the coordinator compared the foot of the last column (the end of the chapter on touch and the opening of the sixth gate) with the print's page image. The print's page images are small. No human review.

Textual evidence

Manuscript images: fol. Xr, fol. Xv, fol. Zr, fol. Zv. The text runs Xr, Xv, Zr, Zv, two columns each, with no gap between the leaves. Abbreviations are resolved silently; square brackets mark text lost or not read.

Variants seen: totus for potius, infra for intra, ardet for vivit, falsi doctores ... simplicium for falsa docentes ... fidelium, puto quod nec servares et michi for probabile est quod nec servares mihi; the manuscript's rubrics lack the print's chapter numbers. The record's stray mention of Boethius is explained by the print's next words after the fragment ends, "iuxta Boecii nostri ...".

Edition and attribution

The edition is Servasanctus de Faventia, Antidotarius animae, Louvain: Johannes de Westfalia, about 1486 (GW M41855, ISTC is00476000), read in the Wolfenbüttel, Herzog August Bibliothek copy, images 97–102 (fols. xlv–xlvii). The attribution is that of the incunable catalogues; the mention of Faenza in the text fits it, and the question of authorship was not examined further.

No claim is made about the fragment's date or origin, or about its place among the manuscripts of the work.
