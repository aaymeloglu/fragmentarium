NAL 2317 (F-1szi)

Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France · catalogued as Commentarium biblicum (unidentified) | Super Mt. 6,25-31, 951 – 1000 CE

Pseudo-Haymo, Liverani homiliary (anonymous Carolingian reworking of Haymo of Auxerre): homily on Matthew 6:24-33 (Barré 67, Liverani LIII), ravens and lilies

The ravens passage on fol. 6r and the Solomon and lilies passage on fol. 6v ('lilium in uirginibus rosam in martiribus uiolam in confessoribus') agree in sequence and wording with Paris, BnF, Latin 3806, fols. 163v-165r, read as page images by a research reader, and with Liverani's 1863 print read through Google Books snippets; the coordinator read the lilies passage on the image. The leaf is a homily, not the biblical commentary of the catalogue title. Its lilies section is shorter and partly reworded against both witnesses, and two of its glosses were not found in either, so the recension is undetermined. It belongs to the same manuscript as NAL 2317 ff. 4-5 and 7 (F-kr2n, F-vpe8). Liverani was not seen as page images; no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-1szi: Pseudo-Haymo, Liverani homiliary, homily on Matthew 6:24-33 (Barré no. 67, Liverani no. LIII)

The leaf is identified with high confidence as part of a homily, not of a biblical commentary: the homily on "No man can serve two masters" from an anonymous Carolingian homiliary that circulated as homilies of "Haymo", a reworking of the homiliary of Haymo of Auxerre known as the Liverani homiliary. The recto has the exposition of the birds and the ravens, the verso that of the lilies of the field, in the order of the Gospel. The second half is shorter and partly reworded against both witnesses compared.

The Fragmentarium record describes a parchment leaf, Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, NAL 2317, f. 6, 305 x 248 mm, dated 951-1000, catalogued as an unidentified biblical commentary on Matthew 6:25-31. The verses are as catalogued; the text is a homily for a Sunday after Pentecost, which expounds the Gospel reading phrase by phrase and so resembles a commentary. The catalogue names no author or work.

  • Identification: identified, high. The leaf agrees in sequence and in distinctive wording with homily no. 67 of Barré's inventory of the Liverani homiliary (Liverani no. LIII, pp. 463-467, the part on pp. 465-466): the raven lore with "tale est ac si diceret Si deus tam uilia uolatilia ...", "Hoc non adfirmando sed abnegando dixit", and Solomon set against Christ who "habet lilium in uirginibus rosam in martiribus uiolam in confessoribus". Haymo of Auxerre's own homily for the Sunday (Patrologia Latina 118, printed as Haymo of Halberstadt) has the same exegesis in different words and is the source of the reworking, not the text on the leaf. Heiric of Auxerre's homilies (Corpus Christianorum, Continuatio Mediaevalis 116B), searched by phrase, have none of the wording tested. Haymo's own homily (PL 118, cols. 682-684) ends that phrase "violam in continentibus". Qualification on the recension (that is, the distinct version of the text): in the lilies section the leaf is shorter than Liverani's print and Latin 3806 and words several comments differently, and its glosses on "minime fidei" and on "duo ... tria" were not found in either. Which version of the collection it represents is not determined.
  • Transcription: source-assisted selected reading of fol. 6r column b lines 9-22 and fol. 6v column b lines 1-13; the image-only first reading covers all four columns. A research reader reread those two passages on enlarged crops; everything else rests on the first reading. Fol. 6r column a has lost the beginning of every line and is not read continuously; fol. 6r is rubbed and overwritten with 16th-century notes. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the ravens passage (fol. 6r) with page images of Paris, BnF, Latin 3806, fols. 163v-164r, and with Liverani's printed text as returned by Google Books search-within snippets, and the lilies passage (fol. 6v) with page images of Latin 3806, fols. 164v-165r. The coordinator read "lilium in uirginibus ... uiolam in confessoribus ... Si autem fenum agri" on the image of fol. 6v. Liverani's edition was not seen as page images. No human review.

Differences: on fol. 6r the leaf reads "procurat" for "sic pascit", "ponitur" for "posuit" and "aut nigrum" for "nec alterum nigrum", and puts the psalm verse before "Sed postquam" as Latin 3806 does. On fol. 6v the leaf lacks the discussion, present in Latin 3806 and in Liverani (p. 466), of why Solomon is named before other kings and of "hodie" and "cras"; it comments on "lilia agri" and "fenum agri" in short sentences of its own, then glosses "minime fidei" ("qui ex parte credit et perfecte non credit") and notes that the Gospel first named two cares and now three. For those last two comments no counterpart was found in the Liverani snippets or on the pages of Latin 3806 that were read; Haymo's own homily has a looser parallel for the first (PL 118, col. 683). They may belong to this copy's version of the homily or be additions.

The collection is the one H. Barré calls the Liverani homiliary (Les homéliaires carolingiens de l'école d'Auxerre, Studi e Testi 225, 1962, pp. 113-122, inventory pp. 195-208): an anonymous homiliary on the Gospels transmitted as Homelie domni Aimonis, which Barré judges a later reworking of the homiliary of Haymo of Auxerre. It was printed by F. Liverani, Spicilegium Liberianum (Florence, 1863), from a manuscript of S. Maria Maggiore in Rome, under the name of Haymo of Halberstadt, an attribution no longer accepted. Barré distinguishes an Italian recension (Liverani's manuscript and others) from a French one, to which he assigns Paris, BnF, Latin 3806, a homiliary from Vilabertran in Catalonia of the 11th or 12th century according to the BnF notice. No page scan of Liverani was found; his text and Barré's inventory were read through Google Books search-within snippets, which give the printed wording and page but can drop or garble words. Latin 3806 was read from the digitised microfilm on Gallica. The pages of Latin 3806 used here are fols. 163v-164r and fols. 164v-165r; the homily occupies fols. 161-166 of that manuscript according to Barré.

The manuscript, and what is not claimed

Three Fragmentarium records for NAL 2317 are leaves of one manuscript: this one, ff. 4-5 (F-kr2n) and f. 7 (F-vpe8). The catalogue describes them separately, with three different dates, and does not connect them. The grounds for joining them are the same small rounded Carolingian minuscule and spelling habits on all four leaves (abebat, ominum, scripturra, ut for uel, "&" inside words, ".;" at sentence ends), the same two-column layout of 31 to 33 lines with a written area that the catalogue itself measures as 236 x 184, 238 x 185 and 235 x 184 mm, and the content: homilies of one collection in its liturgical order, Barré nos. 55 to 57 on ff. 4-5 and nos. 67, 68 and 69 across ff. 6 and 7. The leaves are not consecutive. This is a reader's comparison of images, not a codicological examination.

The text order confirms that the face numbered 6 is the recto. The recto carries later notes: the years 1574 to 1579, a letter "A", and "8 janvier 1574 - 27 juin 1579", from its use as a wrapper. No claim is made about the date or place of origin; the catalogue dates were not reassessed, and the spelling and the agreement with a Catalan witness only suggest, without showing, a southern origin. The recension is not determined: a full collation with Latin 3806, with Berlin, Staatsbibliothek, theol. lat. fol. 595, and with a page copy of Liverani would be needed, and the Catalan homiliary of Luculentius, another reworking of Haymo, was not tested because no searchable text was found. Whether these leaves are already known to students of the collection was not checked.
