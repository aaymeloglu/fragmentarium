Readings and comparison details

F-kr2n: Pseudo-Haymo, Liverani homiliary, homilies for the Sundays after Pentecost on Mark 8:1-9, Matthew 7:15-21 and Luke 16:1-9

The bifolium is identified with high confidence as two leaves of an anonymous Carolingian homiliary that circulated as homilies of "Haymo", a reworking of the homiliary of Haymo of Auxerre known as the Liverani homiliary. Fol. 4 carries the homily on the feeding of the four thousand (Barré no. 55, Liverani no. XLII), fol. 5 the end of the homily on the false prophets (Barré no. 56, Liverani no. XLIV) and, under the rubric "Dominica X", the start of the homily on the unjust steward (Barré no. 57, Liverani no. XLV). The wording is that of this collection, in a form that differs in detail from both witnesses compared.

The Fragmentarium record describes a parchment bifolium, Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, NAL 2317, ff. 4-5, 325 x 500 mm, dated 1001-1050, catalogued as an unidentified homiliary. The genre is as catalogued. The catalogue description already notes that the homily on fol. 4r is close to Haymo's (Patrologia Latina 118, cols. 637-639) and transcribes the rubric and incipit on fol. 5r; it does not name the collection.

  • Identification: identified, high. Three consecutive homilies agree in sequence and in distinctive wording with the Liverani homiliary: the etymology "Sporta quippe ex iunco et palma efficitur ... palma quasi pacis alma" and the seven churches, candlesticks and horns on fol. 4v, the close of the false-prophets homily on fol. 5r, and the opening of the steward homily on fols. 5r-5v. Haymo of Auxerre's own homily for the Sunday (Patrologia Latina 118, printed as Haymo of Halberstadt) has the same exegesis in different words and is the source of the reworking, not the text on the leaf. Heiric of Auxerre's homilies (Corpus Christianorum, Continuatio Mediaevalis 116B), searched by phrase, have none of the wording tested. Qualification on the recension (that is, the distinct version of the text): the leaf words the incipit of the steward homily differently from both witnesses ("solercius considerandum est" against "solerter attendendum est"), lacks sentences that one or the other has, and shares readings now with Liverani's print, now with Latin 3806. Which version of the collection it represents is not determined.
  • Transcription: source-assisted selected reading of fol. 4v column b, fol. 5r column b and fol. 5v column a; the image-only first reading covers fols. 4r, 4v and 5r and did not include fol. 5v. A research reader reread fol. 4v column b lines 5-23 and all of fol. 5v column a on enlarged crops, and fol. 5r column b at reduced size; fol. 4r and the rest of fol. 4v rest on the first reading. Fol. 5r column a (upper two thirds) and fol. 5v column b are abraded or stained and were not read. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared four passages: two on fol. 4 with Liverani's printed text as returned by Google Books search-within snippets, and two on fol. 5 with page images of Paris, BnF, Latin 3806, fols. 137r-138r, and with the snippets. The coordinator read the passage of fol. 4v column b quoted below ("In cuius tipo et figura ... Sporta quippe ex iunco et palma efficitur ... quasi pabisalma ... iuxta fontem scripturrarum") on the image and found it as transcribed. Liverani's edition was not seen as page images. No human review.

Differences: on fol. 4r the leaf lacks Liverani's sentences on the land of the living between "discubuerunt" and "Per foenum"; on fol. 5r it lacks the sentences that Latin 3806 has around the quotation of Matthew 12:50 and ends the homily at "scripta sunt", where Latin 3806 continues. In the steward homily it has "dicturus dominus", "de mamona iniquitatis", "Primum" and "domino suo" with Latin 3806 against Liverani, keeps "Homo natus est in ea. Et rursum" with Liverani where Latin 3806 omits it, and alone reads "solercius considerandum", "nobis dedit" and "de domo dauid". On fol. 4r-4v it also has the comparison of Scripture to a river "in quo et eleuas natat et agnus pedibus ambulat", which was found in Liverani only in another homily (p. 264) and stands in Haymo's own homily (Patrologia Latina 118, col. 639). The rubric number "Dominica X" agrees with Barré's inventory; Latin 3806 has "Dominica VIIII".

The collection is the one H. Barré calls the Liverani homiliary (Les homéliaires carolingiens de l'école d'Auxerre, Studi e Testi 225, 1962, pp. 113-122, inventory pp. 195-208): an anonymous homiliary on the Gospels transmitted as Homelie domni Aimonis, which Barré judges a later reworking of the homiliary of Haymo of Auxerre. It was printed by F. Liverani, Spicilegium Liberianum (Florence, 1863), from a manuscript of S. Maria Maggiore in Rome, under the name of Haymo of Halberstadt, an attribution no longer accepted. Barré distinguishes an Italian recension (Liverani's manuscript and others) from a French one, to which he assigns Paris, BnF, Latin 3806, a homiliary from Vilabertran in Catalonia of the 11th or 12th century according to the BnF notice. No page scan of Liverani was found; his text and Barré's inventory were read through Google Books search-within snippets, which give the printed wording and page but can drop or garble words. Latin 3806 was read from the digitised microfilm on Gallica. The pages of Latin 3806 used here are fol. 137r and fols. 137v-138r. Liverani's homily XLII occupies pp. 417-421, XLIV pp. 425-428 and XLV pp. 429-433 of his edition.

The manuscript, and what is not claimed

Three Fragmentarium records for NAL 2317 are leaves of one manuscript: this one, f. 6 (F-1szi) and f. 7 (F-vpe8). The catalogue describes them separately, with three different dates, and does not connect them. The grounds for joining them are the same small rounded Carolingian minuscule and spelling habits on all four leaves (abebat, ominum, scripturra, ut for uel, "&" inside words, ".;" at sentence ends), the same two-column layout of 31 to 33 lines with a written area that the catalogue itself measures as 236 x 184, 238 x 185 and 235 x 184 mm, and the content: homilies of one collection in its liturgical order, Barré nos. 55 to 57 on ff. 4-5 and nos. 67, 68 and 69 across ff. 6 and 7. The leaves are not consecutive. This is a reader's comparison of images, not a codicological examination.

Fols. 4 and 5 are conjoint but not consecutive: fol. 4v ends within the homily on Mark 8 and fol. 5r begins near the end of the homily two places later, so several leaves once lay between them. Fol. 4r carries the later dates "1572 a 1573" in the upper margin. No claim is made about the date or place of origin; the catalogue dates were not reassessed, and the spelling and the agreement with a Catalan witness only suggest, without showing, a southern origin. The recension is not determined: a full collation with Latin 3806, with Berlin, Staatsbibliothek, theol. lat. fol. 595, and with a page copy of Liverani would be needed, and the Catalan homiliary of Luculentius, another reworking of Haymo, was not tested because no searchable text was found. Whether these leaves are already known to students of the collection was not checked.
