F-h8oz — anonymous appeal procedure; Durantis parallel

Re-adjudicated 22 September 2026. The two-fragment pilot is included in this full-set review.

  • Identification: partial; medium confidence in the source connection. An anonymous procedural text on appeals has a close parallel to Guillelmus Durantis, Speculum iudiciale, II.3, De appellationibus, § Nunc breviter, nn. 13–14. Rewording and a different continuation prevent identification as that printed text. The compiling work and the relationship to Durantis remain unresolved.
  • Transcription: selected, source-assisted readings; accuracy unmeasured. The isolated image-only reader yielded little usable text. CATMuS also produced substantial recognition and segmentation errors. The excerpts below are the coordinator's subsequent image reading with the legacy report and Durantis available. They are not independently recovered full lines or specialist-approved text.
  • Verification: a manuscript crop and the actual edition image were compared. The coordinator checked the native crop of fol. [1]v column a, principally lines 2–3 and 9–13, against the left column of the printed leaf numbered 229, scan n483, nn. 13–15. This review does not verify the rest of either side or exclude all related works and manuscript recensions.

Fragmentarium record. Durantis, Lyon edition catalogued as 1578, scan n483. The archive's derivative filenames say 1576; the item metadata says 1578. This review identifies the exact scan without resolving that bibliographical discrepancy.

The new image check supports the old report's cautious classification. It does not turn its existing observation of a divergent continuation into a new discovery. That continuation is a useful challenge to a simple literal identification, but a recension, source, adaptation, or common source could still explain the relationship. A modern printed witness alone cannot resolve these possibilities.

What the new tools contributed

The isolated reader failed to recover enough distinctive wording to support a useful independent search. CATMuS produced 30 detections for approximately 23 visible physical lines: physical line 7 was split into detections 7–10, two of those empty; detection 15 omitted the start of physical line 12. These outputs remain preserved as failed or partial measurements, not filled in from the known answer.

A candidate-aware search using four source-assisted manuscript anchors recovered only sit datus auditor at the relevant Durantis locus, tolerating OCR fit for sit with one edit. The other anchors did not match in either the Durantis OCR or the selected Bellemera OCR. That is a limited retrieval result, not an exclusion of either work. The page image establishes the substantive comparison.

The previous broad “Ruled out” list is not carried forward as proved exclusions. The useful next research step remains comparison with manuscript states of the Speculum or related appeal treatises. The uncertain initial and absent citation formulas do not establish author or composition date; retain the catalogue dating pending specialist review.
