Status

IDENTIFIED (with a recension query) — Guillelmus Durantis, Speculum iudiciale, Lib. II partic. III, tit. De appellationibus, § Nunc breviter transcurrendum est intra quae tempora debeat appellatio exerceri, the passage on prosecuting an appeal at the curia (auditor obtained within the legal time; commissions "mandat dominus Papa magistro ... quod causam ... audiat et diffiniat"). Eleven consecutive lines of [1]v column a match the printed text (nn. 13-14 of the 1578 Lyon edition) allowing abbreviation. The rest of the leaf (all of [1]r, [1]v col. a from line 12, [1]v col. b) is on the same subject (appeals in election/provision cases; whether the appellee must prove the appeal to proceed against an absent appellant; witnesses of a chapter) with the Speculum's own cross-reference style ("ut dixi", "j. de proba.", "ar. j. de suc. ab intestato"), but is NOT in the printed vulgate at this point. Either the leaf carries a fuller/earlier recension of the Speculum or a text Durantis condensed; see queries below.

Not a Decretum/Liber Extra text and not a lemma gloss: there are no lemmata, the text is a continuous procedural treatise with "Sed pone quod ...", "Item ...", "Quid si ..." cases, each opened by a red paraph.

Layout

Two columns per side, c. 62 lines per column, column c. 6.9 cm wide, written space c. 19.5 x 15 cm; the leaf is trimmed close to the text on all sides (no marginal apparatus survives, no running titles). Red paraphs mark case openings; a 2-line red initial at [1]v col. a line 2 (S), col. a line c. 30 (I) and col. b line c. 20 (S). Later cursive-influenced textualis; Arabic-free, all numerals roman ("xx", "iiij. q. iiij"). The canvas labelled "[1]v" reads before or after "[1]r"? Content order is r col. b -> v col. a, so the catalogue's r/v assignment is consistent with the text (recto = canvas 0, the faded side).

Divergences: after line 11 the print continues "Sed pone, auditor, a quo appellatur, praefixit decem dies ..." (n. 15); the fragment instead continues "Si autem solus ipse appellans est presens, si vult quod procedatur altera parte absente, necesse habet ostendere de appellatione ..." and runs for c. 50 more lines (proof of the appeal by the party present, Nam quidam dicunt / Alii dicunt, "Predictum magis placet", Item quid de modo probandi, executores) before the witness material of col. b. Likewise [1]r col. b (c. 62 lines on election/provision appeals, "ad impediendam provisionem", "receptionem seu provisionem", procurator capituli) precedes line 1 where the print has only c. 15 lines (n. 12-13: "Est etiam et alius terminus qui datur hodie cum appellatur super electione, postulatione seu provisione ... Sed nunquid videtur tempora observasse qui intra ea auditorem impetravit ..."). The auditor's name in line 10 is an initial that reads "h." or "b.", not "G."; a zoomed crop (region 760,1730,1980,230 of canvas 1) does not resolve it.

Queries (not verdicts): (a) the leaf may carry the first recension of the Speculum (1271-76) or an expanded manuscript state, the printed vulgate being the 1289-91 revision with Johannes Andreae's and Baldus's additiones; (b) alternatively the leaf is a curial ordo/tractatus de appellationibus by an auditor "H."/"B." that Durantis condensed, substituting his own name. Either way the datable core (Gregory X's Statuimus of 1274 on election appeals, cited as "de elec. statuimus" and as a "constitutio", never as "libro sexto") fits a text written 1274-1298 and a 14th-century copy.

Ruled out

  • Decretum Gratiani, Liber Extra, Sextus, Clementinae text: no canon text or lemmata; procedural first-person treatise ("ut dixi", "servamus").
  • Glossa ordinaria / Rosarium / Innocent IV / Hostiensis / Johannes Andreae Novella / Panormitanus on X 2.28: form is wrong (no lemma commentary); Bellemera on X 2.28.59 Ut debitus (Google Books vK9mAAAAcAAJ etc.; archive.org bub_gb_M5GlkzSb4ccC, OCR l. 57200-57300) merely reports "Spe(culator) ubi supra ... sufficit auditorem impetrasse" — he is citing this very Speculum passage. Oldradus, Consilia (Google Books YNKsc_Io2qUC) likewise cites "Speculator in § Nunc breviter" for the point.
  • Hostiensis Lectura 1581 (archive.org bub_gb_SLHK_v-Z5S8C, partial OCR) and Friedberg II (archive.org BD1141952) checked for the phrases: absent.
  • Google Books queries "auditorem impetrare" "appellans"; "impugnandum appellationem" "absente"; "expectetur per viginti dies"; "mandat dominus papa magistro"; "audiat et diffiniat" "ad cuius instantiam"; "turpitudinem allegat" "appellans" "absente"; "fingeret" "non appellasse"; "predictum magis placet"; "post decisionem" "executores" "appellationis": only the Speculum and works quoting it hit; the divergent fragment phrases hit nothing.
  • archive.org full text: "auditorem impetrare" "appellans" -> only Bellemera; "expectetur per viginti dies" "appellans" and "procedi potest in partis absentia" -> 0.
