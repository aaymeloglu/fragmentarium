Readings and comparison details

F-rdns: Jacobus de Voragine, Legenda aurea, parts of the Elizabeth and Clement chapters

The parchment bifolium Stams Frg. 122, reused as the cover of an urbarium (a register of rents) for 1661-1666, comes from a copy of the Legenda aurea of Jacobus de Voragine, a collection of saints' lives arranged by the church year. One leaf carries parts of the chapter on Elizabeth of Hungary: her devotion in marriage, with the maid who woke her for prayer by touching her foot, and the young man who burned while she prayed for him and then entered the Friars Minor. The other leaf carries part of the chapter on Clement: the recognition of Clement's mother and brothers, the pirates who sold the brothers to Justina, the old astrologer who proves to be their father Faustinianus, Simon Magus's face put upon him, and the author's comment that the Itinerarium Clementis is an apocryphal book. In Graesse's edition (2nd ed., 1850) these are passages of cap. 168 (pp. 754-755 and 762) and cap. 170 (pp. 781-783). The identification covers the passages read; how the two Elizabeth pages relate to each other is unexplained (see the last section).

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 122: parchment and paper, one fragment item, 395 x 264 mm (measured as 332/395 x 189/264 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no date and no linked description, with two images labelled 122_01r and 122_01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: a parchment bifolium folded as a limp cover, with printed paper waste pasted on part of the inside, which accounts for "paper".

  • Identification: identified, high, for the passages read. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and sequence with Graesse's text over four passages, one on each page: the Elizabeth passage from "incultos homines doceret" to the maid and her husband's foot, the burning young man, the pirates and the old man's challenge to Peter's companions, and the recognition of Faustinianus through to the comment on the Itinerarium Clementis. The names Faustinianus, Aquila and Niceta also suggest the Pseudo-Clementine Recognitiones in Rufinus's Latin, but that work is the source the Legenda aurea condenses, not the containing work here: the copy has the Legenda aurea's condensed wording and its author's own comment that the Itinerarium is apocryphal and not to be accepted "in talibus". Elizabeth lives other than the Legenda aurea were not tested separately; the wording agrees with the Legenda aurea chapter, which stands next to Clement in the same manuscript. The copy differs from Graesse in small readings: "maxime ad pauperes" for "misericordiae ad pauperes", "patienter sustinens" for "patienter sufferens", "doceret" for "edoceret" and "statum" for "statum mentis", "Apinionem et arbionum" for "Apionem et Ambionem", "faustianus" and "faustino" for "Faustinianus" and "Faustiniano", "nullorum credendum" for "nullo modo credendum", "scripta fuerunt" for "scripta sunt", and the omission of "mutatis nominibus" in the pirates' story. The sentence on Elizabeth's shining face in prayer ("Saepe quoque ... prodibant") is absent, as Graesse reports it absent from the first printed edition.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading and the source-assisted reading. A research reader reread on the native images canvas 0 left, lines 1-12 and the last eight written lines; canvas 0 right, lines 6-19; canvas 1 right, lines 12-24; and the visible lower lines of canvas 1 left. All other lines rest on the first reading. Much of the inside face is stained or covered by printed waste, and the right page of the outside face is partly overwritten by the later title. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the four passages with Graesse on page images of the archive.org scan (pp. 754, 755, 762, 781, 782 and 783), and checked two Elizabeth sentences against Maggioni's critical text as given in Corpus Corporum. Checked again by the coordinator on the image: canvas 0, left column foot, region 1500,2900,2400,1100, from "Dixitque petrus faustino" through "Ideo itinarium clementis in quo hec scripta fuerunt liber apocrifus est nec ut quibusdam placet suscipiendus in talibus. Verumtamen ..." to "non sibi dixit ut simonem [ma]gum", read on the image against the page image of Graesse p. 783: agreement, with "faustino" for "Faustiniano". Coverage: on every page roughly the lower third of the ruled column carries no legible text, the upper part of the inside left page is under printed waste, and the Clement leaf's recto is matched only on its legible lines. No human review.

The differences are of single words and small omissions; the order of sentences agrees with Graesse wherever the copy is legible, and no sentence of Graesse is missing within a passage read except the Elizabeth sentence that Graesse himself reports absent from the first edition. The Itinerarium comment ties the Clement leaf to the Legenda aurea rather than to its source.

(Leipzig, 1850), archive.org legendaaureavulg00jacouoft: Elizabeth on p. 754, p. 755 and p. 762; Clement on p. 781, p. 782 and p. 783. The critical text, G. P. Maggioni (ed.), Legenda aurea (Florence: SISMEL, 1998), was read only for two Elizabeth sentences, as text in Corpus Corporum. Graesse's title page attributes the work to Jacobus de Voragine; the attribution is standard.

Leaf order, other items, and limits

On the Clement leaf the text runs from the inside right page (canvas 1, right, Graesse p. 781) to the outside left page (canvas 0, left, pp. 782-783), as recto and verso of one leaf. The Elizabeth pages do not fit that pattern. The outside right page (pp. 754-755) and the inside left page (p. 762) appear to stand back to back on the same leaf, yet the text between them is about 220 lines of Graesse, some three pages of this copy. This is unexplained: the copy might omit a long stretch of the chapter, or the inside left text might be on a separate piece under the printed waste. A further observation, not an explanation: the outside left column stops in mid-sentence after "non sibi dixit ut symonem [ma]gum" and the rest of its ruled column is blank, so that the copy was left unfinished or broken off at that point. The lower parts of the other three columns are also without legible text. Whether these blanks and the Elizabeth gap are connected is not known. Lifting the printed waste, or a raking-light view of the inside, would show more.

Relation to other Stams fragments. Stams Frg. 222 (F-bwv7), from the Legenda aurea chapters on Elizabeth and Cecilia (caps. 168-169), is a candidate for the same manuscript. On images photographed at the same scale (about 137 pixels per centimetre on the rulers), the line spacing here is 71 pixels (about 5.15 mm) against 71-72 pixels (about 5.2 mm) there, the written column is about 149 mm wide on both, and both use the same red paragraph marks in a similar small textualis. This sheet is smaller (395 x 264 mm against 427 x 314 mm), which fits trimming for the cover. The candidate rests on measurement and appearance only, without a palaeographer's comparison of the hands, and the text quantities do not fit the two bifolia standing in one gathering. Stams Frg. 219 (F-n2rx), another Legenda aurea, is ruled differently (about 6.4 mm line spacing) and is not shown to be from this manuscript.
