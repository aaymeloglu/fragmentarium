Jacobus de Voragine, Legenda aurea, one bifolium: opening of Francis (cap. 149) and end of Dionysius, Calixtus, opening of Leonard (caps. 153-155), Graesse 1850 pp. 662-665, 685-688; pasted over with printed waste of Peter Lombard's Sentences with Henricus de Gorinchem's conclusions (Basel: Nicolaus Kesler, 19 February 1492)

A research reader reread about 50 lines on both leaves and compared them with Graesse on page images (pp. 662-663, 687-688) and OCR (pp. 664-665, 685-686): sustained agreement, with two eye-skips supplied in the margin by a corrector, five reasons for Francis's name in the main text where Graesse has seven (the corrector adds the other two in the margin), and readings such as 'cum multis' for 'cum militibus'. From the same manuscript as F-n2rx (Frg. 219), confirmed by a Fragmentarium editor: same line spacing (about 7.2 mm), text block, script, rubrication and sewing stations, and its text falls before and after F-n2rx's Francis/Dionysius bifolium. A second research reader confirmed lines 12-27 of the Francis page against Graesse p. 663 on the page image. The printed sheets are Lombard's Sentences with Gorinchem's conclusions from Kesler's edition of 19 February 1492 (GW M32486), named by a Fragmentarium editor and confirmed on the Lisbon copy (BNP inc. 56): the distinction 1 piece agrees line for line, including the readings that set aside the 1489 printing; the turned-in strips are Legenda aurea caps. 125 and 131, possibly from another copy. The version of the Legenda aurea remains open.

Readings and comparison details

F-c7me: Jacobus de Voragine, Legenda aurea, chapters on Francis, Dionysius, Calixtus and Leonard

This parchment bifolium (a sheet folded to make two leaves) in Stams is from a copy of the Legenda aurea of Jacobus de Voragine, the collection of saints' lives arranged by the church year. One leaf has the opening of the chapter on Francis of Assisi; the other has the end of the chapter on Dionysius, the chapter on Pope Calixtus and the opening of the chapter on Leonard. In the chapter numbering of Graesse's edition (2nd ed., 1850) these are caps. 149 and 153-155, pp. 662-665 and 685-688. The bifolium was reused as a book cover, and its inner side is pasted over with waste sheets of Peter Lombard's Sentences with the conclusions of Henricus de Gorinchem, from the edition printed at Basel by Nicolaus Kesler on 19 February 1492. The bifolium is from the same manuscript as Stams Frg. 219 (F-n2rx), four bifolia of the Legenda aurea.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 242: parchment and paper, one fragment item, 454 x 332 mm (measured as 451/454 x 328/332 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description, with two images labelled 242_01r and 242_01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: one opened parchment bifolium, photographed on the written outer side and on the inner side, which is covered with printed paper and with strips of a second manuscript folded in at the edges.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the manuscript bifolium. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and order with Graesse's text in four passages, two on each leaf, with the Legenda aurea's own layout of an explanation of the saint's name, marked "De nomine" in red in the margin, followed by the legend. The chapter sequence Dionysius, Calixtus, Leonard is the book's own order. Abridgements and sermon collections built on the Legenda aurea were not tested; the full name explanations and the continuous legend text favour the work itself. The copy differs from Graesse in two places where the scribe's eye skipped between repeated words and a corrector supplied the missing text in the margin (Francis: "unde dicitur in legenda ... unde dicitur in legenda"; Leonard: "in pectore ... in pectore"). In the explanation of Francis's name the main text counts five reasons where Graesse has seven, and renumbers them; the corrector supplies the other two in the margin, the second ("officii divulgandi") beside line 4 and the sixth and seventh ("[t]erroris in demonu(m) fuga[tione] / + ... r(ati)one securitatis ... (et) op(er)um p(er)fectione", faded) beside line 11. The main text also lacks "in securitatem" at the end of the name explanation. Other differences are of wording: "mynisterij" for "opinio mysterii", "feritas" for "veritas", "kalixtus papa", "cum multis" for "cum militibus", "penitus" for "protinus", "non [pari] uidebant parentes" for "non de pari ludebant hujus parentes". Graesse's own note records that the first printed edition lacks both the stretch of reasons and "in securitatem", the same two places, so the passage varies among copies; which version of the Legenda aurea this copy follows is not determined. The printed sheets are identified to the edition (see below).
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading of 31 October 2026 and the source-assisted reading. A research reader reread on the native images lines 1-12 of the right page and lines 1-11 and 24-33 of the left page of the outer side, lines 1-9 and 14-23 of the left page of the inner side, the visible pieces of its right page, two turned-in strips and one printed piece. All other lines rest on the first reading, and much of the inner side is under paper. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the Francis opening (outer side, right page, lines 1-12) and the Calixtus and Leonard passages (outer side, left page, lines 1-11 and 24-33) with page images of Graesse pp. 662-663 and 687-688, and the Francis passage on the inner side and the visible end of Dionysius with Graesse's archive.org OCR text for pp. 664-665 and 685-686. A second research reader independently read lines 12-27 of the outer side, right page (canvas 0, region 4200,1800,2500,1700), which the report had not transcribed, before opening Graesse, though it knew from the report that the page opens the Francis life. That reader compared them with the page image of p. 663 (page image) and found continuous agreement with copyist-level variants ("commutauit et transformauit" for "subito transformavit", "causa orationis" for "causa devotionis", "ab inceptis" for "a coeptis"); that reader also read the margin note beside line 11 and a second printed piece. The first reader compared the bifolium physically with F-n2rx and compared one printed piece with page images of the 1487 and 1489 Kesler editions. After a Fragmentarium editor named the 1492 edition, the same piece was compared with page images of that edition (see below).

The printed sheets

The paper pasted over the inner side is waste from an edition of Peter Lombard's Sentences (the standard twelfth-century textbook of theology) "cum conclusionibus Henrici Gorichem", in the page layout of the editions printed at Basel by Nicolaus Kesler. The pieces include Lombard's prologue ("... in gazophilatiu[m] ... mittere: ardua [scandere] ... opus ultra vires ..."), entries from the list of chapter titles of book I ("Di. XIIII. De gemina processione spūssancti", "Utrum spūssanctus debeat dici ingenitus", "Di. XX"), and the summary of book I, distinction 1, region 5420,1080,880,1080 of canvas 1, read rotated (image): "... In spe/ciali v(er)o sententia magistri stat in tribus proposit[io]/nibus: quarū prima est. Totaꝫ sacram paginam [con]/stat specialiter circa res (et) signa versari ...". A research reader compared this piece with page images of the Kesler editions of 23 May 1487 (MDZ bsb11191972) and 29 November 1489 (MDZ bsb00038309) and with the OCR text of the 1502 edition (archive.org ARes04322). The 1487 edition reads "intentio magistri" and breaks its lines differently, and the 1502 edition breaks "In speciali / vero", so neither is the source. In this piece the 1489 edition breaks every visible line at the same word ("termi/nos", "instituentis: sed / quasdam", "aliqd / significātes", "materijs / tam", "res / absolutas"), but it reads "significātes. cuiusmōi ... cum" where the fragment has "significātes: cuiusmodi ... cuꝫ". A second research reader compared a prologue piece (canvas 1, region 5100,3000,1100,1450) with the 1489 edition's page image (scan 7): the page contents and nearly all line contents agree, but hyphenation, word breaks and spelling differ ("ostende-/re" against "osten-/dere", "ape-/rire" against "abdita / apire", "quas / bigas" against "flagitātiuȝ. / q̄s bigas", "charitas" against "caritas"). The sheets are therefore from a printing of the same layout, page for page, but not the 1489 printing. A Fragmentarium editor reviewed the record in November 2026 and identified the sheets as Kesler's edition of 19 February 1492 (GW M32486, ISTC ip00495000). The distinction 1 piece was then compared with the Lisbon copy (Biblioteca Nacional de Portugal, inc. 56; purl.pt/32686), image 18, right column (image): every visible line breaks at the same word, and it has the readings that set the 1489 printing aside, "significātes:cuiusmodi" and "cuꝫ", as well as "Oīs doctrina" and "ordinatiōe". The chapter-title list and prologue pieces were not compared with an edition on page images.

Leaf order, other items, and limits

On the outer side the right page is the recto of the first leaf and the left page is the verso of the second; the inner side shows the first leaf's verso (left) and the second leaf's recto (right). Text runs from the Francis recto to its verso, and from the end of Dionysius on the second leaf's recto (inner side, right) to Calixtus and Leonard on its verso (outer side, left). The leaves are not consecutive: about 20 pages of Graesse's text separate them.

Same manuscript as F-n2rx. A Fragmentarium editor reviewed the record in November 2026 and confirmed it. The physical evidence: both records are photographed at the same scale; the line spacing measured on the images is 98 native pixels here and 97-99 on F-n2rx (about 7.2 mm), the text block is about 16 x 23.5 cm in both, with 33 to 34 lines, the same script, the same red initials, paragraph marks and marginal "De nomine" rubrics, and sewing holes along the fold at matching heights; the sheets measure 454 x 332 and 442 x 330 mm. F-n2rx's second bifolium has a later part of the Francis chapter (the seraph and the stigmata) on one leaf and the opening of Dionysius on the other, so this bifolium's text falls before and after it, which fits this sheet lying outside it in the same gathering (a group of nested folded sheets). The number of leaves between could not be counted from Graesse's text, because F-n2rx's Francis leaf leaves out long passages that Graesse prints.

Other items: on the outer side, a later display line in brown across the head of the right page, an oval paper label "VI / L", a later large number written over lines 2-3, pencil shelfmarks and sewing slits. On the inner side, besides the printed sheets, strips of a second manuscript are folded in along the edges. They also carry the Legenda aurea: "[..]pueru(n)t (et) singuli singulas meruisse se letati sunt. ¶ Theodolina regina" (cap. 125, the Beheading of John the Baptist; canvas 1, region 1400,180,2200,200) and "Ille igitur excitatus uita(m) mutauit (et) relig[ionem] ... ¶ Apud siciliam anno d(omi)ni .d.xxx[..] ... [Ful]berthus carnotensis ep(iscopu)s. qui ..." (cap. 131, the Nativity of Mary; canvas 1, region 860,2900,440,1250), compared with Graesse's OCR text only. Whether they come from the same manuscript as the bifolium is not determined.

Not claimed: date, origin, which version of the Legenda aurea the copy follows, or whether the turned-in strips come from the same manuscript as the bifolium.
