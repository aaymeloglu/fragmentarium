Frg. 55 (F-py5e)

Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Fief reverse: the guardians ([..] zu Jaufenburg, Jakob von Brandis zu Leonburg, [Ca]sparn von Montanj, Pfleger zu Schlanders, and Casparn Fueger von Fr[..]) of the children of the late Cristoff Cr[..]ssen(?) and Georg Philipp von Schrofenstein acknowledge enfeoffment by the abbot of Stams (name not read) with 'Der hof der da leit zum Steg zu ober Lana zu Rechtem(?) Lehen', with the duty to escort the abbot from Finstermünz with six or eight horses; 29th day, month and year lost [1546 or later, probably 1546-1558]

The regest is read on the image: the action (fief reverse), the abbot of Stams as lord, the Schrofenstein children (Magdalena, Vo?l/t?iri(?), Margretha) through their named guardians and a Lehenträger, the farm zum Steg at Oberlana, the escort 'Von der Vinstern Müntz vntz auff den Ve/orren(?)' on pain of forfeiture, and 'Beschehen am Neunvnndzwaintzigisten tag'. Two fresh readers, given 21 shuffled neutral crops of this document and of Frg. 166 with no transcription, confirmed Brandis zu Leonburg, Cristoff and Georg Philipp von Schrofenstein, the children's names, Montanj, Fueger and the horses; the coordinator reread every disagreement on the image, leaving the abbot's name unread, the first father's second name as 'Cr[..]ssen(?)', 'Rechtem Lehen' and 'Jaufenburg' probable, and confirming the 29th. The month and year are lost; the window (1546 or later, most likely 1546-1558; 1562-1564 under Maximilian II not excluded, since the wards may still have been minors) is supplied from the royal title and, for its lower bound, from a Google Books snippet on the 1546 extinction of the Schrofenstein male line. No outside record of the act was found (monasterium.net Stams regests, Google Books, MDZ); no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-py5e: The guardians of the children of Cristoff Cr[..]ssen(?) and Georg Philipp von Schrofenstein acknowledge enfeoffment by the abbot of Stams with the farm zum Steg at Oberlana, 29th day, month and year lost [1546 or later, probably 1546-1558]

This parchment strip in the archive of the Cistercian abbey of Stams is the lower part of a German fief reverse (Lehenrevers: the vassal's counter-letter acknowledging a grant of a fief and accepting its conditions). The opening is lost; the surviving text begins with a guardian "zu Jaufenburg Ritter", named together with "dem Edlen Vesten herren Jacoben von Brandiß zu Leonburg", both councillors of the "Römischer Khünigclicher Mayestat" (the Roman king), as guardians of the children of the late "Cristoff Cr[..]ssen(?) von Schroffenstain", namely Magdalena and Vo?l/t?iri(?). With them are "[Ca]sparn(?) von Montanj", the king's "Pfleger zu Schlanders" (district officer at Schlanders), and "Casparn Fueger von Fr[..]", guardians ("Gerhaben") of the children of the late "Jeorg Phillippen von Schrofenstain", namely Margretha [...]. One of the guardians, whose name is lost, writes as their appointed "Lehentrager" (the person who receives a fief on behalf of minors or a group). He states ("Bekhennen") that "Der Erwirdig vnnd Geistlich herr. Herr [..]y[..]en(?) Abbt", whose name is not read, for himself, his convent and successors, has granted to them as guardians, according to his sealed fief letter, "Der hof der da leit zum Steg zu ober Lana zu Rechtem(?) Lehen": the farm "zum Steg" at Oberlana near Meran, probably as a proper fief. The abbey is named in line 10, "Abbt zu Stambs". The condition is that the guardians, on behalf of their wards, shall escort the abbot of Stams and his people ("belaitten") "Von der Vinstern Müntz vntz auff den Ve/orren(?)", from Finstermünz to probably the Fern pass, when they travel "mit Sechs oder Acht pferdten" (with six or eight horses), and something is owed once each year to him or his chaplains. If the guardians fail in this, the fief falls back to the abbey and its successors, "widerumb ledig vnnd loß". The Lehenträger seals for himself and his fellow guardians. The dating clause reads "Beschehen am Neunvnndzwaintzigisten tag" (on the 29th day); the month and year stood on the cut last line and are lost.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 55, one parchment item, 322 x 82 mm (measured 319/322 x 81/82 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), online since 18 September 2021, with the title "x" and no date, type or description. The image labelled 01r is the text face and 01v the back. Nothing in the record is wrong; it lacks the regest (a summary of a document's issuer, parties, action and date). The modern note on the back gives "1572 Jun. 9.", the date of the charter this strip was used to back, not of this deed.

  • Identification: identified. The regest is read on the image: the action (a fief reverse), the lord (the abbot of Stams, name not read), the vassals (the Schrofenstein children, Magdalena, Vo?l/t?iri(?) and Margretha [...], through named guardians: [..] zu Jaufenburg, Jakob von Brandis zu Leonburg, [Ca]sparn von Montanj and Casparn Fueger von Fr[..]), the object ("Der hof ... zum Steg zu ober Lana"), the escort condition with its forfeiture clause and the day (the 29th). The protocol with the issuer heading is lost and several names rest on uncertain readings, listed here. Not read: the abbot's name (a capital like the D of "Der" followed by about five minims), the lead guardian's forename, the Lehenträger's name, the second element of the first father's name ("Cr[a/i]ssen", "Craisen" or "Crishen"), the place after "Fueger von Fr[..]", and the last two words of the tenure ("Rechtem Lehen" probable). Lost: the month and year. Supplied, not read: the date window. The royal title read on the image fits Ferdinand I as Roman king (1531-1558), Maximilian II (1562-1564) and Rudolf II (1575-1576). Both fathers are "weillennd" (late). The lower bound, 1546, is the extinction of the Schrofenstein male line with Georg Philipp, known only from a snippet of Das Trautson-Auersperg-Archiv (1996). So the act falls in 1546 or later, most likely in 1546-1558; 1562-1564 is not excluded, since the wards may still have been minors then. Outside sources name the brothers Christoph Ehrenreich and Georg Philipp, sons of Rueland, who left only daughters; "Ehrenreich" is not on the image and is a lead only. The Jaufenburg knight is probably Christoph Fuchs von Fuchsberg und Jaufenburg, Statthalter in 1542 (Beiträge zur Familiengeschichte Tirols, 1954, snippet); only "zu Jaufenburg Ritter" is read. "Fueger von Fr[iedberg]" is plausible but not read. "Fern" is an interpretation of "Ve/orren", not a reading. No outside record of the act was found.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers the text face and the back; its reader had no proposed identification. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's (Claude) reread of all fifteen lines at native resolution, in four columns by two bands, with detail crops of the abbot's name, the fathers, the end of line 4, line 10 and the cut last line, then revised after the word check described below. Against the first reading it gives "zum Steg" ("hin Steg"), "Rechtem(?) Lehen" ("Erblehen"), "Vinstern Müntz" ("Künstern Muntz") and "So solt der" ("Desolder"), and reads the barred capitals (E, K, R) as ornament, not deletion. Line 16 shows only the tops of letters. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: there is no outside text of the act to compare. For the word check, two fresh readers who had seen no transcription were each given the same 21 neutral crops, twelve from this document and nine from Stams Frg. 166 (F-c1cn), shuffled into a different order for each reader, with a neutral question for each crop (for example "What is the personal name before 'Abbt'?"). The coordinator reread every disagreement on the image. Both readers read "Jacoben von Brand(i)ß zu Leonburg", "Cristoff", "von Schrofenstain", "[Jö]rg Phillip(p/s)en von Schrofenstain", "Magdalena", "Margretha", "Fueger"/"Fugger" followed by "von Fr[..]" or "von Grus", "Montanj"/"Azomtanij" and "Sechs oder Acht pferdten". For the second guardian's forename one reader gave "Casparn", the other "Cassian" with "Casparn" as alternative. For the office one reader read "Pfleger zu Schlanders", the other "zu Schlauonien"; the coordinator's reread gives Schlanders. Both readers read the abbot's name as a D-like capital and minims ("Dyenen", "Dynnen"), which is why it is left unread. One reader read "Neunvnndzwainzigisten tag"; the other was unsure of the tens element; the coordinator reread it on the image as 29th. The object line was read "zum Steg zu ober Lana zu Hochenleben" and "... Otag zu ober Laun zu Hochenwalhey", so "zum Steg zu ober Lana" stands and "Rechtem Lehen" is probable. The place in line 1 was read "Jüntenburg" and "Fünstenburg", so "Jaufenburg" is probable. The guardian identities and the 1546 lower bound were seen only as Google Books snippets. Not consulted: J. Tarneller, Die Hofnamen des Burggrafenamtes (for the farm zum Steg), the published Stams rent registers, the backed charter (Lade O XXXIV n. 20) and the Tiroler Landesarchiv fief records. No human review. The coordinator reread the object clause (line 8, canvas 0, native box 4300,2610-6280,2725) on the image: "Der hof der da leit zum Steg zu ober Lana" is clear; the last two words are cursive and only probably "Rechtem Lehen".

Physical notes and limits

  • One document: fifteen full lines and the tops of a sixteenth in one German chancery Kurrent hand, written right across the strip. The top is cut, so the protocol (the issuer's opening) is lost; the bottom is cut through the line that held the month and year. Both side margins survive. The sealing area is lost, so whether this is the sealed original cannot be confirmed; the first-person sealing clause and the absence of deletions fit an engrossed original.
  • The back, canvas 1 (image), has modern notes only: "Diente bis 1963 als Rücken zu Urkunde / arch. Lade O XXXIV n. 20 / 1572 Jun. 9." and the pencil "Stams, STA, Frg. 55v". The strip was used until 1963 as the backing of the Stams charter in drawer O XXXIV no. 20, dated 9 June 1572.
  • Not claimed: the month and year (1546 or later, most likely 1546-1558, from the royal title and an outside snippet), the abbot's name, the identity of the Jaufenburg knight, the full names of the first father and of Fueger's seat, the location of the farm zum Steg beyond Oberlana, the end point of the escort route, and what was owed yearly to the abbot or his chaplains.
