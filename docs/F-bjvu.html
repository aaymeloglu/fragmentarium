Bifolium, two columns, red-underlined lemmata and red marginal source sigla (ag, c) matching PL's [Aug.], [Cassiod.]; a research reader collated one passage from each face with the PL 191 page images (cols. 949-950, 963) and found sustained agreement, with small differences such as a compressed repeated clause, 'per quod' for 'per quem' and no 'hic' in 'Talis locutio est'. Gilbert of Poitiers's Media glosatura, read in Cambridge, Queens' College MS 18 at Ps 104:12 and 104:14-15, lacks passages the fragment shares with Lombard; this rests on one witness and two verses. The coordinator verified on the images that the text runs across the join into F-qslo (Stams Frg. 213), 'si non moy-|-ses electus eius', so the two bifolia are one manuscript, F-bjvu nested inside F-qslo. A machine-read manuscript text search (CoMMA) ranks Lombard manuscripts highest and is supporting only. A typed label records use as the wrapper of Stams VIII/F-37 (Fasciculus temporum 1481 and Glareanus, Liber de asse 1550).

Readings and comparison details

F-bjvu: Peter Lombard, Commentarius in Psalmos, on Ps 104:5-19 and 105:6-22

Stams, Frg. 187 is a parchment bifolium (a sheet folded to make two leaves) from a two-column copy of Peter Lombard's commentary on the Psalms, the "Magna glosatura", completed about 1158. One leaf carries Ps 104:5-19 and the other Ps 105:6-22 (Vulgate numbering), which is PL 191, cols. 948-952 and 961-966. The psalm lemmata (the quoted verses being explained) are underlined in red. Red source marks in the margins, "ag" for Augustine and "c" for Cassiodorus, each with a red line showing how far the borrowed passage runs, match the bracketed source notes of the printed edition. The bifolium belongs to the same manuscript as Stams Frg. 213 (F-qslo, report) and sits immediately inside it in the quire.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 187, parchment, one fragment item, 539 x 349 mm (measured 407/539 x 218/349 mm by Claudia Sojer), online since 18 September 2021. It has two images (01r, 01v), the title "x", and no date, description or identification. The bifolium was used flat as a limp binding wrapper, with its corners cut and its top and bottom edges turned in. A typed label pasted on face r reads "diente als Einband zum VIII/F - 37 Nr. 396 / Fasciculus temporum Cartusiani et Liber de Asse, Loriti / 1481 - 1550". So it covered Stams volume VIII/F-37 (no. 396), which held the Fasciculus temporum of Werner Rolevinck, the Carthusian (edition of 1481), bound with Heinrich Loriti (Glareanus), Liber de asse (1550). Two pencil notes on the label are uncertain: one is probably "Entnommen" (removed), and the other ends in what may be a year. The wrapper also carries "B 98" in ink on the spine, a paper slip with "F" at the head of the spine, and pencil "Stams, STA, Frg. 187r" and "187v".

  • Identification: identified, high confidence. The basis is sustained agreement in wording, sequence and source marks with Lombard's text in PL 191, on both faces. Face r has the division and Augustinian matter at Ps 104:12 ("Tercia pars. Dixerat deum gratis esse colendum ... Exequitur historiam"). Face v has the gloss on Ps 105:13-15 ("Illi vero festinaverunt fieri beati in terrenis, in quibus est ydria cupiditatis ... Talis locutio est ut supra"), with "ag", "ag", "c", "ag" in the margin where PL has [Aug., Cassiod., Aug.] and [Aug.].
    • Gilbert of Poitiers's Media glosatura, which Lombard reworked, was compared in one manuscript, Cambridge, Queens' College MS 18, at two verses (Ps 104:12 and 104:14-15). Gilbert supplies the frame ("Pars tertia. Exequitur historiam et primo breviter tangit quod in egyptum transierunt"). He has none of the Augustinian passage the fragment carries ("Dixerat deum gratis esse colendum ... iustificationes et legem dei requirunt"), and he words Ps 104:14-15 differently ("Hec verba non sunt historia, sed intelligendum est", with Pharaoh before Abimelech). The fragment follows Lombard ("Hec verba licet in historia non sint tamen", Abimelech before Pharaoh), so Lombard is the containing work. Augustine's Enarrationes is Lombard's main source and not the text here.
    • A search of machine-read manuscript text (CoMMA) ranked manuscripts of Lombard's commentary highest for six fragment passages, among them Troyes 141 and 58, Saint-Omer 32 and Reims 153. This supports the identification, but it is machine-read text that was not checked on the images.
    • The fragment differs from PL in a few places, listed under Textual evidence. The main one is that it compresses PL's repeated clause on the "subsidia temporalis vitae" to "scilicet que subsidia temporalia prestitit".
  • Transcription: source-assisted. The image-only first reading was made with the binding label masked out. It covered all eight columns but left many lines uncertain. In the source-assisted reading, a research reader reread three columns on the native images. These are face r, left leaf, column a (about 35 lines); face v, left leaf, column a (20 lines); and the first five lines of face v, right leaf, column a. The reader also reread the last lines of face v, left leaf, column b at the join with F-qslo. The other columns rest on the first reading, checked against the edition for their order only. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared two passages, one from each face, with the PL 191 page images (cols. 949-950 and 963), and read Gilbert's text on the Queens' College images. The same reader tested the link with F-qslo on both joins and against ruler-calibrated line spacing. Checked again by the coordinator on the image: verified the join with F-qslo on the images. The end of the inner column on F-bjvu's verso, canvas 1, native box 2400,3350,3600,3950, reads "Et dixit ut disperderet eos. si non moy-". The F-qslo verso column, canvas 1, native box 4700,150,6200,700, continues "-ses electus eius stetisset in confractione ...", PL 191 col. 965 (page image). Gaps: Gilbert is tested in one witness at two verses only.

The differences are few. The fragment compresses PL's repeated clause "Dicit enim quae subsidia temporalis vitae praestitit patribus et filiis eorum, tam carne quam fide" to "scilicet que subsidia temporalia prestitit". It has "quoniam autem" where PL has "quoniam ante"; the abbreviation could also stand for "ante". It omits "hic" in "Talis locutio est hic". At the head of canvas 1 it has "per quod vocabat" where PL has "per quem vocabat". The rest is spelling (ydria, hystoria, Tercia).

Edition: Petrus Lombardus, Commentarium in Psalmos, in J.-P. Migne, Patrologia Latina 191 (Paris, 1854). The passages are on archive.org patrologiaecur191mign: cols. 947-948 on n469, cols. 949-950 on n470, cols. 961-962 on n476 and cols. 963-964 on n477. The two collated passages were read on the page images. The running heads name "Petri Lombardi" and "Commentarium in Psalmos", and the work's authorship is secure. Gilbert of Poitiers's gloss was read in Cambridge, Queens' College MS 18, vol. 2, ff. 57v-58r (Cambridge Digital Library, MS-QUEENS-00018-AST, images 116-117).

Leaf order, the sister bifolium, and what is not claimed

The text gives the leaf order, and the sister bifolium shows where they sat in the quire:

1. F-qslo leaf 1, Ps 103:29-104:5. 2. F-bjvu leaf 1: face v, right leaf, then face r, left leaf, Ps 104:5-19. 3. Lost inner leaves, Ps 104:19-105:6. 4. F-bjvu leaf 2: face r, right leaf, then face v, left leaf, Ps 105:6-22. 5. F-qslo leaf 2, Ps 105:22-43.

Judging from the length of the missing text, about two or three inner leaves are lost.

The text runs on across both joins:

  • F-qslo leaf 1 ends "... in altitudine [sue deitatis] que auolat ab intellectu ... nemo", and F-bjvu leaf 1 opens "[..]re? solem in rota sua dico uobis minoribus [sa]ltem. Mementote" (PL 191 col. 947D-948A). At most a word or two is hidden by damage at the end of F-qslo's line.
  • At the other join the word "moy|ses" is split between F-bjvu and F-qslo.

The two photographs are at different scales, so each was measured against its own ruler. Both bifolia have two columns of about 40 lines. The line spacing measures 6.7-6.9 mm on F-bjvu and 6.2-7.0 mm on F-qslo, where damage makes the figures noisier. The written columns are about 27 cm high and 10 cm wide on both, an approximate measurement from the overviews. The red underlining and the red "ag" and "c" marks are the same on both, and to the eye so is the hand.

Not claimed:

  • The date and origin of the manuscript. The hand looks protogothic to early gothic, which was not examined palaeographically.
  • Whether F-qslo also came from volume VIII/F-37; its record has no label.
  • The textual place of this copy among Lombard manuscripts.
  • Any identification of the other six columns beyond their order against the edition.
