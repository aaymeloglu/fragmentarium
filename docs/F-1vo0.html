Frg. 210 (F-1vo0)

Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Matins lessons of a twelve-lesson office: for St Barnabas, Gregory the Great, Homiliae in Evangelia 27, 1-3 (PL 76, 1205-1206), then the Gospel John 15:12-16 and the Barnabas collect; for Sts Peter and Paul, Ps.-Augustine, Sermo Caillau-Saint-Yves I, 58 (end; PLS 2, 1009-1010) and Maximus of Turin (attrib.), Homilia 70 'Beatissimorum apostolorum' (PL 57, 399-402).

A research reader reread lines on all four faces and compared them with page images of PLS 2, col. 1010, PL 57, cols. 399-402 and PL 76, cols. 1205-1206. There is sustained agreement in wording and order with all three: a full column of the Ps.-Augustine sermon, about twenty lines of the Maximus homily and sixteen lines of Gregory. The leaves have some better readings than PLS (Habet profecto mysterium nobilitas ista penarum; probabatur) and liturgical adaptations of the sermon's ending, and they differ from PL in small points (magisterium tam, Quanti, cum diligendo). The rubric for lesson xi is visible but that for lesson xii is not seen, so lesson xii is inferred. The Barnabas collect was matched only to later printed missals. The two lines after the heading for the Nativity of John the Baptist are not identified. Twelve lessons with the Gospel written out after them fit monastic, probably Cistercian, use; that is inference, not tested against a Cistercian book.

Readings and comparison details

F-1vo0: Gregory the Great, Homiliae in Evangelia 27; Ps.-Augustine, Sermo Caillau-Saint-Yves I, 58; Maximus of Turin (attrib.), Homilia 70, Matins lessons for St Barnabas and for Sts Peter and Paul

Two parchment leaves at Stams come from the Matins lessons (the readings of the night office) of a breviary or office lectionary. The office has twelve lessons, as in monastic use. One leaf has the last lessons for St Barnabas (11 June): the red rubric "Undecima." is visible, and the rubric for lesson xii, which would fall in the hole or the cut column, is not seen, so lesson xii is inferred from the Gospel that follows. They are Gregory the Great's homily 27 on the Gospels, §§1-3 (PL 76, 1205-1206), on John 15:12-16. After the last lesson the leaf writes out the Gospel John 15:12-16 and the Barnabas collect Quaesumus omnipotens Deus ut beatus Barnabas apostolus. Then comes the heading for the Nativity of St John the Baptist. The other leaf has lessons ii-v for Sts Peter and Paul (29 June). Lessons ii-iii are the end of the anonymous sermon In natali apostolorum Petri et Pauli printed as Ps.-Augustine, Caillau-Saint-Yves I, 58 (PLS 2, 1009-1010). From lesson iv the leaf has the homily Beatissimorum apostolorum Petri et Pauli inseparabilem fidem (PL 57, 399-402), printed as Maximus of Turin, Homily 70.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 210: parchment, two fragment items, 399 x 248 mm (measured on Frg. 210_1 as 349/399 x 182/248 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", online since 18 September 2021, with no date or linked description and four images. The record does not name the text. Pencil notes on the leaves read "29.VI." and "11 Juni", the dates of the two feasts, and "aus I/70 ... 1670" and "aus I/71 ... 1670", which record the archival volumes the leaves were taken from. The physical description fits the images.

  • Identification: identified, high. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and order with three printed texts, read on page images. The agreement runs over 24 lines of the Peter and Paul sermon (canvas 1, column b) and its surviving line-ends in column a, 20 lines of the Maximus homily and 16 lines of Gregory, besides shorter line-ends. The Peter and Paul lessons are not one text: the rubric "Quarta." with a large initial begins the Maximus homily at its incipit, right after the end of the other sermon. Against PLS the leaf has Habet profecto mysterium nobilitas ista penarum (PLS Habet perfectum mysterium nobilium status poenarum), probabatur (PLS probatur), Non sum itaque dignus ait (PLS Non sum, inquit, dignus), festinat (PLS festinare) and the liturgical ending prestante domino qui vivit et regnat (PLS praesta, Domine, qui vivis et regnas). Against PL 57 it has magisterium tam (PL et magisterium tamen), Quanti igitur meriti (PL Quantum) and posset (PL possit). Against PL 76 it has in odio repente exarscimus (PL in odium ... exardescimus), cum diligendo (PL dum) and possimus (PL possumus). After "In nat(ali) sancti Joh(annis) bap(tiste)" the leaf begins an item "S[...]ollempni[...] / [...]tas nobis", which is not identified.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers all four faces. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's reread at reading size. It covers canvas 1, both columns in full; canvas 0, column a lines 1-20 and column b line-beginnings; canvas 2, column a lines 15-27 and column b lines 1-16; and canvas 3, the last nine lines of column b. Other lines rest on the first reading. The reread corrected the first reading in several places (Quos ... visitavit, imperito, ut purgatiore, quid ergo potissimum, inuicti). On canvas 1, column a, the line after Ecce tran[siit] is faint and does not agree with PLS. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the reread lines with PLS 2, col. 1010 (page image n510), PL 57, cols. 399-402 (page images n203-204) and PL 76, cols. 1205-1206 (page image n602), all on Internet Archive scans of the Migne volumes. The Barnabas collect was matched only against full-text hits in later printed missals, and it is a common formula. A second research reader chose canvas 2 (210-2r), column a, lines 1-13, native box 700,1100,1650,3200, which the report had not transcribed, read it on the image and then compared it with PL 76, col. 1205 (page image). All thirteen line-ends follow PL 1205 A-C in sequence, and no textual variant is established; "dominicis" carries an interlinear correction "ci". That reader recognised the homily from memory partway through, before opening PL. Not consulted: the CCSL editions of Maximus (CCSL 23) and Gregory (CCSL 141), and any medieval breviary or lectionary on the page.

The differences are the variants listed under Identification, the ordinary spellings (penarum, celestis) and the liturgical adaptation of the sermon's ending. Some of them are better readings than the printed PLS text, which was edited from Monte Cassino, Archivio dell'Abbazia, cod. 12. They do not bear on the identification. On canvas 0, column b, the line-beginnings "[...] eum celum. rapt[...]" suggest that the leaf quotes 2 Cor 12:2 more fully than PL 57 does; the line-ends are lost.

Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

  • Two separate leaves, two columns of 29-30 lines, red rubrics and initials. Each was trimmed and folded as binding waste, so one column on each face is cut. Leaf 2 has a large hole in the lower centre, and the numeral "xxxiii" stands in its upper margin.
  • On leaf 1 the text runs from canvas 1 (pencilled "210-1v") to canvas 0 ("210-1r"), so the pencilled r/v labels are reversed for that leaf. Leaf 2 runs from canvas 2 to canvas 3.
  • The twelve-lesson Matins and the Gospel written out after the last lesson fit monastic use. Stams is a Cistercian house, so the book was probably Cistercian, but this is inference and has not been tested against a Cistercian breviary or lectionary.
  • Not claimed: the date, the origin, the identity of the item after the John the Baptist heading, or any relation to a surviving manuscript.
