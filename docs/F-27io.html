Readings and comparison details

F-27io: Albertus Magnus, De anima, II tract. 3, end of chapter 14 to the opening of chapter 18

This parchment leaf at Stams carries a continuous stretch of Albertus Magnus's commentary De anima, book II, tractate 3, from the end of chapter 14 to the opening of chapter 18, with the rubricated chapter headings xv to xviii: chapter 15, why nothing can be seen through a vacuum, and on seeing in a mirror; chapter 16, on the sight of luminous things; chapter 17, on hearing, and first on things that sound well and badly; chapter 18, how sound is generated in air and in water. The text follows the Borgnet edition, Opera omnia vol. 5, pp. 261-265, in wording and sequence.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Frg. 76, title "x", parchment, one fragment item, 243 x 181 mm (measured on the fragment as 190/243 x 140/181 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), no date given, online since 18 September 2021, with two images. It has no description and does not name the text. A modern pencil note in the upper margin of the face labelled 76v reads "aus Einband zu Homer Odyssee, Dillingen 1627" with "III d/M 44", recording the binding the leaf was taken from; pencil labels "Stams, STA, Frg. 76r" and "76v" mark the faces.

  • Identification: identified, high. Sustained agreement in wording and sequence with Borgnet's text of De anima II.3.14-18 on both faces, across all four columns, including the chapter numbers and titles of the rubrics (xv, xvi "de visu lucencium", xvii "de auditu et primo de bene sonantibus et male sonantibus", xviii "Qualiter sonus generatur in aere et in aqua"). The work is undisputedly Albert's. The leaf is the text itself, not an abridgement: the differences from Borgnet are ordinary scribal variants and one omission by eye-skip, listed below. No other candidate was needed. The critical Cologne edition (vol. 7/1, ed. C. Stroick, 1968) was not read; its search snippets on archive.org give the same chapter titles and the incipit of chapter 16.
  • Transcription: source-assisted, selected passages. The image-only first reading was made from the two images alone; its reader states that nothing in view named the text. The images it saw carry the modern pencil note on the source binding ("aus Einband zu Homer Odyssee, Dillingen 1627") and the shelfmark labels, which do not name the text. A research reader (Claude) reread on the native images, with Borgnet at hand, the top 12 lines of 76r column a, the top 14 lines of 76r column b and 13 lines of the lower part of 76v column b; that revision is the source-assisted reading. An independent research reader read the upper part of 76v column a on the image. These are the only lines, of roughly 190 on the leaf, read again on the image after the first reading; the rest of the leaf (lower 76r column a, most of 76v column a, upper 76v column b) rests on the first reading, which follows Borgnet in sequence but was not reread on the image. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader (Claude) collated the reread lines with Borgnet vol. 5, pp. 261-262 and 265, on the archive.org page images, and checked the first reading of the remaining columns against Borgnet's sequence. An independent research reader (Claude), reading the upper part of 76v column a (canvas 1, native boxes 2700,1500,3720,2000 and 2700,1950,3720,2450) on the fragment image before seeing this transcription, compared it with Borgnet p. 263 on the archive.org page image (scan n270), continuing to p. 264 (scan n271), across the boundary of chapters 16 and 17 with the rubric of chapter 17, and found sustained agreement in wording and sequence, with the variant "post de olfactu" for Borgnet's "postea de auditu". No other manuscript was compared, and the Cologne edition and its apparatus were not consulted. No human review.

Each face has two columns of about 46-50 lines in a gothic textualis, with red rubrics, blue and red penwork initials, and a running-title "II" (for book II) on 76v. The edges are folded back as former binding turn-ins; a horizontal band of abrasion crosses each face, and the lower right of 76r column b and the lower left of 76v column a are lost. Canvas 0 has the end of chapter 14, chapter 15 and the start of chapter 16; canvas 1 has the end of chapter 16, chapter 17 and the start of chapter 18, in the edition's order.

The fragment omits "propter exemplarem explanationem dicta sunt" between two occurrences of "dicta sunt", an omission by eye-skip. Its "colores non nisi in tali medio videntur" gives better sense than Borgnet's "modo"; "coniuncte per medium?" stands against Borgnet's "per mentum". Its "post de olfactu" against Borgnet's "postea de auditu" is clear on the image ("olfcu" with a suspension mark). Its chapter 17 rubric adds a second "sonantibus". Other differences are of word order ("triangularibus et pyramidalibus", "celo uidelicet aere et igne") and small wording ("nisi respirancia" for "non respirantia", "ubi agetur de odore dicetur" for "dicemus quando de odoratu agetur", "qui tenet" for "tenens"). Whether the Cologne edition records any of these readings was not checked.

Leaf order and what is not claimed

The face labelled 76r precedes the face labelled 76v in text order, as in the edition. The leaf's date and origin are not assessed, the Dillingen 1627 Homer named in the pencil note was not traced, and the fragment's place in the manuscript tradition of De anima was not examined. No claim is made about date, origin, version or novelty.
