Frg. 75 (F-aegc)

Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Missal, probably for use in the diocese of Brixen: end of the Mass of SS. Ingenuin and Albuin, Mass of St Dorothy, readings 1 Cor 10:14-17, Jn 6:27-35, 1 Pet 1:1-3; Mal 3:1-4, Ezek 44:1-3 with the Annunciation collect; a late Sunday after Pentecost (Absolve, Col 1:9-14, Mt 9:18-22)

The secret and complenda of SS. Ingenuin and Albuin, patrons of Brixen, agree with the Brixen Mass printed by Resch (1760) from the 14th- and 15th-century Brixen books, read on page images; this probably places the book in the diocese of Brixen, where Stams lay (the formulary was not tested against other dioceses' books), without deciding between diocesan and Cistercian use. A research reader read the larger leaf and the strip and compared the prayers with Resch and the readings with the Vulgate; a second reader compared strip lines (Col 1:9-14, Mt 9:18-22) with the Milan missal of 1474 on page images and found small variants (deo patri, & impone without tuam, duodecim annis, dixit illi). The larger leaf is written on one side only and refers to other folios by Arabic number, so it may be an added leaf of supplementary Masses; the rubric over 1 Cor 10 / Jn 6 is read only probably as Dominica infra octavas corporis Christi. Dorothy texts were seen only as printed-missal snippets, and the cross-reference block is partly unread.

Readings and comparison details

F-aegc: Missal, probably for use in the diocese of Brixen, Masses of SS. Ingenuin and Albuin (end) and of St Dorothy, readings for Corpus Christi octave(?) and Cathedra Petri; Marian lessons and a late Sunday after Pentecost

Two pieces at Stams come from a missal, the book of the Mass texts, written in a cursive hand with red rubrics and with cross-references to other places in the book by Arabic folio number ("folio 121", "132", "178"). The larger piece (75-1) is written on one side only. It has the end of the proper Mass of SS. Ingenuin and Albuin, bishops of Säben and Brixen and patrons of the diocese of Brixen (5 February). These are its secret Summe deitati sacrificia oblata, its complenda (postcommunion) Veneranda celebritate sanctorum presulum tuorum Ingenuini et Albuini and the cues Sint lumbi, Laetamini, Ego vos elegi. They agree with the Brixen Mass of the two saints printed by Joseph Resch from Brixen missals. The leaf goes on with the Mass of St Dorothy (6 February), the readings 1 Cor 10:14-17 and Jn 6:27-35 under a red rubric probably reading "Dominica infra octavas corporis Christi", a block of folio cross-references, and the beginning of 1 Pet 1, the epistle that Roman missals give for the Chair of St Peter (22 February). The narrow strip (75-2) has on one face the lessons Mal 3:1-4 and Ezek 44:1-3 followed by the Annunciation collect Deus qui hodierna die verbum tuum. On the other face it has the Mass of a late Sunday after Pentecost: the collect Absolve quaesumus domine, Col 1:9-14 and Mt 9:18-22.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv (STA), Frg. 75. It describes the fragment as parchment, two fragment items, 286 x 191 mm (Frg. 75_1 measured as 122/286 x 137/191 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no date and no linked description, with two images, Stams_STA_Frg_075_01r02r and _01v02v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: the pencilled labels mark the larger leaf "75-1" and the strip "75-2".

  • Identification: identified, high, for the Masses and readings named. The diagnostic basis is the Ingenuin-Albuin secret and complenda. They agree in wording with the Mass that Resch prints from the 14th-century Brixen choir book and the 15th-century Brixen missals (Annales Ecclesiae Sabionensis II, 1760, pp. 462-463), read on page images. Resch says the modern missals of his time keep this Mass with different prayers. The fragment has the prayers he reports from the older books. A Mass of the Brixen patrons with the older Brixen prayers points to a book made for use in the diocese of Brixen; this is probable rather than shown, since it was not tested whether the formulary occurs in books of other dioceses, and the leaf may be a supplement. Stams lay in that diocese, so the evidence does not decide between a missal of the secular clergy and one of a religious house there, the Cistercians of Stams included. The Dorothy Mass has an Alleluia ending "o dothea & pro tuo grege ora d(omi)n(u)m" and a postcommunion asking "stipendia necessaria et eterne vite suffragia". Both agree with the Dorothy Mass of the Passau missal of 1503 and the Augsburg missal of 1510, seen as snippets only. The red rubric over 1 Cor 10 and Jn 6 is read probably, not securely, as "Dominica infra octavas corporis Christi". On the strip the collect Absolve stands with Col 1:9-14 and Mt 9:18-22 under the introit cue Dicit dominus. That is the pairing of the northern series of Sundays after Pentecost. The Milan missal of 1474 instead gives Absolve and Mt 9:18 on the 23rd Sunday and Col 1:9-14 on the 24th. The strip does not show which Sunday of its book this was. Against Milan the strip reads gratias agentes deo patri (Milan deo et patri), veni & impone manum (Milan veni impone manum tuam), duodecim annis (Milan annis duodecim) and dixit illi (Milan dixit); the first three agree with the Vulgate.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers both faces of both pieces. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's reread at reading size of 75-1 lines 1-34, the rubric of line 12 also at twice size and in a red-only channel, strip 75-2 face A (rotated), and the top lines of face B. A second research reader reread face B lines 4-14. The cross-reference block (75-1 lines 27-29) and the red note after the 1 Peter rubric are only partly read. The superscript numbers 63, 21 and 32 in line 29 are unexplained. Several faded words in the Dorothy prayers are uncertain. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the Ingenuin-Albuin prayers and cues with Resch pp. 462-463 on MDZ page images. Dorothy texts were compared with Passau and Augsburg missals as Google Books snippets. The Bible readings were compared with the Vulgate from knowledge. A second research reader compared a passage the first had not collated, strip face B lines 4-14 (Col 1:9-14 and Mt 9:18-22), with the Milan missal of 1474 in Lippe's edition, pp. 297-298, on archive.org page images, and independently reread the Resch pages and the rubric of line 12. That check confirmed the readings and the Resch agreement. It corrected the description of the Sunday ("a late Sunday", not "the last") and recorded the red "22" in the margin. Not checked on a source page: the Dorothy prayers in full, the 1 Cor 10 / Jn 6 readings against a German or Brixen missal for that Sunday, and the Marian Mass on strip face A.

The coordinator did not make a separate comparison for this record; the check rests on the research reader and the independent checker named above.

The prayers of the Ingenuin-Albuin Mass agree with Resch in wording and in their items. The fragment seems to lack Deus, intercedentibus sanctis tuis between "tue" and "Ingenuino", but the line end is at the edge of the writing and the point is uncertain. The fragment has deleat and efficiat where Resch has the plural deleant, efficiant; both MS forms are doubtful readings. The fragment lists the Gospel, offertory and communion cues after the complenda, where Resch gives the items in Mass order. The differences in the Col 1 and Mt 9 readings from Milan are listed under Identification. They show that the two texts differ, not how they are related.

Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

  • 75-1 is written on the recto only; the verso is blank. With its folio cross-references to Masses elsewhere (132, 178 or 198) and its contents (the Brixen patrons, Dorothy, Corpus Christi octave readings), the leaf may be an added leaf of supplementary Masses rather than part of the main sanctorale. Against that, its feasts of 5, 6 and 22 February run in calendar order. This is not established.
  • The cross-reference block at 75-1 lines 27-29 (4250,3500,2150,300) has "[..]coli 132" with 132 in red, "Ecce? i(n) holocausto 178" with 178 or 198, and "Q(ua)re d(eu)s? iudica? [..]" with the numbers 63, 21 and 32 written above three words. It is not identified. A red note after the 1 Peter rubric is mostly lost in holes.
  • The red "22" beside the epistle on strip face B may be a Sunday number or a reference. It is not explained.
  • The occasion of the Marian Mass on strip face A (Mal 3:1-4, Ezek 44:1-3, the Annunciation collect borrowed "de Annunctiacione") is open.
  • The two pieces share hand type, layout and folio references; that they come from the same book is likely but not proved.
  • Not claimed: the date (the cursive script and the Arabic folio numbers suggest the late Middle Ages, but no date is established); whether the book was a secular or a monastic (Cistercian) missal; its origin; any relation to the printed Brixen missals or to a surviving manuscript.
