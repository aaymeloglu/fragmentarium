Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Terence, Andria III.5-IV.2 (lines 607-686), manuscript copy of the play text (pieces 3-4)

Pieces 3-4 are two nested paper bifolia with a continuous copy of Terence, Andria 607 to about 686, with scene headings and pen initials but no commentary. A research reader collated six pages with page images of Melanchthon's edition (Strasbourg: Knobloch, 1527). An independent reader collated a further page (lines 647-657) against the same print and found no substantive difference. The text has the 16th-century humanist readings (subducere, Factum est hoc, tu aliud reperi, aliquid iam inuenturum, C. scio) and differs from the 1527 print only in small points (no 'etiam' at 644, 'prosim', 'ancila', possibly 'cognoueris'). No single printed exemplar is claimed, and the copy is not dated: the pencil year 1534 refers to the host volume II/6 36. Pieces 1-2 carry German letter-draft pen trials, and pieces 5-9 a Latin-German proverb exercise with letter drafts; these are unidentified.

Readings and comparison details

F-t8sd: Terence, Andria, III.5-IV.2 (lines 607-686)

Pieces 3 and 4 of this record are two nested paper bifolia (a bifolium is a folded sheet making two leaves) carrying a continuous manuscript copy of Terence's comedy Andria. The text runs from the opening of Act III scene 5 ("Ubi illic scelus est, qui me perdidit?", line 607) to the first lines of Act IV scene 2 ("Iam ubi ubi erit, inventum tibi curabo", about line 686). It is the play text alone, with scene headings naming the characters and decorated pen initials. There is no commentary. The wording shares the readings of the German Terence prints of the 1520s and 1530s, and it agrees closely with Melanchthon's edition (Strasbourg: Knobloch, 1527).

The Fragmentarium record describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv (Stams, STA), Frg. 39: paper, 9 pieces, 201 x 165 mm (from piece 1), title "x". It gives no date and no description, and nothing in it identifies the text. The pieces carry a modern pencil note "aus Einband II/6 36 (a. 1534)": they were taken from the binding of the volume with shelfmark II/6 36, and the year in that note refers to that volume.

  • Identification: identified, high. About 90 manuscript lines on six pages agree in wording and sequence with Andria 607-686. The scene headings give the characters of III.5 ("Pamphilus adolescens. Dauus seruus"), IV.1 ("Carinus, Pamph[ilus] ...") and IV.2 ("Mysis ancila. Pamphilus. Carinus adolescentes. Dauus seruus"). The text form is the 16th-century humanist one, not the modern edition. Its readings include "subducere" at 644 (the 15th-century prints have "seducere"), "Factum est hoc, Daue" (665), "tu aliud reperi" (680), "aliquid iam inuenturum" (615), "Parum successit" (679), and "C. scio" (669), where the modern text gives the line to Pamphilus. Every reading compared agrees with the 1527 Melanchthon print except these:
    • "nunc me subducere" lacks the print's "etiam" (644);
    • "iniuriam hoc expostulem" has "hoc" for the print's "hanc" (639);
    • "ancila" is spelled for "ancilla";
    • "cognoueris" may stand for the print's "cognoris" (652); this is uncertain.

Mainz 1520, Strasbourg 1521 and Basel 1532 share most of these readings and were not collated, so no single printed exemplar is claimed. A commentary or school exercise on the play was considered and rejected: these pages carry only the text and speaker marks, with no glosses.

  • Transcription: image-only first reading and source-assisted reading.
    • A research reader reread on the images every page of pieces 3 and 4 except the 4v right and 3v left pages.
    • Those two pages were read by a second research reader, who checked the match.
    • Pieces 5-9 rest on the first reading only.
    • Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification:
    • A research reader collated the 3r, 4r and 4v left and 3v right pages with the page images of the Knobloch 1527 edition (Munich copy). Scan 75 was read at full size; scans 76-79 were read at reduced size.
    • Independently, a second research reader made a reading of the 4v right page (lines 647-657), which had not been used in the identification, before opening the source. That reader then compared it with the page image of the same edition (scan 77) and found no substantive difference in 14 lines.
    • The same checker confirmed the leaf order. They also read the top of the 3v left page as lines 616-621, without collating it.
    • The coordinator compared canvas 1, piece 4v right page, lines 1-8 (native box 2520,120,3960,1250, "Nisi me lactasses amantem ... quid istuc tam mirum est? de te exemp[lum]", Andria 641-647) with the Melanchthon 1527 page image (MDZ bsb11265863, scan 77): every legible word agrees in order.
    • Gaps: only one print was collated; pieces 5-9 were not read at reading size by either reader.

Differences: the manuscript omits "etiam" at 644 and "omnia" after "interturbat" (663). It has "hoc" for "hanc" (639), "C." before "hem quid ais" missing or not read (665), "prosim" for "prosit" (677), and "ancila" for "ancilla". "Dauos" against "Dauus" is minor. At 628 a sign that looks like a cancelled letter stands before "cogit", and the "Et" before "tamen" is not clearly legible. On the 4v right page the checker read "cognoueris?" for the print's "cognoris" (652); this is uncertain. Several words fall in holes or at torn edges and could not be compared ("perdidit", "adoriemur/adgrediemur", the 680-682 line beginnings). The manuscript writes the verse as run-on lines, not in the print's verse lines. Spelling (u/v, ae/e, "caussa") is not counted.

For the text family, dated full-text hits in the Munich digitised prints were used. "seducere istis dictis" is the reading of prints from Venice 1482 to Strasbourg 1514. "subducere" appears from Mainz 1520 and continues in Basel 1532 and 1534. The word order "qua fiducia id facere audeam", which the manuscript shares, is found in Mainz 1520, Strasbourg 1521 and the 1527 edition, while Tübingen and Strasbourg 1516 have "facere id". These checks used OCR and snippets, not page images, except for the 1527 edition.

Leaf order, other pieces, and limits

The text runs across the two sheets in this order: piece 3 right page (607-614), piece 3 left page of the other face (615-624), piece 4 right page (625-638), piece 4 left page of the other face (638-647), piece 4 right page of that face (647-657), piece 4 left page (657-666), piece 3 right page of the second face (667-678), piece 3 left page (678-686). Piece 3 is the outer sheet of the gathering and piece 4 the inner one. The pencil labels r and v do not follow the reading order.

The other pieces of the record are not Terence:

  • Pieces 1 and 2 are blank except for German letter-draft pen trials.
  • Pieces 5 to 9 hold a school exercise of Latin proverbs and phrases with German renderings, numbered and bracketed in pairs. Examples are "Oculis magis habenda fides quam auribus", "Coturno versatilior", "Iracundior Adria" and a run of "Prius ..." sayings. These pieces also carry German letter drafts ("Mein sonnder freundtlich vnnd ganntz willig dienst ... lieber herr landrichter ...") and alphabet trials.
  • The proverbs are unidentified; the lead is the humanist adage and sententiae collections used in schools.

Not claimed:

  • A date for the copy. The pencil year 1534 belongs to the host volume II/6 36, not to the pieces. The agreement with prints of the 1520s gives only the kind of text the scribe followed, not a date.
  • An origin or scribe.
  • That the copy was made from the 1527 edition, or from any one print.
  • Whether the Terence pages and the proverb pieces were written by the same person.
