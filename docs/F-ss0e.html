What the strip is

A vertical strip cut from the gutter side of one leaf. Side "r" preserves the first ~12-15 letters of ~34 lines (line beginnings); side "v" preserves the last ~12-15 letters of ~34 lines (line ends). Small, heavily abbreviated book hand. Uses the Arabic numeral "2" with superscript "o" (secundo), "p̄batur", "pph̄a" (propheta), tironian et, "q3" (que), "als̄" (alios). These features prompted a dating query, but the vocabulary and abbreviation system have not received specialist palaeographic assessment. No replacement date or place of origin is established here.

Content

A scholastic argument that God forgives the sinner who returns, every time, structured as numbered proofs: (1) from the Gospel: Mt 18:22 "seventy times seven", Chrysostom on that verse, and the "turpe est doctori" proverb (God teaches us to forgive our offenders all offenses in the Lord's Prayer; a teacher convicted by his own fault is shameful; therefore he forgives us); (2) "secundo probatur (auctoritate) prophetica": Zech 1:3, Isa 55:7, "item alius propheta" Jer 3:1. Side r (the preceding page) concerns contrition (Ps 50:19). Genre: quaestio / summa / sermon-quaestio on penance; composition date unresolved. "Ex hoc arguo" and "2° probatur" are disputation formulae.

Consequence for the record: report the Cherubino parallel with the date query unresolved. A passage in a fifteenth-century compilation does not by itself establish original authorship or a terminus post quem for the fragment. The Franciscan Authors entry documents Cherubino's use of other sermon material, although borrowing of this particular passage has not been demonstrated. A 1502 edition also does not establish that the manuscript was copied before 1500.
