Fragm. 297 a-b (F-ln58)

Antwerpen, Erfgoedbibliotheek Hendrik Conscience · catalogued as Unidentified text, 1301 – 1400 CE

Nicolaus de Byard, sermon for All Saints Congregate illi sanctos eius (Ps 49:5), opening and two later passages; piece A only; agrees with Paris BnF lat. 15951 ff. 336rb-336va

Piece A keeps the opening of the All Saints sermon Congregate illi sanctos eius and two later stretches of it, which agree in wording and order with Paris BnF lat. 15951 ff. 336rb-336va: the opening after the initial (Solent re[ligiosi] ... congregati ut orationes ... quasi impossibile erat), fourteen line-ends of verso col. a (quanto plures ... in omnibus populis ... nos laudabunt ... bien procu[rat] ... non est iustum quod p[ost]) and eleven line-beginnings of verso col. b (Esther 4:16, [man]davit ... [rep]eris in S[usis] ... ibi illi). A research reader compared them with page images of lat. 15951; an independent checker reread 21 of the weakest lines on the native images and found 17 in agreement and in order, corrected several readings of the witness, and confirmed the marginal note 'fr. N. de biart.' at the foot of f. 336r, which stands below the sermon's column but does not name it. The fragment differs at several points (the theme in short form, an extra clause 'sed vellera ovium', 'omnibus' for 'cunctis populis', two unmatched lines in the Esther passage), and the text before the sermon is not the end of the sermon preceding it in lat. 15951. The attribution rests on Schneyer (IV p. 244, Byard, Sermones de sanctis no. 194; V no. 2016, Robert of Sorbon), not rechecked, and on that note; a Fragmentarium editor judged Byard correct and the Sorbon listing one of Schneyer's over-ascriptions; piece B is unplaced and the collection the strips come from is not established. A Fragmentarium editor reviewed the identification and attribution (Nov. 2026), not the transcription.

Readings and comparison details

F-ln58: Nicolaus de Byard, All Saints sermon Congregate illi sanctos eius, opening and two later passages (piece A)

The larger of the two strips in Antwerp, Fragm. 297 a-b (piece A), comes from a two-column copy of a sermon for All Saints on the theme (opening Bible verse) Congregate illi sanctos eius (Ps. 49:5). On one face it keeps the blue initial and the opening of the sermon; on the other it keeps two later stretches of it, the first on the saints being gathered for the feast (with Sophonias 3:20) and the second on Esther sending Mordecai to gather the people (Esther 4:16). These agree in wording and order with the sermon in Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, lat. 15951, ff. 336rb-336va. J. B. Schneyer's Repertorium der lateinischen Sermones des Mittelalters, the standard listing of medieval Latin sermons by their opening words, gives the sermon as no. 194 of the Sermones de sanctis (sermons for saints' days) of Nicolaus de Byard and also lists it under Robert of Sorbon; lat. 15951 has the note "fr. N. de biart." in the lower margin of f. 336r, below the column in which the sermon begins. The text that precedes the sermon on the strip is not the end of the sermon that precedes it in lat. 15951. The smaller strip (piece B) has not been placed.

The Fragmentarium record is Antwerpen, Erfgoedbibliotheek Hendrik Conscience, Fragm. 297 a-b: 68 x 32 mm, dated 1301-1400, titled "Unidentified text", with four images labelled fol. [1]r (a), fol. [1]r. (b), fol. [1]v (a) and fol. [1]v (b). The description fits the images: two small strips, each photographed on both sides with a ruler, each with a blank upper margin and cut through the text at the foot. The record does not name the text. The text runs from the face labelled fol. [1]r (a) to the face labelled fol. [1]v (a), so the catalogue's recto and verso labels for piece A agree with the order of the text.

  • Identification: identified, high confidence, for piece A. Three separated places on the strip agree with lat. 15951 in wording and order: the opening after the initial on the recto ("Solent re[ligiosi] ... congregati ut orationes ... quasi impossibile erat ... [sigil]latim. Sic enim s[unt]"), fourteen line-ends of the first column of the verso ("quanto plures ... tanto melius est ... tempore quo congregabo ... in omnibus populis ... nos laudabunt ... delicias et hono[res] ... expectando ut dicitur ... bien procu[rat] ... non est iustum quod p[ost] ... [m]orte"), and eleven line-beginnings of its second column ("[man]davit ... et congrega ... [rep]eris in S[usis] ... pro me ... [con]gregan[di] ... ibi illi ... fid[eles]"). The agreement is in the sermon's own argument, examples and transitions: religious meet in chapter so that the prayers of all are asked together, because asking one by one would be nearly impossible; the saints are gathered for one feast; the French proverb "bien festivatur qui bien procurat"; Esther's message glossed "in Susis, i. in paradiso"; "Sed qua intentione congregandi sunt". It does not rest on the three biblical verses the sermon cites. The strip's copy differs from lat. 15951 at several points: the theme is written in short form, on a single line; "congregati" for "congregari"; no "quia difficile vel" before "quasi impossibile"; an extra clause "sed vellera ovium" (the fleeces of sheep) between the two "quanto plures" sentences; "omnibus populis" (the Vulgate's wording) for "cunctis populis"; "ut" for "sicut"; a verb form in the proverb line that may differ ("[fe]stiva^ri^(?)" for "festivatur"); "ab[" after "quod p(ost)", unexplained; and two line-beginnings in the Esther passage that do not match ("tiōes e[" where lat. 15951 has "Hest. iiii. Hester volens rogare", and "inc^a^ ei[" where it has "i. in paradiso"). These show that the two copies differ; they do not show how they are related. The attribution to Nicolaus de Byard rests on Schneyer's listing and on the marginal note in lat. 15951. That note stands below the only sermon that begins in its column, so it is reasonably taken as that sermon's ascription, but it does not name the sermon. A Fragmentarium editor reviewed the identification in November 2026 and judged the attribution to Nicolaus de Byard correct, and Schneyer's second listing, under Robert of Sorbon, one of the over-ascriptions for which Schneyer is known. The strip itself names no author. Whether the strips come from Nicolaus de Byard's Sermones de sanctis as a collection, or from another compilation that included this sermon, is not established.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers all four faces; the source-assisted reading covers piece A. A research reader who knew the sermon's opening words from Schneyer reread on the native images recto column b, lines 10-15, verso column a, lines 1-13, and verso column b, lines 1-11, before opening lat. 15951, and reread a few of them afterwards (recto column b, line 14). An independent checker then read recto column b, lines 12-15, verso column a, lines 9-14, and verso column b, lines 1-11, on the native images before opening lat. 15951 at those places, and supplied verso column a, line 14 and the readings "orationes" (recto b, line 12) and "[fe]stiva^ri^ cu bien procu[rat]" (verso a, line 12). Recto column a, recto column b above the initial (lines 1-9) and both faces of piece B were read by the research reader only for a few words and otherwise rest on the first reading; none of them was matched. Each line of piece A keeps only four to twenty letters, and minims (u, n, m, i) are often ambiguous, so "festina-" and "festiva-" cannot be told apart on the strip. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the three passages of piece A with page images of lat. 15951, ff. 336rb and 336va, on Gallica. An independent checker, who had read the research reader's account and transcriptions before choosing its lines, took the lines that account had read worst or left out: verso column a, lines 9-14 (canvas 2, native box 215,1100,255,300), verso column b, lines 1-11 (canvas 2, 470,740,150,520) and recto column b, lines 12-15 (canvas 0, 400,1090,265,260). It read them on the native images, then compared them with page images of lat. 15951, f. 336rb and f. 336va. Of these 21 lines, 17 agree with lat. 15951 in the same order, including the proverb line. Two (verso b, lines 3 and 7), single lines inside the Esther passage between lines that agree, do not match. The checker corrected several readings of lat. 15951 given here ("orationes", "festivaverunt", "festivatur"), read the marginal note on f. 336r itself, and confirmed that the end of the preceding sermon in lat. 15951 shares no wording with recto column b, lines 1-9. Its readings of lines the research reader had already transcribed are not blind; its new evidence is mainly verso column a, lines 12-14. Gaps: Schneyer's pages were not rechecked for this report; no other witness of the sermon was compared; the hand and date of the marginal note were not assessed; recto column a, recto column b above the initial and piece B were not matched. Both comparisons are model readings of page images. A Fragmentarium editor has reviewed the identification and attribution, not the transcription.

The differences are listed under Identification above. Along the fourteen line-ends of verso column a the strip and lat. 15951 run in the same order from "quanto plures" to "[m]orte", with the fleece clause as the one addition; in verso column b the order "[man]davit ... et congrega ... [rep]eris ... pro me ... [con]gregandi ... ibi illi ... fideles" is the same, and lines 5-6 divide "rep-eris" where the wording requires. Verso b, line 7 may carry a different gloss on "Susis" ("in c(uri)a ei(us)" has been suggested); that is a conjecture. These differences show that the strip's text is not identical with that of lat. 15951; whether either depends on the other is not established.

Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

Leaf order, from the text: on piece A the second column of the recto backs the first column of the verso. The sermon opens in recto column b and runs on in verso column a, from about f. 336va, line 4 of lat. 15951, and then in verso column b, from about line 30. In verso column a a line of the strip carries about as much text as a line of lat. 15951. On that basis the column lengths implied by the text (about 24-25 lines for recto column b, about 28 for verso column a) are only roughly consistent, three or four lines apart; variant text such as the fleece clause could account for the difference, but the estimate is not evidence for the identification, and the full height of the column does not survive.

Before the initial, recto column b, lines 1-9 ("sicut vestis s[ / tis sine pelle n[ / et panno non suffi[ / sed opinione hn̄t[ ... / n(on) opus sine ini[ ... / ꝑcutis(?) ex^m^ de v[ ... / titi s; dupliciter"), and the line-ends of recto column a, are the end of a preceding text that is not identified. It shares no wording with the end of the sermon that precedes Congregate in lat. 15951 ("Certe ista bestia terribilis ... Hec est amor sui ... et facit omnia mala excusari"). So at this point the strip's book does not have the order of lat. 15951; the rest of lat. 15951 was not searched for this text.

Piece B is the outer edge of one column, with a wide margin carrying a paraph-like sign and blue and red penwork from an initial that is not on the strip. The first reading saw the same hand and size of script as on piece A, but nothing on the images proves a join. Its lines do not continue piece A's lines at the same height, and its words ("de ip̄o uō ... / [.]p̄m de celo" on one side, "ut ip̄m cō ... / filios eiu[s] ... / q̃nto ei" on the other) were not found on ff. 336r-336v of lat. 15951. It is unplaced, and the identification is claimed for piece A only.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the copy (the catalogue gives 1301-1400; the script was not dated here, and lat. 15951 was not dated); which collection the strips' book contained, and its order of sermons beyond the one place noted; the strip's relation to lat. 15951 or to any other copy; authorship beyond the listings and the marginal note described above; the identity of the text before the sermon and of piece B.
