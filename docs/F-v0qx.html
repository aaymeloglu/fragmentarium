Johannes Matthaeus, Concionum dispositiones (Wittenberg, 1581; 2nd ed. 1590), end of the outline "De templorum dedicatione" (handwritten copy of pp. 865-868)

Four paper pieces are a pen copy of the last four pages of a printed book, with its running titles, page numbers 865-868 and signatures Kkk and Kkk2. A research reader compared six passages from all four pages with page images of the 1581 Wittenberg edition (part 2, pp. 408-412), where the text agrees closely, including the Epilogus, the Greek doxology and the print's slip "1. Pet. 3"; the coordinator compared the Epilogus and doxology on the fragment image with the 1581 page image of p. 412. Differences: page and line division, and a few words (significabat for significat, purgarit for purgaverit, ita for ibi facere). The page numbers fit the catalogued collation of the 1590 edition (VD16 E 4591: 8 leaves, 868 pages, 2 leaves), which was not seen, so the edition copied is inferred and the variants are unassigned. Aretius, De encaeniis, and Legenda aurea c. 182 are Matthaeus's sources, not the work copied.

Readings and comparison details

F-v0qx: Johannes Matthaeus, Concionum dispositiones, "De templorum dedicatione" (end)

The four paper pieces are a handwritten copy of the last four pages of a printed book: Johannes Matthaeus, Concionum dispositiones in Evangelia dominicalia with its second part on the gospels of feast days, lives of saints and the Passion (Wittenberg: Simon Gronenberg, first printed 1581). They carry the end of the last sermon outline in the book, "De templorum dedicatione" (on the dedication of churches), from the criticism of fairs and drinking at dedication feasts to the "Epilogus" and the closing Greek doxology. The identification is made with high confidence against page images of the 1581 edition. The copy reproduces the page numbers 865-868 and the signatures Kkk and Kkk2 (a signature is the letter mark at the foot of a page that tells the binder the order of the printed sheets). These do not belong to the 1581 edition; they fit the catalogued make-up of the 1590 edition, which was not seen.

The Fragmentarium record describes four paper pieces, Odense, Syddansk Universitetsbibliotek, RARA Musik L 46, envelope 9, 152 × 131 mm, dated 1570-1600, in humanistic cursive, catalogued as "Text on churches", an "Early Modern manuscript copy of an unidentified Latin text on churches" taken from paste-laminate binding boards. The description fits: it is a manuscript, and the subject is churches. The catalogue names no work or author. If the copy was made from the 1590 edition, as its page numbers suggest, it dates from 1590 or later; that follows from an edition that was not seen and is not a claim about the catalogue date.

  • Identification: identified, high. On six passages spread over all four pages the fragment has Matthaeus's text in his wording and order, including the numbered heads ("IIII. Cur?", "V. Ubi?", "VI. Quando?", "VII. De prophanatione"), the "Epilogus", and the printed book's own slip "1. Pet. 3" for 2 Peter 3:18. Two printed texts share long stretches with the fragment and were considered: Benedictus Aretius, Problemata theologica, locus "De encaeniis", and Jacobus de Voragine, Legenda aurea, chapter 182 "De dedicatione ecclesiae". They are sources that Matthaeus reworked, not the work copied: the fragment has Matthaeus's own connecting sentences, his numbering and his epilogue, none of which are in either. The edition is a separate question. The 1581 edition has two paginations (478 and 412 pages) and the passage stands there on pp. 408-412 of the second, signature Cc. The 1590 edition (VD16 E 4591, variant issue VD16 ZV 5597) is catalogued as 8 leaves, 868 pages, 2 leaves, in octavo; a text ending on p. 868 in a final gathering of two printed and two blank leaves is exactly what the fragment shows. No scan of the 1590 edition was found, so this rests on the catalogue description. A few small differences from the 1581 text (listed below) may be readings of 1590 or the copyist's own.
  • Transcription: source-assisted selected reading of pp. 865-868. The image-only first reading covers only the first four images (the lower parts of pp. 867 and 868). A research reader read pp. 865 and 866 and the heads of pp. 867 and 868 from the images, joining the narrow strips to the main pieces, and rechecked parts of the lower pp. 867-868; the remaining lines of those two pages rest on the first reading. Letters lost between the pieces are supplied in square brackets, where possible from the 1581 print. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the fragment images with page images of the 1581 edition, pp. 408-412, for the passages tabled below. The coordinator compared the Epilogus and the Greek doxology on the fragment image with the 1581 page image of p. 412. Not checked: the 1590 edition; the first leaves of the outline (before p. 865), which the fragment does not have; a letter-by-letter comparison of the lower halves of pp. 867 and 868.

How the copy is made, and what else is in the images

Handwritten, not printed. At full resolution repeated letters differ from one another (for example the t and d in "tota die bibitur, tota die luditur"), strokes are joined by the pen, the ink varies in density within words and the baseline wanders; the signature marks and the flourish after the doxology are drawn with the pen. The scribe nevertheless kept the furniture of a printed page: running titles, page numbers, signatures, a catchword ("Pa-" at the foot of p. 865, for "Papistae" on p. 866) and double hyphens at line ends.

Arrangement. The pieces fit together as one sheet folded twice. Pages 865/866 (signature Kkk) and 867/868 (signature Kkk2) are two leaves joined head to head, so that the top of one appears upside down above the other, and each has a blank leaf beside it. The narrow strips are the outer edges of the two written leaves and supply the line ends and beginnings missing from the main pieces. The text ends on p. 868. In the 1581 edition the eight preliminary leaves are signed A; if the 1590 edition is made the same way and its text begins with gathering B, Kkk1 is p. 865, as in the fragment. Why a final gathering was copied by hand is not known; supplying leaves missing from a printed copy is one possibility.

Sources of the printed text. Matthaeus's outline draws on Aretius, "De encaeniis" (for example "tota die bibitur, tota die luditur ...", and "semel factae, quotannis non sunt repetitae" under "VI. Quando?") and on Legenda aurea, chapter 182 (the five reasons for dedicating a church, and the three profaners Jeroboam, Nebuzaradan and Antiochus with the quotation from Bernard). This was checked against the text of Aretius's 1589 edition and Graesse's 1850 edition of the Legenda aurea, both as OCR text.

Other images in the record. The manifest also lists seven images labelled "L 42 130 front cover", "back cover", "spine" and so on. They show the parchment cover of a different volume and belong to the record F-x06a, catalogued there as Cicero, De natura deorum II 54-58 and 64-68. They are not part of this fragment.

No claim is made about the scribe, the place of copying, or the copy of the printed book for which these leaves were written.
