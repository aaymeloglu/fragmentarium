Readings and comparison details

F-sqjv: Noted Mass book, Good Friday, Adoration of the Cross, Improperia verse Quid ultra and antiphon Ecce lignum crucis

These two small paper scraps at Stams come from one leaf of a noted Mass book, a book of Mass chants written with their melodies (a gradual or a noted missal). They carry chants sung on Good Friday during the Adoration of the Cross. One face has the verse of the Improperia (the Reproaches) Quid ultra debui facere tibi ... Ego quidem plantavi te vineam meam speciosissimam (Cantus ID 008453). Its text runs from the larger scrap onto the smaller one on the same line, so the two scraps were neighbours on the leaf. The other face has the antiphon Ecce lignum crucis in quo salus mundi pependit, venite adoremus (Cantus ID 002522), then a red division stroke and the opening of its psalm verse Beati [immaculati] (Psalm 118:1). The script is a Gothic textualis under square (Hufnagel-type) notation on four-line red staves.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 23, paper, two fragment items, 99 x 43 mm (measured on item 1, 50/99 x 17/43 mm, by Claudia Sojer), title "x", with two images labelled 01r02r and 01v02v. The record gives no date, description or identification. Nothing in it conflicts with the identification; it did not name the text.

  • Identification: identified, high. The basis is the wording read on both faces, each face identifying its own chant: "debui facere tibi / ... quidem plantavi te vineam meam spe-" on one, "mundi pependit / ... adoremus / Beati" on the other. The text agrees with the Graduale Romanum (Vatican edition, 1908), pp. 182-183, with no difference in the surviving letters. The responsory verse Ego quidem plantavi te vinea mea electa (Cantus 007887a) begins with the same words but was set aside because the line above has "debui facere tibi" and the line continues "meam spe-", both of which belong to Quid ultra. The surviving words have "speciosissimam", the common form of the verse, not the "fructu decora" form that some books have. The verse Beati immaculati after Ecce lignum is not in the Vatican rite. It is in older uses, for example the ordo printed by Martène, and Cantus Index gives it as the chant that most often follows Ecce lignum. The use of the book (Cistercian or other) is not determined.
  • Transcription: source-assisted. The image-only first reading covers every text line except face 2 of the smaller scrap. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's reread of all text lines on both faces of both scraps at reading size. It corrects the first reading's "ue biu facte te ta" to "[d]e bui face re ti[bi]", "urte uin" to "ui te uine[am]" and "mica spe" to "[m]eā spe c[iosissimam]". The opening of the line with quidem reads "ecto" or "ego" with a damaged g. "Ego" is not secure, and the identification does not depend on it. On face 2 of the smaller scrap only "te" is in ink; a grey "m" beside it is modern pencil. The neumes were not transcribed. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared every text line with the Cantus Index standard texts. An independent research reader then reread the weakest lines on the images: the opening of the quidem line, the smaller scrap on both faces and the "a do re mus" line. That reader compared all surviving words with the Graduale Romanum 1908, pp. 182-183, on its page images, and the verse Beati immaculati with Martène's printed ordo on its MDZ page image. Gaps: no medieval noted gradual or Cistercian book was compared on the page, and the melody was not compared.

Leaf, other items, and what is not claimed

  • Two scraps of one paper leaf: item 1 about 9 x 4.5 cm and item 2 about 4 x 4 cm, torn on all sides. Each face keeps parts of two or three staves with red lines and Hufnagel-type notes. The pencil marks "Stams, STA, Frg. 23-1r / 2r / 1v / 2v" are modern shelf labels.
  • Leaf order is open. The 1908 gradual puts Ecce lignum before the Improperia and Martène's ordo puts Quid ultra first, so the chants do not show which face is the recto.
  • Not claimed: the date (the catalogue gives none, and none was tested), the origin, the use (Cistercian or other), whether the book was a gradual or a noted missal, or any relation of the melody to other books.
