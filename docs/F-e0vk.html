Readings and comparison details

F-e0vk: Missal, Common of apostles: orations for one and for several apostles, and the common epistles (Eph 2:19-22, Rom 8:28-39, Ecclus 44:10-15, Eph 1:3-5)

This parchment leaf at Stams comes from the Common of apostles in a missal, the section with the Masses used for any apostle who has no proper Mass. The face pencilled "1v" was the recto of the original book: it carries the red folio number cxvij (117). In reading order the leaf holds:

1. The end of a Mass for one apostle: the secret (the prayer over the offerings) Debitum domine nostre reddimus servitutis ... ut suffragiis apostoli tui N. in nobis tua munera tuearis, cuius honoranda confessione laudis tibi hostias immolamus, the complenda (the prayer after communion) Conserva domine populum tuum et quem ... non desinis adiuvare presidiis, perpetuis tribue gaudere remediis, and "Alia collecta ... ut supra". 2. "In festo plurimorum apostolorum" (for several apostles): the prayer Deus qui nos annua apostolorum tuorum N. sollempnitate letificas, concede propicius ut quorum gaudemus meritis instruamur exemplis, the secret Munera domine que pro apostolorum tuorum N. sollempnitate deferimus and the complenda Quesumus domine salutaribus repleti misteriis. 3. The common epistles: Ephesians 2:19-22, Iam non estis hospites; Romans 8:28-39, Scimus quoniam diligentibus, running across the turn of the leaf; "Lectio libri Sapientie", Ecclesiasticus 44:10-15, Hi sunt viri misericordie; and, under a partly faded rubric "Ep(isto)la ... in festo ...", Ephesians 1:3-5, Benedictus deus et pater, breaking off at "per Iesum".

The orations for several apostles and the first two epistles agree with the Roman Missal printed at Milan in 1474. The use of the book is not identified.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 214: parchment, four fragment items, 374 x 257 mm (340/374 x 175/257 mm in the Reconstruction Summary), no date, with the title "x", no linked description, and two images labelled 01r04r and 01v04v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: one large leaf, two columns of about 28 lines in gothic textualis with red rubrics and alternating red and blue initials, folded as a binding wrapper, with three loose narrow strips of the same script beside it.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the texts and their place in the book. The basis is canvas 1 column a and canvas 0 columns a and b read on the images and collated with the 1474 Roman Missal, pp. 406-408 ("In natali plurimorum apostolorum").
    • Agreements: the three orations for several apostles agree in wording, and Eph 2:19-22 and Rom 8:28-39 in sequence and wording, apart from the differences below.
    • Differences from the Roman print: the collect has "concede propicius" where the print has "presta quesumus", and a single "N." where it has "N. et N.". The print's epistle series runs Eph 2:19-22, Eph 4:7-13, Rom 8:28-39, then Ezekiel 1:10-14; on the leaf "Ad Romanos" follows Eph 2:22 directly, so Eph 4:7-13 is absent, and Romans is followed by Ecclus 44:10-15 and Eph 1:3. The one-apostle orations differ from the print's (secret Sacrandum, postcommunion Perceptis). The secret Debitum is otherwise the secret of St Thomas, here made general with "N." (seen in the York missal in a search snippet only); the complenda Conserva is a Gelasian postcommunion for many saints (snippet only), where the leaf seems to read "ap(osto)l(i)cis" for "sanctorum tuorum".
    • Use (not identified): the book is not of the Roman use of the 1474 print, by the collect wording, the epistle series and the one-apostle orations. A Cistercian missal from Kaisheim (Munich, BSB, Clm 7901, illuminated 1531), read on page images, also calls the post-communion prayer "complenda", as the leaf does and the Roman print does not; but its readings for apostles are arranged differently and do not match (Eph 2:19 is followed directly by the gospel, with no Rom 8:28-39, Ecclus 44:10-15 or Eph 1:3), and its common orations were not located. That is one late and selective witness. No printed Cistercian missal and no missal of Brixen, Augsburg or Passau was compared.
  • Transcription: source-assisted. The image-only first reading covers both columns of both faces wherever ink survives. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's reread of canvas 1 column a in full, the middle of canvas 0 column b, the faded rubric before Eph 1:3 and the loose strips; an independent checker reread canvas 0 column a lines 1-12 and the end of Eph 2 on canvas 1 column b. The rest of canvas 1 column b and canvas 0 column a rests on the first reading, checked against the overview and the edition. The torn bands across both faces, the feast name in the rubric before Eph 1:3 (partly under a pasted paper label) and the backs of the strips are unread; "ap(osto)l(i)cis" is uncertain. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the orations for several apostles and the epistles with page images of the 1474 Roman Missal (pp. 406-408), and the rubrics and apostles' readings with page images of the Kaisheim missal. The St Thomas and Gelasian parallels rest on search snippets only. An independent checker read canvas 0, column a, lines 1-12 (Rom 8:32-35; native boxes 2200,950,3550,1750 and 2200,1700,3550,2500) before opening the source and compared them with the 1474 Roman Missal, p. 407, lines 33-40 (page image). They agree in sequence and wording, except that the leaf lacks "ergo" in "Quis nos separabit", may read "immo qui et" where the print has "imo et qui resurrexit", and spells "condempnet" and "Tribulacio". The same check read the red folio number "cxvij" on canvas 1 (native box 2350,500,3050,800) and the end of Eph 2:22 followed directly by the red "Ad Romanos" (canvas 1, 2650,1900,4000,2750). The checker had seen a one-line summary of canvas 0 column a first. Gaps: no person has reviewed the reading. No printed Cistercian missal, no complete Cistercian manuscript missal with the common orations, and no diocesan missal of the region was compared. The Corpus Orationum numbers of Debitum and of this form of Conserva were not resolved.

Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

  • Reading order: canvas 1 comes first. Its column b ends "Qui etiam proprio filio suo" (Rom 8:32), and canvas 0 column a continues "[n]on pepercit". The red folio number "cxvij" in the top margin of canvas 1 marks that face as the recto of f. 117 of the original book; the third character might also be a "v" with an ascender.
  • The faded rubric before Eph 1:3 (canvas 0, 3550,2950,4850,3250) reads "Ep(isto)la · [..]tur? in festo [.....] · Ad Ephesios". So Eph 1:3-5 is assigned to a named feast, not to the apostles in general; the feast name is not read.
  • The loose strips (the catalogue's items 214_2 to 214_4; canvas 0, 80,2600,1250,4080) are in the same script. Strip 214_3r has "salomonis · Ceter[orum]", which may be Acts 5:12-13 ("in porticu Salomonis. Ceterorum autem nemo audebat se coniungere illis"), an epistle used for apostles; that is a lead only. Strip 214_2r has a red-touched "Sancti", possibly Hebrews 11:33, not verified; 214_4r shows letter groups only.
  • Other marks: blue pencil "Einband von IVa/F2" and a pasted paper label in a later hand, "Triumphus B. / Mariae / Disputationes / variae philosoph[icae]", both describing the host volume; modern pencil "Stams, STA, Frg. 214_1r" and "214_1v".
  • Not claimed: the date, the origin, the use of the book, that it was made or used at Stams, and any relation to the Kaisheim missal.
