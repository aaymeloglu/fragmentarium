Readings and comparison details

F-2ma4: Pseudo-Aristotle, Secretum secretorum (Latin of Philip of Tripoli), end of Part II to Part III, ed. Steele pp. 109-120

Stams, Frg. 234 is a two-column parchment leaf from a copy of the Secretum secretorum, the pseudo-Aristotelian "Secret of Secrets" addressed to Alexander the Great, in the Latin translation of Philip of Tripoli. It is a medieval handbook of rulership, health, astrology and natural lore. The leaf runs from the end of Part II through the start of Part III, matching Robert Steele's edition (1920), pp. 109-120:

  • Bloodletting, scarification (shallow cutting of the skin) and laxatives timed by the Moon.
  • The prologue on herbs and stones.
  • The "stone that is animal, vegetable and mineral".
  • The Emerald Tablet ("Et pater noster Hermogenes qui triplex est in philosophia...").
  • The chapter on marvellous stones.
  • The opening of the chapter on plants and the planets.

The text begins on the side pencilled "234v" and continues on the side pencilled "234r".

The Fragmentarium record describes the leaf as follows:

  • Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 234, parchment, one fragment item, 329 x 205 mm (measured 328/329 x 200/205 mm by Claudia Sojer), online since 18 September 2021.
  • Two images (01r, 01v), title "x", and no date, description or identification. The text was unidentified in the catalogue.
  • The side pencilled "r" carries a later inscription, written vertically across the lower half of column b and reading bottom to top: "Vrbarium / Mitlkhellner Ambts, auf das Sechzehen- / hundert dreyzehent Jar", followed by a large flourish containing the letters M, F(?) and A. This is the cover title of the 1613 Urbar (land and rent register) of the Mittelkellner office (Mittelkellneramt), presumably of Stams Abbey, so the leaf served as that register's cover.
  • Identification: identified, high confidence.
    • The basis is sustained agreement in wording and sequence with Philip of Tripoli's Latin text in Steele's edition, on both faces and in all four columns.
    • Diagnostic passages:
    • The laxative rule "Si volueris sumere medicinam laxatiuam consistat luna in scorpione uel in libra".
    • The prologue "De qualitatibus et proprietatibus et virtutibus quarundam herbarum ... Et scire debes O alexander".
    • The Emerald Tablet "Ex igne facta est terra. Separa terram ex igne quia subtile dignius est grosso et rarum spisso".
    • The red and white stones that make an army's horses neigh or fall silent.
    • The leaf carries the base text, not the glosses of Roger Bacon that Steele prints with it.
    • Version differences:
    • It reads "planetarum" or "planetis" where Steele has "plantarum" or "plantis", several times.
    • It has "philosophando" where Steele's Emerald Tablet has "prophetando".
    • It keeps the Arabic weight "derham" (dirham) where Steele has "pondus denarii".
    • It has "luna accrescente in lumine" against Steele's "decrescens".
    • It carries as running text a note citing Pliny on the humours waxing and waning with the Moon, which Steele reports only as a footnote ("appears as part of some texts").
    • It omits several clauses by eye-skip, the copyist's eye jumping between repeated words. These are the Mars and Sun plant classes and the palm example, "spectat ad philosophos, cognicio vero operacionum naturalium", and "et ruit de celo in terram. Et inde interficit" in the Emerald Tablet.
    • Chapter openings are marked by the red rubric "Ad item" rather than Steele's chapter headings.
  • Transcription: source-assisted.
    • The image-only first reading covered both faces. On the side pencilled "r" it left the lower half of column b unread under the later inscription.
    • In the source-assisted reading, a research reader reread on the native images:
    • Canvas 1, column a, the top 14 and the last 11 lines.
    • Canvas 1, column b, the top 15 and the last 17 lines.
    • Canvas 0, column a, the top 12 lines.
    • Canvas 0, column b, the top 27 lines.
    • A second research reader read canvas 0, column a, lines 13-25 and the later inscription.
    • The damaged middle of both columns on canvas 1 and the lower half of canvas 0 column b rest on the first reading.
    • Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification:
    • A research reader collated passages from all four columns line by line against Steele's page images (pp. 109, 114-120). The one exception is the Pliny note, compared only with the OCR of Steele's p. 110 footnote.
    • A second research reader independently read canvas 0, column a, lines 13-25 before opening the edition. That reading agreed with Steele p. 118 over 13 lines and found the reading "derham".
    • Passage checked independently: canvas 0, column a, about lines 13-26 (the white and red stones), native box 2760,1330,3980,2250, against Steele p. 118 lines 13-25 (page image); "pondus derham" for "pondus denarii".
    • Gaps: the version is not placed within the Latin manuscript tradition. No human review.

The differences beyond those named under Identification are small:

  • Word order: "secretum maximum secretorum" against "secretorum maximum secretum"; "qualitatibus et proprietatibus" reversed.
  • Small substitutions:
    • "earundem" for "earum"; "capitulis" added; "nostris" omitted.
    • "virtutibus" for "viribus"; "insite" for "indite"; "reperiuntur" for "inveniuntur".
    • "Sunt enim duo" for "Sunt et duo alii"; "uolo ... non latere" for "nolo ... latere".
    • "ab ortu" for "in ortu"; "super faciem aquarum" for "in superficie aquarum".
    • "complexionatur et mutatur" for "complexionatur et imitatur".
  • Spelling: "exercitus" for "excercitus", "dyademata".

The intervening passage on giving medicine by the Sun's sign (Steele p. 110) is present in the damaged middle of canvas 1, column a.

The edition is Robert Steele (ed.), Secretum secretorum cum glossis et notulis, Opera hactenus inedita Rogeri Baconi, fasc. V (Oxford, 1920), on archive.org as operahactenusine05baco. The page images used are p. 109, p. 114, p. 115, p. 116, p. 117, p. 118, p. 119 and p. 120. The Pliny note was compared with the machine-read text of p. 110 only.

Steele edits Philip of Tripoli's Latin translation of the Arabic Sirr al-asrar from Oxford, Bodleian Library, MS Tanner 116, a copy glossed by Roger Bacon. The attribution to Philip rests on the translator's dedication that Steele prints ("Philippus suorum minimus clericorum"). The ascription to Aristotle is pseudepigraphic: the work is falsely presented as his.

Leaf order, other items in the images, and what is not claimed

The text order is the reverse of the pencil foliation. Canvas 1 (pencilled "234v") carries the earlier text, Steele pp. 109-117. Canvas 0 (pencilled "234r") carries the later text, pp. 117-120. The last line of canvas 1, column b, "secundum disposicionem maioris mundi currit hec operacio", runs on into the first line of canvas 0, column a, "[et] propter hoc nom[inatur] Hermogenes triplex in philosophia".

Besides the 1613 cover title, the images show a horizontal fold and surface loss across the middle of canvas 1, and pencil shelfmarks "Stams, STA, Frg. 234r" and "234v".

Not claimed:

  • The date and origin of the manuscript. The hand is a gothic textualis, which was not examined palaeographically.
  • The copy's place in the Latin tradition of the Secretum. The readings "philosophando" and "derham" and the Pliny note may help place it but were not tested against a manuscript classification.
  • The text of the lower half of canvas 0, column b, under the later inscription.
  • Whether the 1613 Mittelkellneramt Urbar survives in the Stams archive.
