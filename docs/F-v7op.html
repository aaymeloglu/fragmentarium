Frg. 91 (F-v7op)

Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

John of Garland (attrib.), Nomina et verba defectiva, with a commentary closely related to the printed one: Greek nouns in -e ('Em dat et en quart[.] ... / Sic primo varia sed pluralia caruere'), 'Dictio quecumque sit si fuerit materialis / Est substans neutrum indeclinabile nomen', and the commentary on impersonal verbs; outer side, edge strip, textualis turn-ins and a glossed Isaiah flap not identified

On the inner side of a paper binding leaf, four verse lines and the commentary of two text blocks follow the Mainz c. 1493 print of Nomina et verba defectiva (GW M13741) in sequence, argument and examples. A research reader compared the first block with the page images of scan 35 (and the Hagenau 1491 print, scan 27) and the lower second block with scan 45. An independent checker compared the upper lines of the second block (impersonal verbs) with the page image of scan 45 and found the same points and examples in the same order, with additional material in the manuscript. The commentary is worded differently throughout and adds a first notandum and examples of impersonals ('interest penitet pudet', 'amatur docetur'), so it is closely related to the printed commentary, not the same text; which way the relation runs is not shown. The first verse line is also Doctrinale v. 339, but the other verses are not in the Doctrinale. Canvas 0, the edge strip and the turn-ins are unread, the Isaiah flap (Is 9:6, 62:3) is identified only as Bible text, and there is no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-v7op: John of Garland (attributed), Nomina et verba defectiva, with a commentary, on Greek nouns in -e, words used as names of themselves, and impersonal verbs

The paper binding leaf of this record carries, on its inner side, a fifteenth-century school copy of the verse treatise Nomina et verba defectiva ("defective nouns and verbs"), which the incunable editions attribute to John of Garland. Each group of verses (lemmata), written in a larger script with small glosses between the lines, is followed by a prose commentary in a smaller script.

One text block covers two passages:

  • the Greek nouns in -e such as Penelope and Pentecoste, with the verses "Em dat et en quart[...] reliquos recto sociamus / Sic primo varia sed pluralia caruere";
  • the rule that any word taken as the name of itself is an indeclinable neuter noun, "Dictio quecumque sit si fuerit materialis / Est substans neutrum indeclinabile nomen".

The other block has the commentary on impersonal verbs and on participles formed from them ("a curro curritur, a sto statur", "iam cantatur missam iam pulsat campanam", "a delector delectat"). The commentary is closely related to the one printed with the verses in the incunable editions. It has the same argument, examples and order, but different wording and some additional material.

The Fragmentarium record describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 91: paper, one item, 327 x 290 mm (282/327 x 235/290 mm), with title "x". It gives no catalogue date and no linked description, and it names no text. The reconstruction summary is by Claudia Sojer. There are five images:

  • Stams_STA_Frg_091_01r (canvas 0) is the outer side of the leaf. It is blackened and torn, with traces of two text blocks. It is not a leather cover.
  • ..._01v_1_ to ..._01v_4_ (canvases 1-4) show the inner side four times, with the folded flaps in different positions.

The catalogue's paper material fits the main leaf. The turn-ins (edges folded over from neighbouring material) along the top and left are parchment strips from an older book in textualis, a formal gothic book script.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the main text on the inner side. It is Nomina et verba defectiva (attributed to John of Garland) with commentary, compared with the Mainz edition of about 1493.
    • Diagnostic basis: the four verse lines read on the leaf stand in the same sequence in the print, and in both blocks the commentary follows the printed commentary in argument, examples and order. In the first block that means the two causes of defectiveness and the objection that other Greek nouns in -e are passed over. In the second it means the two kinds of impersonal, the examples curritur, statur, cantatur missam, pulsat campanam and comestum comedendum, the participles from impersonals in all their cases ("dilecto creaturas diligendum est creatorem"), and a delector delectat.
    • Alternatives considered: the first verse line is also line 339 of Alexander de Villa Dei's Doctrinale ("em aut en quartus; recto reliquos sociamus", with the variant "em dat et en" in some manuscripts), and the impersonal section cites Alexander by name. But "Sic primo varia sed pluralia caruere", "Dictio quecumque sit si fuerit materialis" and "Est substans neutrum indeclinabile nomen" are not in the Doctrinale (Reichling's edition), so the Doctrinale is a source quoted by the work, not the work.
    • Differences: the commentary on the leaf is worded differently from the print throughout. It opens "In illa parte autor ponit defectum? et declinacionem" where the print has "Hic autor ostendit defectum et declarationem", and "Hic consequenter autor ponit [reg]ulam" where the print has "Hic ponit aliam regulam". In the impersonal section the manuscript has material the print lacks:
    • a first notandum on why impersonals lack the first and second persons, which makes the print's "Notandum" its "Secundo notandum";
    • examples of active impersonals (interest, penitet, pudet) and of passive impersonals (amatur, docetur);
    • the question narrowed to "impersonalia passive vocis";
    • the answer worded "est natum venire unum impersonale".

It reads "pulsat campanam" where the print has "pulsatur campanam". In the verses the ending of "quart-" in "Em dat et en quart[...]" is unclear; the print has "quarto", the Doctrinale "quartus".

  • Transcription: the image-only first reading and the source-assisted reading.
    • A research reader reread on the native images of canvas 1 the verse lines and commentary of the first block (three bands) and the second block (two bands). The first block was read before the source was found; the second block was read with the print in hand.
    • An independent checker read the upper seventeen lines of the second block, as described under Verification. Its readings of those lines are used in the table below.
    • The paper is heavily foxed, and many words of the second block remain uncertain.
    • The outer side (canvas 0), the cursive strip at the left edge, the textualis turn-ins and the glossed flap at the bottom rest on the first reading or are unread.

Accuracy has not been measured.

It compared them with the page image of the Mainz print, scan 45. It found the same sequence of points and examples, together with the additional material listed above. That checker had read the research report first, so its reading of the lines with the report's examples was not fully blind.
    • The coordinator compared canvas 1, col. b, lines 1-5 (native box 4150,1380,5650,1800, the notandum on impersonal verbs of active and passive form, "... a quibus verbis nata sunt ... Dicendum ...") with the Mainz c. 1493 print, scan 45 (MDZ bsb00039573): the faded lines agree in sequence where legible; the commentary's wording is close to, not identical with, the print's.

Differences. Besides those listed under Identification:

  • the manuscript has "illorum trium? penolpe et penthecoste" where the print has "istorum nominum penthecoste et penolpe";
  • it has "aliqua?" where the print has "talia";
  • it answers the objection about other Greek nouns differently ("de quibus autor non facit mencionem ... includat et quod similiter declinentur uti ista in littera hic posita");
  • it introduces the figure tmesis (the splitting of a word by another word) with "Ultimo notandum".

The verses preceding "Em dat" in the print ("Costeque penolpe reserabo pentheque coste / Cum tenet e grecus ...") fall in the rubbed top lines of the first block and were not read. Line 5 of the second block shows the vocative and accusative cases only in part.

These differences show that the manuscript commentary and the printed one are related but distinct texts. They do not show whether the manuscript expands the printed commentary, the print shortens a fuller one, or both draw on a common school commentary.

Editions used. Nomina et verba defectiva [with commentary], [Mainz: Peter Friedberg, about 1493], GW M13741, Munich, BSB, 4 Inc.s.a. 844, MDZ bsb00039573, scans 35 (f. 16r) and 45, read as page images. The same work [Hagenau: Heinrich Gran, 1491], GW M13735, BSB 4 Inc.s.a. 843, MDZ bsb00070934, scan 27, read as a page image; it agrees with the Mainz print in the first-block commentary. The MDZ full text was used only to find the pages.

The attribution to John of Garland rests on the catalogue records of these editions in MDZ and GW (Hain 7490-7491). The commentary is anonymous in the prints. The scholarship on the ascription was not reviewed. The Doctrinale was checked in D. Reichling's edition (Berlin, 1893), archive.org, line 339 and its apparatus.

Leaf order, other items and limits

The second text block has a ruled frame: double vertical bounding lines on both sides (the left pair runs the full height of the gap between the blocks, from the top margin into the lower margin) and ruled lines at top and foot. No ruling is visible around the first block, and no vertical fold runs between the blocks. The layout alone does not show whether the blocks are two columns of one page or two pages of an opened sheet. Their texts come from places about ten pages apart in the Mainz print (scans 35 and 45), which is more than one column of this commentary would bridge, so they are not consecutive text; how the leaf was laid out and gathered is not established.

Other items in the images, not identified:

  • The outer side (canvas 0): blackened, with two text blocks, unread.
  • The left edge: a narrow cursive strip with lines running vertically, unread.
  • The top and left turn-ins: parchment strips from an older book in textualis with alternating red and blue flourished initials and red rubrics, unread.
  • The flap folded up from the bottom (canvas 4, native boxes 2650,2650,1500,650 and 4400,2850,2000,450): a cursive text with glosses between the lines. In it a research reader read "[...] Paruulus en(im) natus est nobis filius da[tus] / est nobis Et factus est principat(us) sup(er) humeru(m)" (Vulgate Isaiah 9:6) and "[... glo]rie in manu d(omi)ni (et) diadema regni in manu dei [tui]" (Isaiah 62:3). This shows that the flap carries glossed Isaiah text. It was not examined further.
  • The lower piece (canvas 4, below the green-stained band, about native box 2000,3250,4400,1250): two columns of textualis written perpendicular to the main text, with red and blue paragraph marks and a red-and-blue numeral at the edge. It reads normally when the image is rotated 90 degrees clockwise ("... que sit bonu(m) habitu ...", "... tto mobiles ..."), so it is written text, not an offset. It was not read further.

Not claimed:

  • the date or origin of the copy (the cursive suggests the fifteenth century, but the leaf itself gives no date, and the editions compared are dated by their printers, not by this copy);
  • that John of Garland wrote the verses;
  • who wrote the commentary or which redaction of it the leaf carries;
  • any relation between the binding leaf and the other items in the images.
