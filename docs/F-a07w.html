Fragm. 140 a-b (F-a07w)

Antwerpen, Erfgoedbibliotheek Hendrik Conscience · catalogued as Unidentified canon law text, 1301 – 1325 CE

Aegidius de Fuscarariis, Ordo iudiciarius, cc. LXIX (Conclusio causae, second half) - LXX (Forma sententiae diffinitivae, opening); ed. Wahrmund pp. 124-126

Both pieces come from one two-column leaf and agree with Wahrmund's edition in wording and order over about 33 partial lines on each of two consecutive columns, including the author's opinion on the assessors' fee, the citations in sequence, the red chapter rubric and the model sentence of the archpriest of Bologna. A research reader reread every line on enlarged images and compared them with page images of pp. 124-126, text and apparatus; the coordinator checked passages on both faces of strip A against p. 125. Differences are few (rubric as in the edition's MS B, 'cuius malitie' as in M and V, 'et habebis', and as read 'dicte mulieris' as in P); they are recorded without a claim about the fragment's place in the tradition. Piece B supplies the other half of the first nine lines of strip A's columns, and the catalogue's recto/verso labels for B are opposite to those for A. Some rubbed lines were read only in part; no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-a07w: Aegidius de Fuscarariis, Ordo iudiciarius, cc. LXIX-LXX

The two parchment pieces are identified with high confidence as parts of one leaf of the Ordo iudiciarius of Aegidius de Fuscarariis (died 1289), a Bolognese canonist. An ordo iudiciarius is a manual of court procedure, here for church courts, with model forms for each step. The pieces carry the second half of chapter LXIX, "Conclusio causae" (how the judge consults his assessors and pronounces sentence), and the opening of chapter LXX, the model text of a final sentence given by the archpriest of Bologna in a marriage case.

The Fragmentarium record describes Antwerpen, Erfgoedbibliotheek Hendrik Conscience, Fragm. 140 a-b: four parchment strips, three of them contiguous and joined with tape, 139 x 24 mm, in Northern Textualis, dated 1301-1325, catalogued as an "Unidentified canon law text" with no further description. The subject label fits in the broad sense that the work was written by a canonist for ecclesiastical courts; it is a procedural manual, not a text or commentary of the Corpus iuris canonici. The record names neither author nor work. The catalogue date has not been reassessed.

  • Identification: identified, high. All four faces agree with Ludwig Wahrmund's edition in wording and sequence over about 33 manuscript lines to a column, each line being a part of the column's width. The agreement covers the author's own connecting prose and opinions ("Et puto equum quod primi duo debeant communicare salarium eorum cum isto tertio, ne de ipsorum dolo commodum reportent"), the legal citations in their order, the chapter rubric in red, and the model sentence naming the archpriest of Bologna. The matched layer is therefore the work itself and not a source it quotes. Guilelmus Durantis's Speculum iudiciale, which treats the same two subjects in the same order, and Bernardus Dorna's Summa libellorum, which has a similar rubric, were considered from the subject only and set aside once this text matched; neither was collated. Differences from the edition's text are few and are listed below; several coincide with variants recorded in the edition's apparatus.
  • Transcription: source-assisted selected reading of the lines collated. The image-only first reading covers every line of the four faces and had many words right or nearly right; a research reader reread all lines on enlarged images with the edition at hand, corrected them and expanded the abbreviations from it. The first eight lines of each face of strip A are rubbed and their reading leans on the edition. Accuracy has not been measured.

Differences. The fragment's rubric is "Qualiter formari debeat diffinitiua sententia" where the edition prints "Forma sententiae diffinitivae"; the edition's apparatus gives the fragment's wording as the rubric of its manuscript B (Bamberg, Staatsbibliothek, can. 86). The fragment reads "cuius malitie" for "Cui malitiae" (so the edition's manuscripts M and V), "et habebis" for "habebis", and, as read, "pro parte dicte mulieris" for "pro parte mulieris" (so the edition's P) and "consilia" for "consilium". Between "suspitionis" and "et puto" the lost part of the line has no room for "cum suis similibus", which the early printed edition also omits according to the apparatus; between "concordia" and "sequendo" there is little or no room for "iudex", which the edition's O and P omit. "Debet" for "Debent" in the first line and "sint" for "sunt" are uncertain readings. These coincidences with single witnesses are recorded as observed; they have not been tested against a fuller collation and no claim is made about the fragment's place in the tradition.

The edition compared is Aegidius de Fuscarariis, Ordo iudiciarius, ed. Ludwig Wahrmund, Quellen zur Geschichte des römisch-kanonischen Processes im Mittelalter, vol. III part 1 (Innsbruck 1916), p. 124, p. 125, p. 126, read as page images in the Internet Archive copy. The attribution rests on Wahrmund's edition, which establishes the text from seven manuscripts and an early print whose titles name "Egidius de Fuscarariis, civis Bononiensis, doctor decretorum", and which dates the work to the years after 1262 in its introduction; the introduction was consulted in the scan's machine-read text, and later scholarship on the author was not consulted. The forms themselves point to Bologna: the model sentence is given by "magister Sinigardus, archipresbyter Bononiensis", and the chapter twice cites Bolognese practice.

The leaf, the labels and limits

The two pieces belong to one leaf written in two columns. Fragm. B verso carries the ends of the first nine lines whose beginnings are on fragm. A recto, and fragm. B recto carries the beginnings of the first nine lines whose ends are on fragm. A verso; the catalogue's recto and verso labels for piece B are thus opposite to those for strip A with respect to the original leaf. Piece B has blank parchment above its first line and strip A blank parchment below its last, so top and foot of the column survive, and where both pieces are present only two to four letters are missing between them. The column on fragm. A recto ends "quidam retine[nt istum modum ut in presentia]" and the column on fragm. A verso begins "Astantium summatim", so the two columns are consecutive. Fragm. A recto also has one or two letters from the line ends of a column to its left, which would make its main column the second column of the recto and the column on fragm. A verso the first of the verso; those letters were not identified. A red bracket-shaped stroke stands in the space to the right of the column on fragm. A verso.

No claim is made about the date or origin of the leaf, about the manuscript it came from or the book it was later used to bind, about its relation to the manuscripts used for the edition, or about its value as a witness to the text.
