Readings and comparison details

F-bm2h: Thomas Aquinas, Summa theologiae, IIIa qq. 33-37 and 40-45, table of questions and articles

Stams, Frg. 143 is a strip from a table of questions and articles to the third part of Thomas Aquinas's Summa theologiae. Such a table lists each question under a heading with its number, and gives the articles (the sub-questions) under it. One face covers questions 33 to 37 and the other covers questions 40 to 45. The question headings are in red: "De temptatione x(pi) .q. xli" and "De transfiguratione x(pi) .q. xlv" are examples. The articles are listed under each heading in brown ink, which is now heavily abraded. The headings use the wording of the standard table of the Summa, as printed in the index of the 1879 Paris and Bar-le-Duc edition. It is not the wording of the question prologues in the text itself. The surviving column holds no text of the Summa itself. These leaves do not show whether the table was a separate booklet or a table bound with a copy of the third part.

The Fragmentarium record describes Stams, Frg. 143 as one parchment fragment, 157 x 90 mm (measured 148/157 x 30/90 mm), with two images labelled 01r and 01v. It gives the title "x", no date and no description. So it says nothing about the content, and there is nothing in it to correct.

  • Identification: identified, high confidence, as a table of questions and articles to Aquinas, Summa theologiae IIIa, qq. 33-37 and 40-45.
    • Basis. Eleven question headings survive with their numbers in an unbroken run: xxxiii to xxxvii, then xl to xlv. Each matches the subject of the question with that number in the Summa.
    • Article lines. Where the brown lines can be read, they give the articles of the right question in order. Under q. 35 the article words follow the order of aa. 2, 3, 4, 5 and 7. They name "[Beth]leem", which is the content of a. 7; the prologue says only "de loco" (on the place). So the table names the contents of the articles, as the printed index does. It does not copy the prologue. The article lines are shortened, and too little is legible to show exactly how.
    • Wording. Against the 1879 index, two headings differ.
    • q. 37 reads "[De circum]cisione pu(er)i(?) le|galib(us) ihu obs(er)uat(is)", where the index has "De legalibus circa puerum Jesum observantiis". The manuscript shares puer and Iesus with the index, against the prologue's "Christum". It also names the circumcision and has "obseruatis", not "observantiis".
    • q. 35 is "De natiuitate saluatoris", as in the index. It was written in the margin and linked to the column by a red guide line.
    • Other works. No other work was found to fit the numbered sequence, and none was pursued further.
  • Transcription: an image-only first reading and a source-assisted reading.
    • A research reader reread on the image every red heading on both faces, and the brown lines under qq. 33, 34, 36 and 40.
    • An independent reader reread, with the Summa known from memory but before opening any source, the brown lines between the q. 34 and q. 36 headings (canvas 1, native box 3180,2180,3900,2920). That reader also reread the q. 37 heading.
    • The coordinator read the red headings on canvas 0.
    • The other brown lines are mostly illegible. These include those under qq. 41-45, where the independent reader could make out only "iudeis t(antu)m"(?) and "[gen]tib(us) p(re)dica-"(?) under q. 42. Those lines rest on the first reading or are unread.
    • Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification:
    • A research reader compared the headings and the legible article lines with the Leonine text of the question prologues. That comparison used the Corpus Thomisticum transcription.
    • An independent reader compared the headings, the q. 35 article lines and the q. 37 heading with page images of the 1879 edition. That edition is Sancti Thomae Aquinatis Summa theologica, vol. 6 (Paris and Bar-le-Duc), on archive.org: the index of IIIa qq. 28-45 at pp. V-VI, and the q. 35 prologue at p. 284.
    • The coordinator read the canvas 0 headings, "De mo[do] co[n]uersationis x(pi) .q. xl" through "De transfiguratione x(pi) .q. xlv", on the image. The coordinator found them to run in the order of qq. 40-45 with the index wording.
    • COORDINATOR CHECK: canvas 1 (Stams_STA_Frg_143_01v), the q. 36 article lines "...[manif]estari debu[it] / ...[i]p(s)e se manifestare ... / ...p(er) q(ue) alia manif(estari)" at native box 3200,3100,3900,3420, against the 1879 index of IIIa q. 36 at archive.org, Index p. VI.
    • Gaps: the Leonine edition was seen only as a transcription. Most brown lines on canvas 0 were not read. No other manuscript table of the Summa was compared. Nobody outside this research has reviewed the reading.

Differences.

  • The headings agree with the 1879 index wording, apart from the q. 37 heading described above.
  • Against the Leonine prologues, the headings differ where the index does (qq. 35, 43, 44).
  • "Christi" is abbreviated "x" with a stroke throughout.
  • The article lines are shorter than the index entries. Their exact wording cannot be established from what survives.
  • The reading "mo(do)" in the q. 40 heading is uncertain.

Sources.

  • Leonine text of the question prologues: Corpus Thomisticum, IIIa qq. 27-39 and qq. 40-59 (transcription).
  • Table of questions and articles: Sancti Thomae Aquinatis Summa theologica, vol. 6 (Paris and Bar-le-Duc, 1879), Index pp. V-VI, archive.org page images.
  • The Summa theologiae is Aquinas's undisputed work, and the question numbers are those of all modern editions. The compiler of the table is unknown.

Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

  • Leaf order. By content, canvas 1 (labelled 01v) comes before canvas 0 (01r) in reading order. Questions 38 and 39 fall in the lost part between them, if the two faces are back and front of one leaf.
  • Other items in the images. The pencil notes on the blank part of canvas 0 are modern and were not used.
  • Not claimed:
    • Whether this was a stand-alone table or the table of a copy of the third part.
    • A date or origin for the copy. The script was not dated in this research.
    • The exact wording of the article lines.
    • Any relation to a particular table tradition beyond the agreement of the headings with the 1879 index.
