Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Antiphoner: Office of the Ascension, First Vespers to Matins nocturn I (Pater manifestavi; Elevata est; Dominus in templo; A summo caelo; R. Post passionem, R. Omnis pulchritudo), preceded by O crux splendidior (Inventio Crucis) and In ferventis olei (John ante Portam Latinam)

Strip 257-2 and pieces 3, 4 and 6 are one leaf of a notated antiphoner (piece 4v continues lines of strip 2r). It runs from the end of O crux splendidior and In ferventis olei to the Ascension Magnificat antiphon Pater manifestavi, invitatory and nocturn I, then R. Post passionem (V. Et convescens) and R. Omnis pulchritudo (V. A summo caelo). This is the order of the Klosterneuburg CCl 1018 inventory in the Cantus Database. A research reader compared the readings with Cantus Index texts and that inventory. An independent reader reread strip 2v and found the Post passionem and Omnis pulchritudo text in order on page images of Munich Clm 4306. The ink set off on the wooden board reads the same run as strip 2v. Two items after the Magnificat and the invitatory text are unidentified. A secular-cursus layout on piece 4r leans against Cistercian use but is not settled.

Readings and comparison details

F-17j8: Antiphoner, In Ascensione Domini, First Vespers to Matins nocturn I, preceded by the end of Inventio Crucis and St John before the Latin Gate

A long parchment strip and several small pieces at Stams, used in a binding, come from one leaf of a notated antiphoner. An antiphoner is the choir book of the sung chants of the Office. The leaf runs through the following chants, with music on four-line staves.

One side:

  • the end of the antiphon O crux splendidior cunctis astris (Inventio Crucis, 3 May)
  • the antiphon In ferventis olei dolium missus Joannes apostolus ... illaesus exivit, here at St John before the Latin Gate (6 May)
  • the Ascension: First Vespers Magnificat antiphon Pater manifestavi nomen tuum hominibus, with a two-line red initial, then two items not identified
  • the Matins invitatory "...[venit]e adorem[us]"
  • the first nocturn antiphons Elevata est magnificentia tua super caelos (psalm cue Domine dominus noster) and Dominus in templo

The other side continues Ascension Matins:

  • A summo caelo egressio eius et occursus eius
  • the responsory Post passionem with its verse Et convescens ... sed expectarent promissionem patris
  • the responsory Omnis pulchritudo with the verse A summo caelo egressio

The same chants stand in the same order in the antiphoner Klosterneuburg, Stiftsbibliothek, CCl 1018, ff. 36v-38r, as inventoried in the Cantus Database.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 257: "Parchment and Wood", seven fragment items, 386 x 33 mm (measured on Frg. 257_2 as 381/386 x 19/33 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck). It has the title "x", no date, no linked description, and four images: the wooden board (257_01r, _01v) and the parchment pieces 257-2 to 257-7, recto and verso (257_02r07r, _02v07v). The record does not name the text. Its physical description fits the images.

  • Identification: identified, high, for strip 257-2 and pieces 257-3, -4 and -6.
    • Basis. The chants above are read on the images. Each one agrees with its Cantus Index standard text: CAO 4019, 3234, 4237, 2634, 2410, 1195, 7403/7403a, 7320/7320b (CAO numbers are the standard catalogue numbers for Office chants). They stand in the same sequence as in the Klosterneuburg inventory. The Ascension run is shared by 42 inventories in the Cantus network, so it places the feast and the kind of book, not a particular use. With the two antiphons for 3 and 6 May added, 26 inventories hold the whole run.
    • The 6 May placement. In ferventis olei is sung both at St John the Evangelist (27 December) and at St John before the Latin Gate (6 May). Its place here, between Inventio Crucis and the Ascension, puts it at 6 May. The saints' feasts are interleaved with the Sunday and seasonal Office (the temporale), as in the Klosterneuburg book.
    • Variants. The verse of Omnis pulchritudo is A summo caelo (CAO 7320b). Klosterneuburg CCl 1018 has Nisi ego abiero here. Munich, Clm 4306 agrees with this fragment, and 42 Cantus inventories have A summo caelo.
    • Not identified. Between Pater manifestavi and the invitatory, there are an item ending "...alleluia" and a chant containing "...altum alleluia" followed by a differentia (a melodic ending for the following psalm, written as the vowels Euouae). There are also two red rubrics beginning "S". None of these is identified, and the invitatory text itself is not read.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers all pieces and the board. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's reread at reading size of every text line of strip 2r, pieces 3r, 4r, 4v and 6r, strip 2v, and a mirrored crop of the board's set-off. A second research reader reread the fourteen text lines of strip 2v. Pieces 3v, 5r, 5v, 6v, 7r and 7v rest on the first reading and are largely illegible (stains, holes, adhesive). Several syllables are uncertain (marked below). Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the readings with the Cantus Index standard texts and with the Cantus Database inventory of Klosterneuburg CCl 1018. That inventory was used as data only: the manuscript's page images (manuscripta.at) could not be opened. An independent research reader then reread strip 2v, a stretch not used for the identification, and compared it with page images of Munich, BSB, Clm 4306, ff. 89v-90v. In the same order, they found "[passi]one[m]", "[e]is lo[quens]", "[vi]dent[ibus]", "[eu]m ab o[culis]", "pre[cepit]", "[p]atr[is]", "[e]t no[men]" and "[...]egre[ssio]", corresponding to R. Post passionem, V. Et convescens, R. Omnis pulchritudo and V. A summo caelo. The coordinator did not make a separate comparison for this record. Gaps:
    • No page image of a book that matches the whole run was seen.
    • The red initial and "du n" after A summo caelo on strip 2v are unmatched.
    • The join of strip 2r with piece 4v and the board set-off were checked by one reader only.

Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

  • One leaf. The strip and pieces 3, 4 and 6 come from one leaf.
    • Piece 4v continues lines of strip 2r ("[talent]um mun[di]" then "dulce lignum dulces cla[vos]"; "[sal]ua pre-" then "[pre]sentem cateruam"), so 4v lay just right of the strip on the same side.
    • The other side carries the continuation of Ascension Matins on strip 2v and pieces 3r and 4r.
    • The pencilled "r" and "v" therefore do not mark one side of the original leaf. Side A is 2r, 4v and 6r. Side B is 2v, 3r and 4r.
  • The board. The ink set off onto the wooden board reads, mirrored, "[...]oque", "[...]ntibus", "[...] oculi", "[...]men", "[...]ssio", then a red initial. That is the same run as strip 2v, whose line-ends are faded, so the set-off most probably comes from that strip. This was judged from the readings, not by overlaying the images.
  • Cistercian use: leaning, not settled. On piece 4r, A summo caelo is followed one line later by the responsory sign and "[quadrag]inta ap[parens]". That is the layout of the secular Office, with three antiphons before the first responsory. A monastic (and so Cistercian) Office puts three more antiphons and a versicle in between, as in Clm 4306, f. 89v. This leans against a Cistercian book. It rests on one uncertain reading on a piece made of overlapped slips, and no Cistercian antiphoner was compared, so it is not claimed.
  • Not claimed: the date of the leaf, its origin or use, the binding it came from, or any relation to the Klosterneuburg or Munich books beyond the shared repertory.
