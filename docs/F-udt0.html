Readings and comparison details

F-udt0: Office of the Crown of Thorns (De spinea corona), Matins, monastic arrangement, with lessons from Chrysostom, Bernard and Augustine

This parchment leaf at Stams, later used as a binding wrapper, comes from a monastic breviary, a book of the daily Office with its readings (lessons) written in full. It carries Matins of the Office of the Crown of Thorns: the chapter Egredimini et videte filiae Sion and R. Flos de spina, the invitatory Adoremus regem Christum coronatum, all of the first and second nocturns, and the third nocturn as far as the opening of the homily. The chants and their order are those of the Office of the Crown of Thorns in the Cistercian antiphoner of Rein (Vienna, ÖNB, Cod. 1799**, ff. 228r-231r) and in the printed Historia de spinea corona (Dillingen, 1584). The lessons are taken from John Chrysostom's homily 84 on John in a medieval Latin version (nocturn I, rubric "De commentario beati Iohannis episcopi"), from Bernard of Clairvaux's sermon for Wednesday of Holy Week, §§8-9 (nocturn II, rubric "Sermo beati Bernhardi abbatis"), and, after the gospel John 19:1-2, from Augustine's Tractate 116 on John (nocturn III).

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 216: parchment, one fragment item, 385 x 256 mm (measured as 310/385 x 179/256 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no date and no linked description, with two images labelled 216_01r and 216_01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images.

  • Identification: identified, high. The basis is the run of chants read on both faces: fifteen antiphons and responsories of nocturns I-III, which a Cantus Database sequence search finds complete in only one inventory, Rein, in the same order; and the lessons and rubrics, which agree in wording with the Historia de spinea corona of 1584 read on its page images. Bernard's passages were also compared with the Patrologia Latina text. The leaf matches neither witness exactly. Its nocturn II psalm cues give Domine Deus salutis (Ps 87) for antiphon 5 and Dominus regnavit (Ps 92) for antiphon 6, where Rein and the 1584 print agree in Te decet (Ps 64) and Domine Deus salutis. Its nocturn III versicle Circumdedit me populus iste agrees with the print against Rein (Eris corona gloriae). Its lessons are divided differently from the print's: it splits the print's Lectio I at "Propterea" and Lectio II at "Tu vero", omits the print's Lectio IIII ("Si contumeliam"), takes "Retribuentes mala pro bonis" as lesson vi (print VIII), and has two lessons not in the print (Bernard §9 "O caritas patiens", and "Divinum illud caput ... usque ad cerebrum confixum est", a passage the Legenda aurea quotes as Bernard's, whose source was not found). Wording variants include respuerent (print respicerent), molliat (molleret), ipsa (ipsum), Vide nunc (Vidimus) and cumulat bona (print cumulat mala, Bernard cumulat bona).
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading covers both faces. The source-assisted reading is a research reader's reread at reading size of canvas 0 column a lines 23-31 and column b lines 1-10 and 23-32, and canvas 1 column a lines 1-13 and column b lines 21-33; a second research reader read canvas 0 column b lines 11-22 before opening the print, correcting some words only with the print in view. Other lines rest on the first reading; the lines on the horizontal fold of canvas 0 and the cut or torn line ends are not read. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the chants with the Cantus Database transcription of Rein (not with its page images), and the lessons, rubrics and chants with the 1584 print on its page images (pp. 8-13); a second research reader compared canvas 0 column b lines 11-22 with the print's pp. 9-10 and rechecked the psalm cues on canvas 1. Passage checked independently: canvas 0 column b lines 11-22 (R. Egreditur, lesson iii Tu vero, R. Quid ergo corona spinea), boxes 1900,2800,1650,900 and 1900,3650,1650,950, against Historia de spinea corona (1584) pp. 9-10 (https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/image/v2/bsb10173227_00013/full/1100,/0/default.jpg). The Chrysostom lessons were compared only with the 1584 print, not with an edition of the medieval Latin version. No human review.

The differences in psalm cues, versicle, lesson division and wording are listed under Identification. They show that the leaf is a different arrangement of the same office, not a copy of either witness; they do not affect which office and which texts it carries. "[P]edibus conculcabitur", written with a large initial and the small note "quere supra ante cantica" ("look above, before the canticles") between the last responsory of nocturn II and the canticle antiphon, is not explained.

Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

  • One leaf, two columns of 31-33 lines, gothic book hand, red rubrics, lesson numbers and initials, no music. Canvas 0 precedes canvas 1: canvas 0 ends with R. Spinis Christus coronatus and canvas 1 begins with its verse Spinis coronavit mater impia. As a wrapper the leaf lost the left side of canvas 0 column a and the right side of canvas 1 column b; folds and a darkened turn-in band obscure some lines.
  • The arrangement is monastic: six antiphons and four lessons with four responsories in each of nocturns I and II, and a single antiphon for the canticles in nocturn III. The whole chant run is known only from the Cistercian antiphoner of Rein, and Stams is a Cistercian house, so Cistercian use is an inference; it is not proved, since the 1584 print of unknown order has the same office and the leaf's order matches neither Rein nor the print. The book is not tied to Stams.
  • Not claimed: the date, the origin, the source of lesson viii, or the edition of the Latin Chrysostom.
