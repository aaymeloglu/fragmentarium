MS Typ 270 (F-qvgd)

Cambridge, MA, Harvard University, Houghton Library · catalogued as Unidentified poem on the passion addressed to Mary, 1401 – 1500 CE

'Die Freuden und Schmerzen Mariä' (anonymous German rhymed cycle), Seventh Sorrow; edited by Hamburger 2010 (identification already in the literature)

The Handschriftencensus (no. 22744, work 6704) lists Houghton MS Typ 270 as part of the only known copy of this anonymous cycle, and Hamburger 2010 prints the leaf's 42 lines; the text names itself as the seventh sorrow. A research reader reread the recto and the last 13 verso lines on the image against Hamburger's print, which shows only that this is the leaf he edits. The print has small slips (siebenden, deynen, forscht, war gewar, Dor er, mit in, Do der, Yemer). The grouping with four sister leaves, the Augsburg 1470s localisation and the derivation from Speculum humanae salvationis ch. 44-45 are Hamburger's and the census's and were not tested. Jörg Zobel's 'Von den Schmerzen Mariae' in Cgm 568 was read on images at three places and is a different poem; no second copy is known.

Readings and comparison details

F-qvgd: 'Die Freuden und Schmerzen Mariä' (rhymed, anonymous), the Seventh Sorrow

The leaf carries the Seventh Sorrow from an anonymous German cycle in rhymed couplets on the Sorrows and Joys of Mary. This identification is not new: it was made in the scholarship and is missing from the catalogue record. Jeffrey F. Hamburger edited the text of this leaf in full in 2010, and the Handschriftencensus (the census of medieval German manuscripts) lists Houghton MS Typ 270 under no. 22744, work 6704, "'Die Freuden und Schmerzen Mariä' (gereimt)", as part of the only known copy.

The Fragmentarium record describes one paper leaf at Harvard University, Houghton Library, MS Typ 270, from Germany, dated 1401-1500, with 42 lines of German verse in bastarda (a script mixing book hand and cursive) and a coloured drawing of the Pietà on the recto, under the title "Unidentified poem on the passion addressed to Mary". The description fits the leaf. It gives no bibliography and does not mention the Seven Sorrows, the edition, the census entry or the related leaves.

  • Identification: identified, high. It rests on three things. The Handschriftencensus entry names Houghton MS Typ 270 under this work. Hamburger's edition prints the text of this very leaf, and the image agrees with his print apart from the slips listed below; that agreement shows the leaf is the one he edits and nothing more. The text names its own place in the series: "Ich man dich magt erbarmerin / des sibenden grosses schmertzen dein". The poem is anonymous and no second copy is known. Three further points are Hamburger's and the census's, reported here and not tested: that four other leaves (Washington, National Gallery of Art, 2004.115.1 and 2004.115.2; two last recorded with the dealers Laurence Witten, 1980, and Jörn Günther, 1997) come from the same book; that the book was made in Augsburg in the 1470s; and that the cycle derives from chapters 44-45 of the Speculum humanae salvationis. On the last, the leaf's numbering does not follow chapter 44, where the deposition is the sixth sorrow. What was checked: the rectos of the two Washington leaves were read from the gallery's open-access images at reduced size (the gallery's catalogue pages could not be opened), and they show the same layout and the same formula ("Der sechsten ser erman ich dich"). One alternative was tested on images and excluded: Jörg Zobel's unedited 'Von den Schmerzen Mariae' (Munich, Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Cgm 568, ff. 250vb-253ra), written in Augsburg in 1468-70, is a different poem. The versified 'Sieben Freuden Mariens' prayers, which use a similar "ich man dich" formula, were not compared.
  • Transcription: image-only first reading by an isolated reader, covering all 42 lines, and a source-assisted reading. A research reader reread the 16 lines of the recto and the last 13 lines of the verso on the image with Hamburger's print open; verso lines 1-13 rest on the first reading. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the image of the leaf with Hamburger's printed text (publisher's PDF, pp. 74 and 77), read the Handschriftencensus entry, and read the opening, one later column and the close of Zobel's poem from the page images of Cgm 568. The coordinating model read the recto text on the manuscript image (lines 2 to 16, "des sibenden grosses schmertzen dein ... Joseph ain reicher gerechter man ... vnd wolt in legen in sein grab") and found it as given; it read the saved Handschriftencensus pages for no. 22744 and the work entry, which name Houghton MS Typ 270 under this title, and the passages of Hamburger's article that describe the leaf, and did not collate his printed text line by line. Gaps: the source is a modern edition of the same leaf, so there is no independent witness to compare; the Verfasserlexikon and Harvard's own catalogue record were not consulted; the versos of the Washington leaves were not seen. No human review.

The differences are slips in the print, not variants of another copy: "siebenden" for sibenden, "deynen" for deynem, "forscht" for forcht, "war" for wart, "Dor" for Do, "mit in" for mit im, "Do der" for Do er, and at verso line 14 "Yemer vnd mer" where the leaf has "ye mer vnd mer". The leaf's "aromati" (for Arimathea) is printed as it stands.

Edition: Jeffrey F. Hamburger, "Typology Refigured: Marian Devotions Derived from the Speculum humanae salvationis", Harvard Library Bulletin 21.1-2 (2010, issued 2011), 73-94, open access; the text is at pp. 74 and 77, the related leaves at pp. 85-91. Repertory: Handschriftencensus no. 22744 and work 6704, which records no other manuscript and no author. The title is the census's description of an anonymous text; Hamburger calls it an independent, free and abridged German verse rendering of the final section of the Speculum that he could not find in any other copy.

Zobel's poem was read in the digitised Cgm 568, scans 544, 548 and 549. It opens "Ach bekrenckt hertz mit unseld", counts sheddings of Christ's blood ("Das sechst vergiessen des hailgen pluts ... Das sibent ist") and closes with the poet naming himself. Only those three places were read.

Leaf order and limits

The text runs from the recto, under the drawing, to the verso without a break in sense. What preceded and followed in the book is not on this leaf; the sequence of the related leaves is Hamburger's reconstruction. Nothing else is in the images.

Not claimed here: the date and place of the leaf (Hamburger: Augsburg, 1470s; the census: third quarter of the fifteenth century or about 1470; the catalogue: fifteenth century, Germany); that the five leaves were one book; the dependence on the Speculum humanae salvationis; or that no other copy exists, only that none is recorded and that phrase searches of printed books found none.
