Second-model review - 20 September 2026

The current results are 8 identified, 12 partial, and 2 not identified. The eight identifications have strong support in the passages checked. F-ss0e has a convincing parallel in Cherubino's sermon collection, but attribution and manuscript dating remain unresolved.

Original research was performed by Claude with lead-LLM image spot-checks. Codex reviewed all 22 reports, their index entries, and the methodology at commit f834e22681490bf640801bb74797805a83e3a206. The current reports incorporate that review's corrections. These are LLM checks, not human palaeographic verification.

Scope and evidence

For the nine previously identified entries, the review inspected manuscript-image samples and compared their wording with digitized editions. Some scans were retrieved afresh and others were preserved from the original research. Bernardus was checked against the edition's OCR, with manuscript-image samples; its printed page images were not inspected in this review.

The remaining thirteen reports received an audit of their evidence, reasoning, and consistency, not a fresh complete image-to-edition collation. This review does not certify every transcribed letter, all passage endpoints, physical leaf order, manuscript dating, literary dependence, exact recension, or witness novelty.

Changes to the partial conclusions

  • F-h8oz: the supplied Durantis comparison contains substantial rewording. It is a close parallel, not eleven verbatim lines. Recension, earlier source, adaptation, and common source remain possibilities. Absence of a Sextus citation does not establish a pre-1298 composition date.
  • F-jaob: the reported pseudo-Bonaventure verse and Hus prose parallels are useful, but do not establish an intermediary compendium or the direction of borrowing. Any claim that the fragment predates Hus is conditional on the catalogue dating.
  • F-ahiy: some source quotations were checked, others traced indirectly, and several remain unchecked. Jean de Fayt remains a candidate. The Cicero parallel involves a temperantia/modestia difference that needs resolution.
  • F-nei7: Sentences I has better support than the exact distinction, school, or author. The widespread unmarked-sheep example does not establish direct borrowing from William of Auvergne. A later question title does not date the origin of the topic.
  • F-szxp: Arnoldus Saxo requires comparison with Oxford, Bodleian, Lat. misc. e.34. Neither an exact attribution nor a current complete witness census supports a second-witness claim yet.
  • F-072h: the Aphorisms sequence is stronger than the proposed Galenic table identification. Works, numbering, and translation in that table need edition-level checking.
  • F-b8xt: Aquinas parallels support a Thomistic connection; Versor, Cologne, and Louvain remain hypotheses.
  • F-nxl7: the Parva naturalia subject is supported; exact base text and author remain unresolved.
  • F-0kql / F-hcpv: Gregory was already recognized in the catalogues. Exact recension, Greek intermediary, and physical reconstruction require further comparison. BHL 3664 remains to be read in full; a differing recorded ending cannot exclude it. No estimate of missing leaves is secure from comparison with a different recension alone.
  • F-3yl2: distinguish the catalogue's existing recognition of Aquinas q.10 a.11 from the proposed additional source loci and legal interpolations. The compiler remains anonymous.
  • F-8bin / F-2a37: remain unidentified. The poem's composition date needs diagnostic evidence; the worn commentary does not supply enough secure text for an attribution.

Standards used in the current reports

Sustained agreement in distinctive prose can establish a text identification while allowing recorded textual variants. A parallel within a compilation may establish a source connection without identifying its compiler or date. These conclusions are recorded separately.

Negative searches mean that no exact match was located in the resources actually searched. They do not establish unprinted status or a new witness. Catalogue descriptions are refined individually; there is no claim that every original genre or date was wrong. The research remains available for specialist checking, with transcriptions, comparison tables, and unresolved questions retained.
