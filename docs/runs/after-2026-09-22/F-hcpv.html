Partial · medium identification confidence last examined 2026-09-22

Ending of a Latin life of Gregory the Illuminator; exact Latin recension unresolved

F-hcpv — Leuven, Archief van de Abdij van Park, VIIIB20/52

  • Identification: Partial; medium confidence in a substantial source-family connection. This is the ending of a Latin life of Gregory the Illuminator. Its distinctive princely retinue, journey to Rome, and return of Gregory's son from Nicaea closely follow the ordered narrative of the longer Agathangelos Vita printed in Acta Sanctorum, September VIII, chapter XV, §§163–171. The exact Latin work or recension remains unidentified: the wording is substantially different, and the manuscript's funeral and royal coda differ from the tested printed endings. Confidence applies to the diagnostic narrative connection, not an exact Latin attribution.
  • Transcription: The preserved image-only reading covers only seven cropped rows. The separate source-assisted record revisits that passage and supplies selected readings from the six written columns. Uncertain names, abbreviation expansions, and the burial place remain explicit. No complete fresh transcription or accuracy score is claimed.
  • Verification, 22 September 2026: A Codex reviewer inspected manuscript images and the actual online Latin text of the longer Vita and the anonymous Vita altera BHL 3664. The comparison used the site's HTML transcription of the edition, not print facsimiles. The retinue, council, and death passages provide separate checks, but all were exposed in the old report and are not held-out evidence. No human palaeographic review was performed.

Catalogue details carried from Fragmentarium: “Legendary. Chapter 1–5 of an unidentified legend on St Gregory the Illuminator”; probably Italy, fourteenth century, Southern Textualis, parchment, one bifolium, 320 × 234 mm. The saint was already recognized in the catalogue. Those dating and provenance assignments were not independently tested in this review.

The six column images were inspected for the specified narrative passages; only selected lines were transcribed anew. The newly usable comparison is Acta Sanctorum, 30 September, Gregory section, reached from the September VIII index. The actual page transcription was downloaded and read locally. Its chapter heading is CAPUT XV, despite the earlier lead calling it XVI. The checked loci are:

  • Longer Greek Vita's Latin translation, chapter XV, §§163–173: Constantine; retinue and Rome; gifts and return; Nicaea; continued pastoral care; authorial conclusion. The Greek text was visible during extraction but was not independently collated. Relevant editorial notes concerning Eusebius/Silvester and death traditions were also read.
  • Vita altera, headed Auctore anonymo, Ex codice Ms. bibliothecæ Barberinæ, and BHL 3664, §§38–43; especially its closing §§41–43. These are now actual textual comparisons, replacing the previous inability to consult the source.

The edition's Latin translation of the Greek Vita is a comparison aid, not automatically the medieval Latin text transmitted by this fragment. No Armenian edition, Lagarde facsimile, BHL supplement, or additional Latin witness was consulted in this review.

Comparison: substantial connection, unresolved Latin text

Manuscript passageActual comparison and implication
h0a Constantine encomium; h0b:1–14 retinue and departureLonger Vita §§163–166 supplies a distinctive ordered assemblage: the districts Norsireorum, Assyriæ, Aruastarum, Masachi; Angelitæ; crowning Aspet; Macitarum, Synistarum, Restunitarum, Sapianarum and Pascapeteorum; seventy thousand soldiers; Ararat and Valarsapat; land/sea journey to Italy and Rome. Several manuscript names are uncertain, but the detailed cluster and sequence provide a stronger connection than a shared saint or generic journey. The Latin is compressed/rephrased and not a sustained verbal match.
h0b:15–32, h1a Roman reception and storiesThe longer Vita §§166–168 has reception, recounting Gregory's sufferings and the king's transformation, friendship, gifts, and return. Its receiving churchman is Eusebius, while the manuscript names Silvester, and its Constantine narrative includes leprosy and baptism by Silvester. The edition's note p discusses the Silvester substitution in a separate tradition; that note does not identify this manuscript's Latin recension.
h1b:9–29, h2a:1–5 Nicaea and pastoral careLonger Vita §169 sends Arostaces to Nicaea and returns him with the canons to the king and Gregory. The manuscript's Erostacius/Erostacis performs the same linked acts, in substantially different prose, following an expanded list of council participants. The vigilant, sleepless pastoral care after this episode corresponds to §170, but its biblical language is not diagnostic alone.
h2a:6–30, h2b funeral and royal codaThe manuscript describes summons to son/king/wife/sister, testament, burial in a named city, church and miracles, succession, and the king's pig/angel contrast. Longer Vita §§171–173 closes with a summary of spiritual work and an authorial statement, without this detailed funeral/coda. Its editorial death note discusses a different solitary-death tradition; this does not turn that note into the manuscript's source.
The same ending versus BHL 3664, Vita altera §§41–43The actually read printed-text transcription moves through withdrawal, the king's penitential visits, written sermons, and a peaceful death. It ends with a feast date and doxology. This is materially different from the manuscript's funeral and final royal passage. The comparison concerns this printed ending, not every witness associated with BHL 3664.

The shorter Vita's generic death wording, including senex & plenus dierum ad Dominum in pace migravit, is not sufficient to identify this version. Nor do the longer Vita's detailed narrative parallels establish that its printed Latin translation is the manuscript text or prove direct translation from Greek. The evidence supports a substantial Agathangelos-family connection while leaving the exact Latin recension unresolved.

Result and remaining work

The principal change is that BHL 3664 and the longer Vita have now been compared at actual passages, rather than inferred from a desinit or declared inaccessible. The earlier search failures are not exclusions and do not show that this version is unedited. No author, translation date, textual stemma, or novelty claim is established.

The proposed relationship to F-0kql remains a research lead from the previous report. This reviewer did not perform a fresh side-by-side manuscript comparison. The former common-manuscript and innermost-bifolium/quire conclusions are not certified here; matching saints or narrative episodes alone cannot establish them. The older complete-looking transcript remains in Git history; the preserved raw reading is untouched.

The next discriminating step is to locate and compare Latin witnesses containing both the Rome/Nicaea sequence and this detailed funeral/royal coda. The present scoped result is partial and explicit: narrative connection supported, exact Latin text not identified. No further repeated no-hit searching or inaccessible-source retries are needed to report that conclusion. No outreach was performed.
