Alphabetical compilation of moral authorities, with Moralium dogma philosophorum and patristic source parallels

F-ahiy — Brugge, Archief Grootseminarie Brugge, Ms. 95/130

Identification: partial, medium confidence. An alphabetical collection of auctoritates, with source passages now checked in the Moralium dogma philosophorum, Ambrose, Jerome and Augustine. The compiling work and compiler remain unidentified. Shared quotations do not distinguish an original compilation from an adaptation of another florilegium.

Transcription: selected source-assisted excerpts, reviewed against manuscript images by a Codex model on 22 September 2026. Both full sides were inspected for layout; the detailed comparisons cover the upper Modestia band, two adjacent Jerome excerpts under Monachus, and one Mors item. No complete transcription, accuracy measurement, or human palaeographic review is claimed.

Verification: manuscript images were compared with electronic Latin texts; the Moralium dogma comparison used the extracted text of Holmberg's edition. A separate Augustine passage on side B provides a further source-layer check, not confirmation of a proposed compiler. The standard Manipulus florum index and its Monachus entry were also checked. Their shared wording is evidence of circulation, not an attribution of this leaf.

Object and images

The Fragmentarium record and cached IIIF catalogue metadata describe an upper flyleaf, one partial parchment leaf, 285 × 199 mm, Latin, Northern Textualis, dated 1351–1400. The catalogue title is “Unidentified encyclopaedia”. The date and script description are catalogue statements, not newly established here.

The cached reading images are rotated 180° relative to these originals. Both sides have two main columns. Side A visibly contains Modestia, Moyses, Mollis, Monachus, Mons and Monstra; side B begins with Mora, Morbus and Mors. This makes A-before-B the natural textual order, but the neutral side labels are retained. Later writing in the lower margin of side B has a different orientation; it is not part of the excerpts below.

The frozen image-only reading was preserved before source assistance. It covers only the crop subsequently identified as aL0.jpg, not a whole column. Its numerous gaps and provisional words remain unchanged. The source-assisted record preserves the present revisions and their sources separately.

Selected manuscript readings and comparisons

Parentheses mark proposed abbreviation expansions; ? marks uncertainty. These are selected excerpts, with physical line breaks retained. Crop names identify the cached derivatives recorded in the assisted reading, not additional witnesses. The crops overlap the full-side images.

Side A, left column: Modestia

aL0.jpg is the same band as image-001.png in the frozen packet. Its clipped first row counts as line 1. Crop lines 4–7 below correspond to the sixth through ninth extant physical lines of side A's left column, counted from the top of the full rotated image. The opening ia continues Modest- at the end of crop line 3.

aL0.jpg:04 ia tocius vite ornatu(m) tollit. id(em) e. Et de off(ici)o ei(us)? et utilita-
aL0.jpg:05 te. lib(ro) de dog(ma)te ph(ilosoph)or(um) [reference uncertain]. Modestia cultu(m) et motu(m) et om(n)em
aL0.jpg:06 n(ost)r(a)m occupat(i)onem ult(ra) defectu(m)? et cit(ra) excessu(m) sistit. unde
aL0.jpg:07 Modestia est in a(n)i(m)o (con)tine(n)s mod(er)atio cupiditatu(m). tullio? 2?

The cultum et motum definition has a direct parallel in Moralium dogma philosophorum, ed. John Holmberg (1929), Latin p. 42, “De modestia”, lines 1–2. The edition reads “Modestia est cultum et motum et omnem nostram occupationem ultra defectum et citra excessum sistere.” The manuscript apparently omits est and ends with sistit. Its earlier reference visibly names a liber de dogmate philosophorum, although the numeral and its exact attachment remain uncertain. The source assists the proposed defectum and citra, but the former remains uncertain on the image. This replaces a previously tentative decentiam/contra reading; it does not justify silently importing the edition's sentence. The comparison used extracted edition text; the attempted PDF screenshot was unavailable.

The preceding tocius vite ornatum tollit corresponds to Ambrose's wording about modesty at De officiis I.45, §218: “eam quae totius vitae ornatum attollit modestiam”. This is the locus of the checked parallel; I.18 is not the correct location for these words. The manuscript's visible tollit has not been replaced with attollit. The following definition, beginning Modestia est in animo, and its “Tullius” attribution remain unresolved here; the apparent modestia/temperantia issue has not been settled by inspecting Cicero's original text.

Side A, right column: the final Monachus quotation before Mons

In aR1.jpg, count the clipped top row as line 1. Lines 5–7 lie in the upper third of side A's right column and end immediately above the Mons heading. Line 5 begins by completing the previous quotation; the new one starts at prima.

aR1.jpg:05 mu(n)dus ante me(n)dicos. J(e)r(onimus)? ad heliodor(um) x. ep(istola)? xii? g. prima v(ir)tus mo-
aR1.jpg:06 nachi est (con)te(m)pne(re) ho(m)i(nu)m iudicia. et s(em)p(er) ap(osto)li recordari dice(n)tis
aR1.jpg:07 Si ad(huc) ho(m)i(n)ib(us) placere(m) x(rist)i s(er)vus no(n) esse(m). J(e)r(onimus)? ad pa(m)machiu(m).

A boxed reference beside the following Mons line is read separately as xi? ep(istola)? vi? a.; those uncertain numerals should not be normalized to modern letter numbering.

The quotation was compared with Jerome, Ep. 66.6, ad Pammachium, in the electronic Latin transcription. The manuscript's prima virtus monachi est changes the source's order Prima virtus est Monachi. Its placerem agrees with the main reading displayed there, which also records placere vellem as an alternative. The agreement extends through the judgment-of-men clause and the apostolic quotation; the visible addressee supports the source connection.

The preceding paragraph in the same crop, about wealthy monks under a poor Christ, was also compared with Jerome Ep. 60.11 on that page. The sequence concerning the poor Christ, rich devil, wealthy church members and their earlier poverty agrees, but the manuscript compresses and alters the wording: sunt seculares and possident omnia differ from the displayed fuerant saeculares and possideant opes. This second connection confirms the use of Jerome material, without identifying the surrounding compilation or certifying every letter of the preceding paragraph.

Side B, right column: a summary of Augustine on death

bR0.jpg covers the top of side B's right column. Its first main-text row counts as line 1; the quoted item occupies lines 2–3. The cached crop script records the rectangle (1260, 180)–(2420, 900) in the 2884 × 4000 rotated full image.

bR0.jpg:02 Q(uo)d mala mors no(n) e(st) q(uam) vita bona p(re)cessit. na(m) mala(m) mortem
bR0.jpg:03 facit q(uo)d ea(m) sequitur. no(n) q(uo)d p(re)cedit. i de civit(ate) x? d. g. [next item omitted]

The source is Augustine, De civitate Dei I.11, Latin Library text, with the same passage checked in the Logic Museum's Latin text. Augustine begins “Mala mors putanda non est, quam bona vita praecesserit” and explains that what follows death makes it bad. The manuscript makes a Quod summary, omits putanda, changes word order, and adds non quod precedit. Its uncertain chapter numeral is retained as x?, not silently corrected to XI. This is an independently located passage on the other side, but the reader already knew the earlier report's Augustine proposal; it is an additional assisted check, not blind or held-out verification.

What this establishes about the compilation

The alphabetical lemmata, multiple named authorities, short quotations, Quod summaries and internal references support describing the leaf as an alphabetical tabula or florilegium of authorities. They do not establish a particular compiler, order, institutional affiliation, or direct use of the original authors.

A concrete comparison with the Electronic Manipulus florum Project's index shows Modestia, Monachus and Mors consecutively in that part of its standard edition. It lacks the intervening lemmata visible here. Nevertheless, Monachus f contains the same prima virtus monachi saying and attribution to Jerome. Its surrounding extract arrangement differs from the inspected manuscript sequence. This supports rejecting a straightforward copy of the standard edited sequence; it does not exclude adaptation, rearrangement, shared sources or dependence. The Modestia entry did not load and was not compared.

No actual candidate entry by Jean de Fayt, from Flores paradisi, or from a Kilwardby-related tabula was examined in this pass. Likewise, no comprehensive comparison against Catholicon, Papias, Hugutio, Brito, biblical Distinctiones, Vincent or Bersuire was made. Genre differences may guide a search, but they are not checked exclusions. The old categorical exclusions are therefore not maintained.

The compilation remains unresolved. A useful next comparison would seek the consecutive excerpts and the manuscript's distinctive reference system, particularly the Jerome addressees and boxed numerals, in an actual candidate witness. Additional matches to Jerome or Augustine alone would not resolve that question. Most of both sides, especially the natural-philosophical entries and the lower Mors material, still lacks a fresh word-by-word source comparison. No claim of unprinted status, recension, novelty, or full transcription accuracy follows from this result.
