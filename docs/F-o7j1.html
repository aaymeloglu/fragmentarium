Readings and comparison details

F-o7j1: Hostiensis (Henricus de Segusio), Lectura in Decretales, on X 1.31.8-11

These two long parchment strips in Stams are from a two-column copy of the Lectura of Hostiensis (Henricus de Segusio, died 1271), his commentary on the Decretals of Gregory IX (the Liber Extra, cited as X), on book I, title 31, De officio iudicis ordinarii. One face carries the commentary on chapter 8, Ad reprimendam; the other carries the end of the commentary on chapter 9, Duo simul, the whole of chapter 10, Quod sedem, and the opening of chapter 11, Pastoralis. In the Strasbourg edition of 1512 this is vol. 1, fols. 168v-170r. The strips are lengthwise pieces of one column, and the text runs across the cut between them.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 225: parchment, two fragment items, 409 x 89 mm (measured on Frg. 225_1 as 324/409 x 20/89 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description, with two images labelled 225_01r02r and 225_01v02v, each showing both strips side by side. The record does not name the text or its kind. Its description fits the images: two narrow vertical strips, stained across the middle and taped at top and bottom.

  • Identification: identified, high. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and order with the commentary and its legal citations in the 1512 edition over five passages on the two faces, and over a further band of 14 lines on face A, with a blank line after "[pri]uilegium speciale ut hic" where the edition ends chapter 9. The commentary calls Innocent IV "d(ominus) n(oster)" (our lord), cites Goffredus of Trani as "Gof.", and argues in the first person ("hoc solum puto esse de mente quicquid alii scripserint"), as Hostiensis does. Innocent IV's Apparatus supplies arguments but differs in wording throughout and is far shorter on Quod sedem; the Glossa ordinaria of Bernard of Parma is a short gloss in other words; Antonius de Butrio paraphrases this doctrine in the third person and names it as Hostiensis's ("Hosti. dicit"); Goffredus's Summa is cited by the text, so cannot contain it; Hostiensis's own Summa aurea is arranged by title and does not comment on chapter words, and its text was not tested. Johannes Andreae's Novella was tested on face A and excluded on four points, where the fragment sides with Hostiensis: the fragment's first-person "hoc solum puto esse de mente" against the Novella's "Host. dicit, quod ... putat hoc esse de mente iuris"; its "in hoc concordat d. n." and the sentence "subaudiendum ... nolle propter rationem", against "unde dicebat Innocen." without that sentence; its citation of "per tuas, ubi loquitur de appellante", which the Novella lacks; and its "secundum d. n. Obiectum ... que sit ratio" after "§ quod si", where the Novella goes on "secundum Innoc. Ego puto ...". The commentaries of Abbas antiquus and Vincentius Hispanus were not tested. The copy differs from the 1512 edition in small readings: "ex antiqua consuetudine" for "de antiqua consuetudine", "fuit" for "fuerit" twice, "sic" for "sit", ".ı̄." (infra) for "et" before "de sen. ex. sacro", and, uncertainly, "uoluit" for "voluerit" and "[respo]ndetur" for "respondet".
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading of 31 October 2026 and the source-assisted reading. A research reader reread on the native images the upper and lower bands of face A across both strips and the upper half of face B, and corrected the first reading where the edition's page image made the reading certain (for example "Dicas q(uod) in his", not "dicunt quidam", and "de fo. compe.", not "de eo. com."); those corrections were made with the edition in hand. An independent checker read the stained middle band of face A, 14 lines. The stained band of face B, the far edges of the columns and the marginal notes rest on the first reading or are unread. Many readings are phrases rather than whole lines, and gaps at the cut and in the stain are supplied from the edition in brackets. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared five passages, three on face A and two on face B, with page images of the 1512 Strasbourg edition (scans 338-341), after locating them through snippets of the Venice 1581 edition. An independent checker read canvas 0, face A, the stained middle band across both strips, native box 1180,2450,1670,950 (14 lines), on the image before opening the edition, and compared it with the 1512 edition, vol. 1, fol. 169r col. a, on the page image (scan 339). Everything legible agrees in the same order for about 14 lines, from "ipsum absoluere ... episcopo diocesano" to "vinculum contrahit", apart from the small readings listed above; many of the checker's first readings were wrong in detail and were settled on the page image ("quod semper [debet] nolle propter rationem", "certum est tenere", "remittendus"). The checker found that the words run across the cut line after line ("siue n|on", "tu|as", "remittendu|s"), so the strips are pieces of one column; that face A reads "vbi ⁊ h(oc) ho(die) optime (com)p(ro)bat(ur)", with the edition's "hodie"; and that the reading before "nec ob(stat)" is probably the edition's "exhibere", not "exhiberet", though not settled. The checker also compared face A with the Novella (Venice 1612, scan 499) on a page image; the first of the four points rests on a passage the checker did not reread. Face B was compared only by the research reader. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

Leaf order, other items, and limits

Face A carries chapter 8; the rest of chapter 8 and chapter 9 would have stood in the lost part of the leaf; face B carries the end of chapter 9, chapter 10 and the opening of chapter 11, so the order of the text is consistent with one leaf. The two strips are lengthwise pieces of the same column, with only a few letters lost at the cut. The research reader observed that strip 2 also keeps the line openings of the leaf's other column on face A and its line endings on face B; the independent checker did not test this.

Other items: a manicule (a pointing-hand mark) with a note in the margin of face B and a reddish note in the margin of face A, both unread. A line near the foot of face A's right strip, "...gem francie si personal...", probably answers to the edition's "videtur papam et regem Franciae si personas..." after passage 4, but was not collated.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the copy (the work was finished by about 1271), its place among the manuscripts of the Lectura, the reading of face B's stained band, and the exclusion of Abbas antiquus and Vincentius Hispanus.
