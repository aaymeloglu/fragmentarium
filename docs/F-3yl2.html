Natural and civil justice

The preserved image-only response covers only the top three rows of a larger band. It contains gaps and erroneous conjectures. The selected source-assisted reading of the larger upper crop includes:

… repugnat civili iusti(ci)e, sic filiu(m) carente(m) / usu r(ati)o(n)is auferre parentib(us) repugnat n(atur)ali iusti(ci)e …

These are parts of physical rows 2–3 of that crop. Proposed expansions appear in parentheses. The Corpus Thomisticum text, q.10 a.12 corpus, paragraph39269 reasons from civil ownership to the natural relation between parent and child, then distinguishes a child's condition after attaining use of free choice. It says contra iustitiam naturalem esset si puer … a cura parentum subtrahatur, not the fragment's compact filium carentem usu rationis auferre parentibus. This is a close argument/wording connection with condensation, not a verbatim copy.

A separate manuscript crop centred on the legal references reads across its first two rows:

… ut l(ege) finali C(odice) de i(m)puberib(us) et aliis su[b]- / stitutio(n)ib(us) …

The following causa/quaestio numeral is uncertain and is not expanded into an exact canon here. The explicit Codex reference does not occur in the compared Aquinas paragraph. It supports a modified or compiled text; it does not identify a particular compiler or establish legal training biographically.

Additional check: heresy

The bottom front-cover crop shows, in its final two text rows, the selected sequence:

… heresis grece ab electio(n)e dicitur … / … q(ue) sibi melior videtur. xxiiii q. iii. Heresis.

The same electronic edition, q.11 a.1 argument1, paragraph39276 cites Jerome and Decretum C.24 q.3: haeresis Graece ab electione dicitur … eligat disciplinam quam putat esse meliorem. The fragment differs in its phrasing, including the apparent doctrinam and que sibi melior videtur. That quotation is shared canonical material, so by itself it would not prove dependence on Aquinas. Its placement after the natural-justice discussion supports the proposed sequence.

Result and limits

The Aquinas-related source layer is supported; the compiling work remains unresolved. The manuscript's condensed prose and additional legal reference prevent this sample from being described as an exact copy of the Summa. No named compiler is established or excluded by these comparisons. A comparison locating this distinctive condensation together with its legal references would be needed for a full identification. The remaining lines, date, provenance and physical reconstruction require separate evidence.
