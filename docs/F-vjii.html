Frg. 142 (F-vjii)

Stams, Stiftsarchiv · catalogued as x, not given

Sermons: Mandatum novum do vobis ('Homo, ecce forma vite tue'; listed by Schneyer under Alan of Lille, no. 73; = BnF NAL 999, ff. 173v-174r) and De sobrietate et IV vigiliis (anonymous; = Saint-Omer 86, ff. 164v-165r); a third, Marian sermon, Fecit Deus duo luminaria magna, unidentified

A research reader collated verso cols. 3-4 and recto cols. 1-2 with page images of BnF NAL 999, ff. 173v-174r, and recto cols. 3-4 with the page image of Saint-Omer 86, f. 164v, which has the same rubric and no author. An independent checker read verso col. 1, lines 1-7, before seeing the source and matched every line in sequence on the page image of Saint-Omer 86, f. 164v col. b to f. 165r col. a, confirming the leaf order; it also found on the page image of NAL 999, f. 173v, that the strip's sentence 'Hec lux [..] sub umbra, mel sub cera, et ueritas sub figura' is absent there, so the strip is the same sermon but not NAL 999's text form. Schneyer I lists Mandatum novum under Alan of Lille, no. 73 (NAL 999 'f. 273va', against the Gallica foliation 173v, and Sainte-Geneviève 2787 f. 144), seen only as snippets, so the author is provisional; the Marian sermon is unidentified. The catalogue joins this strip with Frg. 123 (F-o5pg, Peter Comestor) in reconstruction F-dr4l, which is not public; same scale, line pitch, column width, hand and rubric style make one manuscript plausible, not proven; no human review.

Readings and comparison details

F-vjii: Alan of Lille (as listed by Schneyer), sermon Mandatum novum do vobis, and the anonymous De sobrietate et IV vigiliis, with an unidentified Marian sermon

This parchment strip, Stams Frg. 142, comes from a two-column Latin sermon collection and holds parts of three consecutive sermons. The first, on Mandatum novum do vobis (John 13:34), is a sermon on charity that also survives in Paris, BnF, nouv. acq. lat. 999, ff. 173v-174r; J. B. Schneyer's Repertorium der lateinischen Sermones des Mittelalters lists it under Alan of Lille. It ends on the strip and is followed by a sermon with the rubric (red heading) De sobrietate et .iiii. uigiliis, on the theme (opening Bible verse) Sobrii estote et vigilate (1 Pet. 5:8), which is anonymous in its other known copy, Saint-Omer, Bibliothèque d'agglomération, ms. 86, ff. 164v-165r. The third sermon, rubricated In festivitate sancte Marie virginis, begins Fecit Deus duo luminaria magna (Gen. 1:16) and is not identified.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 142: parchment, 383 x 82 mm (measured as 287/383 x 1/82 mm by Claudia Sojer), title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description, with two images labelled 01r and 01v. Its reconstruction summary refers to "Reconstruction F-dr4l of Stams, STA, Frg. 123+142", joining this strip with Frg. 123 (F-o5pg); that reconstruction is not public. The description fits the images. The record does not name the texts. A pencil note on the recto, "Aus Inkun[abel] IV/48 vom J. 1636(?)", records the host volume, which is not identified.

  • Identification: identified, high, for two of the three sermons.
    • Mandatum novum do vobis ("Homo, ecce forma vite tue, regula discipline, tue religionis vinculum ..."): the same sermon as BnF NAL 999, ff. 173v-174r (the Gallica opening foliated 173 | 174). Four columns of the strip agree with it in wording and order, from the old and new commandment through the speeches of the flesh and of charity to the "three uses" of charity compared with the Trinity. The strip's copy is not the same text form as NAL 999: it has a sentence absent there, "Hec lux [..] sub umbra, mel sub cera, et ueritas sub figura", and the order "tenens temporis uetustatem" where NAL 999 has "temporis tenens uetustatem". Schneyer I lists the sermon under Alanus ab Insulis, no. 73, with the witnesses "Paris, Nat. lat. nouv. acqu. 999 f. 273va" and "S. Genev. 2787 f. 144"; his anonymous volumes list the Sainte-Geneviève copy without an author. Both were seen only as full-text snippets. Schneyer's "f. 273va" against the Gallica foliation 173v is unexplained. The author is therefore given as listed by Schneyer, provisionally. The sermon shares long passages with Alan's Summa de arte praedicatoria, caps. VI and XX (PL 210:124-125, 153), but the strip and NAL 999 agree against the Ars (gluttony and lust where the Ars has avarice and cupidity, and the clause "per potus diluuium ... ad ebrietatem"), so the Ars is a parallel, not the containing text.
    • De sobrietate et IV vigiliis: the same text as Saint-Omer 86, ff. 164v-165r, which has the same rubric and no author. No printed edition was found.
    • The Marian sermon Fecit Deus duo luminaria magna is unidentified; only the theme and a few words survive above a water stain.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading and the source-assisted reading. A research reader reread on the native images recto cols. 1-4 (cols. 3-4 also with the blue channel stretched), the lower half of verso col. 1, the upper half of verso col. 2, and verso cols. 3-4, with the sources in hand. An independent checker read the upper half of verso col. 1 (seven lines) before opening the source, and the last lines of verso col. 3. The right half of recto col. 3 and the lower half of verso col. 2, under the water stain, are unread. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader collated verso cols. 3-4 and recto cols. 1-2 with the page images of NAL 999, ff. 173v-174r, after finding the passage in the machine-recognised text (HTR) of the CoMMA corpus, and checked every quoted phrase on the image. The same reader collated recto cols. 3-4 with the page image of Saint-Omer 86, f. 164v; the lower half of verso col. 1 and the upper half of verso col. 2 were compared only with the HTR text of Saint-Omer 86. An independent checker read verso col. 1, lines 1-7 (canvas 1, native box 1300,1950,1420,610), before looking at the source, and then compared them with the page image of Saint-Omer 86, f. 164v col. b foot to f. 165r col. a top (region of f. 164v, region of f. 165r). Every line fell into place in sequence (intellect, will and flesh failing "per ignorantiam ueritatis ... per inediam ... per inopiam fortitudinis", "corpus quod corrumpitur aggrauat animam", "Necesse est ergo ut ratio illuminetur"); its blind errors were misdivided abbreviations, none of which affects the match, and the passage confirms that recto col. 4 runs on to verso col. 1. The checker also read the last lines of verso col. 3 (canvas 1, native box 4020,2520,1060,540) against the page image of NAL 999, f. 173v, foot of col. a and top of col. b (Gallica, view 196), and found that NAL 999 goes straight from "pectoris retinens nouitatem" to "Mandatum uero nouum et innouans est", without the "Hec lux" sentence. The checker had read the research reader's account, though not a transcription of this passage, before choosing it. Gaps: Schneyer's pages, the Sainte-Geneviève manuscript and M.-T. d'Alverny's list of Alan's sermons (Alain de Lille: Textes inédits, 1965) were not seen; NAL 999 was not read past f. 174r col. b, so whether its sermon ends as the strip does is not known. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

The strip is cut across the middle of an opened bifolium (a sheet folded to make two leaves) and keeps about 12-13 lines of each of four columns per face. The hand is a small book hand of the late twelfth or early thirteenth century, about 6.3 mm between lines and about 6.4 cm to a column, with orange-red rubrics set in the text column and plain red initials. The v1 readings are the independent checker's, made before it saw the source; the rest of verso col. 1 and the upper half of verso col. 2 agree with the HTR text of Saint-Omer 86 ("Sed ad hec quis idoneus ...", "Bonitatem et disciplinam et scientiam doce me", "partim ratione capitur, partim doctrina et consilio, partim exemplo", "Sobrii ergo estote et uigilate"), which was not reread on its page image.

Differences in Mandatum novum: the "Hec lux" sentence and the order "tenens temporis" in the strip; "ab aliquo non est" against "non est ab alio"; "caro" against "propria caro"; "solueris" against "dissolueris"; "exponit tribulationi" against "tribulationibus"; NAL 999's "Qui caritatis est secretarius mundanorum non est amicus" is not in the strip's reading of verso col. 4. These show that the strip's text is not identical with NAL 999's; whether either depends on the other is not established. In De sobrietate: "agnoscas" against "cognoscas"; the ordinals without a repeated "uigilia"; "uero" against "enim"; "scilicet" against "uidelicet"; ".iiii. uigilias nostre" without "noctis"; "Sobrii ergo" in verso col. 2; possibly "affectus"/"affectum" against "affectionis"/"affectionem". The sequence is the same throughout. Saint-Omer 86 has the rubric in the margin, the strip in the text column.

Witnesses compared: Paris, BnF, nouv. acq. lat. 999 (Sermones et sermonum themata, 13th century), ff. 173v-174r on Gallica, page images; and Saint-Omer, Bibliothèque d'agglomération, ms. 86 (from Clairmarais, 13th century), item 15, ff. 164v-165r, IRHT IIIF manifest, canvas "f. 164v - 165", page images. The Catalogue général des manuscrits des bibliothèques publiques des départements, quarto series III (1861), p. 52, gives the Saint-Omer item as "De Sobrietate et IV vigiliis", without author. Its neighbour in Saint-Omer 86, De octo virtutibus monachorum (Sapientia aedificavit sibi domum), is printed among Alan's Dicta alia in PL 210; that is a pointer to the same milieu, not an attribution. Attribution of Mandatum novum: Schneyer, Repertorium I, Alanus ab Insulis no. 73, and his anonymous volume for the Sainte-Geneviève copy (anonymous no. 12), both seen as snippets; NAL 999 names no author for the sermon. A Luxembourg, Bibliothèque nationale manuscript with the same incipit is described in N. van Werveke's catalogue (1894), seen as a snippet without its shelfmark.

Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

Leaf order, from the text: verso cols. 3-4 and recto cols. 1-2 are the two faces of one leaf; recto cols. 3-4 and verso cols. 1-2 are the conjugate leaf. The text runs from recto col. 2 to col. 3 across the fold (the end of Mandatum novum) and from recto col. 4 to verso col. 1 (the vigils, confirmed by the checker's passage), and verso col. 3 to recto col. 2 follows NAL 999's order. So the recto face is a continuous opening, and the strip is probably from the middle bifolium of a quire.

Frg. 142 and Frg. 123 (F-o5pg), which has sermons of Peter Comestor under the rubric Sermo Manduc(atoris), are joined by the catalogue in reconstruction F-dr4l, which is not public. Measured on images at the same photographic scale (about 137 pixels to the centimetre), the two strips have nearly the same line pitch (about 6.3 and 6.0 mm), the same column width (about 6.4 and 6.5 cm), the same small hand, and the same rubric practice: an orange-red title split over two lines to the right of the theme's opening words, with a plain red initial. That makes one manuscript, a collection of sermons by several authors, plausible, not proven; these features are common in sermon books of the period, and the letter forms were not compared systematically. The two strips came from different host bindings, which fits a dispersed codex.

Not claimed: Alan of Lille's authorship of Mandatum novum beyond Schneyer's listing, any author for De sobrietate, the identity of the Marian sermon, the date and origin of the copy beyond the general character of the script, or the identity of the collection.
