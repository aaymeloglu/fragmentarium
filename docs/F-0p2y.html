Readings and comparison details

F-0p2y: Anonymous compilation, Liber novem iudicum, questions of the fourth house (land, houses, hidden treasure)

These two paper leaves are from the Liber novem iudicum ("Book of the Nine Judges"), a twelfth-century Latin compilation of astrological judgements from Arabic and other authorities, in its section on questions of the fourth house of the horoscope, which governs land, houses, inheritances, buildings and hidden treasure. The first leaf has chapters on acquiring, buying and selling land and houses, which the Venice 1509 print gives under the names of Aomar, Dorotheus and Zael; the second has the end of a chapter on hidden treasure that the print gives to Aomar, followed by a chapter on treasure and hidden things headed in red with the name "Alchindus" (al-Kindi). There is no legal text on the leaves: the chapters on buying, selling and inheriting land are astrological. The marginal notes in other hands are, by the first reading, medical recipes.

The Fragmentarium record describes Innsbruck, Universitäts- und Landesbibliothek Tirol, Frg. D3: Latin, paper, two pastedowns, each broken in two because the wooden boards of the book are broken, 245/278 x 163/186 mm, with the title "Astrological text (?), Juridical text" and no date. It names no work or author. "Astrological" fits; "Juridical" does not describe any text on the leaves. The record gives Venice as place of origin and Vahrn-Neustift as provenance. The host volume, ULBT Ink. 106 F 7, is Mesue's Opera medicinalia printed at Venice in 1478/79 and came from Neustift near Brixen, so those places may describe the book rather than the leaves; they are not taken here to date or place the copy of the text.

  • Identification: identified, high confidence; the recension (the form and arrangement of the text in this copy) is open. On three faces, the first lines of 01_r, of 02_r and of 02_v, the text agrees in wording and sequence with the Venice 1509 print of the Liber novem iudicum, read on page images; the middle and foot of 01_v agree with the same print on the first reading. The work is the compilation, not one judge's treatise: chapters of different authorities follow one another under topic rubrics (headings in red) closed by the authority's name, as in the print, and on leaf 1 the sequence of chapters is the print's. The separate treatises of Zael, Aomar and al-Kindi are therefore not the containing work; they were not compared individually. The earlier Liber trium iudicum, compiled from Aomar, Zael and al-Kindi only, was not compared, because no text of it was available. A chapter on 01_v under a rubric read "D(o)rotheus(?)", an authority outside those three, speaks against it, but the text of that chapter rests on the first reading, and the contents of the Liber trium iudicum are known here only from a general account. The leaf has the al-Kindi chapter where the 1509 print has a chapter of Jergis, and the 1571 print places it earlier; several rubrics are worded differently (see Textual evidence); the al-Kindi chapter itself was matched only through search snippets of the Basel 1571 print.
  • Transcription: the image-only first reading and the source-assisted reading. A research reader reread on crops of the images about thirty lines: 01_r lines 1-9, 02_r lines 1-10 and 02_v lines 1-13, with the first reading at hand and some lines after seeing the print. 01_v was seen only reduced and not reread; the rubrics lower on 01_r and 02_r were seen on the overview images only. The rest of the text rests on the first reading. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the reread lines of 01_r, 02_r and 02_v with page images of the 1509 print in the Warburg Institute facsimile, and the first reading of 01_v with the same print; the al-Kindi chapter on 02_v was compared only with search snippets of the 1571 print. The coordinator (Claude) read 02_v lines 1-8, "Quot proprii signi transierit gradus" to "erit di[videnda]" (canvas 3, within native box 700,330,3700,2400), on the image against the page image of the 1509 print, Warburg facsimile, PDF p. 47: the leaf agrees with the print, with "obtineat" for "continet", "videlicet" for "scilicet" and "moretur" for "commoretur". Two fresh readers, given four neutral crops of this record without transcription or source, confirmed the rubric "De acquirendis domibus vel agris patrimoniis" on 01_v (one read "dotibus" for "domibus") and gave the name in the faded rubric below it as "Drotheus" or "Dorocheus", with "[..]odem" after it. They did not confirm the name that closes the first of these rubrics (both read something like "Domac" or "donac", not "Aomar"), the two-line red rubric at the head of 02_r, or the first word of 01_r, where both saw a large initial of C or T form; the coordinator reads that word "[Tr]ansferens(?)", and the next words, "propriis manib[us]", fit both readers. These readings are marked uncertain below. Gaps: 01_v was not reread or collated on crops; the al-Kindi chapter was not seen on a page image; no manuscript of the Liber novem iudicum was compared; no palaeographer or historian of astrology has reviewed the reading or the match.

Differences. Order: on the leaf the al-Kindi treasure chapter follows Aomar's "Utrum possit inveniri". In the 1509 print Aomar's chapter is followed by "De thesauro vel re qualibet occulta. Jergis" (PDF p. 47, col. b), and in the 1571 print the al-Kindi chapter stands at p. 422, before the land chapters (p. 431) and Jirjis (p. 436). The opening of the al-Kindi chapter was not located in the 1509 facsimile. Rubrics: the leaf has "De acquirendis domibus vel agris patrimoniis" followed by an uncertain name where the print has "Utrum quis adipiscatur domum vel hereditatem" without a name; "D(o)rotheus(?) ... [e]odem(?)" where the print has "De acquirendis domibus vel patrimoniis. Dorotheus"; an uncertain two-line rubric at the head of 02_r inside the chapter where the print has none; a heading "de qualitate et natura thesauri" in text ink inside Aomar's chapter; and, by the first reading, a marginal note instead of a rubric at "De edificiis questio". Wording, in the lines reread: "emacione" for "emptione", "pro agris" for "pro emendis agris", "obtineat" for "continet", "moretur" for "commoretur", "videlicet" for "scilicet", "ab uno" for "ab alio" (first reading), "determinata(?)" for "indeterminata". The arrangement of this copy differs from both prints at the al-Kindi chapter; whether that is a form of the Liber novem iudicum found in manuscripts or a rearrangement by subject cannot be told from two leaves.

Edition compared: Liber novem iudicum in iudiciis astrorum, Venice, Petrus Liechtenstein, 1509, in the Warburg Institute facsimile, PDF pp. 38-39 and 46-47, page images; and the Basel 1571 printing of the same compilation, pp. 422-423, 431 and 436, in a volume with Haly Abenragel's De iudiciis astrorum (Google Books R1NRAAAAcAAJ), through search snippets only; its title page was not seen. The compilation is anonymous in the print. Charles Burnett's notice prefixed to the Warburg facsimile describes it as a mid-twelfth-century collection of judgements from al-Kindi, Umar ibn al-Farrukhan (Aomar), Sahl ibn Bishr (Zael), "Aristotle", Abu Ali al-Khayyat (Albenait), Jirjis and Dorotheus, compiled from Latin translations "probably supervised by the translators Hermann of Carinthia and Hugo of Santalla", extant in about twenty manuscripts and printed at Venice in 1509 and Basel in 1571; it cites F. J. Carmody (1956), pp. 103-112, and C. Burnett, "A Hermetic Programme of Astrology and Divination in mid-Twelfth-Century Aragon: The Hidden Preface in the Liber novem iudicum" (2006), which were not consulted. The authorities of the single chapters are those of the print's rubrics; the leaf's own rubrics give "zael" (01_r, reread), "Alchindus" (02_v, reread), "D(o)rotheus(?)" (01_v, read by the blind readers) and "Aomar" in "Utrum possit inveniri an non Aomar" (02_r, first reading only).

Leaf order, other items and limits

On leaf 1 the text of 01_v comes before that of 01_r in the print, so the face labelled verso is the true recto. 01_v has 37 lines and then blank paper; its last line, "luna ... quoque ab uno ad alium lumen", is continued at the head of 01_r by "[Tr]ansferens(?) propriis manibus", so no text is lost at the turn if that reading holds. On leaf 2, 02_r (about 50 lines) runs on, by the first reading of its foot, into 02_v (about 48 lines, ending above a blank foot). Why 01_v and 02_v stop above blank paper while the text runs on is unexplained. The two leaves are not consecutive: in the 1509 print about seven pages stand between the end of leaf 1 and the start of leaf 2. Each face has one column (the catalogue gives 41-44 lines; the faces hold from 37 to about 50) in a small cursive book hand, with red rubrics set at line-ends and the first lines of each chapter indented for an initial that was not supplied.

The upper and lower margins of 01_r and 02_r carry notes in other, more cursive hands, which the first reading gives as recipes ("Unguentum laxativum ...", "R(ecipe) ...", German plant names in "-laub"); they were not reread. The catalogue also notes an offset of 02_v on the back board and unrelated parchment strips and scraps in the host volume, which are not part of this record.

Not claimed: a date or origin for the copy (the record gives no date, and the dates of the host volume and of the 1509 print date those books); the recension of the text or any relation between this copy and the prints or other manuscripts; that the al-Kindi chapter matches the 1571 text beyond the snippets quoted; the chapter and authority of the opening of 01_v (first reading "cavendum summopere videtur ne in nono ..."), which falls on the print page before PDF p. 38 and was not compared; the names in the uncertain rubrics.
