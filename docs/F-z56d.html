Readings and comparison details

F-z56d: Petrus Hispanus (non-papa), Summa "Absoluta cuiuslibet"

One face of the fragment is identified with high confidence as the grammatical Summa "Absoluta cuiuslibet" attributed to Petrus Hispanus, a treatise on syntax in the tradition of Priscian's Institutiones XVII–XVIII (not the logician Peter of Spain, author of the Tractatus). About six consecutive lines, from a discussion of whether ille answering quis is demonstrative or relative, agree in wording and order with the printed edition. The identification rests on that one passage; the other face is too faded to add a second.

The Fragmentarium record describes a parchment bifolium fragment, Leuven, Archief van de Abdij van Park, VIIIB20/89, 220 × 223 mm, dated 1300–1350, catalogued as an unidentified treatise on logic. The text is grammar. The catalogue date has not been reassessed; the work itself is of the late twelfth century.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the passage read. The argument is specific (the example "ille qui scripsit Georgica scripsit Eneida", then the two-step exclusion of demonstration ad oculum and ad intellectum) and the wording is continuous, not a shared example. Material alternative: a later grammatical work copying this passage verbatim; searches found the Georgics/Aeneid example reused elsewhere in different words, but no other text with this prose.
  • Transcription: source-assisted selected reading of six lines, from contrast-stretched crops. The image-only reading recovered no connected text. Accuracy has not been measured; the lines before and after are not securely read.
  • Verification: two model readers compared the manuscript image with search snippets of the edition. The edition's pages were not seen: there is no page number, only the editor's folio mark of his base manuscript. No human review.

ManuscriptEdition (snippets)
"... ut sit responsio: ille qui scripsit georgica scripsit en[eida]. Sed queretur utrum ille ibi sit demonstrativum aut relativum. Quod non sit demonstrativum inde videtur quoniam nec ad oculum nec ad intellectum demonstrationem facit. Si enim ad oculum non nisi de isto quem video diceretur. Si ad intellectum non nisi propter absentem vel incorporeum. Nunc autem nulla ratio est cur? magis ad absentem quam ad presentem dicatur. Ergo [ille ibi] ponitur [relative] ...""'ille qui scripsit Georgica, scripsit Eneida'. Sed queretur utrum 'ille' ibi sit demonstratiuum aut relatiuum. Quod non sit demonstratiuum, inde uidetur, quoniam nec ad oculum nec ad intellectum facit demonstrationem. Si enim ad oculum, nonnisi pro isto quem uideo, diceretur. Si ad intellectum, nonnisi propter absentem uel incorporeum. Nunc autem nulla ratio est quare magis dicatur ad ... ad absentem quam ad presentem; ergo ibi 'ille' ponitur relatiue nec est assignare ad quid referatur nisi ad 'qui'. Quare 'qui' et 'ille' ad se inuicem referuntur; ergo utrumque in respectu alterius primam et secundam notitiam facit. Idem ergo primam /f. 51vb/ et secundam respectu eiusdem facit."

Differences: word order (demonstrationem facit against facit demonstrationem; ille ibi against ibi ille), de isto against pro isto, and the manuscript's word after ratio est, read doubtfully as cur against quare. A second reader continued the manuscript text through "primam et secundam [notitiam facit]"; those lines were not rechecked and are not relied on.

The edition is C. H. Kneepkens, Het Iudicium constructionis: het leerstuk van de constructio in de 2de helft van de 12de eeuw, vol. IV: Werkuitgave van Petrus Hispanus' Summa "Absoluta cuiuslibet" (Nijmegen, 1987). The passage stands just before the editor's mark "/f. 51vb/". It can be found by searching Google Books (volume id tWdJAAAAYAAJ) for "nec ad oculum nec ad intellectum facit demonstrationem".

Attribution and limits

Author and title are those of Kneepkens's edition, which is the scholarly basis for calling the work the Summa "Absoluta cuiuslibet" of Petrus Hispanus; the name is the edition's and the identity of this Petrus is a separate question not addressed here. Nothing is claimed about which recension the fragment carries, about the faded face, or about the bifolium's other leaf. A later "Registrum" inscription on the fragment belongs to its reuse.
