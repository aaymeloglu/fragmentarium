Readings and comparison details

F-5z6w: Noted breviary, Good Friday Matins (nocturn III) to Holy Saturday Matins (nocturn I), rubrics agreeing with the papal-court Ordinal

This parchment bifolium at Stams, the centre of a gathering with its text running continuously across all four pages, comes from a noted breviary: a book of the Office with the lessons written out in full, the chants under musical notation and the rubrics (red directions) for performing them. Its use (secular, monastic or Cistercian) is not established. In text order it carries the end of Good Friday Matins, nocturn III: the end of a lesson from Hebrews (Heb 6:8), the responsory Iesum tradidit impius (Cantus ID 007035), a lesson Heb 6:9-7:5 and the responsory Caligaverunt oculi mei (006261); Good Friday Lauds with its five antiphons Proprio filio, Anxiatus est, Ait latro, Dum conturbata, Memento mei domine deus (004395, 001442, 001316, 002444, 003736); rubrics for the end of Lauds, for Vespers with the Magnificat antiphon Cum accepisset acetum (001970) and for Compline; and Holy Saturday Matins, nocturn I: the antiphons In pace in idipsum, Habitabit, Caro mea (003265, 002987, 001775), the first lesson Lam 3:22-66 with the responsory Sicut ovis (007661), and the second lesson Lam 4 with the responsory Jerusalem luge (007032). The rubrics agree closely with the Ordinal of the papal court of Innocent III, the order of the Roman curia that the Franciscans adopted; which book of that family the leaf comes from is not established.

The Fragmentarium record is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 113: parchment, one fragment item, 588 x 230 mm (measured 587/588 x 224/230 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), marked restored, title "x", no date and no linked description, with two images labelled 01r and 01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images, a wide strip of a bifolium trimmed above and below.

  • Identification: identified, high, for the book type, the days and the sequence of texts. The basis is the run of lessons, chants and rubrics in this order, read on all four pages, and its agreement with the Ordinal of Innocent III as edited by Van Dijk and Walker, pp. 244 and 259-260: the responsories Iesum tradidit and Caligaverunt with their verses and repeat cues, the Lauds antiphons, the Vespers rubric "antiphone preteriti diei dicuntur non cantando sed recitando", "Et in his omnibus non dicitur Gloria patri. Quibus expletis dicatur Christus factus est", the Pater noster "sine kyrieleison sub silentio", the prayer Respice, "Ad completorium sicut in die Iovis", the three Holy Saturday antiphons, and lessons beginning at Lam 3:22 and Lam 4:1 with the responsories Sicut ovis and Jerusalem luge. The structure (three antiphons and a versicle before the first lesson, nine responsories, Caligaverunt the last before Lauds) is that of the secular cursus, but monastic books also used this nine-responsory form on these days, so it does not decide whether the book was secular, monastic or Cistercian. The chants alone do not place the use: a Cantus Database sequence search found all thirteen in 51 inventories. The phrase "antiphone ... preteriti diei" alone is not diagnostic, since it also occurs in other books (for example a printed Benedictine breviary and the Avignon Caeremoniale); the full rubric run supports the curial order without separating the curial breviary from its Franciscan or later Roman-use derivatives. Differences from the Ordinal: the leaf has "sine capitulo et sine ymno et sine versiculo" where the Ordinal has "sine lectione, sine ymno, sine versu"; "sine capitulo" agrees with a Franciscan breviary rubric (Rome, Biblioteca Casanatense, quoted in Archivum Franciscanum Historicum 19, 1926, p. 92). The leaf lacks the Ordinal's note that Cum accepisset "est in antiphonario curie romane" and its alternative antiphon Ait latro. In Lam 4 the leaf omits filiae (4:6), mulierum (4:10) and sacerdotum (4:13), apparently scribal slips, and writes no letter Ioth before 4:10.
  • Transcription: source-assisted. The image-only first reading covers every text line of both faces; the source-assisted reading is a research reader's reread on the native images of canvas 0 columns 1 and 2 complete and the top half of column 3, and of canvas 1 column 2 lower half, column 3 complete and column 4 lower half, made with the Ordinal and the Vulgate at hand. It corrects the first reading's red "phōe" to the second half of the rubric "In lau(dibus) anti-phone", and resolves the interleaved "ne ... rant P." on canvas 1 column 3 as the end of the verse Adduxerunt ("conve-ne-rant") written on a short inserted staff, with the repeat cue "P(etrus)". The rest of Lam 3 and Heb 6:17-7:2 rest on the first reading. Unresolved: one red word after "crucis" (canvas 0 column 2, perhaps "p(re)ces"). The neumes were not transcribed. Accuracy has not been measured.
  • Verification: a research reader compared the rubrics, responsories, antiphons and lesson incipits with the Ordinal of Innocent III in Google Books snippets of the edition (page images not seen), and the Lamentations and Hebrews lessons with the Vulgate. An independent research reader read canvas 1, column 1, region 750,1380,1250,2470 (Lam 4:4-14), on the image before comparison and found sustained agreement with the Vulgate, with the omissions listed above. Passage checked independently: canvas 1, column 1, region 750,1380,1250,2470, Lam 4:4-14 ("Deleth. Adhesit lingua lactentis" to "Erraverunt ceci in plateis polluti sunt in sanguine"), read on the image against the Vulgate (Latin Library text). Gaps: the Ordinal was seen only in snippets; the extent of the Good Friday nocturn III lessons (ending Heb 6:8 and Heb 7:5) was not checked against any breviary, since the Ordinal gives only the incipit; about eight to ten lines are trimmed from the foot of every column. No human review.

Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

  • Leaf order: the text runs from canvas 1 right half (leaf 1 recto) to canvas 0 left half (leaf 1 verso), canvas 0 right half (leaf 2 recto) and canvas 1 left half (leaf 2 verso). Canvas 0, labelled 01r, is therefore the inner face of the bifolium, and the bifolium is the centre of its gathering. The trimmed foot of each column accounts for the gaps (Lam 3:22-27, 3:48-55 and 4:1-3, Heb 6:12-16, the end of Lauds).
  • Physical features: Gothic textualis in two columns per page; red rubrics, Hebrew letters and initials; black notes on red four-line staves with C and F clefs. The only other marks are the modern pencil shelfmarks "Stams, STA, Frg. 113r" and "113v".
  • Not claimed: the date and origin (the catalogue gives no date; none was tested), whether the book was Franciscan or another breviary of curial or Roman use, how or when it reached Stams, and the division of the Good Friday nocturn III lessons in any other book.
