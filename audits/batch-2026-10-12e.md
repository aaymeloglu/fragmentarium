# Fifth twenty-fragment batch of 12 October 2026

Twenty records absent from the 122-entry ledger were taken from the saved
20 September catalogue sweep, which had 100 records left. This is a selected
batch, not a random sample, and its yield is not a success rate for the method.

## Selection

`tools/survey.py` was run over the whole remaining pool for the first time. It
listed 90 records with native image sizes (four more return HTTP 404 for their
manifests) and made contact sheets, which the coordinator looked at for all of
them. The linked catalogue description of 36 shortlisted records was read with
`tools/catalogue.py` before the choice.

Twenty-six records were downloaded at native size and looked at as gridded
overviews. Three were dropped at that point because the catalogued fragment is
only a sliver inside a binding and the legible writing in the photographs
belongs to something else or is a few letters: F-z34f (two spine strips; the
legible cover is another item), F-3wqe and F-cqa7. F-hjc4, F-7x8n and F-qycu
replaced them. Three further alternates (F-mc4a, F-tfms, F-8plb) were not used.

Two things went wrong at selection:

- F-4r0j: the linked description ties these strips to MdS 45 Da 5, whose two
  records (F-3u3b, F-ho7z) an earlier batch had already left unidentified. The
  coordinator read the description and did not check the ledger for the
  sibling shelfmark.
- F-fuvo: the catalogue has a reconstruction record, F-5oah, that joins this
  strip with MdS 58 Ca 19/F1-2 (F-uvx5), and the previous batch had examined
  F-5oah. The three records are one leaf. The research session found this; the
  selection did not.

Records with images that cannot be read at the published size were still
chosen where nothing better was left: F-la3m, F-pnkr, F-hjc4, F-t6bi, F-jnvq.

## First readings

Each record got a packet of reduced overviews and overlapping native-pixel
tiles (`tools/tilepacket.py`). Five fresh reader sessions, given only their
packets and the reader brief, wrote one response per packet.

Two of the five reported that images had dropped out of their context before
they wrote (packets 05, 06, 13, 14: F-vui8, F-qtiw, F-t6bi, F-pnkr). Those four
packets were read again by two further fresh sessions told to view a few images
at a time and write as they went. The second responses are the ones frozen; the
first are kept in the private notes and were not used. All twenty readings were
frozen and committed before any source search.

Limits:

- The coordinator had seen reduced images of every record before the readings
  were frozen, and chose the tile areas.
- All readers listed their packet directory to learn the image file names,
  which shows the name of `packet.json`; none opened it.
- The reader of F-w8ii did not notice that the legible strip is upside down in
  the photographs, so that first reading is of little use.
- The tiles of F-fw0f, F-hjc4 and F-t6bi are under 1,000 pixels wide because
  the published photographs are small.

## Source research

Ten research sessions took one to three records each with
`prompts/research-brief.md` (the three Ghent paper pieces HS.2583/074, /078 and
/084 went to one session as one manuscript). Six sessions were sent back for a
second pass with named candidates or a narrower search space:

- F-18n8: the first pass misread a line of allegations and found nothing. Told
  to read the Accursian gloss on page images around the sibling leaf's passage
  (D. 4.8.40 to 4.9.7), the session found the gloss and corrected its reading.
- F-qycu: the first pass tested five works by a word-sequence window test and
  stopped. Sent back with the medical and optical texts on the eye it had
  named but not tested, it found the passage in Avicenna.
- F-fw0f: sent back to read William of Auxerre on page images and to find a
  second witness of the abridgement. The first was done and changed the
  account of which wording is William's own; the second failed.
- F-7x8n, F-2fz6, F-fuvo, F-3h60, F-qtiw: candidates tested and rejected, or
  not reached; see the ledger notes.

The Google Books quota ran out early in the research (HTTP 429) and stayed
out, so most phrase searches ran on the Internet Archive, Corpus Corporum and
the Munich library only. Gallica, the Erfurt digital library and BNM-I could
not be reached by script.

## Results

Four identifications, each read by the coordinator on the manuscript image and
on a page image of the source:

| Record | Result | Compared with |
|---|---|---|
| F-33ow | Advent orations of the Gregorian sacramentary, from 3 to 6 letters a line | Wilson's and Mohlberg's editions, page images |
| F-qycu | Avicenna, *Canon* III, fen 3, tract. 1, ch. 1-2 | Padua 1479 print, page images |
| F-1kdv | Justinian, Codex 7.62.31-32 | Krueger 1877 and two other editions, page images |
| F-18n8 | Accursian gloss to Digest 4.9.1.8, the paragraph of sibling Ms. 1032 | Paris 1566 print, page images |

Three of the four rest on the order and spacing of short line ends or
beginnings, not on connected phrases. In each the number of consecutive lines
placed is large (36, 46 and 9), and the reports say which letters were read
before the source was found and which with it.

Eight partial results: F-fw0f (an abridgement of the *Summa aurea*, probably
Herbert of Auxerre's), F-7x8n (rules of consequence built on Ockham), F-fuvo
(*Ianua*-type paradigms with *soror*), F-3h60 (Middle Dutch saints' lives in
numbered sections), F-vui8 (*Ave verum corpus*), F-2fz6 (a non-Isidorian
ending to *De barbarismo*), F-f0ff (a book arranged by Sundays after
Pentecost) and F-qtiw (one question title).

Eight unidentified: F-8epz, F-jnvq, F-la3m, F-pnkr, F-4r0j, F-w8ii, F-hjc4,
F-t6bi.

The ledger has 142 entries and 39 published identifications.

## What limited the batch

- Image size and condition. Six of the eight unidentified records gave no
  searchable phrase: the writing is washed out, hidden in a binding or a few
  pixels high. No search was run on them, so they are not negatives.
- A coordinator's guess can mislead. The brief for F-f0ff suggested a red
  heading read "dist. xiiij" with "magister" (a Sentences commentary); the
  session tested that on the image and found "Dominica xiiij, Mattheus".
- Unprinted works. F-fw0f has a named candidate with one manuscript read and no
  second witness reachable; F-qtiw has a question title and no list of
  questions to check it against.
- Sessions stopping early. Two sessions stopped at about a third of their
  budget with candidates they had named and not tested; one of the four
  identifications came from sending such a session back.

## Review after submission

A second model reviewed the four identifications against selected manuscript
and source images on 12 October 2026, accepted all four, and made three
points. The coordinator checked each on the images and agrees with all three.

- F-33ow, verso line 2: the strip reads "s dne" (*quesumus domine*), not
  "s nra" (*nostras*); the abbreviation is the one used for *domine* in verso
  line 11. The prayer is the same (*Mentes nostras quesumus domine*), and the
  corrected reading evens out the spacing: the supplied letters before and
  after that line become about 22 and 29, where the report had 18 and 33. A
  corrected source-assisted reading was added beside the first one.
- F-qycu, later side, line 16: the 1479 print reads "et ut lacertus" on the
  page; the missing "et" was an omission of the machine-read text, not a
  variant. The coordinator has now read lines 13 to 16 of that side on the
  print's page images, so no line rests on machine-read text alone. The
  *simile* / *similiter* doubt in line 14 remains.
- F-7x8n: "Ockham-derived" claimed more than one shared rule can show. The
  ledger now says the rule is closely parallel to Ockham's and that dependence
  is not shown.

The reviewer called F-18n8 the weakest of the four and noted that its
belonging to the sibling leaf is unproven.
