# Twelve melody retries, 20 October 2026

All twelve records in the [retry shortlist](retry-melody-2026-10-20.md) were
examined. **No identification was established.** Five supplied at least a
provisional searchable passage; seven did not supply four consecutive distinct
pitches. This is an image-readability and candidate-verification result, not
negative evidence that the chants are absent from Cantus.

The work used the merged melody tool from PR #49. No downloader changes,
Rescribe, paid corpus or institutional access were used. Catalogue pages,
IIIF manifests, every listed canvas at overview size, and selected native-size
music crops were inspected. F-30ta's reconstruction was mapped against its
constituent catalogues before choosing the unknown piece. Covering paper,
folds, abrasion and cropped note groups were not silently bridged.

Each fragment received a separate fresh image-only reader, with neutral image
names and no proposed chant, catalogue transcription, or prior reading. Complete
responses and input provenance were preserved and committed before their
candidate searches. Subsequent coordinator interpretations and all comparisons
below are source-assisted. These are model readings, not human palaeographic
verification. Raw first readings include tentative and potentially erroneous
text; preservation does not certify their accuracy.

## Candidate checks

**F-82z1.** Complete Cantus records were retrieved for all ten results of the
11-pitch query. After case-folding and collapsing adjacent repeats, eight
published encodings did not contain that exact diatonic-step sequence under
transposition. They are not excluded chants, but the returned rows themselves
are insufficient evidence for the proposed melodic match. This includes the
thematically tempting Marian [Corde et animo](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/293432/).

Two full records do contain the relative contour:
[Paratur nobis mensa domini, Utrecht 406, 137v](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/495687/)
and [Kyrie fons bonitatis, Porrentruy 18, p.357](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/644939/).
They also contain the preceding pitch used in the 12-pitch extension. The
fragment's earlier visible repeated/stepped group does not continue into either
encoding in the same sequence: for Paratur the collapsed prefix is `khkl`, and
for Kyrie it ends `...fedh`, at their respective absolute placements. A
matching internal formula is not a full-staff match. The concealed underlay
cannot corroborate either text, and the fragment's other exposed words remain
too uncertain. Differences between witnesses are possible, so these candidates
remain unverified rather than globally excluded.

The server-transposed 12-pitch query returned zero despite those two encodings.
Exact source-derived controls `lklnmlkjklkh` and `hghkjhgfghge` recovered the
Paratur and Kyrie records respectively. The official site's JavaScript sends
the same `t`, `start` and `transpose` parameters as the tool. This establishes
a limitation of the observed server retrieval; it does not diagnose its cause.
As a fallback, all twelve legal diatonic pitch placements of the fragment's
12-pitch sequence were searched exactly. Only those two placements returned
results: seven network records and one catalogue melody in total. The additional full records were compared as described below. Exact-pitch fallback
is a research action here, not a change to the merged tool.

The exact-placement fallback adds other [Paratur](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/230549/)
[witnesses](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/377029/), two
[Qui pacem ponit](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/230538/)
[witnesses](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/255668/),
[Crux fidelis](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/412436/), and the catalogue melody
[Cum appropinquaret](https://cantusindex.org/melody/001975m1a).
Their complete encodings contain the short sequence, illustrating reuse of a
formula across different chants. Their preceding figures do not reproduce the
fragment's broader visible repeated/stepped sequence, and no surviving text
corroborates one of them. All these comparisons use database encodings and
syllable alignment where available, not the linked witnesses' manuscript images.
No witness-image verification or chant attribution is claimed.

**F-gs4f.** Full [Quae beatum virum](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/285336/)
contains `kjhhfgf`, reproducing passage A's seven-head pattern including its
repeated third degree. But it is sung to `con-fi-te`, which does not corroborate
the fragment's tentative `...m de...`; no second passage supports the identity.
[Ne derelinquas me](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/255502/) has the collapsed
contour on `pa-ter`, but the repeated pitch is at the beginning rather than the
third degree. These are short formula-level leads. Full encodings and syllable
alignment were inspected, not the linked witness images. Catalogue-derived
searches for `deificum`, `cristicole`/`christicolae` variants, `sacerdotum` and
`favus` supplied no corroborated sequence. The related binding
[F-4xnu](https://fragmentarium.ms/view/page/F-4xnu/2177/26248), already catalogued
with O Adonai/O radix/O clavis, identifies a different fragment.

**F-5qb0.** Complete records for
[A facie furoris tui, Klosterneuburg 1012](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/286874/)
and [Fribourg Ms.2](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/255651/) were checked.
Their `fa` begins `cde` and `ffg` respectively, not the proposed four-head
ascending pair-group. Neither supplies a corroborated fragment sequence. Other
witnesses or different readings remain possible; uncertain four-note input
cannot sustain an identification by itself.

identification date. No public identification pages are added. Seven no-query

is rebuilt to record the attempts without increasing the identification count.
