# Stams uncatalogued fragments, batch 5, November 2026

The fifth batch from the uncatalogued Stams fragments (collection C-ugo5;
title "x", shelfmark, material and dimensions only). Twenty records from the
triage's middle group, chosen for legible text in an identifiable genre:
eight theological, school or legal texts, eight liturgical, two archival
documents and two mixed records. The 44-piece court record F-lv0w was again
left for later.

## Process

1. Tile packets from native images; a pencil note on F-hjri naming its host
   volume was masked. First readings by fresh model readers given only the
   prompt and tiles (`stams5-2026-11-01-image-only`), frozen before research.
   The environment caps how many images one session can display: in packets
   02 and 14 the first reader never saw several images, and further fresh
   readers read only those images (their readings are appended to the same
   response); the first reading of packet 17 transcribed images that had
   not displayed and was discarded and replaced by a fresh reader's.
2. Research by ten sessions, two records each. Nine were sent back: to read
   sources on page images rather than OCR or snippets (F-e8dj, F-fns2,
   F-nf5f, F-f14r), to test named candidates (Hugutio and the *Catholicon*
   for F-hjri; Cistercian breviaries for F-dxj5), to read leaves or faces left
   out (F-cf1m, F-xoto, F-vujo, F-owoj, F-dxj5 piece 33-2) to fix the day
   (F-cwhu), or to reread the faded band of a deed and search outside records
   (F-d358).
3. One batched CoMMA search after research, complete. It found the F-vujo
   parallel in BnF lat. 455, collated afterwards on its page image, and a
   further witness of F-cf1m's commentary (BnF lat. 15265, not checked on
   images); the other queries gave nothing above the shuffled control.
4. Checks. For every identification a fresh checker chose a passage the
   research had not used, read it on the image and compared it on a source
   page image. For the two documents, two fresh readers with no transcription
   were given 20 shuffled neutral crops, and the coordinator reread every
   disagreement on the image. The coordinator compared one passage with the
   source page image for eight records and read a passage on the image for
   four more; F-aegc and F-17j8 rest on the research and the checker.

## Corrections made by the checks

- F-gi8c: the edition cited as the Sarum Missal is the York Missal (ed.
  Henderson, 1874).
- F-t8sd: the pencil "a. 1534" dates the host volume, so it is not a terminus
  for the copy.
- F-v7op: the research had misquoted the print in four places; the
  commentary is "closely related to", not a version of, the printed one.
- F-nf5f: the Advent clause is *Ecclesiastica officia*'s own wording, not
  the compiler's expansion.
- F-cwhu: PL 65, col. 727B-C; "probably Cistercian" throughout.
- F-cf1m: about 32-33 lines a column; the inference that the commentary was
  excerpted was dropped.
- F-aegc: "a late Sunday after Pentecost", not "the last".
- F-f14r: face 2 also carries the Mt 11 objection; the 1603 glossed Bible on
  archive.org is the Venice (Giunta) printing.
- F-dxj5: the inference that the book was not Stams' own choir book, drawn
  from one printed Cistercian breviary, was dropped.
- F-e8dj, F-t6qr, F-fns2, F-xoto: smaller readings, column references and
  layer descriptions.
- Documents: the word check confirmed F-0kh0's year word and F-d358's month
  and year; both readers misread the looped Z of "Zeinß" as J; F-d358's
  place and noble surnames were cut back to what the image shows.
