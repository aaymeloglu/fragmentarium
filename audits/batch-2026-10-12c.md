# Third twenty-fragment batch of 12 October 2026

Twenty records absent from the 82-entry ledger were taken from the saved
20 September catalogue sweep, which had 140 records left. This is a selected
batch, not a random sample, and its yield is not a success rate for the method.

## Selection

The coordinator looked at reduced images of sixty of the 140 remaining records
and kept the twenty with the most legible connected text. Sixteen are Latin;
four are vernacular (two Middle Dutch prose, one Middle Dutch verse, one German
verse), the first vernacular records attempted here, chosen because they are
far more legible than the Latin strips that remain. Three leaves (F-hwsh,
F-21br, F-jfv6) were taken together as sister leaves of F-7smu.

- F-31tn was chosen and then dropped before any reading: its legible text is
  the printed pastedowns that the catalogue describes under another record, and
  the catalogued cover is washed off. F-imyy replaced it.
- The selection list carried only catalogue titles. For F-koak the full record
  already cites the 1927 edition and the repertory entry that the research
  then found; reading the full record at selection would have shown that.

## First readings

Each record got a packet of reduced overviews and overlapping native-pixel
tiles (`tools/tilepacket.py`). Ten fresh reader sessions, given only their
packets and the reader brief, wrote one response per packet; all twenty were
frozen and committed before any source search. Limits:

- The coordinator had seen reduced images of every record before the readings
  were frozen, and chose the tile areas.
- F-pleo: the reader saw both sides of four of the fourteen guards. F-jfv6 has
  one usable image, because the catalogue links one image to both canvases.
- F-cbfe was given in three rotations of the same area.
- The two Orselina in-situ records (F-ja1n, F-cjzj) and F-ho7z were read from
  photographs taken at an angle inside bindings.

## Source research

Ten research sessions, one to three records each, received the first readings,
the cached images and the search tools with the research brief, a budget of
about 150 tool calls a record and an instruction to stop without a candidate
after about 80. They reread the images before searching. For every proposed
identification the coordinator read one matched passage on the manuscript
image, and for F-ja1n on the page image of the Vatican manuscript as well; each
report says exactly what that check covered. For the other four the coordinator
compared its reading with the research session's quotation of the source, not
with the source's page. F-ybvx was sent back for a second pass, which lowered
it from a proposed identification to partial. All of this is model work without
human palaeographic review.

## Results

Five identified, eleven partial, four unidentified. Ledger: 102 examined, 31
published.

Identified, with a public report:

- **F-x60p**: Justinian, Digest 4.8.42-49 and 4.8.52-4.9.3 pr., with the
  Accursian gloss; six strips of one leaf. The catalogue had the genre.
- **F-w36h**: the Southern Middle Dutch *Legenda aurea*, chapter on the
  dedication of a church. The catalogue's suggestion of Duranti's *Rationale*
  was tested and does not fit. Compared with the edition text of one manuscript.
- **F-n8nv**: a Middle Dutch translation of the *Expositio in regulam sancti
  Augustini* printed under Hugh of St Victor's name, chapters 6-7. Which Dutch
  translation it is stays open: the edition was seen only in search snippets.
- **F-ja1n**: Johannes Faventinus, *Summa* on the Decretum, D. 86, against one
  manuscript of an unprinted work (BAV Borgh. lat. 71). Single passages also
  stand in his sources Rufinus and Stephen of Tournai; the identification rests
  on their combination and order. Other summae built on Rufinus were not
  collated.
- **F-cjzj**: Accursius's gloss on the Authenticum, Collatio IX; the gloss
  only, from line-ends, against one print. The main text is a lead.

Partial: F-ybvx (an abridged or adapted text built on the *Manipulus
curatorum*; three prints agree against it), F-qvgd (already edited and listed
as a unique copy of an anonymous cycle; the catalogue lacks the literature),
F-21br and F-hwsh (an exposition of Peter of Spain, *Tractatus* V, matching no
printed commentary compared; with F-7smu), F-8wp3 (a gloss on the *Doctrinale*,
vv. 2090-2096), F-pleo (one hand is a commentary on Institutes 4.6.35-38),
F-cbfe and F-imyy (a source of one passage each), F-pdds (a charter, not a
literary text), F-otf6 and F-1jo7 (genre only).

Unidentified: F-ho7z, F-jfv6, F-fzqt, and F-koak, which the literature itself
lists as unidentified.

## What limited the batch

- Source access: Kramp's edition (F-n8nv) and Schulte's Stephen of Tournai
  (F-ja1n) were available only as search snippets; DBNL full-text search was
  rate-limited; Corpus Corporum was slow and the Internet Archive search
  failed on several queries.
- Image size: F-pleo (about 640 pixels wide) and F-8wp3 (1377) could not be
  read much beyond lemmata.
- Unprinted school texts: the logic leaves and F-ho7z gave readable text and
  no printed match.
