# Recheck of the 11 October batch's unresolved records

The [twenty-fragment batch](batch-2026-10-11.md) identified two records and left
eighteen unresolved, ten of them without any dependable reading. Its audit
suggested that selection and reading were the bottleneck. This recheck tested
that on the same eighteen records and the same cached images, the same day.

## Procedure

The coordinator (Claude, Opus) knew the batch's ledger entries and audits, so
nothing here is an independent first reading. It read three fragments directly
from enlarged crops of the cached images and searched connected phrases. Five
further model sessions then each took three or four of the remaining records
with a common brief: read the images yourself at reading size, obtain several
consecutive lines of connecting prose, search Google Books, archive.org full
text and Corpus Corporum, and report an identification only for about five
consecutive lines matching a specific edition. Those readers also knew the
earlier ledger entries. The coordinator then rechecked each proposed
identification against the manuscript image and the source before accepting it.
No isolated readings were made, so no new image-only records were frozen; the
three accepted identifications have new source-assisted records that cite the
existing image-only bases. No human palaeographic review took place.

## Results

Three records reach the publication threshold:

- **F-au0s**, Bernard of Parma, *Casus longi*, on X 3.4.11–12 and X 3.5.5–6.
  Found by the coordinator from one correctly read sentence. Compared with the
  OCR text of the casus printed in a glossed Decretals; printed page images and
  a separate *Casus longi* edition were not collated.
- **F-su3k**, *Pharetra doctorum et philosophorum*, "De amore inimicorum".
  Found by a reader through Google Books snippets of the Mentelin print, which
  surfaced only when the title phrase was added to the query. The coordinator
  then read fols. 39v–40v of the Munich copy. The leaf has the same entries in
  a different order of author groups.
- **F-z56d**, Petrus Hispanus (non-papa), *Summa "Absoluta cuiuslibet"*. Found
  by a reader from six lines. A second pass then followed the edition through
  Google Books search-inside results, which give page numbers, and read the
  rest of the leaf against it: the lower recto and the whole verso agree with
  pp. 8–10 in order. The edition's pages were still not seen; the responses
  are saved beside this note.

Two are leads short of that threshold and stay partial: **F-51ct** (Peter of
Spain, *Tractatus* XI, or a commentary that follows it; half-lines against
snippets) and **F-qnuy** (Godfrey of Fontaines, Quodlibet V q. 12; the argument
in sequence without verbatim agreement).

For most of the rest the base text or genre is now fixed, and several earlier
ledger statements were wrong: F-mfvu is a commentary on Priscian XVII and not
law; F-ldh6 concerns the four causes and is not law; F-2u3k's lower text
comments Psalm 50 and not Psalm 118; F-4t7o's exemplum has a youth speaking to
his mother, not a monk to his soul; F-zkhv is on Codex 7.53 and not on the
Digest title *de rei vindicatione*; F-3oab concerns consequences and not matter
and form. F-dr6y glosses the *Doctrinale*; F-481s comments Peter of Spain's
*Tractatus*; F-664n and F-lwy7 are sermons for identifiable Sundays; F-opyx is
a set of questions on angels. F-516x yielded one tentative passage and F-01ku
remains unread. Apart from F-qnuy (one passage spot-checked) and part of
F-664n, these partial findings are single-reader reports that the coordinator
did not recheck; the ledger notes say so.

The batch therefore stands at 5 identified, 14 partial and 1 unidentified of 20.

## What this does and does not show

The earlier isolated readers returned `[illegible]` for hands that are legible
at reading size; F-au0s and F-su3k are regular book hands. With no dependable
reading, the first coordinator recorded that no source search was justified.
That stopping rule, not the difficulty of the leaves, accounts for at least the
three identifications here. It does not follow that isolation is worthless: the
readers in this recheck were assisted throughout, and their readings were
accepted only where a source confirmed them. The remaining sermon and
university-commentary fragments were read at length and still matched nothing
in the searched corpora, which is consistent with unprinted material but does
not establish it. These are the same twenty non-random records, so the changed
count is not a measured yield for the method.

One preserved-evidence caution: the F-su3k assisted reading was made largely
from a reduced whole-page view. It supports the sequence of entries; it is not
a letter-accurate transcription.

## Tooling added

`tools/localcrop.py` cuts reading bands and boxes from a cached native image
(rotation, contrast stretch, native coordinates printed). `tools/gw.py` lists
digitised copies of an incunable from its GW number, the step that turned the
F-su3k snippet match into a page comparison. METHOD.md records the stopping
rule and the two search lessons.

Validation is recorded in the pull request that adds this file.
