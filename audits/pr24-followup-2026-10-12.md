# PR 24 follow-up, 12 October 2026

Codex review of F-bcte and F-cckq after the batch identification. Source-assisted throughout: the existing reports, preserved readings, proposed identifications and comparison texts were known. No isolated reader or human palaeographer was involved. Existing reading records remain unchanged. This follow-up strengthens verification and narrows interpretation; it does not claim newly recovered fragment text.

## F-bcte

Opened the native-resolution images of both faces (10523 × 6640 pixels) linked in the report. Compared the report's selected passages with Giles vol. 6, printed [p. 232](https://archive.org/download/completeworksofv06bede/page/n253.jpg) and [p. 233](https://archive.org/download/completeworksofv06bede/page/n254.jpg), not just OCR. The Archive download indices differ from the scan leaf numbers; the printed page numbers were read on the images.

The chapter identification holds. The damaged recto lines 17–18 remain uncertain; the printed image does not authorize replacing visible manuscript wording with the edition's text. Recto line 4 was also examined using a blue-channel autocontrast crop, against the unaltered colour image, without a reliable new reading. Whole-face and enlarged colour crops of the abraded verso did not yield a reliable extended passage. Scattered possible matches farther down were not promoted to a transcription.

The surviving verso opening falls inside the next sentence of the edition. The report now makes explicit that the intervening words beginning "Legimus namque" have not been recovered. Jones's 1943 edition and its 1977 CCSL revision are distinguished bibliographically; neither critical apparatus was accessed. No date or witness-family claim is added.

## F-cckq

Corrected the dating sentence: about 1285 is compatible with the later part of the catalogue's 1250–1300 range.

Located the previously unavailable [Kurtscheid study](https://mandadb.hu/dokumentum/1020570/franziskanischestudien1914_1_3.pdf), *Franziskanische Studien* 1 (1914), 269–290. Read its dating, redaction and source discussions as PDF-extracted text; inspected the printed images of pp. 276 and 280, including the explicit assignment of Leipzig Ms 885 to the first redaction. This led to a manuscript test, rather than an assignment of the fragment by date alone.

[Leipzig, Universitätsbibliothek, Ms 885](https://digital.ub.uni-leipzig.de/object/viewid/0000055446), [IIIF manifest](https://iiif.ub.uni-leipzig.de/0000055446/manifest.json): located Appellatio with overview images, then inspected native-resolution ff. 21r–22r (3840 × 5760 pixels). The detailed comparison is f. 22r, left column, approximately native box 610,960,1710,4200. Compared the renunciation sequence with the fragment verso and the preserved assisted reading. Abbreviations below are expanded; ellipses omit intervening text.

| Test | Leipzig f. 22r | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Return to the original judge | "ab episcopo ... ad episcopum" | Agrees with Bremen against the fragment's "ad papam ... ad ordinarium". |
| Delegated judge loses jurisdiction | Proceeds from "nulla remanet ei iurisdictio" to "Unde si redit ad delegatum" | The fragment's "e. ut debitus" is not present at this point. |

| After payment | "ad quem appellatum ... extra ... iurisdictionem" | Does not explain the fragment's next distinction, "Aut appellatur super causis maioribus". |

The early witness confirms the work identification but does not resolve the fragment's recension. In particular, its differences from Bremen and Bruges cannot simply be attributed to the fragment preserving the first redaction. Cues 267 and Metz 117 were not collated. No textual genealogy or redating is established.

Also inspected the Bremen half-pages left unchecked in the batch report: f. 28v upper, f. 29r upper right, f. 31v lower. The unplaced continuation was not located there; this remains a negative search result. The report no longer treats the case discussions as demonstrably original composition by the compiler, since the study documents extensive use of earlier canonistic sources.

No new PR was opened. Changes belong to the existing identification PR; no stronger confidence label or new fragment transcription is manufactured from the extra checks.
