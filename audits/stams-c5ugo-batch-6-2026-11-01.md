The sixth batch from the uncatalogued Stams fragments (collection C-ugo5;
title "x", shelfmark, material and dimensions only). Twenty records from the
triage's middle group: school and theological texts, liturgy, early modern
part-books, printed waste and archival pieces. F-tnqw was left out at
selection because its strips are mounted with typed modern transcriptions
that would have to be masked almost entirely; the 44-piece court record
F-lv0w was again left for later.

## Process

1. Tile packets from native images, one packet per record, at most 45
   images; the typed transcription mounted beside F-wmq3 piece 3 was masked.
   First readings by fresh model readers given only the prompt and tiles
   (`stams6-2026-11-01-image-only`), one reader per packet, each writing
   every tile up before opening the next. No reader stopped on the image
   limit; two lost their earliest images to it after writing them up. Pieces
   mounted upside down on F-r30z (first face) and F-mnxg (32-1, 32-3) were
   read by a second fresh reader from a 180-degree rotation; those images and
   readings are part of the frozen reading. The F-xvty reader saw modern
   pencil labels naming two Sundays and said so; the F-zp2w reader saw a
   spine label of the host volume.
2. Research by ten sessions, two records each, with the document brief for
   the four archival records. Five were sent back: to read pieces left out
   (F-r30z), to test named leads (F-8u0s printed newsletters; F-yslj
   Henriquez and the *Vita Bernardi*; F-r30z LMU 812's letter section), and
   to read a source on its page image rather than OCR (F-wmq3, Sartorius).
   The coordinator found the 1409 foundation charter for F-wmq3 on
   monasterium.net.
3. One batched CoMMA search after research, alone and complete, over the
   Latin queries of eight records: no hit rose meaningfully above its
   shuffled control.
4. Checks. For each identification a fresh checker read a passage on the
   image, mostly one the research had not transcribed, and compared it with a
   source page image; most checkers had seen the research report first, so
   these are rechecks, not blind readings. The coordinator read one matched
   passage on the image for F-jt31, F-xvty, F-ssa8, F-r30z and F-yslj and
   compared F-rnlr's p. 469 with the BSB page image. For the six records with
   archival text, two fresh readers with no transcription read 31 shuffled
   neutral crops of dating clauses and names, and the coordinator reread every
   disagreement on the image.

## Corrections made by the checks

- F-r30z: two legend faces the research had not read are also *Legenda aurea*
  (101-10r Elisabeth, c. 168; 101-5v Clement, c. 170), so the legends run in
  order; the research's view that the Elisabeth text was not *Legenda aurea*
  was wrong. LMU 812 does have *Epitheta ad* headings (not in red).
- F-ssa8: three sermon loci found from OCR were wrong or imprecise; the r/v
  labels are reversed on four pieces; pieces placed only by OCR are left
  without loci.
- F-jt31: the Leonine text runs over pp. 166-170; the apparent word-order
  variant carries interlinear transposition marks giving the edition's order.
- F-rnlr: the 1593 and 1598 printings are other settings, so the 1584
  Steinman setting stands; the surname in the letter is "Schillbrig[...]",
  not "Schillerin", and the signature "H. Pa[n/r]storffer".
- F-xvty: use stated as inference; one printed missal (Passau 1498) confirmed
  on page images; two small variants added.
- F-7l2c: the twelve heavens are Clavius's count and six lines are not in
  Coimbra, so the notes are not a plain abridgement and direct use of Coimbra
  is not proven.
- F-gkmr: the printed piece is one almanac page printed face down, not a
  strip with two layers; its old/new-style table dates it 1644-1699.
- F-yslj: Henriquez is the probable source (his own margin headings, absent
  from Surius 1572, though short enough that an intermediary is not excluded); the protocol's month is March, so the archivist's pencil
  "Aug." is wrong; the surname is "[W/Cr]öber(?)", not "Lorber".
- F-j80e: the creditor "Kilgen" holds; the place "Rhum(?)" and the third
  digit of the year are uncertain.

## Review

An independent review of the pull request agreed with the core identifications
of F-jt31, F-rnlr, F-ssa8 and F-xvty on spot checks, and raised three points,
all accepted: F-r30z's legend text is an unidentified adaptation of the
*Legenda aurea*, so the record is partial, not identified; F-yslj's dependence
on Henriquez, and with it the 1623 lower limit, is probable rather than
proven; F-xvty's group of uses is not confined to southern Germany and the
Alps.
