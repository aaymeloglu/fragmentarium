# Fourth twenty-fragment batch of 12 October 2026

Twenty records absent from the 102-entry ledger were taken from the saved
20 September catalogue sweep, which had 120 records left. This is a selected
batch, not a random sample, and its yield is not a success rate for the method.

## Selection

The coordinator looked at reduced images of about a hundred of the 120
remaining records, leaving aside those in Hebrew, Coptic and Greek, music
without text, and records whose title already names the work. Most of what
remains is narrow strips. The twenty kept were those with the most connected
text visible: one whole leaf (F-t273), printed waste (F-1sxr), three German and
two Middle Dutch records, and thirteen Latin strips and part-leaves. The
catalogue record of each was read before the choice was final.

Three things went wrong at selection:

- F-xzix was chosen and dropped before any reading: its only published images
  are 205 and 216 pixels wide. F-hzbg replaced it.
- F-0h5j: the coordinator took the legible column in the first image for the
  fragment. It is the host manuscript's page; the fragment is the sewing
  guards beside it, about 150 pixels wide. The first reading therefore
  transcribes the host page, and the record is unreadable as published.
- F-voqv: the record's title says "Liturgica (unidentified)", but a full
  description linked from the record already names most of its texts. The
  coordinator read the record's summary fields and not the linked description.

## First readings

Each record got a packet of reduced overviews and overlapping native-pixel
tiles (`tools/tilepacket.py`). Eight fresh reader sessions, given only their
packets and the reader brief, wrote one response per packet; all twenty were
frozen and committed before any source search. Limits:

- The coordinator had seen reduced images of every record before the readings
  were frozen, and chose the tile areas.
- The Antwerp strips are 300 to 520 pixels wide in the published images; three
  readers enlarged or contrast-stretched their tiles to read them and said so
  in their responses.
- F-m05m and F-28bq were read from photographs of strips in place inside
  bindings, turned upright; F-l6yp and F-5oah likewise in place.

## Source research

Ten research sessions, one to three records each, received the first readings,
the cached images and the search tools with the research brief, and reread the
images before searching. Five records were sent back for a second pass because
the first had stopped with an untested candidate (F-hzbg, F-5oah, F-vl52,
F-0h0g, F-ln58). For every proposed identification the coordinator read
matched passages on the manuscript image, and for F-a07w and F-t273 one page
of the printed source on its page image as well; each report says exactly what
that check covered. All of this is model work without human palaeographic
review.

## Results

Four identified, nine partial, seven unidentified. Ledger: 122
examined, 35 published.

Identified:

- **F-t273**: the sermon between the Maximus and Gregory pieces that the
  catalogue already names on this homiliary leaf is Caesarius of Arles,
  Sermo 144, complete. Compared with a machine-read copy of Morin's text and,
  for most of the sermon, with page images of an 1836 printing. The kind of
  homiliary is not established.
- **F-a07w**: Aegidius de Fuscarariis, *Ordo iudiciarius*, chapters 69-70, a
  manual of court procedure; the catalogue had "canon law text". Compared with
  page images of Wahrmund's edition. The two pieces join.
- **F-xukl**: Psalms 149-150 in the Middle Dutch translation of Geert Grote's
  book of hours, on two leaves that carry the same stretch. Compared with a
  transcription of the edition, not page images, at low image resolution.
- **F-n5r2**: the Canticle of Moses, Deuteronomy 32:1-27, on a guard strip
  that keeps four to six letters a line. The kind of book is not established.

Partial: F-voqv (an office lectionary; most of its texts were already in the
linked description, the Passion of Bartholomew and the assignment of one
lesson to Bede are added), F-l6yp (two of four pieces are Daniel 9:16-17),
F-1sxr (the two pieces are leaves B3 and B6 of one edition; three French
translations of Cyprian excluded, edition not found), F-5oah (noun paradigms
in the form of the *Ianua*; three prints compared, none with the same series),
F-hzbg (a commentary on the topics, two printed commentaries rejected on the
page), F-vl52 (an article *Elemosina* from a preaching aid), F-0h0g (a
canonistic text on marriage), F-rxxv (a moral exposition touching Joshua
11) and F-ln58 (an All Saints sermon whose opening
agrees with the repertory incipit of Nicolaus de Byard, *Sermones de sanctis*
no. 194; the body is uncollated and the manuscript that would settle it, Paris
BnF lat. 15951, could not be opened).

Unidentified: F-m05m and F-28bq (legible German prose that matched nothing in
the Middle High German reference corpus or the editions searched; the
manuscript census lists them as unidentified), F-0lqw (four couplets of a
Middle Dutch romance without a proper name), F-ap52, F-wlir, F-3uad and
F-0h5j.

## What limited the batch

- Image size: the Antwerp and Vienna strips are a few hundred pixels wide;
  F-wlir, F-0h5j, F-0h0g and the 13th-century layer of F-3uad gave no run of
  connected words to search.
- Source access: USTC and Gallica were unreachable for F-1sxr; the Dutch
  manuscript database BNM-I is behind a login; Schmitt's edition of the
  *Ianua* and the alphabetical preaching aids most likely to hold F-vl52
  have no searchable text; Gallica refused the manuscript needed for F-ln58.
- A public search of the whole Corpus Middelnederlands
  (corpusmiddelnederlands.ivdnt.org, BlackLab server) and an offline copy of
  the Referenzkorpus Mittelhochdeutsch were used for the vernacular records
  and are worth adding to the retrieval notes.
