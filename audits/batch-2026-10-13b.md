# Second twenty-fragment batch of 13 October 2026

Twenty records absent from the 162-entry ledger were taken from the saved
20 September catalogue sweep, which had 60 records left. This is a selected
batch from the end of the list, not a random sample, and its yield is not a
success rate for the method.

## Selection

`tools/survey.py` listed the 60 remaining records with native image sizes and
made contact sheets, which the coordinator looked at for all of them. Twenty
were set aside before choosing: seven whose title is already an identification
(F-07fr, F-s2ym, F-cpn6, F-6hjl, F-211s, F-l3a5, F-tpid), four Coptic scraps,
two washed Hebrew leaves, four whose manifests return HTTP 404 (F-w3kn,
F-gaff, F-ukx6, F-ugvb), the printed calendar F-hnre, F-uvx5 (one leaf with
F-5oah) and F-xzix (published 205 pixels wide). The catalogue text of the
other forty was fetched with `tools/catalogue.py` and read in condensed form
(summary fields and the prose of any linked description).

The twenty chosen were those of the forty with the most writing visible on
the contact sheets. Three had been set aside in earlier batches as slivers
still in their bindings (F-cqa7, F-3wqe) or as least readable (F-orcw); they
were taken now because little else is left. Not chosen: the records with
musical notation and little or no text, single offsets, and strips the
catalogue itself describes as showing isolated words.

## First readings

Each record got a packet of reduced overviews and overlapping native-pixel
tiles (`tools/tilepacket.py`). The tile areas were chosen by a helper session
that saw the catalogue text and gridded overviews and did not read the text.
Seven fresh reader sessions, given only their packets and the reader brief and
told to view two or three images at a time and write as they went, wrote one
response per packet. All twenty readings were frozen and committed before any
source search.

Limits:

- The coordinator had seen reduced images of every record before the readings
  were frozen.
- Three readers listed their packet directories to learn the image file
  names, which shows the name of `packet.json`; none opened it. One reader
  made rotated and mirrored copies of the F-uf5g tiles to settle the
  direction of the writing, and says so in the response.
- Two packets held more than twelve tiles (F-orcw 13, with overviews 19
  images; F-tfms 12).
- For F-uf5g an overview shows the printed title of the host book, which the
  reader noticed and did not use.
- F-orcw has 40 images; the packet covers pieces from the first eight.
- Most first readings hold line beginnings, line ends or isolated words only,
  because that is what the pieces preserve.

## Source research

Ten research sessions took one to three records each with
`prompts/research-brief.md`, each asked to keep to about forty minutes. For
eleven records the session found no connected phrase to search and ran no
search or only a few; those results are not negatives.

Three sessions were sent back once, for twenty minutes, where the first pass
had stopped with a candidate untested:

- F-3wqe: the first pass read scholastic words on the upper strip and
  proposed, as a subject, the discussion of the infinite in Aristotle's
  Physics. The second pass excluded Thomas Aquinas on Physics III-IV and
  Metaphysics XI, and a search on two words of the lower strip led to the
  Graecismus, where its four lines are four consecutive verses.
- F-orcw: the first pass matched the lemmata to the verse Summula de Summa
  Raymundi through text recognition of a print. The second pass read the
  print's pages, found the manuscript's commentary different from the printed
  one and from the apparatus in Kiel Cod. ms. Bord. 37, and looked at the
  uncached images for a heading or explicit (none).
- F-iv5c: the second pass set the glosses beside William of Moerbeke's
  translation of De sensu chapter 3 and compared Albert the Great and extracts
  of Adam of Buckfield; the gloss agrees with none in wording.
- F-xja1: the Legenda aurea, named and untested in the first pass, was
  searched and excluded.

## Coordinator's checks

For each identification the coordinator read a matched passage on an
enlargement of the manuscript image: all sixteen verso line ends of F-ne56,
six of the seven lines of the first strip of F-x6c5, and the four lines of the
lower strip of F-3wqe. The source was read on a page image for F-ne56
(Arbuthnott missal, p. 134) and F-3wqe (Wrobel, p. 186), and in a
transcription for F-x6c5. The recto of F-ne56 and the second strip of F-x6c5
were not checked in this way.

## What the results are

- F-ne56: the catalogue already named Mark 15:43-46 and Psalm 68. The result
  is a substantial catalogue refinement: it adds the missal identification,
  Tuesday and Wednesday of Holy Week, the Isaiah lesson and the precise
  gradual. It remains published for those additions. The complete book's
  plenary format is not established by the strip.
- F-x6c5 and F-3wqe each identify one of two visible strips. The second strip
  of F-x6c5 is probable only; the upper strip of F-3wqe is unidentified.
- F-orcw and F-iv5c are partial: the text commented on is established, the
  commentary or gloss is not found.

## After this batch

Forty records of the sweep remain. Twenty are those set aside above. The
other twenty were looked at and not chosen: mostly notated chant with little
text, an offset, and strips showing isolated words. A further batch of twenty
unidentified texts is not there to be drawn from this list.

## Review of 13 October 2026

Codex compared the three published identifications with the manuscript crops:
F-3wqe against Wrobel p. 186 on a page image, both faces of F-ne56 against
Arbuthnott pp. 133-134 on page images, and F-x6c5 against the cited Vulgate
transcription. All three scoped text identifications retain high confidence.
The F-ne56 report qualifies the complete book's plenary format and describes
its contribution as a catalogue refinement. F-x6c5's second strip remains
probable, and its proposed same-column reconstruction is compatible with the
text sequence but unproved. Its book type remains open. The two partial cases
were reviewed from the research notes; their source comparisons and the
unidentified cases' searches were not independently repeated. This review does
not certify every transcribed letter.
