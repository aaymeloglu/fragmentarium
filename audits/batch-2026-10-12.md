# Twenty-fragment identification batch, 12 October 2026

Twenty records absent from the 42-entry ledger were taken from the saved
20 September catalogue sweep. This is a selected batch, not a random sample, and
its yield is not a success rate for the method.

## Selection

Thirty Latin records with substantial surviving area were shortlisted, leaving
out liturgical and musical pieces, vernacular texts, records whose "unidentified"
refers to decoration, and all but one of each group of sibling fragments. The
first two images of each were downloaded. Four BnF records (F-4usd, F-kr2n,
F-1szi, F-vpe8) failed with HTTP 403 and were set aside; the cause was a blocked
image address and is now fixed in the tools (below), so they remain available.
From contact sheets of the other twenty-six the coordinator dropped six that
looked least readable (F-qtiw, F-hwsh, F-orcw, F-pdds, F-6sg6, F-m248). The twenty
kept therefore lean towards legible leaves.

## First readings

Each record got a packet of one reduced overview per image plus overlapping
native-pixel tiles of the text area, ten to twenty lines each
(`tools/tilepacket.py`). Six fresh reader sessions, given only the packets and a
short instruction file, wrote one response per packet. All twenty were frozen
and committed before any source search. Limits to the isolation, as the readers
themselves reported:

- Four readers ran one directory listing of their packet folders (file names only).
- The F-jtck images show a library stamp and shelfmark.
- F-jtua was photographed upside down and its first reader gave only a layout
  survey. A rotated packet went to a seventh fresh reader; both responses are
  preserved (`batch-2026-10-12-image-only-unrotated` and `batch-2026-10-12-image-only`).
- The packets covered the first two images of each record. For F-bcte those do
  not show the leaf later identified (fol. F), so that identification has no
  independent first reading.
- One reader noted that images from two packets had dropped out of its context
  after it wrote those responses, re-opened several and found the files matched.

The coordinator had seen contact sheets and two tiles (F-bcte, F-qmfx) before the
readings were frozen; the readers had not been given anything from them.

## Source research

Five research sessions, four records each, received the first readings, the
images and the search tools, and were told the catalogue titles as leads to test.
They reread the images themselves before searching. The coordinator then sent
follow-up tasks for candidates left untested and, for each proposed
identification, compared manuscript and source on the images before recording it.
All of this is model work without human palaeographic review.

## Results

Three records reached the publication threshold:

- **F-bcte**, Bede, *De temporum ratione*, chapter 43. Found by reading the
  record's later images; compared with the OCR text of Giles's edition.
- **F-7nce**, Aldobrandinus de Toscanella, *Scala fidei*. Found through
  Schneyer's sermon repertory; the work is unprinted, so the comparison is with
  page images of Vatican, BAV, Pal. lat. 449.
- **F-03xq**, John of Abbeville, sermons for saints' days. Found through
  Schneyer; unprinted, compared with page images of Charleville-Mézières BM 218.

Seventeen stay unresolved, with ledger notes: six with a base text or source
established (F-qmfx, F-cckq, F-333n, F-x8vo, F-as3x, F-die2) and eleven placed by
genre or subject only. Several catalogue titles do not fit the text: F-njqk is a
theological question, not medicine; F-xmrx, F-03xq, F-rmhf, F-60uk and F-wp85 are
sermon material, not commentaries or treatises; F-as3x is a verse rendering of a
canon-law summa, not satire. Two unresolved records carry a specific next step:
F-as3x (no second verse version of Raymond's Summa has been located to compare
with; the lost versification credited to Arnulf of Louvain is a possibility that
cannot be tested) and F-xmrx (incipits absent from Schneyer as searchable).

Two of the three identifications were made against a digitised manuscript
because the work has no edition. A repertory incipit located the work; the
collation of sermon bodies is what carries the claim.

The ledger now holds 62 attempted records and 16 published identifications.

## Tools changed

- `tools/iiif.py` and `tools/acquire_batch.py` rewrite BnF image addresses from
  `gallica.bnf.fr/iiif`, which returns HTTP 403 to scripts, to
  `openapi.bnf.fr/iiif/image/v3`, which serves the same images. `iiif.py` also
  resolves newer Ghent manifests that point at `libcatalog.ugent.be`.
- `tools/tilepacket.py` builds the overview-plus-tiles packets used here.
