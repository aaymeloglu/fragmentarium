# Second twenty-fragment batch of 12 October 2026

Twenty records absent from the 62-entry ledger were taken from the saved
20 September catalogue sweep, which had 160 records left. This is a selected
batch, not a random sample, and its yield is not a success rate for the method.

## Selection

Thirty Latin records were shortlisted from the 160: the larger surviving pieces,
the four Paris records set aside in the previous batch for an image-server fault
since fixed (F-4usd, F-kr2n, F-1szi, F-vpe8), early texts likely to be in the
Patrologia Latina, and two early-modern paper items. Up to four images of each
were downloaded and the coordinator looked at a contact sheet of each. Ten were
dropped: F-tfms, F-t1mc, F-3y8q and F-mc4a (almost no text), F-orcw, F-qtiw and
F-8epz (abraded), and F-ybvx, F-otf6 and F-fzqt (held in reserve). The twenty kept
lean towards legible leaves, and the remaining pool is mostly narrow strips.

## First readings

Each record got a packet of reduced overviews plus overlapping native-pixel tiles
(`tools/tilepacket.py`). Ten fresh reader sessions, given only their packets and
a short instruction file, wrote one response per packet. Every response was
frozen and committed before any source search for that record. Limits:

- The instruction file asked each reader to end with a line listing the files it
  opened. Those lines contained the coordinator's scratch directory path. Before
  freezing, the directory prefix was removed from them mechanically (file names
  kept); nothing else in any response was changed.
- One reader ran a file-name listing of its packet folders and made rotated
  working copies of two tiles of F-0y54, whose lower strip is upside down.
- The F-lg7g response was frozen as soon as its file appeared, while its reader
  was still working on another packet; the file was compared with the reader's
  final version afterwards and was identical.
- The packets covered at most the first four images of a record. For F-ziuq,
  F-ktuw, F-lg7g, F-v0qx and F-8zoy most of the leaves therefore have no
  independent first reading.
- **F-osrv was read from the wrong page.** The coordinator's packet showed the
  large sideways Priscian leaf bound beside the fragment, not the fragment (see
  below). The preserved reading is a reading of that Priscian page.
- For F-g3zk the reader was given the host volume's computus leaf and a page of
  pen trials; the catalogued strip was not in the tiles.
- The coordinator had seen the contact sheets before the readings were frozen
  and had recognised the subject of several leaves from them.

## Source research

Nine research sessions, one to three records each, received the first readings,
the images and the search tools, with the catalogue titles as leads to test and
a budget of about 150 tool calls a record. They reread the images before
searching. The coordinator sent second and third passes where a candidate was
untested, and for every proposed identification read at least one of the matched
passages on the manuscript image, and for three of them on the source's page
image as well, before recording it. All of this is model work without human
palaeographic review.

Part-way through, the coordinator found that the first result for F-osrv
(Priscian, *Institutiones* XIV) was a text the catalogue already names for the
sibling record F-fwl6, and that F-osrv itself is a small leaf from another
manuscript bound with it. The research session was sent back to the right leaf,
and every session was then told to read the manifest's metadata and canvas
labels first and to state what the catalogue already knew. METHOD.md now says so.

Google Books search ran through the local service with a key. It returned
intermittent errors throughout and its daily quota ran out during the second
passes; later searches used archive.org, Corpus Corporum and the Munich digital
library instead.

## Results

Eight records reached the publication threshold:

- **F-kr2n, F-1szi, F-vpe8**, three leaves (ff. 4-5, 6, 7 of Paris, BnF, NAL 2317)
  of one manuscript of the anonymous Carolingian homiliary transmitted under the
  name of Haymo and printed by Liverani in 1863. Compared with page images of
  another manuscript of the collection (Paris, BnF, Latin 3806) and with snippets
  of Liverani's edition, of which no page scan was found. The leaves differ in
  places from both, so the recension is left open. The catalogue had noted that
  one page is close to Haymo; it treats the three as separate items.
- **F-4usd**, the pseudo-Galenic *Liber de dynamidiis*, compared with page images
  of the 1625 Giunta print and, at two places, of Cologny, Cod. Bodmer 58.
- **F-eflq**, Johannes Versor, questions on *Posterior Analytics* II, in a form
  reworded against the two Cologne prints compared.
- **F-ziuq**, Henricus de Piro, *Super Institutiones*, book 1; five of 22 faces
  collated with page images of the Cologne edition of about 1481.
- **F-v0qx**, a handwritten copy of the last four pages of Johannes Matthaeus,
  *Concionum dispositiones*, compared with page images of the 1581 edition. Its
  page numbers fit the 1590 edition as catalogued, which was not seen. Found by
  full-text search of the Munich digital library after Google Books and
  archive.org searches had found only the author's sources.
- **F-x61h**, the end of the Italian verse regimen known as the *Cibaldone*,
  compared with page images of the first edition. The Latin recipe and the list
  of women's faults on the same leaf are only partly traced. Whether the leaf
  was copied from a print or from a manuscript is left open.

Twelve stay unresolved, with ledger notes:

- A source or base text established: F-8zoy (an abbreviated sermon book reworking
  two sermons of Berthold of Regensburg, matched on page images of Freiburg UB
  Hs. 152 and Leipzig UB Ms 498, and a third sermon from another source; recorded
  as partial because the book itself is not found), F-0y54 (a Jeremiah commentary
  built on Hugh of St Cher), F-wbbg (a florilegium: two of its three extracts are
  traced, a gloss on Wisdom 1:7 is not), F-ktuw (a logic course of the Paris type, not theology as catalogued),
  F-m7i4 (kin to Nicolaus de Byard's distinctio on preparing the heart), F-yjo1
  (notes following Melanchthon's logic), F-10zj (an unfound hymn for St Lawrence),
  F-7smu (an exposition of Peter of Spain's fifth tract, not music as catalogued),
  F-x3yo (the contents leaf of a composite volume of the canons of St Leonard near
  Liège: one more entry matched to an edition and one to a work, and the list dated
  after February 1492; two books from the 1884 Vergauwen sale, Den Haag, KB, KW 169
  G 7 and Brussels, KBR, ms. II 734, are candidates for pieces of the volume, on
  catalogue evidence that does not establish the link).
- Placed by subject only or unread: F-lg7g (a student's philosophy course notes,
  37 leaves, of which helper readers covered the top half of each page; its
  research session spent far more reading than any other and was stopped without
  a tested candidate), F-osrv, F-g3zk.

Catalogue descriptions that do not fit the text: F-1szi and F-vpe8 are homilies,
not commentaries; F-ktuw is logic, not theology; F-7smu is logic, not music; the
Fragmentarium manifests of F-v0qx and F-yjo1 carry the cover images of
another record (F-x06a), and the F-lg7g manifest serves the first three images
again in place of fourteen later ones.

The three homiliary leaves were compared mainly with another manuscript, and
they and F-eflq carry a stated difference of wording from every printed or
manuscript witness compared.

The ledger now holds 82 attempted records and 26 published identifications.

## Tools changed

- `tools/iiif.py` and `tools/acquire_batch.py` now notice when a manifest gives
  several canvases one image. A canvas is corrected when the record's other file
  names are numbered in step with their canvases and the image at the expected
  number has exactly the size the manifest gives for that canvas; otherwise it is
  marked as a fault and not fetched. On F-lg7g this corrects all fourteen affected
  canvases; on F-jfv6, whose two canvases share one image with nothing to judge
  by, the second is refused.
- `tools/contactsheet.py` makes labelled thumbnail sheets of every image in a
  record, for the survey step METHOD.md now asks for with many-leaved records.
- `tools/register.py` writes the ledger row a reading needs from the cached
  manifest.
- `tools/mdzsearch.py` searches the OCR full text of the Munich digital library.

## Review after submission

A second model (Codex) reviewed the reports against selected manuscript and
source images on 12 October 2026 and agreed with the eight text identifications
and with leaving F-8zoy unpublished. Two claims were changed in response:

- F-x3yo had been submitted as an identification on the strength of the two
  located books. Shared ownership, similar size, one sale and matching modern
  bindings make them candidates, not established pieces of this volume, so the
  record is now a partial result with a ledger note and no public report.
- The F-x61h report had said the leaf "was not copied from" the first edition
  because of its spelling, a missing couplet and better rhymes. A scribe copying
  a print could produce all three; the report now leaves the question open.

The same review corrected two statements in this note (F-wbbg's extracts are
not all traced; F-v0qx's catalogue description already calls it a manuscript
copy).
