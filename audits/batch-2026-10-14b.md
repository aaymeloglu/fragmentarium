# Twenty-fragment batch of 14 October 2026 (second)

Twenty records were chosen from the 14 October catalogue sweep
(`data/unidentified-2026-10-14.csv`). This is a selected batch, not a random
sample, and its yield is not a success rate for the method.

## Selection

`tools/survey.py` listed the 160 sweep records without ledger rows and made
contact sheets. A helper session screened all of them on the sheets and read
the catalogue text of 38 to the end (`tools/catalogue.py`), excluding the forty
records passed over in earlier batches, records with missing manifests,
Coptic and washed Hebrew records, records whose title already names the work,
chant tabs with isolated words, offsets and near-blank strips. It found
eighteen good candidates and two weak reserves (F-oc1a, F-4lwf); all twenty
were taken. The remaining pool of the 14 October sweep is now mostly chant
tabs, spine strips and offsets.

Where a record's catalogue already identified part of its content, the batch
worked only on the unidentified part: F-bgku (the Agnes poem on 1v, not the
antiphoner side), F-q5ti (the containing compilation, not the sources of its
stories), F-kcu7 (the commentary, not Peter of Spain's base text).

## First readings

A helper session that saw the catalogue text and reduced overviews, and did
not read or search the text, chose the tile areas (`tools/tilepacket.py`).
Eight fresh reader sessions, given only their packets and the reader brief,
wrote one response per packet.

Two readers reported that many image reads had failed ("request limit") and
that they had drafted text for packets they had not yet seen, then corrected
the drafts after the images loaded. Those five responses (F-2bor, F-7dam,
F-hi8u, F-6zm1, F-nfu2) were discarded unfrozen, and each packet was read
again by a fresh session given that one packet only, with an instruction to
write nothing for an image that did not load. All five second readers report
seeing every image.

Readings were frozen and committed before any source search on that record:
fifteen together (57206b0), the five re-read records as their readings came in
(c28fdd2, 2ab9059, 9de2ce0).

Limits:

- The coordinator had seen reduced contact sheets of every record before the
  readings were frozen.
- Several readers listed their packet directories to learn the image names,
  or made enlarged, contrast-enhanced or rotated copies of their tiles; each
  says so in its response.
- Two readers noted a library stamp and pencil shelfmark visible on the leaf,
  and one that the Agnes poem names its subject in its last line.
- For F-k81e, four faces of the 24 were tiled; for F-oc1a, ten of 31 images.

## Source research

Fifteen research sessions took one or two records each with
`prompts/research-brief.md`, amended to forbid any reading from an image that
failed to load and to stop after about thirty minutes without a candidate.
Google Books returned errors or ran out of quota for parts of several
sessions; those searches went to Internet Archive, Corpus Corporum and the
Munich digital library instead.

Six sessions were sent back once, for about twenty minutes:

- F-nfu2: the first pass had only OCR of the 1527 edition; the second read its
  page images (MDZ bsb10139648, scans 709-712) and corrected two readings.
- F-rjkx: the second pass checked the key passages on Wagner's page images and
  looked for any record of an octosyllabic Anglo-Norman Tundale.
- F-0olk: Giles of Rome, rejected first on snippets, was read on the Venice
  1500 page images and excluded.
- F-8q1h: a dated Peraldus manuscript (Clm 2654) was read to test the
  fragment's order; it has the printed order.
- F-7dam: Hugh of St Victor's De grammatica and Hagen's Anecdota Helvetica were
  searched and excluded; the Excerptiones de Prisciano were not reachable.
- F-1tv3: Berengaudus (1554 page image) and Olivi (transcription OCR) were
  excluded; Gorran was not reachable.

## Coordinator's checks

For each identification the coordinator read one passage on the native
manuscript image and the source's page image:

- F-nfu2: canvas 1, Textus XXVII, against the 1527 Giunta edition, scan 712.
- F-kcu7: front strip, canvas 0, against Nuremberg GNM Hs 27773, fol. 128ra.
- F-rjkx: canvas 3, col. b, against Wagner 1882, p. 41, ll. 2-8.
- F-q5ti: canvas 1, f. 25v col. b, against Cambridge CCCC MS 327, f. 176r.

## What the results are

- F-nfu2: Hippocrates, Regimen acutorum IV with Galen's commentary in Latin,
  Textus XIX-XX and XXIV-XXVII. The red lines are the Hippocratic text, not
  rubrics. One wording difference from the 1527 edition is recorded.
- F-kcu7: the anonymous Compilationes supra Summulas Petri Hispani ("Omnes
  homines"), Tracts IX-XI, in fuller wording than the one copy compared; the
  redaction is open.
- F-rjkx: an Anglo-Norman octosyllabic verse translation of the Visio
  Tnugdali, compared with the Latin; no French witness or edition was found,
  and no novelty is claimed.
- F-q5ti: section IV of an anonymous, unedited preaching compilation of
  exempla, compared with the Cambridge CCCC 327 copy.
- Partial: F-0olk, F-1tv3, F-2bor, F-2gqg, F-7dam, F-8q1h, F-bb5g, F-bgku,
  F-hi8u, F-hz5c, F-k81e, F-oc1a, F-plko, F-qcz8. Unidentified: F-4lwf,
  F-6zm1.
- Catalogue corrections without an identification: F-2bor is not medical;
  F-plko expounds Hebrews 11, not Job; F-qcz8 is on Gratian's Decretum, not a
  theological treatise; F-0olk's last lemma is 424b22, not 427b22; F-bb5g's
  "nees perilleront" means ships will be imperilled; the 1397 date of F-bgku
  stands on 1r, not under the Agnes poem.

Codex retained the four identifications and asked for two corrections, both
made after the coordinator rechecked the images:

- F-kcu7: the headline now names the *Compilationes supra Summulas* "or a
  close derivative", since the fuller wording leaves a reworking open.
- F-rjkx: the report had quoted "Le poer ke deu lur ad done / Solum deu ne le
  unt use" as the end of fol. [1]v column b. Five legible lines and at least
  three faint ones follow, and the foot is trimmed. The column reaches the
  Latin "Potentes potenter tormenta patiuntur", and fol. [2]r column a opens
  with the angel's answer that follows it in the Latin, so no sentence of the
  Latin is skipped; how many French lines are lost cannot be determined.
