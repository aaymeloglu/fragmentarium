# Nine-fragment batch of 17 October 2026

Nine records from the 14 October catalogue sweep, chosen after a second
screen of the records the first screen had rejected. This is a selected batch,
not a random sample, and its yield is not a success rate for the method.

## Selection

The batch of 14 October (second) had chosen 20 of 159 unattempted sweep
records, largely from contact sheets. On 17 October the 95 rejects that were
not firm exclusions (missing manifests, Coptic or washed Hebrew, images too
small, titles that name the work) were screened again: the full catalogue
description read to the end, and a native-resolution sample of each object
looked at (`tools/probe.py`). Result: 4 worth an attempt, 13 possible, 78 not
(most because the catalogue already identifies the text, or it is music only,
painted over, under pasted paper or blank at native size). The first screen's
blanket exclusion of offsets was wrong: two of the nine are offsets (F-4h06,
F-nlzw). The batch is the four attempts and the five best of the possible.
Manifests for F-8jvq, F-j63y and F-0k3i returned 404, and F-ze3h's images 404,
so those four could not be screened.

## First readings

A helper session that saw the catalogue text and the screen's notes, and did
not read or search the text, chose the tile areas. The two offsets were tiled
mirrored (`tilepacket.py` "mirror"), so that readers saw them the right way
round. Each of nine fresh reader sessions read one packet only, with the rule
that nothing is written for an image that failed to load; all nine report
seeing every image. All nine readings were frozen and committed (aef401d)
before any source search.

Limits:

- The coordinator had seen samples of every record during the screen.
- Several readers listed their packet directories or made enlarged crops of
  their tiles; each says so in its response.
- F-67mf's homily face (IIv) was never scanned; only the separate strip at the
  top seam was read.
- F-psr5 has 29 guards but only six are imaged.

## Source research

Six research sessions took one or two records each. Two were sent back once,
for about twenty-five minutes, with named candidates to read:

- F-psr5: Rockinger's index lead (I, p. 135), Guido Faba's Summa, Dictamina and
  Epistole, and part of Boncompagno were read; none has the strips' wording.
- F-4h06: Bartolus on C. 6.43 was read on the Venice 1577 page images and
  excluded. Cino da Pistoia, Odofredus and Baldus on the Codex were not found
  in a searchable print and remain untested.

## Coordinator's check

F-v7a4: canvas 1, column b (Matthew 25:31-34), read on the native image, and
its liturgical placement checked against the 1519 Teutonic Order missal page
image (MDZ bsb10197441, scan 91).

## What the results are

- F-v7a4: the cover is a leaf of a Mass book, probably a plenary missal, with
  the readings for Monday after the first Sunday of Lent, and a strip has the
  Gospel for Monday after Reminiscere. The catalogue described only a music
  fragment. The texts are Vulgate pericopes; the result is their liturgical
  placement, and the use is open.
- Partial: F-4h06 (offset of a civil-law Codex lectura, probably on
  C. 6.43.3), F-67mf (the unscanned homily follows Jerome and is close to
  Heiric of Auxerre, from the catalogue's transcription only; the strip is
  John 17:7-8 with an Ascension rubric, from a Mass book).
- Unidentified: F-isvn (the same photograph as F-x33a), F-nlzw, F-722c, F-3d30,
  F-8dod, F-psr5.
- Catalogue observations without an identification: F-722c's recto looks like
  pen trials and notes, not book text; F-8dod was read in numbered Office
  lessons and its recto and verso labels are probably swapped; F-3d30's red-Q
  piece belongs to F-0df1.

## Review corrections, 17 October 2026

Codex retained F-v7a4's texts and liturgical placement after comparing the
images with the 1519 missal (scans 91, 93, 106) and asked for two
corrections, both made:

- The Gospel on the cover is Matthew 25:31-35; verse 36 is represented only by
  a possible initial under a loose flap, so the report, ledger and
  source-assisted reading no longer say 31-36.
- "Probably a missal" now holds throughout: the opening no longer states the
  book type categorically, and the layout argument says what the hidden text
  would hold in a missal, since no chant or prayer is visible.
