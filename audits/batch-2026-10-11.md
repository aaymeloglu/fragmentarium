# Twenty-fragment identification batch, 11 October 2026

Twenty records absent from the existing 22-entry attempt ledger were selected
from the saved 20 September catalogue sweep. Selection favored Latin texts with
substantial surviving area and excluded obvious duplicate manuscript siblings
and records whose “unidentified” label referred only to decoration. This is a
selected research batch, not a random sample or an accuracy benchmark.

## Evidence procedure

Three fresh reader sessions received neutral image packets, with no catalogue,
repository reports, candidate texts, or web research. Each was instructed to
inspect layout and useful passages, preserve uncertainties, and write its complete
response unchanged. The readers reported no candidate exposure. Their original
responses and image checksums are preserved in each fragment's
`readings/batch-2026-10-11-image-only.json`. The coordinator registered and
committed the responses before starting source discovery for those fragments.
The coordinator knew the catalogue metadata; later research was source-assisted.
This was model research, without human palaeographic review.

The initial packets contain the first two catalogue canvases per record. They
are a limited sample where more images exist; a binding/spine photograph is not
an independent passage. The records cite the exact requested image URLs.
Whole-image downloads were capped at 5,000 pixels wide for F-su3k, F-ldh6,
F-516x, F-qnuy, F-lwy7 and F-mfvu. When those readers say “native crop,” they
mean pixels of the supplied image, which is smaller than the library original.
The acquisition helper now defaults to true native width and separates cached
resolutions. Any additional source-assisted inspections are reported separately.
No original response has been silently corrected.

All six affected records were subsequently reread from verified library-native
images, with new isolated responses preserved before renewed source comparison.
See the [native-resolution retry audit](native-retry-2026-10-11.md) for coverage,
findings and the limits of comparing this batch with earlier ones.

F-8zoy was considered but its Gallica image endpoints returned HTTP 403; it was
replaced by F-qnuy before reading and is not counted among the twenty. A temporary
manifest failure for F-rl44 recovered on retry. Image files are not committed.

## Outcome recording

The ledger holds the result and stopping reason for every attempt. Only high
confidence identifications receive public reports; a quoted source, subject or
plausible author lead is insufficient. A failed search does not exclude a work,
and unreadable selected passages do not show that the entire fragment is
unreadable. No HTR run was made in this batch.

The coordinator also inspected a library-native upper-left crop of F-516x after
the initial reading. The selected area still did not yield dependable connected
prose; this does not establish that its other columns cannot be read.

## Results

Twenty new records were examined. Two containing texts reached the publication
threshold: **F-rl44**, Serapion Senior's *Breviarium*, tract VII, and **F-rygw**,
Galen's medieval Latin *De regimine sanitatis*, book VI. Eighteen remain unresolved
(eight partial source or subject connections, ten without an established
identification) and receive ledger entries without public reports. The repository now contains
42 attempted records and 10 published identifications. These counts describe
this selected batch, not a measured success rate for the method.

For F-rl44, Avicenna's *Canon* initially supplied a substantial parallel, but the
adjacent recipes did not establish that containing work. The matching sequence
in Serapion's 1497 edition resolved the distinction. This is a concrete benefit
of checking surrounding structure; it does not establish literary dependence
between the works. For F-rygw, one passage located the 1490 edition and a second
passage, previously included in the frozen reading but not used for retrieval,
provided additional verification. The coordinator directly inspected both
manuscript/edition comparisons for Galen and the principal Serapion sequence.
These were source-assisted model checks.

The native-resolution acquisition change fixes a provenance and image-quality
problem exposed during this batch. It is not a demonstrated identification-yield
improvement. No HTR-versus-image-reading experiment or token-efficiency benchmark
was performed.

Initial validation: 79 tests passed; all 75 initial records passed the preservation
check against the merged main branch. Six retry readings bring the preserved
total to 81: 50 previous records, 20 original image-only readings, five separate
assisted additions and six native retry responses. The original
22 ledger entries are unchanged, and the generated site contains exactly the
10 identified/high entries.
