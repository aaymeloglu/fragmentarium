# Twenty-fragment batch of 13 October 2026

Twenty records absent from the 142-entry ledger were taken from the saved
20 September catalogue sweep, which had 80 records left. This is a selected
batch, not a random sample, and its yield is not a success rate for the method.

## Selection

`tools/survey.py` listed the 80 remaining records with native image sizes and
made contact sheets, which the coordinator looked at for every record that
has images. Four records return HTTP 404 for their manifests (F-w3kn, F-gaff,
F-ukx6, F-ugvb).
The catalogue text of every remaining record was fetched with
`tools/catalogue.py` and the summary fields and descriptions of about fifty
were read, in condensed form, before the choice.

Left out as having no unidentified text to find, or none that can be read:
records whose title is already an identification (F-07fr, F-s2ym, F-cpn6,
F-6hjl, F-211s, F-l3a5, F-tpid), the two washed Hebrew leaves, the four
Coptic scraps, the printed calendar F-hnre, the record F-uvx5 that is one leaf
with F-5oah, and the slivers set aside in the last batch.

One thing went wrong at selection. For F-0df1 the coordinator read the three
initials the linked description gives for strip F4 (*Protexisti*, *Quia*,
*Subito*) and proposed Psalm 63 to the research session as a guess, without
noticing that the same description already says "Psalmus 63, vv. 2-6" further
down. The record title says "unidentified"; the description does not. Nothing
new was found for that record.

## First readings

Each record got a packet of reduced overviews and overlapping native-pixel
tiles (`tools/tilepacket.py`). The tile areas were chosen by a helper session
that saw the catalogue text and gridded overviews and did not read the text.
Eight fresh reader sessions, given only their packets and the reader brief and
told to view two or three images at a time and write as they went, wrote one
response per packet. None reported losing images. All twenty readings were
frozen and committed before any source search.

Limits:

- The coordinator had seen reduced images of every record before the readings
  were frozen.
- Every reader listed its packet directories to learn the image file names,
  which shows the name of `packet.json`; none opened it. Five readers made
  rotated or contrast-stretched copies of packet tiles for strips that are
  upside down or dark in the photographs, and say so in their responses.
- Seven packets held more than twelve images (up to seventeen, F-e0rn).
- The helper session that chose the tile areas discarded the uncommitted
  ledger rows that `tools/register.py` had written, taking them for a side
  effect of its own run. The rows were written again from the same cache
  before the readings were frozen; nothing else was lost.
- The first readings of F-lodh, F-llvm, F-mc4a, F-m248, F-drrc and F-sk67
  hold letter shapes or isolated words only.

## Source research

Ten research sessions took one to three records each with
`prompts/research-brief.md`, each asked to keep to about forty minutes. Eight
were sent back once, for twenty to thirty minutes, with named candidates:

- F-ypxa: the first pass read both faces, tested the ordinary gloss on two
  titles of the Decretals and stopped with Hostiensis, Innocent IV and
  Johannes Andreae named and untested. Sent back to fix the title from the
  allegations and read those apparatus, it found the passage in Hostiensis; a
  third pass read it on a page image and compared Johannes Andreae.
- F-lodh: the first pass placed one face in Psalm 68 from memory of the text.
  Sent back, it collated that face against a transcription of the psalter and
  worked out the other faces by counting lines.
- F-j0wm: the first pass found that the strips are sermons, not an office,
  and matched one join of two sermons to Schneyer's repertory. Sent back for a
  manuscript, it found that the one witness of that part is not online.
- F-3l5z: sent back for the printed page reference and a doubtful line.
- F-ib4j, F-8znq, F-t12t, F-3y8q, F-e0rn, F-m248, F-skg1, F-sk67, F-srfk,
  F-ravu: candidates tested and rejected, or not reached; see the ledger
  notes. For F-m248 the second pass withdrew the first pass's reading of a red
  word as "Error" (its second letter is the x of the numerals beside it).

## Results

Three identifications. For each the coordinator read a matched passage on
enlargements of the manuscript image; the source was read on a page image for
one and in a transcription for two.

| Record | Result | Compared with |
|---|---|---|
| F-ypxa | Hostiensis, *Lectura* on the Decretals, X 3.30.33-34 | Strasbourg 1512 print, page image |
| F-3l5z | Geert Grote, *Getijdenboek*, Hours of the Eternal Wisdom, Compline | Van Wijk 1940, p. 111, DBNL transcription |
| F-lodh | Liturgical psalter, Psalm 68:15-22 on one face | Weber's Gallican text, transcription |

F-lodh is the weakest. The photographs are soft and dark; the identification
is of one face, from ten line beginnings in order, and the research session
first held it at partial. The coordinator read six of those line beginnings
and proposed that the one that did not fit reads "tuo", not "me", which the
session confirmed on the image; the coordinator's proposed line breaks for
three further lines were not confirmed. The other three faces are probable
only, and the monastic division of the psalm at verse 17 was checked in a
modern table, not in a medieval psalter.

Eight partial results: F-j0wm (an anonymous Sunday sermon cycle in Schneyer,
known from Kremsmünster CC 273, not compared), F-skg1 (the mortuary roll's
letter, sender unnamed in both witnesses), F-0df1 (Psalm 63, already in the
catalogue's description), F-8znq (a chain of authorities), F-3y8q (a
scholastic text on the Old and New Law), F-ib4j (probably on *De generatione
et corruptione* I), F-t12t (a scholastic text abridging Augustine), F-sk67
(one phrase found only in Sedulius Scottus on Matthew).

Nine unidentified: F-3qzb, F-e0rn, F-mc4a, F-6sg6, F-m248, F-srfk, F-ravu,
F-drrc, F-llvm.

The ledger has 162 entries and 42 published identifications.

## What limited the batch

- Image size and condition. F-mc4a, F-6sg6, F-m248, F-drrc and F-llvm gave no
  searchable phrase and no search was run on them, so they are not negatives.
  F-llvm is about 13 pixels to the millimetre; F-drrc is under a pastedown.
- The legible page is not always the fragment. On F-3qzb the page with a
  large majuscule line is the last page of the host manuscript (the end of
  John Climacus, *Ad Pastorem*), and the bifolium itself is faded cursive.
- Unprinted or unsearchable works. F-j0wm's cycle has one known witness on
  microfilm. The Middle Dutch legend collections that F-srfk would be tested
  against are not in the searchable corpus. Several commentaries on the
  Sentences named for F-3y8q and F-t12t have no searchable text.
- Sessions stopping early. One session stopped after about a third of its
  budget with nothing tested on either of its records.
- Two image links: canvases 2-3 of F-m248 and canvas 3 of F-6sg6 are served
  from other records' image sets (F-qdpq, F-jnye). `tools/iiif.py info` did
  not flag the first.

## Codex review, 13 October 2026

A source-assisted review of both Hostiensis faces, the Dutch strips and scrap on
both faces, and the Psalm 68 face supports retaining all three text
identifications. Hostiensis was compared with the Strasbourg 1512 page image;
the Dutch and psalm passages were compared with the cited online transcriptions.
This was a selected-passage check, not a certification of every reading or a
rerun of the unresolved cases.

The F-lodh comparison column now follows the cited Weber transcription:
"liberer" in verse 15, "confusionem et" in verse 20, and "respice me" in verse 17.
The first two fall in lost portions and are not readings of the manuscript.
The manuscript's reported "respice in" remains a stated difference. The
source's spellings "inproperium" and "expectavit" are retained in that column.
Monastic use remains provisional, and the other faces remain probable only.

The categorical exclusion of Johannes Andreae's Novella is softened in the
Hostiensis report and ledger: the inspected OCR snippets favor Hostiensis, but
no Novella page image has been checked. Preserved readings are unchanged; these
corrections concern the comparison text and the strength of the conclusions.

## Novella read on the page, 13 October 2026

After that review the coordinator read Johannes Andreae's Novella on X 3.30.33-34
on page images of the Venice 1581 edition (Munich, BSB, bsb10489396, scans 292
and 293, leaves 146v and 147r). At the comments on *Per censuram
ecclesiasticam* and *De iure* it has "ergo mortale peccatum est ... [Quibus]
quod non aliis ... [De iure] dicit Inn. quod de consuetudine non dixit: quia de
praedialibus loquitur", with no comment on *Ecclesiis* and without the
sentence "hic nihil iungit ... tantum que ex lege ... sed et subaudiri" that
the fragment carries. On *Nisi cum ipsis* it reports Hostiensis by name ("novum
speciale privilegium, quod saepe impetrant, ut dicit Hostiensis") where the
fragment has "renouetur per s[peciale] / hiidem faciunt". The Novella is
therefore excluded for those lines on the page, and the report and ledger say
so. Its comment on *Componendum* was not read. No reading of the fragment
changed.
