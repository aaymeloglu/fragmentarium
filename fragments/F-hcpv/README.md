# F-hcpv — Leuven, Archief van de Abdij van Park, VIIIB20/52 (bifolium)

- Fragmentarium ID: F-hcpv — https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-hcpv
- Shelfmark: Leuven, Archief van de Abdij van Park, VIIIB20/52
- Catalogue: "Legendary. Chapter 1-5 of an unidentified legend on St Gregory the Illuminator"; probably Italy, 14th c., Southern Textualis, parchment, red initials, line fillers
- Dimensions: 320 x 234 mm (one bifolium)
- Images (IIIF manifest https://fragmentarium.ms/metadata/iiif/F-hcpv/manifest.json): ab_park_20200303c_0077 (fol. [1]r), 0079 (fol. [1]v), 0078 (fol. [2]r), 0080 (fol. [2]v, blank)
- Layout: 2 columns x 32 lines, ruled; rubrics "Caplm. <roman numeral>"; 2-line red initials with pen flourishing; red-touched capitals; a red and brown pen-work line filler closes the text on fol. 2r col. b. Text complete on every line. Later notes: fol. 1r top, 16th-c. archival title and "1562" (the bifolium wrapped a 1562 Park Abbey register); pencil "62" and "VIII B20/52"; fol. 2v pencil "Varia", "Ecriture d'Ecole Italienne", "XV. s.".
- Collation: fol. 1v and 2r are textually continuous and face each other (the conjoint leaf with the text of 0078 is visible at the right of 0079), so this is the innermost bifolium of its quire. The text of the work ends on fol. 2r col. b line 13; fol. 2v is blank (only offset from the facing page). The manifest labels are correct.

## Status

**PARTIAL.** Ending of a Latin life of Gregory the Illuminator in the Agathangelos tradition: Trdat and Gregory in Rome with Constantine and Silvester, Aristakes at Nicaea, and Gregory's death. Probably related to F-0kql; same-manuscript and quire proposals are discussed below. The saint was already recognized in the catalogue. No full comparison with BHL 3664 was possible. Its recorded desinit differs, but this alone does not establish a distinct or unedited recension; BHL 3664 remains to be compared.

## Identification notes

- The rubric on fol. 1r lines 1-3 is the end of a section title ("[Pars/Liber ... de ...] ab ipso, et de hiis que gesta sunt a beato Erostacio, et de obitu sancti Gregorii et felici statu regis, per V capitula distinctus"): the work is divided into titled sections, each subdivided into numbered chapters. The same structure appears in F-0kql (chapters 5-7 of the Rhipsime section, 3-6 of the section on Gregory's return and the baptism).
- Contents against Agathangelos (Aa = Armenian, Thomson's paragraphs; the Greek Ag follows Aa closely): ch. 1 = Aa 873-875 (Trdat hears of Constantine; retinue of Gregory, Aristakes, Albianos and the princes, with "Aspet who by custom crowns the king" = the Bagratid aspet t'agadir, and "Spascapeton" = sparapet); ch. 2 = Aa 876-879 (reception by Constantine and Silvester, Trdat tells his story, Constantine his own leprosy and baptism); ch. 3 = Aa 880-882 (treaty of friendship, gifts, return to Valarshapat); ch. 4 = Aa 884-885 (Nicaea, Aristakes brings back the canons); ch. 5 = death of Gregory. The Latin ch. 5 diverges from Aa/Ag (where Gregory retires to the mountains and his body is found by shepherds and buried at Thordan): here he dies after summoning Aristakes, Trdat, the queen and Khosrovidukht, and is buried in a city ("in civitate Valleroisia(?)", perhaps a corruption of Valarsapath) under a church where miracles occur "usque hodiernum diem"; then a closing paragraph on Aristakes and Trdat (Trdat "found in the shape of a pig ... afterwards found in the shape of an angel"). These differences warrant comparison with Latin recensions; they do not by themselves establish an independent reworking.
- Name forms Terdatius, Erostacius, Albinus, Valarsapath, Ararath, Asichina and Cusarducta (F-0kql) suggest a possible Greek intermediary, to be tested by textual comparison.
- Searches for the distinctive wording (list in F-0kql README, plus "in porci figuram mutatus", "per mare et terram venit in Italiam", "de Nicena synodo asportaverat", "ad eius tumulum accedentes" + Gregorius, "cupiens magis esse cum Christo" + Gregorius, "Erostacius", "Valarsapath") found nothing in Google Books, archive.org full text or Corpus Corporum.

## Proposed relation to F-0kql (VIIIB20/51)

Evidence for a shared manuscript: identical ruling and layout (2 columns x 32 lines), same script, same "Caplm." rubric formula placed at the end of the preceding line, same 2-line red flourished initials, near-identical leaf size (313 x 226 vs 320 x 234 mm), same text, both reused as wrappers of Park Abbey registers (1551 and 1562).

Quire hypothesis: if F-hcpv is an innermost bifolium carrying the ending, and F-0kql is an outer bifolium with both leaves earlier in the narrative, they would not belong to the same quire. This depends on confirming the conjoint leaves and textual sequence. The Armenian paragraph references help locate episodes but cannot securely determine how many leaves this Latin version has lost. The earlier estimate of one to three missing leaves is withdrawn; physical reconstruction and the Latin text's extent remain to be checked.
