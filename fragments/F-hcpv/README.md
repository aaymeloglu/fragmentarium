# F-hcpv — Leuven, Archief van de Abdij van Park, VIIIB20/52

- **Identification:** **Partial; medium confidence in a substantial source-family connection.** This is the ending of a Latin life of Gregory the Illuminator. Its distinctive princely retinue, journey to Rome, and return of Gregory's son from Nicaea closely follow the ordered narrative of the longer Agathangelos Vita printed in *Acta Sanctorum*, September VIII, chapter XV, §§163–171. The exact Latin work or recension remains unidentified: the wording is substantially different, and the manuscript's funeral and royal coda differ from the tested printed endings. Confidence applies to the diagnostic narrative connection, not an exact Latin attribution.
- **Transcription:** The [preserved image-only reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-hcpv/readings/review-2026-09-22-image-only.json) covers only seven cropped rows. The [separate source-assisted record](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-hcpv/readings/review-2026-09-22-source-assisted.json) covers that passage and supplies selected readings from the six written columns. Uncertain names, abbreviation expansions, and the burial place remain explicit. No complete transcription or accuracy score is claimed.
- **Verification:** A Codex reviewer inspected manuscript images and the actual online Latin text of the longer Vita and the anonymous *Vita altera* BHL 3664. The comparison used the site's HTML transcription of the edition, **not print facsimiles**. The retinue, council, and death passages provide separate source-assisted checks. No human palaeographic review was performed.

Catalogue details carried from [Fragmentarium](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-hcpv): “Legendary. Chapter 1–5 of an unidentified legend on St Gregory the Illuminator”; probably Italy, fourteenth century, Southern Textualis, parchment, one bifolium, 320 × 234 mm. The catalogue identifies the saint. Those dating and provenance assignments were not independently tested for these comparisons.

## Comparison: substantial connection, unresolved Latin text

| Manuscript passage | Actual comparison and implication |
|---|---|
| `h0a` Constantine encomium; `h0b:1–14` retinue and departure | Longer Vita §§163–166 supplies a distinctive ordered assemblage: the districts Norsireorum, Assyriæ, Aruastarum, Masachi; Angelitæ; crowning Aspet; Macitarum, Synistarum, Restunitarum, Sapianarum and Pascapeteorum; seventy thousand soldiers; Ararat and Valarsapat; land/sea journey to Italy and Rome. Several manuscript names are uncertain, but the detailed cluster and sequence provide a stronger connection than a shared saint or generic journey. The Latin is compressed/rephrased and not a sustained verbal match. |
| `h0b:15–32`, `h1a` Roman reception and stories | The longer Vita §§166–168 has reception, recounting Gregory's sufferings and the king's transformation, friendship, gifts, and return. Its receiving churchman is **Eusebius**, while the manuscript names **Silvester**, and its Constantine narrative includes leprosy and baptism by Silvester. The edition's note p discusses the Silvester substitution in a separate tradition; that note does not identify this manuscript's Latin recension. |
| `h1b:9–29`, `h2a:1–5` Nicaea and pastoral care | Longer Vita §169 sends **Arostaces** to Nicaea and returns him with the canons to the king and Gregory. The manuscript's **Erostacius/Erostacis** performs the same linked acts, in substantially different prose, following an expanded list of council participants. The vigilant, sleepless pastoral care after this episode corresponds to §170, but its biblical language is not diagnostic alone. |
| `h2a:6–30`, `h2b` funeral and royal coda | The manuscript describes summons to son/king/wife/sister, testament, burial in a named city, church and miracles, succession, and the king's pig/angel contrast. Longer Vita §§171–173 closes with a summary of spiritual work and an authorial statement, without this detailed funeral/coda. Its editorial death note discusses a different solitary-death tradition; this does not turn that note into the manuscript's source. |
| The same ending versus BHL 3664, *Vita altera* §§41–43 | The actually read printed-text transcription moves through withdrawal, the king's penitential visits, written sermons, and a peaceful death. It ends with a feast date and doxology. This is materially different from the manuscript's funeral and final royal passage. The comparison concerns this printed ending, not every witness associated with BHL 3664. |

The shorter Vita's generic death wording, including *senex & plenus dierum ad Dominum in pace migravit*, is not sufficient to identify this version. Nor do the longer Vita's detailed narrative parallels establish that its printed Latin translation is the manuscript text or prove direct translation from Greek. The evidence supports a substantial Agathangelos-family connection while leaving the exact Latin recension unresolved.

## Result and remaining work

The checked passages support a connection to the longer Vita but do not identify the exact Latin work. Search failures do not establish exclusions or show that this version is unedited. No author, translation date, textual stemma, or novelty claim is established.

The proposed relationship to [F-0kql](https://aaymeloglu.github.io/fragmentarium/F-0kql.html) is unverified. No side-by-side manuscript comparison supports common origin or an innermost-bifolium reconstruction here; matching saints or narrative episodes alone cannot establish them.

The next discriminating step is to locate and compare Latin witnesses containing **both** the Rome/Nicaea sequence and this detailed funeral/royal coda. The present scoped result is partial and explicit: narrative connection supported, exact Latin text not identified.
