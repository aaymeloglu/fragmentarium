# F-cf1m: Anonymous, Apocalypse commentary *Vidit Iacob* (printed as Thomas Aquinas, *Expositio I in Apocalypsim*), chapter 16

Stams, Frg. 92 is a bifolium from a manuscript of the Apocalypse commentary that opens *Vidit Iacob in somnis*, printed among the works of Thomas Aquinas as *Expositio I in Apocalypsim*. Three of its four pages carry the commentary on Apocalypse 16, where the seven phials (bowls) of the angels are read as preaching against the seven vices. One leaf has the sixth phial, on avarice (Apoc. 16:12), into Apoc. 16:13. The other leaf's recto has the evils of sloth (accidia) under the seventh phial and Apoc. 16:17. On that recto the commentary stops in the middle of the second column, and the last page, headed *De confessione*, carries a different text, not identified.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-cf1m) (Stiftsarchiv Stams, Frg. 92, online since 18 September 2021) gives the title "x", parchment, 315 x 188 mm (240/315 x 126/188 mm in the reconstruction summary by Claudia Sojer), and two images, Stams_STA_Frg_092_01r and _01v. It identifies no text and links no description. The images show one bifolium used as a limp wrapper, so the record's single fragment item fits.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence, for the commentary. The diagnostic basis is the commentator's own connected prose and its chain of citations, not the biblical quotations alone: on avarice, *talis est omnis avarus et ideo ab omnibus est maledictus ... Unde aranee comparatur avarus ... Ut autem tamquam pestis mortifera fugiatur avaritia ... ostendentes quedam mala que ipsa facit*, followed by the numbered evils (*Secundum malum est turbacio cordis et tocius domus*); on Apoc. 16:17, *a doctoribus qui ex officio sedent in throno idest cathedra magistrali ... consumata est malitia mundi ut veniat finis eius*. This runs in the same order and wording as the Parma print, pp. 467-468 and 470, including the Apocalypse lemmata themselves. Later Apocalypse commentaries reported to draw on *Vidit Iacob* (by Peter of Tarentaise and Nicholas of Gorran) were not compared, so a close reuse cannot be excluded by test; the sustained agreement across three pages, with the lemmata and the sequence of the exposition, is what places the text in this work. The manuscript differs from the print in several places: it adds a clause, *quia sicut aranea eviscerat se ut telum texat sic avarus* ("because as the spider disembowels itself to weave its web, so does the miser"); it adds *mala* in *quedam mala que ipsa facit*; it has *sectatur* for *sequitur* in Proverbs 15:27, *Saturitas autem* for *Saturitas*, *mens quiescit* for *mens eius quiescit*, and *vanitatem* for *vanitates*. The text on the last page (canvas 1, left page) is not part of this commentary as printed and is not identified.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-cf1m/readings/stams5-2026-11-01-image-only.json) was made before any source was consulted; the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-cf1m/readings/stams5-2026-11-01-source-assisted.json) gives the lines relied on. A research reader reread on the images, with contrast enhancement where the ink is faint: canvas 1, right page, column a, lines 1-12, and column b, lines 1-13; canvas 0, left page, column b, lines 1-8; canvas 0, right page, column b, lines 4-9. An independent checker read canvas 1, right page, column a, lower half (about 17 lines) before seeing the source, and rechecked two lines on the image afterwards. All other lines, and the scattered words cited from the last page, rest on readings at reading size that are fragmentary. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared four passages, on both leaves and both sides of the first leaf, with page images (not OCR) of the Parma edition, vol. 23, pp. 467, 468 and 470. An independent checker then compared a passage the research reader had not used, canvas 1, right page, column a, lower half (Isaiah 59:5-6 and 30:1, the Ephesians and Matthew citations with their glosses, to *ostendentes quedam mala que ipsa facit omnibus habentibus*), with the page image of Parma p. 467, column b, and found it in the same sequence and wording, the manuscript adding *mala*. Gaps: the faded first page of the second leaf (canvas 0, right page, column a) was read only in isolated words and not collated; the last page was searched in the edition's OCR only; one edition was used. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

The coordinator compared canvas 1, right page, col. a, lower half (native box 3980,2130,4880,2700, "[pepe]rerunt iniquitatem. Ova aspidum ruperunt et telas aranee texuerunt ... ## Leaf order, other items and limits

Canvas 1 shows the second leaf's verso on the left and the first leaf's recto on the right; canvas 0 shows the first leaf's verso on the left and the second leaf's recto on the right. Canvas 1 is therefore the outer side. Each page has two columns of about 32-33 lines. The text missing between the end of the first leaf (Parma p. 468, column b) and the start of the second (p. 470, column a) comes to roughly four manuscript pages, which fits one lost bifolium inside this one; on that estimate this was the second bifolium from the centre of its quire.

On the second leaf's recto, column b, about 10-14 lines are written; below them the ruled column shows no script on contrast-enhanced images, though the page is stained and abraded. The last page (canvas 1, left page) does not continue the commentary as printed (p. 470 goes on *Et facta sunt fulgura ... Cumanis ...*). Its readable words include *penitens*, *peccavit*, *a loco ... posuit*, *residens neque illa* and *misere(re) anime tue(?)*, with a marginal *Anselm(us)?*; above it, on the turned-in upper margin, stands *De confessione*. The commentary thus stops in mid-column and a text headed *De confessione* follows. That text is not identified. Short marginal words beside the second leaf's recto, column b, were not read.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the manuscript, the author of the commentary, the copy's relation to other manuscripts or to the printed text, and whether the manuscript held the whole commentary.
