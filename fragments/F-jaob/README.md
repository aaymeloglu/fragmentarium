- **Catalogue:** "Commentarium super IV librum sententiarum Petri Lombardi (unidentified), dd. 15-17, 19-22, 32-34, 38-40"; France, 1351-1400; Cursiva; 241 x 313 mm

- **Layout:** single column of long lines (c. 40-45 lines/page, cursive), red/blue paragraph marks numbering chapter summaries; 2- to 4-line red/blue initials at each distinction with the distinction number in the margin (16, 17, 32, 33); running head "IIII" on f. 71r. Each distinction closes with a block of mnemonic hexameters written in TWO narrow columns. Leaves are complete (no cut line ends); a strip of text is lost only to damage/staining on f. 70v.

## Status

**IDENTIFIED as to text type, both layers matched to printed parallels; author anonymous.** (1) The verse blocks that close every distinction are verbatim the mnemonic "Sententiae Sententiarum" (versus memoriales on Lombard's Sentences) printed among the pseudo-Bonaventurean opuscula (ed. Peltier, Opera omnia vol. 6, Paris 1866, lib. IV pp. 651-655; earlier in the 1504 Parvorum opusculorum, 1596 Vatican Opera, etc.). (2) The prose layer of numbered chapter epitomes ("Primum c°... 2m c°...") is the same anonymous "summa distinctionis" text that Jan Hus incorporated wholesale into his Super IV Sententiarum (Prague 1407-09; ed. Flajšhans 1906, IV d. 15 pp. 593-594 and d. 32 pp. 662-663): at d. 32 all six capitula and at d. 15 nine capitula match Hus word for word (tables below). The fragment (French, s. XIV ex.) is thus an independent, earlier witness of the chapter-summary compendium Hus used, here combined with a divisio textus, Decretum cross-references and the pseudo-Bonaventure verses (Hus prints a different verse set). It is NOT one of the quaestio commentaries on the candidate list, and NOT the Filia magistri.

The text is therefore not a "commentary" in the quaestio sense: there are no "Utrum..." question titles anywhere on the four leaves. It is an abbreviatio/epitome of the Sentences, distinction by distinction: (1) lemma of the distinction's opening words, (2) divisio textus ("In parte ista postquam magister... dividitur in duas..."), (3) chapter-by-chapter summaries numbered "Primum c(apitulu)m, 2m c°, 3m c°...", each with its source (Augustine, Decretum "33 q. 4 ...", "Magister"), (4) mnemonic verses. That the four leaves cover dd. 15-17, 19-22, 32-34, 38-40 is explained by this density (roughly two distinctions per page).

Differences: the fragment adds the divisio textus, explicit source tags (Augustinus, Decretum C. 33 q. 4 by chapter incipit, "Magister") and an extra capitulum at d. 15 (the adulterer), and its d. 16 summa is worded differently from Hus's (Hus: "perfeccio penitencie consistit in tribus ... secundum quod Deum tripliciter offendimus"; fragment: "tres sunt partes penitentie ... sicut sunt tria peccata ... Crisostomus et Augustinus"), so the fragment is a witness of the underlying compendium, not a copy of Hus. Hus's verse sets (e.g. d. 32 "Vir mulierque pares sunt iuris condicione / Infringit votum...") are a different tradition from the fragment's pseudo-Bonaventure verses.

## Prose layer: what else was established and what was ruled out

- The prose is an epitome of Lombard's own text: the d. 15 "capitula" reproduce Lombard IV d.15 cc. 1-4 (five modes of flagella; Egyptians/Sodomites; the adulterer beheaded; "cum quis flagellatur pro peccato cuius non est conscius..."; "quamdiu res per quam peccatum est non redditur... non agitur penitentia sed fingitur", Aug. ad Macedonium) and d. 32 cc. 1-4 with the Decretum parallels (C. 33 q. 4 "Si dicat", "Qui uxorem", "Quotiescumque", "Non oportet"). Each distinction opens with a divisio textus ("In parte ista postquam magister egit de penitentia in generali, hic agit de partibus penitentie in speciali...").
- **Not** Aquinas (Scriptum has no such capitula/verses; his divisio wording "Postquam determinavit Magister..." differs), **not** Bonaventure (Quaracchi vol. 4 OCR checked: his divisio at d. 16 reads "Supra egit Magister de poenitentia in generali, determinans veritatem..."; no chapter epitomes; the flagella passage in the fragment is Lombard's text, not Bonaventure's dubium), **not** Scotus/Durandus/Richard of Middleton/Peter of Tarentaise/Peter of la Palu/Astesanus (all quaestio-form commentaries; the fragment has no quaestiones at all).
- **Not the Filia magistri** (BnF Latin 3423, Gallica btv1b520004478, ff. 78r and 84r inspected): that abbreviatio is continuous prose under rubricated headings ("De unctione extrema", "De impedimento ordinis", "De impedimento voti"...), without divisio textus, numbered capitula, Decretum cross-references or verses.
- Google Books API: "de partibus poenitentiae in speciali" / "agit de partibus poenitentiae" return only Aquinas Summa/Supplement and Nikolaus von Dinkelsbühl; "postquam magister egit de poenitentia", "specialiter determinat de bono matrimonii" return nothing. archive.org FTS: verse phrases hit only the Bonaventure Opera (Peltier, Vatican 1596 reprints).
- Nothing in the four leaves contradicts the catalogue date (14th c. cursiva, Arabic distinction numbers in the margin, Decretum citations by causa/quaestio all fit s. XIV).

- Google Books (wrapper, 2026-09-20): all four d. 32 summary anchors ("licet in omnibus vir presit uxori", "non enim debet a septuagesima usque in octavam", "sed melius hoc intelligitur quando vir concedit", "quod quibusdam videtur quod mulier non discedens") and the d. 15 anchors ("primo continet quod flagella quinque modis", "sibi ipsis crudeles ... non placentes Deo") hit ONLY the Hus editions (Flajšhans 1903-06 and the 1966 reprint). No other printed witness of the summa text surfaced.
