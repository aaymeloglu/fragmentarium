# F-jaob: anonymous Sentences IV epitome with mnemonic verses

- **Identification: partial, medium confidence.** The sampled verse block corresponds to the pseudo-Bonaventure *Sententiae Sententiarum*. The prose on fol.70r reproduces Peter Lombard's own wording from *Sententiae* IV d.15 c.2-3, in his order. Lombard's text is the common base of every Sentences commentary and epitome, so neither layer identifies the compiler of this combined text or establishes the direction of borrowing for the verses.
- **Transcription: selected source-assisted readings.** A Codex reviewer inspected selected bands of fols.70r and 71r. The separate image-only reading covers five physical lines of fol.70r. A research reader (Claude) reread fol.70r lines 2-8 on the image with the Lombard edition in view. Abbreviations and uncertain wording remain unresolved.
- **Verification: manuscript images against edition images and OCR.** The fol.70r prose was compared with the Quaracchi edition of Lombard (1916) p.829 on its page image. The verse block was compared with Peltier VI p.655 in cached OCR, not printed page images. No human palaeographic review or numerical accuracy assessment is claimed.

[Catalogue](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-jaob): Paris, BnF, Latin9377, ff.70–73, catalogued as fourteenth-century French Cursiva. [Manifest](https://fragmentarium.ms/metadata/iiif/F-jaob/manifest.json); [Gallica fol.70r, image f151](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b525133302/f151.item); [Gallica fol.71r, image f153](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b525133302/f153.item). The reading bands retain cached pixel positions in their labels, but their complete original coordinate transforms were not recovered. No additional enhancement was applied.

## Prose sample: flagella

The [preserved isolated response](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-jaob/readings/review-2026-09-22-image-only.json) is an uncertain five-line reading of the fol.70r band labelled r70_1550. The lines were reread on the BnF image of fol.70r (image f151, native region x 2330-4630, y 1590-2180) with the edition in view, so the reading below is source-assisted. Abbreviations are expanded in parentheses; the expansion boundaries are not all certain:

```text
2 [C] … mo(dis) flagella (con)ting(un)t u(e)l ut iustis merita p(er) patie(n)tia(m) augeant(ur)
3 ut de Iob. u(e)l ad custodia(m) uirtutu(m) ne sup(er)bia te(m)ptet ut paulo. u(e)l ad cor-
4 rigenda pecc(at)a ut m(arie) lep(ra). u(e)l ad dei gl(or)iam ut de ceco nato. u(e)l ad initiu(m)
5 pene ut herodi quate(nus) hic uideat(ur) quid in inferno seq(ua)t(ur). Glo(sa) …
6 … [C] … q(uod) deus genus hu(m)anu(m) diluuio. sodomitas igne
7 egyptios mari. isr(ae)litas in heremo p(er)didit. scitote ideo t(em)p(or)alit(er) p(ro) peccatis punisse
8 ne in et(er)nu(m) puniret …
```

The [Quaracchi edition of Lombard, BookIV d.15 c.2-3, p.829](https://archive.org/details/libriivsententia02pete/page/829) reads: *Quinque enim modis flagella contingunt: vel ut iustis merita per patientiam augeantur, ut Iob; vel ad custodiam virtutum, ne superbia tentet, ut Paulo; vel ad corrigenda peccata, sicut Mariae lepra; vel ad gloriam Dei, ut de caeco nato; vel ad initium poenae, ut Herodi, quatenus hic videatur, quid in inferno sequatur* and, after a sentence on Nahum, the Jerome quotation of c.3, *Quod genus humanum diluvio, Sodomitas igne, Aegyptios mari, Israelitas in eremo perdidit, scitote ideo temporaliter pro peccatis punisse, ne in aeternum puniret.*

| Fragment fol.70r | Lombard, Quaracchi p.829 |
|---|---|
| ut de Iob | ut Iob |
| ut m(arie) lep(ra) | sicut Mariae lepra |
| ad dei gl(or)iam | ad gloriam Dei |
| quate(nus) hic uideat(ur) quid in inferno seq(ua)t(ur) | same |
| rest of line 5 and start of line 6, beginning *Glo(sa)*, not read | *Duplici contritione …* and the Nahum sentence |
| genus humanum diluuio … ne in eternum puniret | same, preceded by *Nam etsi super eundem locum Hieronymus dicat …* |

The fragment thus copies Lombard's sentences, with small variants, rather than summarizing them. The numeral or abbreviation after the opening initial of line 2 remains uncertain; the edition's *quinque* is comparative evidence, not a reading. The [Hus edition, BookIV d.15, p.593](https://archive.org/details/superivsententia41husj_0) gives the same five purposes in the same order; that is a reuse of Lombard's sentence and has no weight for a Hus attribution. A CoMMA search found the same sentence in Sentences manuscripts and Gospel glosses (for example BnF lat. 15323, machine-read text only).

## Additional check: verse layer

Fol.71r's band r71_2240 shows a two-column verse block. Selected assisted reading of the left block, the first seven complete verse rows below the clipped preceding text:

> Vir mulierq(ue) pares s(un)t iur(is) redditio(n)e / In cunctis aliis est vir caput ex ratione / Si contradicat in voto vir mulieri / Sive viro mulier p(ro) nullo debet haberi / Ad ius reddendum dicas utrumq(ue) teneri / Concedente viro si coniunx polliceatur / Hinc habitu(m) mutet votu(m) nunquam revocatur.

The [Peltier edition, pseudo-Bonaventure, volumeVI p.655, distinctionXXXII](https://archive.org/details/srecardinalissbo06bona) has this sequence. OCR errors such as *mulieique* and fused *jurisredditioue* are treated as OCR, not manuscript variants. The manuscript's right-hand continuation is more difficult and is not silently filled from Peltier. A separate fol.70r band r70_3450 shows the transition into distinction16 below a verse block, confirming the alternating verse/prose format locally.

## Result

The fragment is an anonymous epitome of Sentences IV that sets mnemonic verses beside prose excerpted from Lombard's text. The prose layer is Lombard's and the verse layer matches the pseudo-Bonaventure collection; neither establishes the compiler, and nothing here establishes authorship by Bonaventure or Hus. The remaining leaves, broad endpoints, and complete verse variants remain uncollated. Commentary format and unsuccessful searches do not exclude other compilations. A full printed-image comparison of the verses and study of other verse epitomes could identify the compiling work; nothing here independently establishes a recension, date or direction of dependence.
