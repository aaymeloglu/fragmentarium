# F-jaob — anonymous Sentences IV epitome with mnemonic verses

- **Identification: partial, medium confidence.** The sampled verse block corresponds to the pseudo-Bonaventure *Sententiae Sententiarum*. A prose list parallels the summaries printed in Jan Hus's *Super IV Sententiarum*. Neither connection identifies the compiler of this combined text or establishes the direction of borrowing.
- **Transcription: selected source-assisted readings.** A Codex reviewer inspected selected bands of fols.70r and 71r. The separate image-only reading covers five physical lines of fol.70r. Abbreviations and uncertain wording remain unresolved.
- **Verification: manuscript images against edition OCR.** The prose list was compared with Flajšhans p.593 and the distinct verse block with Peltier VI p.655. Those edition passages were read in cached OCR, not printed page images. No human palaeographic review or numerical accuracy assessment is claimed.

[Catalogue](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-jaob): Paris, BnF, Latin9377, ff.70–73, catalogued as fourteenth-century French Cursiva. [Manifest](https://fragmentarium.ms/metadata/iiif/F-jaob/manifest.json); [Gallica fol.71r, image f153](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b525133302/f153.item). The reading bands retain cached pixel positions in their labels, but their complete original coordinate transforms were not recovered. No additional enhancement was applied.

## Prose sample: flagella

The [preserved isolated response](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-jaob/readings/review-2026-09-22-image-only.json) is an uncertain five-line reading of the fol.70r band labelled r70_1550. The assisted comparison uses the visible sequence, not a reconstruction of every word. The following normalized text expands abbreviations without claiming that every expansion boundary has been resolved:

```text
2 … per patientiam augeantur
3 … ad custodiam virtutum ne superbia … ut Paulo … ad cor-
4 rigenda peccata … ad dei gloriam … de ceco nato … ad initium
5 pene ut Herodi …
```

These are selected portions of crop lines 2–5, with omitted text marked. The cached r70_1550 image supports that line range and the physical *cor / rigenda* division. The hyphen above is an editorial word-division marker. The linked image-only response retains such forms as *custodiā (custodiam)* and *virtutū? (virtutum?)*; the normalized quotation does not certify unresolved abbreviation details. The [Hus edition, BookIV d.15, p.593](https://archive.org/details/superivsententia41husj_0) enumerates five purposes of punishment in that order: increased merit (Job), preservation of virtue (Paul), correction of sin (Miriam's leprosy), God's glory (the man born blind), and punishment's beginning (Herod). The sustained list is a useful parallel; both texts summarize Lombard, so it does not establish a distinctive Hus attribution.

The cropped opening's numeral/abbreviation is uncertain; the edition's *quinque* is comparative evidence, not permission to write that word into the manuscript. The form of the Marian/leprosy example also needs a clearer reading before choosing between *Marie lepra* and *Marie leprose*.

## Additional check: verse layer

Fol.71r's band r71_2240 shows a two-column verse block. Selected assisted reading of the left block, the first seven complete verse rows below the clipped preceding text:

> Vir mulierq(ue) pares s(un)t iur(is) redditio(n)e / In cunctis aliis est vir caput ex ratione / Si contradicat in voto vir mulieri / Sive viro mulier p(ro) nullo debet haberi / Ad ius reddendum dicas utrumq(ue) teneri / Concedente viro si coniunx polliceatur / Hinc habitu(m) mutet votu(m) nunquam revocatur.

The [Peltier edition, pseudo-Bonaventure, volumeVI p.655, distinctionXXXII](https://archive.org/details/srecardinalissbo06bona) has this sequence. OCR errors such as *mulieique* and fused *jurisredditioue* are treated as OCR, not manuscript variants. The manuscript's right-hand continuation is more difficult and is not silently filled from Peltier. A separate fol.70r band r70_3450 shows the transition into distinction16 below a verse block, confirming the alternating verse/prose format locally.

## Result

The fragment is an anonymous epitome with two textual layers; neither parallel establishes authorship by Hus or Bonaventure. The prose list and verse sequence were checked in different passages, but the remaining leaves, broad endpoints, and complete verse variants remain uncollated. Commentary format and unsuccessful searches do not exclude other compilations. A full printed-image comparison and study of other epitomes could identify the compiling work; nothing here independently establishes a recension, date or direction of dependence.
