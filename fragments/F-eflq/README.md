# F-eflq: Johannes Versor, *Quaestiones super libros Posteriorum Aristotelis*, book II, questions 1, 2 and 8

The bifolium carries part of Johannes Versor's question-commentary on Aristotle's *Posterior Analytics*, book II: the end of question 1 (whether every question is a question about the middle term), question 2 (whether definition and demonstration are of the same thing) and question 8 (whether the "what it is" can be hunted by dividing a genus by its differences). The wording is close to the printed Versor but reworded and shortened throughout.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-eflq) describes a trimmed paper bifolium, Gent, Universiteitsbibliotheek, HS.2582/298, 214 x 172 mm, in cursive script, dated 1501-1525 and catalogued as an "Unidentified philosophical text" with no description. The subject heading fits; the record names no author, work or base text. The catalogue date has not been reassessed.

- **Identification:** identified, high. On all four pages the manuscript has the same questions, the same order of arguments and conclusions, and long runs of the same wording as Versor's questions on *Posterior Analytics* II, in three separate questions. It is not a straight copy of the printed text: it is reworded and shortened against both Cologne prints compared (Heinrich Quentell, about 1487, and Quentell, 23 July 1497, which agree with each other at these places), and some of its departures move towards the wording of Thomas Aquinas's exposition, on which Versor depends. It may be a recension, that is, a distinct version of Versor's text, or a lecturer's light reworking of it; this was not settled. Alternatives considered: Thomas Aquinas, *Expositio Posteriorum* II, is the source layer but has no questions, no "documenta" and reads "Alcibiades" where the manuscript has "hercules"; the Cologne *Copulata novae logicae* (attributed to Lambertus de Monte) was read at the same place and excluded as the containing work, because it is a running exposition without these question titles, arguments, "documenta" or conclusions. The *Copulata* also glosses Alcibiades as Hercules, so "Hercules" is a Cologne-school gloss and not peculiar to Versor. Gerardus de Harderwijck and Petrus Tartaretus were not tested.
- **Transcription:** [source-assisted selected reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-eflq/readings/batch-2026-10-12b-source-assisted.json) of seven passages. The [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-eflq/readings/batch-2026-10-12b-image-only.json) was made by an isolated reader before any source was known and covers most of both faces. A research reader reread on the image, with Versor's text at hand, the opening of question 2 (fol. 1v col. a), the "documenta" (fol. 2r col. b), the magnanimity example (fol. 2v col. a), the "supra lunam" passage (fol. 1r col. b) and fol. 2v col. b; two further passages quoted below (fol. 1r col. a and fol. 1v col. b) rest on the first reading alone. The hand is a fast, heavily abbreviated cursive and the lower inner columns are lost or skinned. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the manuscript passages with page images of the Quentell print of about 1487 (Munich copy), read question 8 again in the 1497 print, and read the same place in the *Copulata* and in Thomas's text (Corpus Thomisticum transcription). The coordinator read the magnanimity passage on the image (fol. 2v col. a, "ut si velimus habere diffinitionem magnanimitatis ... hercules achilles et aiax"). Not covered: the Cologne 1486 edition by Konrad Welker (the digitised Munich copy holds only the Old Logic part) and any non-Cologne edition; the lowest lines of every inner column; a full collation of any page.

Differences. The manuscript rewords in almost every sentence: "debemus attendere" for "oportet attendere"; "oportet ipsum tria facere" for "oportet tria considerare"; "differentie generis" for "differentie per se per quas dividitur"; "passiones generis" for "passiones consequentes illa genera"; "non potuerunt tollerare" for "non posse tolerare"; "et hoc poterit" for "quod poterit". Its second list of magnanimous men is "lisandrum et socratem", where both prints add Alexander. The 1497 print was read at question 8 and has the 1487 wording at each of these places. Two of the manuscript's departures ("debemus attendere"; Lysander and Socrates without Alexander) agree with Thomas Aquinas, *Expositio Posteriorum* II, lectio 16, and with the *Copulata*; the rest of what the manuscript shares with Versor is in neither. The print's display headings (Sciendum, Conclusio, Dubitatur) appear in the manuscript only as marginal labels such as "prima conclusio" and "tertia conclusio". The source-assisted readings were made with Versor's text at hand, and "hercules" was first read after seeing it.

## Leaf order and limits

The text gives the order fol. 1r, 1v, 2r, 2v, in agreement with the canvas labels: fol. 1 has questions 1 and 2, fol. 2 has question 8, so questions 3 to 7 stood on leaves once nested inside this bifolium, unless the copy was selective. The lower third of each page is blank apart from a three-line addition at the foot of fol. 2v, and the surface around the fold carries offset from printed black-letter type, so the sheet was once in contact with printed matter. Nothing else is shown in the images.

No claim is made about the date or origin of the bifolium, about whether its wording represents a known manuscript version of Versor or a local reworking, or about its relation to any particular printed edition.
