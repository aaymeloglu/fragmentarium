## Edition comparison and differences

The directly inspected comparison source is the NLM copy of the Articella printed at Venice by Philippus Pincius in 1491. Its [item metadata](https://archive.org/details/101321207.nlm.nih.gov) explicitly describes *De regimine* with Galen's commentary. On [image n181](https://archive.org/details/101321207.nlm.nih.gov/page/n181/mode/1up), headed *Liber re. acu.*, the *Hec autem* lemma occupies the broad right column, followed below by *Plurimum autem*. The preceding commentary on labour and digestion appears in the narrow far-right column above *Iam aggregavit*. The agreement of sustained prose in both commentary passages supports identification with the text printed there as Galen's commentary; the claim does not rest on the running title or a medical commonplace alone.

| Check | Manuscript and edition evidence |
|---|---|
| Lemma order | The manuscript places *Oportet ... ab usu sui* before *ad furie quoque ... sicut dicturus sum*. The print puts the *ad furie* clause before *Oportet*. Both blocks are present, but their order differs. |
| Lemma wording | Manuscript r.b2 has *ab usu sui*; the print has *ab usu cibi*. Calling this a scribal error would require further evidence. |
| Correction | Manuscript r.a26 adds *sit* above the line after *ut*. Its placement is retained rather than represented as an ordinary baseline word. |
| Commentary wording | Manuscript r.b10 has *de eo* where the print has *de ea*. At r.b18 the manuscript has *dispositio una*, whereas the print includes *una sua*. |
| Diagnostic sequence | *Plurimum autem sermonis mei* leads through repletion/evacuation, illnesses, sorbition, and *vacuitatem venarum* in both witnesses; the preceding bodily-labour discussion and following *Iam aggregavit* passage also correspond. |

These agreements establish the work and commentary identification despite local textual differences. They do not determine a translator, an exact recension, the source of the transposition, or a manuscript's dependence on another witness.
