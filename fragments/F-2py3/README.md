# F-2py3: Philaretus, *De pulsibus* ("Quid differt" questions, ten genera, non-naturals), and Theophilus, *De urinis* (hypostasis prologue and chapters)

These parchment pieces at Stams, taken from a grain register (Kornurbar) of 1639, come from a bifolium of a medical book with two short texts of the later *Articella* collection. One leaf carries Philaretus, *De pulsibus* (the Latin *Liber pulsuum Philareti*): the questions "Quid differt ..." comparing kinds of pulse, from "[cor]pore. Pulsus vero in operatione virtutis movetur" to the question on the caprizans and bispulsans pulse; a lower strip adds the end of the ten genera of pulse and the passage on the non-natural things (exercise, baths, food, wine). The other leaf carries Theophilus, *De urinis* (*Liber urinarum Theophili*): the end of the list of fatty urines, the prologue to the hypostases (sediments) with the invocation "invocemus Xpistum verum deum ducem fieri", and the first hypostasis chapters ("Que ergo secundum naturam ypostasis habet album colorem ..."); a strip passage on the humours belongs to the same text. A third, separate pulse text on the page labelled 1r left is not identified here.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-2py3) is Stams, Frg. 159, title "x", parchment, one fragment item, 300 x 202 mm (measured on the fragment as 291/300 x 197/202 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), no date given, online since 18 September 2021, with four images. It has no description. The images carry modern material: on canvas 0 an envelope addressed to P. Stefan Köll, novice master at Stift Stams, inscribed "Palimpsest (med.) entnommen dem Kornurbar 1639 - 1965", and a typed slip on the fragment, "Entnommen dem Kornurbar vom J. 1639 squ", with "12/1 1965"; on canvas 2 two typed transcriptions, partly in red, of the Theophilus prologue ("Cum hec convenienter composuerimus ... invocemus Xristum verum dominum ducem fieri ...") and of the Philaretus passages ("Decimum genus pulsuum ... Ego vero longitudini parcens in ... Pulsus vero in operatione virtutis movetur ..."); on canvas 3 a 1964 calendar sheet with ballpoint notes "Boethius 480-526 / Gesamtausgabe Venedig 1492, Basel 1546 / Boetius de Consolatione?". The record thus had "medical", parts transcribed and a guess at Boethius, which is wrong; nothing named Philaretus or Theophilus.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for the two texts. Philaretus, *De pulsibus*: sustained agreement in wording and sequence with the text printed in the *Articella* (Lyon, 1519) over the questions on canvas 1 right page and the ten-genera passage of the strip. Theophilus, *De urinis*: sustained agreement with the same print over the prologue on canvas 0 right page and about 18 lines of the hypostasis chapters on canvas 1 left page. The manuscript's wording is often shorter than the print's: in Philaretus it lacks the question "Pulsus spissus a veloci differt ... continuum dicimus" before "Velox vero discretum"; in Theophilus its list of fatty urines has "pinguis rubea" and "pinguis nigra", which the print lacks, and feminine forms where the print has masculine, and the strip has "sub rubea" for the print's "subrufa". The Boethius of the modern note is rejected: the content is medical. The pulse text on canvas 0 left page is not the Philaretus: see the closing section.
- **Transcription:** source-assisted, selected passages. The [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-2py3/readings/stams-2026-10-30-image-only.json) was made from canvases 0 and 1 only. Its reader saw the envelope ("Palimpsest (med.) entnommen dem Kornurbar 1639 - 1965") and the typed slip on canvas 0, and its working context said the images were from a Stams fragment project; none of these names a text, though "med." indicates a medical one. The typed transcriptions on canvas 2 and the Boethius note on canvas 3 were not among its images. A research reader (Claude) reread on the native images, with the editions at hand, the lower part of canvas 0 right page (Theophilus), the upper part of canvas 1 right page (Philaretus) and three bands of canvas 0 left page (the unidentified pulse text); that revision is the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-2py3/readings/stams-2026-10-30-source-assisted.json). An independent research reader read on the image the upper part of canvas 1 left page (Theophilus) and the right half of the canvas 1 strip. The rest of the strip, including the ten-genera and non-naturals passages, rests on the first reading. Lines under the repair tape are mostly unread, and abbreviation expansions in the faint lines are tentative. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader (Claude) collated the Theophilus prologue with the *Articella* page image (scan n33) and the Philaretus questions with the page image of scan n25, and checked the first reading of the ten-genera strip passage against scan n25. An independent research reader (Claude), reading canvas 1, left page (native boxes 480,3300,2650,3780; 480,3720,2650,4200; 480,4100,2650,4560) on the fragment image before seeing this transcription, compared about 18 lines ("... inspicere in urina hec iiii scilicet ypostasim et colorem et substantiam et tempus ... Hec priori minus digesta") with the *Articella*, fol. xv, on the archive.org page image (scan n34), and found sustained agreement in wording and sequence with a somewhat shorter text; the same reader read the right half of the canvas 1 strip (box 2650,5500,4500,5950) on the image and compared it, and the first reading of the non-naturals passage, with the archive.org OCR text of the print only, not a page image. The critical edition of Philaretus by I. Garofalo was seen only in search snippets, and no critical text of Theophilus was consulted. The faint urine text on the canvas 0 strip, left half, was not collated.

## Textual evidence

Fragment images (manifest canvas order): canvas 0, labelled Stams_STA_Frg_159_01r_Adl_01r (face labelled 1r), 4912 x 7360 pixels; canvas 1, labelled Stams_STA_Frg_159_01v_Adl_01v (face 1v), 4912 x 7360; canvas 2, Stams_STA_Frg_159_Adl_02r03r, the typed transcriptions ([image](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-2py3/Stams_STA_Frg_159_Adl_02r03r.jpg/full/1200,/0/default.jpg)); canvas 3, Stams_STA_Frg_159_Adl_02v03v, the calendar sheet, 7360 x 4912 ([image](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-2py3/Stams_STA_Frg_159_Adl_02v03v.jpg/full/1200,/0/default.jpg)). Native-pixel boxes are given as left, top, right, bottom.

There are two pieces: an upper piece across the fold of a bifolium, one column of about 14-16 surviving lines on each page, and a narrow lower strip, also across the fold, with 6-7 lines on each half. The hand is a small protogothic or early gothic book hand with red and blue initials, red rubrics and red paraphs. There are holes, repair tape and abrasion. No undertext was seen at the published resolution, and the "Palimpsest" of the envelope is unexplained.

Abbreviations are expanded in parentheses where the reading gives them, otherwise silently; square brackets mark lost or uncertain letters, "?" doubt.

### Editions and attribution

*Articella* (Lyon, 1519), archive.org [articellanuperri00gaze](https://archive.org/details/articellanuperri00gaze): Philaretus, *Liber pulsuum Philareti*, [scan n24](https://archive.org/details/articellanuperri00gaze/page/n24/mode/1up), [n25](https://archive.org/details/articellanuperri00gaze/page/n25/mode/1up), [n26](https://archive.org/details/articellanuperri00gaze/page/n26/mode/1up); Theophilus, *Liber urinarum Theophili*, [scan n33](https://archive.org/details/articellanuperri00gaze/page/n33/mode/1up), [scan n34, fol. xv](https://archive.org/details/articellanuperri00gaze/page/n34/mode/1up). The attributions are those of the *Articella* titles, the standard medieval Latin ones: Theophilus Protospatharius for the urines; "Philaretus" for the Latin pulse treatise, whose Greek counterpart I. Garofalo treats as a witness of ps.-Galen, *De pulsibus ad Antonium*. Critical text of Philaretus: I. Garofalo, "Il De pulsibus di Philaretus e il Peri sphygmon di Philaretos", in *Textos médicos grecolatinos antiguos y medievales*, edition at pp. 80-91, seen in search snippets only. The manuscript's shorter wording in both texts may reflect an older form than the 1519 print, but no claim is made about version.

## Leaf order, the third text, and what is not claimed

On the upper piece the texts fix the folding: the page labelled 1r right runs on to 1v left (Theophilus), so 1r is the inside of the sheet, and on the other leaf 1v right (the Philaretus questions) is the recto and 1r left its verso. On the strip the pulse half lies on the right on 1r and on the left on 1v, and its Philaretus text ends "longitudini parcens in-", about four printed lines before the opening of 1v right ("[cor]pore. Pulsus vero ..."). The strip is therefore either turned the opposite way to the upper piece or comes from another sheet; this was not settled. The left half of the canvas 0 strip carries a faint urine text ("ypostasi (et) a sanie (et) a sang(ui)ne?", "uesice ulceratione") that was not collated.

The page labelled 1r left (canvas 0, box 700,3250,2500,5200, [image](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-2py3/Stams_STA_Frg_159_01r_Adl_01r.jpg/700,3250,1800,1950/full/0/default.jpg)) carries a separate pulse text: the pulse by ages ("Infantium itaque ... Puerorum ... Iuvenum vero ... maioris tardior plenus equalis et ordinatus ... Predictis itaque naturalibus ad eos qui preter naturam [sunt] veniamus"), then a list of differences of pulse with Greek-derived names and definitions ("Magnus pulsus est qui cum longitudine latitudine simul et altitudine ad superficiem se porrigit. Vehemens pulsus est qui mobilitate sua cum magnitudine demonstratur. Ingens pulsus est qui ..."). Its section on ages runs close to the Latin ps.-Soranus *Peri sfigmon* ("Soranus filio carissimo salutem"), ed. V. Rose, *Anecdota Graeca et Graecolatina* II (Berlin, 1870), pp. 276-277 ([archive.org](https://archive.org/details/10994831bsb)), compared on page images; the list and definitions that follow differ from Rose's prose and resemble definitions in the "Alexander" text on fevers, pulses and urines (ed. Noßke, 1919). This is a partial parallel. The page is not identified and no identification is claimed for it.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the hand, the relation of the strip to the upper piece, the version of either text, the nature of the "Palimpsest" described on the envelope, and novelty.
