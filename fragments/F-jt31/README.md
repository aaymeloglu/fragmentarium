# F-jt31: Thomas Aquinas, *Expositio libri Posteriorum Analyticorum*, book I, lectio 8

This narrow parchment strip carries part of Thomas Aquinas's commentary on Aristotle's *Posterior Analytics*, book I, lectio 8. That lectio explains Aristotle's argument (*Posterior Analytics* I.3) that demonstration cannot be circular.
- One face has the ends of lines from nn. 1-5. It contains the worked syllogism with "risibile", "animal rationale mortale" and "homo", and the distinction between what is prior "simpliciter" and prior "quoad nos". It ends at "vel sapientibus vel pluribus".
- The other face has the beginnings of lines from nn. 8-9: the same three terms used to show that each premise can be concluded from the other, and the rules for the second and third figures.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-jt31) describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv (Stealth Published), Frg. 105: parchment, one fragment item, 110 x 57 mm (measured by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), online since 18 September 2021, with the title "x". It gives no description, date or identification. The two images, Stams_STA_Frg_105_01r and _01v, show the strip and both its faces. A modern pencil note on each face labels it "r" or "v".

- **Identification:** identified, high. Both faces agree with Thomas's text in wording and order, over about forty partial lines. The wording is Thomas's own exposition (its example syllogisms, its distinction between demonstration and inductio, its definition of "probabilia"), not the words of Aristotle being commented on. No other commentary was compared, because the agreement is with the specific wording of Thomas's exposition throughout.

  Differences from the Leonine text:
  - On the recto, line 14, the scribe wrote "sic demonstratio fieret". Small interlinear letters b and a mark a transposition, giving the edition's order "sic fieret demonstratio".
  - On the verso, lines 3 and 20, the strip reads "et ex duabus" and "et ex praemissa", where the edition prints "et duabus" and "et praemissa". The Leonine apparatus reports "et ex" in these two places from different groups of its manuscripts. This is noted as an observation only; it does not place the strip in a branch of the tradition.
  - The last line of the recto is cut through the letters. Its visible tops do not obviously fit the edition's continuation and are left unread.
- **Transcription:** [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-jt31/readings/stams6-2026-11-01-image-only.json) and [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-jt31/readings/stams6-2026-11-01-source-assisted.json).
  - Research readers reread on the image: recto lines 1-5 and 12-23, and verso lines 1-5 and 8-24.
  - Recto lines 6-11 lie under a stain and verso lines 2-7 are abraded. A few words in them were identified only with the edition in view.
  - Neither face gives a complete line. Many expansions of abbreviations are uncertain.
  - Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the recto with page images of the Leonine edition, pp. 166-168. A second research reader read recto lines 6-11 and verso lines 1-7 before opening the source, then compared both faces with the page images of pp. 166-170, including the apparatus where it bore on those lines. A third reading of the verso, made before the source was reopened, agreed in sequence with n. 8.
  The coordinator read the verso, canvas 1 (Stams_STA_Frg_105_01v), native box 3370,2030-3830,2460 ("[osten]sum est in his que / sillo(gismo) simpl(icite)r q(uo)d sic p(atet) ... ergo omnis homo est risibilis ... Maior sic") on the image and found it in sequence with Leonine I, lect. 8 n. 8.
  Not checked: the illegible parts of the stained and abraded lines.

Apart from the points noted under Identification, the readings agree with the edition as far as they are legible. Verso line 21 may set "probetur" before "propositiones", against the edition's order, but the reading is uncertain. The layout is consistent with one wide column running from recto to verso. Recto lines 1-23 hold about 105 characters of edition text per line. The text missing between the foot of the recto and the head of the verso amounts to about 34 such lines, which suggests a written column of roughly 58 lines. This is an estimate from the edition's text, not a measurement of the leaf.

## The strip and what is not claimed

The strip is cut from a single column, with ruling across the full width and a blank margin on one side. On the recto the line-ends survive and on the verso the line-beginnings, so both faces show the same outer edge of the leaf. Both faces show a top margin. The hand is a small, heavily abbreviated Gothic book hand. A single word stands in the outer margin of the recto beside line 18; it may be a correction ("Sim(pliciter)?") and is not read with certainty.

No date or origin is claimed for the fragment. Nothing is claimed about its place among the manuscripts of Thomas's commentary: the two "et ex" readings are shared with different groups of the edition's witnesses and decide nothing.
