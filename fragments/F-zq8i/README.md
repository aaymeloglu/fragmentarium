# F-zq8i: Zacharias Chrysopolitanus, *In unum ex quatuor*, III.104 (John 7:3-14 and 7:16-24)

This parchment leaf at Stams carries part of the gospel-harmony commentary *In unum ex quatuor* of Zacharias Chrysopolitanus (Zachary of Besançon, died about 1155), book III, chapter 104, on John 7. The face labelled 74v has the comment on John 7:3-14 (the brothers urging Jesus to show himself in Judaea, "my time is not yet come", the feast of Tabernacles, the murmuring of the crowd); the face labelled 74r has the comment on John 7:16-24 (the doctrine of the Father, circumcision on the sabbath, "judge not according to the appearance"), followed by the chain of excerpts on circumcision that Zachary marks as Ambrose, Jerome and Augustine. The text agrees in wording and sequence with the printed edition, Patrologia Latina 186, cols. 327-329.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-zq8i) is Stams, Frg. 74, title "x", parchment, one fragment item, 262 x 186 mm (measured on the fragment as 219/262 x 133/186 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), no date given, online since 18 September 2021, with two images. It has no description and nothing in it names the text. The only modern additions on the images are pencil labels ("Stams, STA, Frg. 74r", "74v", "VI d/L, 19" and a faint "c xiiii"?).

- **Identification:** identified, high. Both faces, all four column fragments, agree in wording and order with Zachary's text in PL 186, cols. 327-329: the gospel lemma written into the running text and followed by its gloss, the "Sensus est" glosses, and the excerpt chain on circumcision, for which the manuscript gives source names in its margin ("Ambr[os.] / ad rom[anos]", "I[eron.]"?) at the points where PL prints [Ambros.] and [Hieron.]. The Glossa ordinaria on John (PL 114) shares the sentence "Non est iudicandum secundum faciem ... nec Moysen dampnabitis" but differs where the fragment agrees with Zachary ("cognoscet de doctrina" against the fragment's and Zachary's "intelleget quod doctrina mea est eius"). Bede, Alcuin and Haymo on John 7 paraphrase the brothers' advice differently ("Facis mirabilia, sed abscondite"), while the fragment has Zachary's "Mira facis sed in abscondito". These are sources of Zachary, not the containing work. The fragment differs from PL in small points of word order and wording, listed below; it writes the full gospel text into the commentary where PL sets it apart.
- **Transcription:** source-assisted, selected passages. The [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-zq8i/readings/stams-2026-10-30-image-only.json) was made from the two images alone; its reader states that nothing in view named the text, and the images show only the modern pencil labels listed above. A research reader (Claude) reread on the native images, with PL 186 at hand, all lines of both columns on the face labelled 74r, all lines of column a on the face labelled 74v, and the upper band and most of the lower band of its column b; that revision is the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-zq8i/readings/stams-2026-10-30-source-assisted.json). Text under the two patches in 74r column a and the faded right halves of that column are only partly read; the marginal note "S cp v"? beside "Nolite iudicare" is unresolved. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader (Claude) collated the reread lines of all four column fragments with PL 186, cols. 327-329, on the archive.org page images. An independent research reader (Claude), reading the lower band of 74v column a (canvas 1, native box 2400,2830,3700,3930) on the fragment image before seeing this transcription, compared its twelve lines ("[iudi]candi sed iniquos tolerandi ... ubi humanam gloriam q[ueritis]") with PL 186, col. 327, on the archive.org page image (scan n167), found verbatim agreement apart from the fragment's "quia ego testimonium", checked the chapter heading "Caput CIV" on col. 326 (scan n166) and the heading of chapter CV on col. 330, and established that the text ends in col. 329. No other manuscript of the work was compared, and the critical tradition of Zachary's text was not consulted. No human review.

## Textual evidence

Fragment images (manifest canvas order): canvas 0, labelled Stams_STA_Frg_074_01r (face 74r), 7360 x 4912 pixels; canvas 1, labelled Stams_STA_Frg_074_01v (face 74v), 7360 x 4912 pixels. Each face shows parts of two columns, about 15-16 lines each, in a protogothic or early gothic book hand without colour; the upper and lower edges are folded over as binding turn-ins.

Abbreviations are expanded silently in the running text below; square brackets mark lost or uncertain letters, "?" doubt.

### Edition and attribution

Zacharias Chrysopolitanus, *In unum ex quatuor*, in J.-P. Migne (ed.), *Patrologia Latina* 186, lib. III, cap. CIV, cols. 326-330, reprinting the Cologne edition of 1535: [col. 326, chapter heading (scan n166)](https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecursu186mign/page/n166/mode/1up), [cols. 327-328 (scan n167)](https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecursu186mign/page/n167/mode/1up), [cols. 329-330 (scan n168)](https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecursu186mign/page/n168/mode/1up). The running head of these pages reads "Zachariæ Chrysopolitani episcopi / In unum ex quatuor liber tertius". The attribution to Zachary, scholasticus of Besançon and bishop of Chrysopolis, is the standard one and was not checked beyond the edition.

## Leaf order and what is not claimed

In text order the face labelled 74v comes before the face labelled 74r: either the labels are reversed or the leaf was photographed from the other side. About one column of text is lost between each surviving block. The text under the patches on 74r and the marginal "S cp v"? are unread. The date of the hand (protogothic, perhaps later twelfth or early thirteenth century) has not been assessed, the fragment's place in Zachary's manuscript tradition has not been examined, and no other Stams fragment has been checked as a sister leaf. No claim is made about date, origin or novelty.
