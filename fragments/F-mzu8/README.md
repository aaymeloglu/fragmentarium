# F-mzu8: Matthias Burgklehner, *Die Fürstliche Grafschafft Tirol*, copperplate wall map, east-central sheet

The three paper pieces of this record are not a manuscript. They are cut from impressions of one printed map sheet, engraved in copper: the sheet with Lienz, the Isel valley, the eastern Puster valley (Toblach, Innichen, Sillian, Welsberg), Cadore and the Ampezzo border, and the table *Inhalt der Tirolischen Landtafel*, from Matthias Burgklehner's wall map of Tyrol in twelve sheets, *Die Fürstliche Grafschafft Tirol*, in the copperplate version engraved by Andreas Spängler. The pieces are from the plate of 1629; the date of these impressions is unknown. An impression is one pull of paper from the inked plate; pieces 2 and 3 repeat areas of piece 1, so at least two impressions of this sheet are present. On the backs is a mirror-image offset (ink transferred from a facing page) of a two-column Latin text in gothic type, which is not identified.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-mzu8) describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 236: paper, three items, 489 x 344 mm (item 236_1, 434/489 x 304/344 mm), with a reconstruction summary by Claudia Sojer, no catalogue date, title "x" and no linked description. Its description fits the images: three paper pieces, printed on one side. The record does not say that the pieces are printed or name the map. The backs carry later notes: "aus Hs 287" in blue ink on 1v, "Hs 308" in pencil on 3v, and pencil archival notes with dates on 2v and 3v.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for the map on the fronts of all three pieces.
  - Basis: the engraved lettering and design agree with the same sheet of the copy in Munich, BSB, Mapp. IX,205 e (image 22) in ten areas: the table, the Lienz area, the Rosenberger and Virgen area, the lion of St Mark with the cartouche *Herrsch: Venedig wegen Ladober*, the *Ritterpurg inn Kärndten* cartouche, the western and eastern Puster valley, Ampezzo and Cadore, the graduated right border and the Drau and Gail edge. Wording, line breaks, symbols and hatching are the same in every compared area, including the plate's own spelling of the degree figure as "XXXXVI". That is the same plate.
  - Version: the pieces are intaglio (printed from incised lines), with fine swelling burin strokes in the cloud hatching, and they match the BSB copperplate. The earlier woodcut version of the map by Hans Rogel (1611) was, according to Brunner, not printed until the 19th century and differs in its cartouches; it was not compared directly. The small Tyrol map of 1608 and the *Aquila Tirolensis* of 1620 are of a different design and do not carry this table or this sheet's layout.
  - Date: 1629 is the date of the copperplate edition given by the BSB catalogue and by Brunner. No date or imprint was seen on the Stams pieces or on the BSB sheets inspected. The plates survived, and only one other exemplar was compared, so the match shows the same plate but cannot tell an impression of 1629 from a later pull. Hence "from the plate of 1629, impression date unknown".
  - The village count in the table is 894 ("894 Dörffer"), not 394 as in the first reading.
  - Scale: Brunner's analysis gives about 1:157,000; the BSB catalogue gives about 1:135,000.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-mzu8/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-image-only.json) covers 1r, 1v and 2r; it recognised an engraved map with the table and many place names, without naming the map. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-mzu8/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json) holds the lines relied on here. A research reader reread on the images the table (1r and 2r), the Lienz area (1r), the Rosenberger area (1r and 3r), the lion and Venedig cartouche (2r), the pencil notes on 2v and 3v, and, mirrored and contrast-stretched, bands of the offset on 1v, 2v and 3v. An independent checker read five further areas of 1r and the Puster valley block of 3r (see Verification). All other place names rest on the first reading or were not read. The offset text was read only in scattered words. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the table, the Lienz area, the Rosenberger area, the lion and Venedig cartouche and the Ritterpurg cartouche side by side with page images of BSB Mapp. IX,205 e, image 22, and found no difference in engraved content. An independent checker, choosing areas the research reader had not compared, read the western Puster valley on 1r (canvas 0, native box 180,2500,1270,1600: "S. Georgen, Bucholtz, S. Martin, S. Niclaß, Im Bseiß, S. Maria Magtalena, S. Peter In Kofl, Toblach, Auf Kirchen, Maria Madelena im Moß, ... welsperg, Nider dorf, Ursprung der Rienz, Herschaft welsperg In prax") and four other areas of 1r on the image before opening the copy, then compared them with the [page image of BSB image 22, region 223,1741,1430,1473](https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/image/v2/bsb00046974_00022/223,1741,1430,1473/full/0/default.jpg) and the corresponding regions. Wording, line breaks, church vignettes and hatching agree in all five areas; its one discrepancy, "Leinberg", was its own misreading of "Lemberg". It also found the same Puster valley block on 3r, overlapping 1r exactly, which shows two impressions. Gaps: not every place name was compared; only one other exemplar was compared, so no state (a stage of alteration of the plate) was tested; paper, watermark and platemark were not examined; the woodcut was not compared directly; the host volumes Hs 287 and Hs 308 were not seen. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

The attribution to Burgklehner and the engraver rests on that catalogue record and on K. Brunner, "Regionalkarten von Tirol des Matthias Burgklechner und ihre Vorläufer", *Mitteilungen der Österreichischen Geographischen Gesellschaft* 144 (2002), pp. 237-256, §§6-7 and 10 ([PDF](https://www.literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Regionalkarten%20von%20Tirol%20des%20Matthias%20Burgklechner%20und%20ihre%20Vorl%C3%A4ufer.pdf)), which describes the large map, the 1611 woodcut, the copperplate of 1629 in twelve plates by Andreas Spängler with copies in Vienna (ÖNB), Innsbruck (TLMF) and Karlsruhe (BLB), the cloud banks bounding the territory and the graduated border, and records that the plates survive at Ambras. Brunner mentions no second state or later restrike. The figures of the table are also quoted in de Luca, *Journal der Literatur und Statistik* (1782), seen only as a Google Books snippet.

## Pieces, other items, and limits

Piece 1 (1r) holds most of the sheet's map field, cut along the graduated border at the right and below the "48 Clöster" line at the foot, with a horizontal fold. Piece 2 (2r) is the lower right part of the sheet from another impression: the Ritterpurg cartouche, the table down to "15. Wasserflüss.", the St Mark lion and the Venedig cartouche. Piece 3 (3r) is the upper half of the sheet down to the Drau valley at Lienz and Nußdorf. Pieces 2 and 3 may be one impression torn apart: same paper tone, the same hand in the pencil notes, and complementary areas with a lost strip between (3r breaks off at S. Veidt, 2r begins at the clouds near Peitlstain). That has not been tested by joining them.

The backs (canvases 1, 3 and 5) carry a brown and black mirror offset of a two-column Latin text in gothic type, probably printed. Scattered words were read mirrored: "Et adhuc dic[o]", "In hoc negoti[o?]", "Dic quo[d]", "Quare" on 2v (canvas 3, 2250,1360,1150,1000) and "Verum autem", "Sed" on 1v (canvas 1, 2080,3200,1170,650). No run of words long enough to search was recovered, and no search was made.

The pencil notes on 2v (canvas 3, 1050,4750,1100,800) and 3v (canvas 5, 2150,1350,900,1350) read, uncertainly, "bis 1565 / [Urbar?] des R[auchen]b[ü]ch[l?] / 1448(?)-1633 (1744?)" and "bis 1565 / ... / 1408(?)-1744(?)-1633 / Hs 308". With "aus Hs 287" on 1v, they suggest that the sheets were taken from the bindings of the archival volumes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Hs 287 and Hs 308, which were not seen. If the reading 1744 is right, those volumes run into the 18th century, so the date at which the map was reused as binding waste is open.

Not claimed: the date of these impressions, the state of the plate, that pieces 2 and 3 are one sheet, the identity or date of the offset text, or the date of reuse.
