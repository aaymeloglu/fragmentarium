# F-ibi1: Ps.-Siricius, letter to Theodorus of Córdoba, "Litteris a tua fraternitate directis" (long form), after the end of an expanded Ps.-Gregory "Statuimus secundum priorem diffinitionem"

Stams, Frg. 147 is a piece of a leaf from a collection of canon-law texts on monks' right to tithes and offerings. It carries most of a forged papal letter in the name of Pope Siricius to Theodorus, bishop of Córdoba. The letter allows a bishop to hand over altar offerings and tithes to the use and ownership of monks. It opens with an inscription (the heading naming sender and addressee), "...fr(atr)i theodoro cordubensi ep(iscop)o", and a red initial: "[L]itteris a tua fr(ater)nitate directis. Sedem ap(osto)lica(m) ... postulasti si liceat". The text runs from canvas 0 onto canvas 1, into the letter's long ending ("...decernim(us) inconuulsa. (et) si q(ui)d eis p(ro)fuerit oblatu(m). n(on) estimet [re]ligio tua da(m)pnosu(m). ubi ni[chil] ..."). Above the inscription are the last six lines of another forged text on monks' tithes. That text is the expanded form of a Ps.-Gregory decree, "Statuimus secundum priorem diffinitionem", whose added tail ends "...modernis nescio quibus ... insufflantibus et invidentibus christiane religioni omnino more displicet". The collection that held these two texts is not identified.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-ibi1) describes Stams, Frg. 147 as one parchment fragment, 136 x 95 mm (measured 30/136 x 65/95 mm), with two images labelled 01r and 01v. It gives the title "x", no date and no description. So it says nothing about the content, and there is nothing in it to correct.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence.
  - **The letter.** It is the Ps.-Siricius letter "Litteris a tua fraternitate directis" in its long form, ending "sed sola fidei caritas". Collation was against the copy in Vatican City, Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana, Vat. lat. 1361, the Collection in 13 Books, 13.42, fol. 245va-b. The agreement covers about twenty lines on the two faces. It includes the inscription to Theodorus of Córdoba and the long ending.
  - **Difference from Paxton's edition.** The short form, printed by F. S. Paxton from Leipzig, Universitätsbibliothek, MS 276, ends "...mercedem apud iustum iudicem obtinebis" and lacks that ending.
  - **Differences from Vat. lat. 1361.**
    - The fragment reads "decime (con)ueniunt" where Vat. lat. 1361 has "dari decimas conuenit".
    - It reads "fuerunt" where Vat. lat. 1361 has "fuerint".
    - It probably lacks the passage "Horum sustentationem ... mercedem apud iustum iudicem obtinebis", about 230 characters. That passage stands in both Vat. lat. 1361 and Leipzig 276. The omission rests on the space between the surviving lines and a probable reading "Ill[ud]" at canvas 1 line 11, not on a clear reading.
  - **The six preceding lines.** They match the end of the expanded "Statuimus" text that F. Pfurtscheller printed from a flyleaf of another Stams book, Stams, Cod. 17, fol. I'r. That edition was seen in Google Books snippets only.
  - **The containing collection.** In the other copies of the letter that were checked, it is placed differently: after Ps.-Jerome to Pope Damasus, or followed by the short "Statuimus". This holds for Escorial a.II.10, the Vatican manuscript described by Kuttner, Vat. lat. 1361 and Leipzig 276 (some from catalogues and editions only). So the leaf does not follow the order of any of those copies. It is not shown to be a copy of any of them, and the collection it came from is not named.
- **Transcription:** an [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-ibi1/readings/stams8-2026-11-01-image-only.json) and a [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-ibi1/readings/stams8-2026-11-01-source-assisted.json).
  - A research reader reread every line of both faces on the image.
  - An independent reader reread canvas 1 lines 9-18, first before seeing Vat. lat. 1361 and then with it.
  - The same independent reader reread canvas 0 lines 1-7 after seeing the Pfurtscheller snippets. That reading corrected "copiam" to "co(m)perimus", "...rius s(an)c(t)i" to "[ser]uis d(e)i", "iuuentibus" to "inuidentib(us)" and "si placet" to "[mo]re displicet".
  - The coordinator read canvas 0 lines 5-7 on the image.
  - Canvas 1 lines 11-15 were identified only with the source in hand. Canvas 0 line 6 ("...hec credi[t]e") is unresolved.
  - Accuracy has not been measured.

**Differences.**
- **Preceding text.** Against Pfurtscheller's text, the fragment has no "peregrinis" after "nisi" and no "videlicet". It confirms "quibus" where the edition prints "quibi". Line 6, "...hec credite", has no counterpart in the snippets seen. It may be a closing phrase or part of a heading that the Cod. 17 text lacks.
- **Inscription.** It is in the dative, "fr(atr)i theodoro cordubensi ep(iscop)o". Vat. lat. 1361 has "ad theodorum cordubensem epm."
- **Upper part of the letter, against Paxton's text.** The fragment reads "sollicita[re](?)" for "sollicite" (uncertain), and "oblationem" for "oblationes".
- **Against both Paxton and Vat. lat. 1361.** The fragment reads "decime conueniunt" without "dari", against "dari decimas conuenit". It probably lacks "Horum ... obtinebis". It reads "fuerunt" for "fuerint".
- These show that the copies differ. They do not show how the copies are related.

## Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

- **Leaf order.** Canvas 0 (01r) precedes canvas 1 (01v); the text runs on from one to the other.
- **Other items.** The modern pencil shelfmark in the lower margin of each face was not used.
- **Relation to Stams Cod. 17.** Pfurtscheller's line divisions for Stams Cod. 17 give lines of about 75 characters, against about 38 here. Unless his divisions are editorial, the Cod. 17 flyleaf is a separate copy, not part of this leaf. Whether the two come from one exemplar or collection is not claimed.
- **Not claimed:**
  - The name of the collection or dossier the leaf came from.
  - A date or origin for the copy. The script was not dated in this research, and the dates of the editions and other manuscripts do not date it.
  - Any dependence between this copy and Vat. lat. 1361, Leipzig 276, Stams Cod. 17 or Escorial a.II.10.
  - The reading of canvas 0 line 6.
