# F-ypxa: Hostiensis (Henricus de Segusio), *Lectura in Decretales Gregorii IX*, on X 3.30.33-34 (De decimis)

This strip carries part of the commentary of Hostiensis (Henricus de Segusio, cardinal
bishop of Ostia, died 1271) on the Decretals of Gregory IX, known as his Lectura or
Apparatus. The two faces hold the end of his comments on chapter 33 of the title on tithes
(X 3.30.33, *Cum non sit in homine*) and part of those on chapter 34 (*Nuper abbates*).

The catalogue record, [F-ypxa](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-ypxa), describes
Antwerpen, Erfgoedbibliotheek Hendrik Conscience, Fragm. 315: a parchment strip of
45 x 80 mm, dated to the 14th century, titled "Unidentified canon law text", used as a
quire guard in a Geneva print of 1577 (EHC C 1049). "Canon law text" fits. The record names
no author, work or passage.

- **Identification:** identified, confidence high. Twenty of the 21 surviving line ends and
  line beginnings, on both faces, agree in wording and order with the printed Lectura,
  through nine successive lemmata (the words of the decretal that each comment starts
  from). Two alternatives were considered. The ordinary gloss on the Decretals has the same
  cross-reference to the chapter *Pastoralis* but none of the surrounding wording.
  Johannes Andreae's Novella, which reuses Hostiensis, was read on the page image of the
  Venice 1581 edition (vol. 3, leaves 146v-147r) and is not the text here. Where the
  fragment has "[eccle]siasticam. ergo morta[le] / [ec]clesiis subaudi. et / hic nichil iu[ngit] /
  ...tantum que ex lege / in fi. sed et subau[diri]", the Novella has "[Per cen. ec.] ergo
  mortale peccatum est ... [Quibus] quod non aliis ... [De iure] dicit Inn. quod de consuetudine
  non dixit: quia de praedialibus loquitur", with no comment on *Ecclesiis* and none of the
  sentence "hic nihil iungit". On *Nisi cum ipsis* it has "vel nisi super his noviter acquisitis
  habeant novum speciale privilegium, quod saepe impetrant, ut dicit Hostiensis", where the
  fragment has Hostiensis's own "renouetur per s[peciale] / hiidem faciunt". The Novella's
  comment on *Componendum* was not read. One difference is
  probable: between lines 6 and 7 of the first face the print has about twice as much text
  as between other lines, so the manuscript may have lacked about one line's worth there.
- **Transcription:** the

  Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared both faces of the fragment, on the published
  images, with the page image of the Strasbourg 1512 edition, and with text-recognition
  snippets of the Venice 1581 edition. The Novella was compared in text-recognition

  1581 edition at the two places described above. No manuscript of the Lectura
  was compared. The first line of the second face is cut through and is not placed.

No difference of wording was found in the words that survive; the manuscript spells
"nichil". Between one manuscript line and the next the print has about 45 to 55 letters,
except between lines 6 and 7 of passage A, where it has about twice that (from "et
clericis" to the lemma "De iure"). The manuscript therefore probably lacked about a line's
worth there, perhaps by a skip from one "tua nobis" to the next; the lost halves of the
lines cannot confirm this. Line 1 of canvas "fol. [1]r" is cut through and is not placed.

Edition compared on the page: *Lectura siue Apparatus domini Hostiensis super quinque
libris Decretalium*, vol. 2, Strasbourg: Schott, 1512 (VD16 H 2164), Munich, Bayerische
Staatsbibliothek, 2 J.can.u. 89-2, fol. 114r:
[viewer, scan 229](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00018041?page=229). The
attribution rests on the title of this edition and of the Venice 1581 edition (*Henrici de
Segusio cardinalis Hostiensis In tertium Decretalium librum commentaria*), whose text
agrees for both passages in text-recognition snippets. The canonist Prospero Fagnani, on
the same chapter, quotes the sentence "nisi antiquum renouetur per speciale privilegium de
novo impetratum, sicut iidem faciunt tota die" as Hostiensis's and says that Johannes
Andreae follows him. In the Novella of Johannes Andreae on these chapters (Venice 1581,
vol. 3, leaves 146v-147r, [page image](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb10489396?page=292);
also text-recognition snippets of two editions) the comment on "De iure" reads "dicit Inno. de
consuetudine non dixit: quia de praedialibus loquitur", and "Quibus" follows directly on
"mortale peccatum est, ut not. supra cap. proxi."; the fragment has Hostiensis's wording
at both places.

## Leaf order and limits

The text runs from the face labelled "fol. [1]v" to the face labelled "fol. [1]r", so the
labels are the reverse of the order of the text. About 68 lines of the 1512 print lie
between the last line of the first face and the first line of the second. Since the blank
margin is on the right of the first face and on the left of the second, the strip probably
comes from the outer column of a two-column leaf with roughly 75 lines to the column; this
is an inference from the amount of text, not something seen. Nothing else is in the images.
Nothing is claimed here about the date or origin of the fragment, about its relation to
other manuscripts of the Lectura, or about whether it is a new witness.
