# F-da74: *Sermones Amici*, distinctions Amicus II, Ascendere I and Converti

This strip from the head of a bifolium (a sheet folded to make two leaves) carries the first four lines of eight columns of the *Sermones Amici*, an anonymous Latin collection of preaching "distinctions" (short schemes that divide a key word into numbered points, each supported by quotations) arranged alphabetically. One leaf has the end of the second distinction on *Amicus* and the beginning of *Ascendere*; the other has part of *Converti*.

The [catalogue record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-da74) (Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 45; paper; 330 x 59 mm; no date given; title "x") describes the object correctly as one fragment with two images and gives no text, author or description.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence. All eight column tops agree in wording and in order with the 1501 Basel print *Sermones Amici ex corrupto reintegrati*: three passages of *Amicus* II and one of *Ascendere* I on one leaf, four passages of *Converti* on the other, each the expected distance after the one before. The passages are chains of quotations (Bernard, Cicero, the Psalms, Luke) that occur singly elsewhere; it is their sequence that fixes the work. The Ash Wednesday sermon *Convertimini* of Conrad of Saxony (printed among the works of Bonaventure, ed. Peltier, vol. 13, pp. 142-143) has the same plan as the *Converti* distinction and shares three of its four passages, but it is not the text copied here: where the two differ (*austro calido*, *solvitur*, *Ecce David dei potentiam invocat ut nos salvos faciat et convertat. Infinitam enim*, *exacerbabitur*, and the whole Bernard passage *nos insequeris*) the fragment has the wording of the *Sermones Amici*, except for one word (*respiro*, print *spero*). The fragment differs from the 1501 print in a few words and in some word order, listed below. The collection is anonymous: the Munich catalogue of the 1495 Basel edition (BSB, 4 Inc.c.a. 1243) rejects the attribution to Erhard Prunner, citing H. Knaus (1967). Authorship was not investigated further.

Differences from the print: *divine voluntati* for *voluntati divine*; perhaps *sanguinem* for *sanguine*; *meam*(?) for *nostram*, twice (45v col. 3 and 45r col. 4: the same abbreviated word in both places, read alike; the psalm and the print have *captivitatem nostram*); *coronet* for *coronaret*; *ut tu salveris?* for *ut sanareris*; *asina* for *asino*; *avertimus* for *advertimus*; *et supplicas* for *supplicas*; the psalm verse written out where the print abbreviates it; *respiro* for *spero*; no *si* seen before *exacerbabitur*. From one column top to the next the print has about 31, 31 and 36 lines on the first leaf and about 29, 23 and 24 on the second, so the second leaf's columns correspond to somewhat less printed text; with four lines surviving per column the reason cannot be told.

In 45r col. 4 the manuscript passes from the first *in aus[tro]* of the psalm lemma to *[flante] enim austro calido*, without the clause the print has between them (*per culpam enim homo est captivus diaboli: bene ergo dicit sicut torrens in austro*), whether by eye-skip between the two *austro* or because the clause was not in its exemplar.

## Leaf order and limits

The leaf with *Amicus* and *Ascendere* comes first: its recto is the right half of the face labelled 45v and its verso the left half of 45r. The leaf with *Converti* is second: recto on the right half of 45r, verso on the left half of 45v. The face labelled 45v is thus the outer face of the bifolium. In the print about five pages lie between the last passage of the first leaf and the first passage of the second, which would be about four leaves of this manuscript if it had the distinctions in the print's order; that order is not known for the manuscript.

Not claimed: a date or place of writing for the fragment; the author or date of the collection; whether the manuscript contained the whole collection or its sermons; any relation between this copy and the printed text beyond agreement of wording; whether the *Sermones Amici* draw on Conrad of Saxony, or both on something else. The few surviving lines per column cannot show whether the manuscript's text was as long as the print's throughout.
