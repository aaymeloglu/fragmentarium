# F-1ax4: Brescia, governor's court under Luis d'Icart, appeal of Antonia de Casariis of Martinengo against the Barberiis brothers over a will, three acts of 4, 13 and 23 February 1514

This parchment sheet in the Stiftsarchiv of the Cistercian abbey of Stams carries three acts of one appeal, written one after another. The court is that of the governor of Brescia in February 1514, when the city was held for the Emperor and the Holy League by a Spanish garrison. Two people inherited claims under the will of the late Antonio de Casariis of Martinengo: his widow Antonia, together with Bartolomeo de Casariis, and Giovannino de Barberiis, with his sons Filippo and Giovanni Maria after his death. The will is said to be defective because its witnesses were not formally summoned ("rogati").

- **4 February 1514.** Hieronymus de Piscatriis (?), doctor of both laws, the governor's vicar and auditor and his delegate in this case, gives a definitive sentence on Antonia's appeal. He confirms the sentence of the former podestà and provveditore of Martinengo for Giovannino de Barberiis, "quia non est probatum legitime quod testes fuerint rogati tempore confectionis dicti testamenti". Each party bears its own costs. The sentence was published in the palazzo nuovo of the commune of Brescia in the presence of Filippo and Giovanni Maria de Barberiis.
- **13 February 1514.** The governor of Brescia and Bergamo hears both sides in the audience hall of the palazzo del Broletto. He commits one question to his judge of malefices (criminal judge), Pino de Numais, knight and doctor of both laws: whether the auditor's sentence agrees with the podestà's, and so whether it can be appealed.
- **23 February 1514.** "Nos Ludovicus Ycart", castellan, lieutenant and governor general of Brescia and Bergamo and their districts, acts on the advice of Pino Numai. He declares that Bartolomeo's further appeal is not to be admitted and the case cannot be appealed, since "a duabus sententiis conformibus non appellari" (no appeal lies from two agreeing sentences, under the statute). He condemns the losing party to the costs.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-1ax4) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 240, one parchment item, 382 x 283 mm (measured 380/382 x 273/283 mm), online since 18 September 2021. Its title is "x" and its document type "Fragment", with no date or description. It does not say that the sheet is an archival document. The image labelled 01v is the text face and 01r the back.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for all three acts. The regests are read on the image: the court and its officers, the parties, the place of Martinengo, the will and the ground of decision, and three explicit dating clauses. Act 1 has the year in words with indiction 2, the day and the hour. Act 2 has "Die xiij februarij 1514". Act 3 has the year in words, indiction 2 and "die vigesimo tertio mensis fe[...]", with the month torn after "fe-" and completed as February from the sequence of the acts. Indiction 2 is correct for 1514. The days convert (Julian calendar) to Saturday 4, Monday 13 and Thursday 23 February 1514. The court was not French: Brescia passed from French to Spanish-imperial hands in 1512 and was recovered by Venice only in 1516. The governor named in Act 3 is the Catalan captain Luis (Luigi, Aloisio) d'Icart, whom Brescian and Bergamasque histories call governor of Brescia in these years. Uncertain or lost: the vicar's surname (Piscatriis?), the abbreviated title after Icart's name, the surnames of Antonia's proctor (de Brianis?) and of a witness (de Coffortis?), the opening of Act 1 above the torn top edge, and a last line below the tear. The text names Icart governor of both Brescia and Bergamo. That is what the document says, though one Bergamo chronicle names a separate Spanish governor of Bergamo in these years.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-1ax4/readings/stams-2026-10-31-image-only.json) covers the whole text face and the back. Its reader had no proposed identification in context. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-1ax4/readings/stams-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json) is a research reader's (Claude) reread at native resolution in bands of about ten lines, half the width. It added detail crops of the dating clauses, the parties' names, the judges' names, the governor's protocol and the endorsement on the back. It agrees with the first reading in substance. It resolves that reading's unexpanded "ſſri, ſſram, ſſta" as the notarial abbreviation for suprascriptus ("above-named"), and it reads the governor's protocol. Act 2, in fainter ink, rests on the first reading together with a reread of its names and date only. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** there is no outside text of these acts to compare. A fresh reader who had not seen the reading was given neutral crops of the words the regests depend on and asked what each says. It confirmed the following:
  - the dates "Die. 4. febr. 1514" and "Die xiij februarij 1514";
  - the year in words with "indictione secunda";
  - Brescia, with "palatium broletti" and "citadella nova";
  - "Ludovicus Ycart ... locumtenens & gubernator";
  - "Pino Numai(j)", "Martinengo" and "Antonia q. Antonii de Casariis";
  - the vicar's surname, read as "de piscatrijs".

  The fresh reader gave the Barberiis forename as "Joannis". The research reader reread it at twice native size in two places and reads "Jo(an)nini", with a nasal bar and then n-i-n-i. The fresh reader could not read Icart's title. The officials are corroborated in print, in Google Books snippets only and not page images. Luis d'Icart appears as governor of Brescia "per la Cesarea Maestà" (*Commentari dell'Ateneo di Brescia*, 1938-1939). Pino Numai of Forlì appears as holding offices at Mantua and Brescia (*Palazzi di Forlì*, 1995, citing Andrea Bernardi's chronicle). Another research reader reread both places on canvas 1 (Stams_STA_Frg_240_01v), boxes 1200,1700-2500,1870 ([image](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-1ax4/Stams_STA_Frg_240_01v.jpg/1200,1700,1300,170/full/0/default.jpg)) and 1700,2980-3000,3120 ([image](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-1ax4/Stams_STA_Frg_240_01v.jpg/1700,2980,1300,140/full/0/default.jpg)), and also reads "Jo(an)nini de barberijs", with a second r, so the name is Giovannino (Joanninus) de Barberiis. Not checked: the Archivio di Stato di Brescia and Bergamo (notarial and chancery records), where a civil suit like this could be traced. No human review.

## Physical notes and limits

- The sheet is an extract of the case record, not a sealed original. There is no seal or seal slit. The vicar's "me subscripsi ac sigillavi ... Sigillata sigillo proprio officii" is copied into the text, and three successive acts share one sheet in two inks. Acts 1 and 3 are in dark ink and Act 2 in a lighter, orange-brown ink. Act 3 opens with a large calligraphic I with penwork running down the left margin.
- The top edge is torn diagonally, so the opening of Act 1 is lost and its first two lines are incomplete. The bottom edge is torn through the last line. Two patched holes at the right affect a few words in Acts 1 and 3.
- The back (canvas 0) carries a brown-ink endorsement, written upside down relative to the image, in box 1800,3150-3900,3650 ([image](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-1ax4/Stams_STA_Frg_240_01r.jpg/1800,3150,2100,500/full/180/default.jpg)). It reads "R[e]lationis(?) de bar[...]", perhaps "de Bar[beriis]", but this is uncertain. The back also has a later number "104" and an abraded block written at 90 degrees, unread. Fold panels show that the sheet was kept folded. The host volume is not recorded.
- Not claimed: what the will disposed, the outcome of any further proceedings, the vicar's exact surname, Icart's full title, or how the sheet came to Stams.
