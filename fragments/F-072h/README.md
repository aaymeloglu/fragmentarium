# F-072h — Hippocratic commentary and a medical chapter table

- **Identification: partial, medium confidence in the text layers.** The larger leaf comments on Hippocratic AphorismsVII; its commentator remains unidentified. A separate strip carries a numbered medical chapter table with an apparent Galen source heading. Its exact work, translation and table tradition remain unverified.
- **Transcription: selected assisted image readings.** The isolated reader saw a narrow damaged lemma crop and recovered little. The coordinator inspected that crop, fuller commentary bands on both sides and two bands of the chapter table. Coverage is limited to those passages.
- **Verification: manuscript images against Articella OCR.** The bloodletting and moist-body lemmata, and an intervening phlegm discussion, were compared with the 1519 text. No printed commentary or exact chapter table was matched. No human palaeographic review or accuracy rate is claimed.

[Catalogue](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-072h): Leuven, KU Leuven Bibliotheken, Bijzondere Collecties, Ms.1206; two partial leaves and four strips, catalogued as fourteenth century. [Manifest](https://fragmentarium.ms/metadata/iiif/F-072h/manifest.json), canvases FL7027849–56. Cached crops were inspected without new enhancement; exact native coordinate transforms were not recovered. The formal commentary hand and the smaller cursive table hand are visibly different, but separate origins cannot be proved from that alone.

## Hippocratic text layer

The [preserved image-only response](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-072h/readings/review-2026-09-22-image-only.json) has only one substantially complete text row, between two clipped rows. Its *loquet?* does not confirm the assisted expansion. In the same crop, the larger-script row appears to preserve *…venit(?) sanguinem*. The rest of the lemma is missing or outside the crop and is not supplied as a visible reading.

The [1519 Articella OCR](https://archive.org/download/articellanuperri00gaze/articellanuperri00gaze_djvu.txt), AphorismsVII.54 in its vulgate-Latin numbering, gives *Quibuscunque convenit sanguinem auferre ex venis … flobothomari in vere*. A second crop of fol.1r (band r1_2, rows around the middle) has selected assisted phrases *… venit ad renes … / … facit dolorem et non potest exire … / … includitur inter renes et ventrem …*. This fits the following phlegm aphorism, VII.55, whose printed lemma has *inter … et ventrem flegma concluditur* and passage into the bladder. The manuscript reading of *renes* versus the printed/OCR *phrenes/rhenes* needs care; it is not silently normalized. Common physiological language and a shared lemma do not identify a commentator.

The bottom verso band v1_4 supplies a separate local check. At its seventh physical row the enlarged lemma begins *[C]orporibus …*, followed by commentary with selected phrases:

> … in afforismo iam habito determinavit ypo(crates) … / … corpus humidum … / … vellem reducere illam superfluam humiditatem … / … fames desiccat corpora …

These excerpts are spaced across the lower rows, with omissions explicitly marked; they are not consecutive complete lines. The text corresponds in subject and lemma to VII.59, *Corporibus humidis et carnes habentibus famem adhibere convenit; fames enim desiccat corpora*. The explanatory linking formula belongs to the commentary and remains unmatched. These comparisons establish selected points, not a continuous VII.53–59 span.

The second Articella passage near OCR line 5200 is another Latin rendering of the aphorisms, with different numbering and wording. Comparison with these lemmata does not exclude Galen's commentary. Two searches for *aphorismo/afforismo iam habito* with the linking formula did not locate a matching commentary; a no-hit is not an attribution or exclusion.

## Separate chapter table

Fol.4v's middle and lower bands show numbered therapeutic headings. Near the bottom of the middle band, *Libri xiiii sunt capitula* introduces entries concerning swellings and their treatment, followed by an apparent heading *Libri interior(um) Gal(ieni) …*. Under it the first entry reads, with expansions, *de cognitione passionum membrorum interiorum in generali*. Subsequent visible entries name body regions; several endings are cut at the strip's edge.

This supports a chapter-list description and a provisional Galenic connection. It does not verify an assignment of the preceding books to *De ingenio sanitatis*, exact book divisions, Gerard of Cremona's translation, or a particular omnibus. No matching edition table was inspected. Full reading of the heading and comparison with an actual table witness are the missing evidence.

## Result

The fragment is partially identified as an anonymous Aphorisms commentary plus a separate medical table. The corpus of Hippocratic lemmata is far better supported than the commentator's identity. Broader authorship exclusions, dates derived from commentary style, and claims about the original codices are not established by these samples. Further work should compare the commentary's connecting prose and the table's sequence with actual candidate witnesses.
