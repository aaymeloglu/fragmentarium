**Status: PARTIAL.** Two distinct texts. (A) fol. 1 + fol. 2: an anonymous scholastic commentary on Hippocrates, *Aphorisms* VII (vulgate-Latin numbering VII.53-59: "Quicumque circa epar dolet" through "Corporibus humidis"); the lemmata are secured against the vulgate text, the commentary itself is not yet matched to any printed commentary. (B) fol. 4 (+ fol. 3): a *tabula capitulorum* (chapter list) of Galen's Latin works, certainly *De ingenio sanitatis* (Methodus medendi, Gerard of Cremona's version) books IV-XIV and *De interioribus* (De locis affectis) books I-VI; not checked against an edition.

The sequence 53-54-55-56 (recto) and 56-57-58-59 (verso) is continuous, so fol. 1 is a single leaf of a commentary on Aphorisms VII in the vulgate (Constantinian) Latin order. Fol. 2 ("Quia in afforismo iam habito determinavit ypo de egritudinibus ... restat ut determinaret ...") is the same commentary, aphorism not located.

Formulae of the commentary: each aphorism opens "Quia in afforismo iam habito determinavit ypo(crates) de X ... restat/restituit ut determinaret de Y", then a physiological explanation with "Unde ...", "Immo ...", "Item ...", "Sic determinat afforismum", occasional "In alio loco" cross-references. This is the divisio-textus style of the late 13th / 14th-century university commentaries, not Galen's commentary (Constantine's version), whose wording (checked in the 1519 Articella, second copy of the text at djvu lines c. 5200) does not match.

The book contents match Galen, *Methodus medendi* in Gerard of Cremona's Latin (*De ingenio sanitatis*): III-IV ulcers and wounds with/without loss of substance, V wounds of veins and haemorrhage (nose, womb, lung), VI wounds of nerves (lengthwise/crosswise), contusion, fractures, VII stomach dyscrasias, VIII ephemeral fever (bath, cold water, repletion), IX synochus (cold water, phlebotomy), X hectic fever (baths, diet), XI putrid fevers, XII syncope/pain, XIII-XIV phlegmon, apostemata, cancer, erysipelas, scrofula; followed by *De interioribus* (De locis affectis) in six books. Fol. 3 continues the same kind of table for further Galen works (the sense-organ headings "morborum venientium in gustu / olfactu / visu" fit *De accidenti et morbo*; the numbered "de cura ..." list is not yet placed). The tabula was presumably the contents list of a Galen omnibus manuscript. Not verified against an edition: the 1490/1515 Latin Opera Galeni have chapter lists but their OCR is not searchable through the Google Books API (queries "de cura effimere per balneum", "de cura ethice per balneum/dietam", "de cura sinochi per aquam frigidam", "de cura fracture ossium capitis", "nimie duritiei" all returned 0; "de cognitione passionum membrorum" returned only Pagel, *Ueber Johannes de Sancto Amando*, 1892, quoting the same book-1 heading of De interioribus).

## Date / catalogue check

Nothing contradicts "14th century". Text A's style (divisio textus, "Ypocras/ypo" abbreviation, "afforismus") is 13th/14th-century university medicine; the cursive tabula uses arabic numerals 1-16 alongside roman numerals, normal for a 14th-century notula hand. The catalogue title "Unidentified medical treatise" covers two texts; the record should read "Commentary on Hippocrates' Aphorisms VII (fol. 1-2); table of chapters of Galen's Latin works (De ingenio sanitatis, De interioribus, etc.) (fol. 3-4)".
