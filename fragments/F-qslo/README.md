# F-qslo: Peter Lombard, *Commentarius in Psalmos*, Ps 103:29-104:5 and Ps 105:22-43

This parchment bifolium (a sheet folded to make two leaves) comes from a two-column copy of Peter Lombard's commentary on the Psalms, sometimes called the "Magna glosatura". The commentary was completed about 1158. The fragment covers Ps 103:29-104:5 and Ps 105:22-43 (Vulgate numbering), which is PL 191, cols. 941-948 and 965-970. It is the outer bifolium of a pair: Stams Frg. 187, [F-bjvu](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-bjvu), from the same manuscript, sits immediately inside it.

The catalogue record, [fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-qslo](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-qslo), describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 213 as parchment, 442 x 357 mm (measured 355/442 x 223/357 mm by Claudia Sojer), online since 18 September 2021. It is in the collection "Stams (Stealth Published)", whose records carry no description. The title is "x", so the record said nothing about the text. Its physical details fit the object.

- **Identification:** Identified, high confidence. The commentary's wording and order agree with PL 191 in five collated passages from both faces, including Lombard's own linking phrases, such as "propter haec scelera dixit comminando", "Duplex misericordia Dei hic exponitur" and "non sustinuerunt consilium ejus, scilicet Dei, quod est de aeternis". Red marginal source sigla, "ag" for Augustine and "c" for Cassiodorus, match the source notes the edition prints in brackets.

  Alternatives considered:
  - The Glossa ordinaria and Augustine's *Enarrationes in Psalmos* share some sentences, but they are shorter sources that Lombard drew on.
  - Gilbert of Poitiers' *Media glosatura* was compared on the nested sister bifolium, using one manuscript witness (Cambridge, Queens' College MS 18). The fragment carries Lombard's expanded text, not Gilbert's.

  Differences from PL are minor:
  - Ps 105:37 has "inficiebant" for "interficiebant", and the Greek word in the same passage is not written.
  - Ps 103:32 has "qualiter illa faciat" for PL "Qua littera illa faciat".
  - Ps 103:33 has "(at)tribuebat" without "vires".
  - Ps 104:1 has "audietur" for "auditur".

The differences are spellings, abbreviations and the small variants listed above. "Inficiebant" goes with an "infecta" form of the psalm verse, but the beginning of that lemma is lost, so the form is inferred. These differences do not show whether the copy depends on any particular text of the commentary.

Edition used: PL 191 (Paris, 1854), [archive.org copy](https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecur191mign). Lombard's authorship of the Psalter commentary is secure and is not in dispute.

## Leaf order, other items, limits

The text gives the leaf order:
- Leaf 1 recto: canvas 0, right page, Ps 103:29-36 and the start of the Ps 104 prologue.
- Leaf 1 verso: canvas 1, left page, Ps 104:1-5.
- Leaf 2 recto: canvas 1, right page, Ps 105:22-31.
- Leaf 2 verso: canvas 0, left page, Ps 105:32-43.

The text runs on into [F-bjvu](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-bjvu) at Ps 104:5. It runs back from F-bjvu into this bifolium at Ps 105:22, where the word "moy|ses" is split across the join. The two bifolia agree in layout, line spacing and source sigla.

Other items in the images:
- Large black mirror-image Gothic letters cover much of canvas 1. They are an offset left by another document pressed against the leaf and are not part of this text.
- Black display letters "Po D..." were added later over canvas 0, column d.

No claim is made about date, origin, the version of the commentary, or whether the copy is independent of the printed text.
