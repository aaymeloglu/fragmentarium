# F-5xnq: Office lessons from 1 Samuel (1 Kings) 7:9-15 and 8:14-9:1, Vulgate

This parchment strip at Stams comes from an Office book that gave the Bible readings for Matins, divided into numbered lessons. One face carries 1 Samuel 7:9-15, with "L(ectio) ii" written beside 7:10 ("Factum est ergo cum Samuel offerret holocaustum") and "L(ectio) iii" beside 7:13b ("Facta est itaque manus Domini super Philistiim"). The other face carries 1 Samuel 8:14-9:1. On that face a red rubric "Feria quarta. L(ectio) I", meaning the first lesson for Wednesday, stands at 8:22, which the book opens as "Dixit ergo Dominus ad Samuelem: Audi vocem populi et constitue super eos regem". The text is the Vulgate. In the medieval Office the books of Kings were read at Matins from the Sunday after the octave of Pentecost into the summer. Lessons 2 and 3 of one day here cover 7:10-8:21 and the next day, Wednesday, begins at 8:22. That suggests the Tuesday and Wednesday of an early week of that series, probably the first, but this placement is an inference.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-5xnq) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 205: parchment, one fragment item, 321 x 126 mm (measured 320/321 x 122/126 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck). It has no catalogue date, the title "x", no linked description, and two images labelled 01r and 01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images, a single strip with one column of writing on each face.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for the text and its lesson divisions. The basis is both faces read in full and compared with the Benedictine critical edition of the Vulgate. Thirty-two lines on each face agree word for word in wording and sequence, apart from the variants below. The Old Latin (pre-Vulgate) version is excluded because the text is Vulgate throughout ("addecimabit", "lapis adiutorii", "subter Bethchar").
  - **Variants the apparatus records:** 7:10 "phylystei inierunt bellum" for "Philistheos inire proelium" ("bellum" in some witnesses, "iniere/ininerunt" forms in others); 7:11 "filii Israel" for "viri Israhel"; 7:13 and 8:19 "samuelis"; 7:14 "tulerunt phylystei ab israel" without "Israheli", and "liberavitque".
  - **Variants not in the apparatus as read:** 7:9 omits "integrum"; 8:16 "optimos iuvenes" for "iuvenes optimos".
  - **8:22:** "Dixit ergo ... ad samuelem. Audi vocem populi" for "dixit autem ... ad Samuhel audi vocem eorum". "samuelem" is recorded; "ergo" and "populi" read as an adaptation so that the lesson opens with a clear subject.
  - **8:18:** like the critical text, the strip lacks "quia petistis vobis regem", which later printed Vulgates add.

  The kind of book (Office lectionary or the lessons of a breviary) and its use are separate questions and are not settled. Three lessons on a weekday read from Kings in summer is the pattern of a secular office, the office of cathedral and collegiate clergy. Under the Rule of Benedict, summer weekdays have only one short lesson said from memory. This is an inference, not a measurement against books of known use.
- **Transcription:** source-assisted. The [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-5xnq/readings/stams-2026-10-31-image-only.json) covers every line. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-5xnq/readings/stams-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json) is a research reader's reread of all 33 lines of both faces on the native images, in bands of eleven lines. It confirms the first reading almost throughout. It reads the oddly formed word on canvas 1 line 4 as "d(omi)n(u)s" and supplies only letter tops for the trimmed line 33 on each face. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared both faces with the page images of the Rome Vulgate, vol. V, pp. 102-103 and 105-106, including the apparatus. The OCR text of that volume was used only to find the pages.
  Passage checked independently: canvas 0, lines 8-25 (1 Sam 8:16-21 and the rubric "FERIA QUARTA L(ectio) I"), native boxes 1650,2380,3450,3500, 1650,3350,3450,4500 and 1650,4250,3450,4800, read on the image and compared with the Rome Vulgate, vol. V, pp. 105-106 ([page image](https://archive.org/details/bibliasacraiuxta0005unse/page/n124/mode/1up), [page image](https://archive.org/details/bibliasacraiuxta0005unse/page/n125/mode/1up)). The text agrees word for word; the only differences are the genitive "samuelis" at 8:19, which the apparatus records, and the spelling of the name. The reader had seen the report's transcription of lines 1-9, 14-16 and 24-33, so only lines 10-13 and 17-23 were read without it.
  Gaps: no person has reviewed the reading. No printed or manuscript Office lectionary with the same weekday divisions was compared, so the week and the use rest on inference.

## Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

- Leaf order: canvas 1 (labelled 01v) comes before canvas 0 (labelled 01r) in the text. The text missing between them, 7:15-8:14, would fill almost exactly two columns of this strip (about 1745 characters against about 940 a column). The free margin is the same physical edge on both faces (left on canvas 1, right on canvas 0). The strip is therefore most likely the column next to the fold of a two-column leaf, canvas 1 being column a of the recto and canvas 0 column b of the verso. If so, the r/v labels run against the text order. This is an inference from measurement.
- Other marks: modern pencil "Stams, STA, Frg. 205r" and "205v", and on canvas 0 "S IV" (underlined) and "n 58".
- Not claimed: a date (the catalogue gives none, and the hand was not dated here), origin or use, the week of the year, and any relation to a known lectionary.
