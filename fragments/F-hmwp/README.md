# F-hmwp: Aristotle, *Posterior Analytics*, Latin, I.11-12 and II.13-19

Stams, Frg. 36 is a set of nine strips cut from a glossed manuscript of Aristotle's *Posterior Analytics* (*Analytica posteriora*, his treatise on demonstration and scientific knowledge) in the medieval Latin translation of the James of Venice tradition. The text is written in a Gothic bookhand with capitals touched in red. Small interlinear glosses and marginal notes mark the parts of the argument ("quarta pars", "Quarta differentia", "Solutio ... questionis"). The surviving text comes from book I, chapters 11-12, and book II, chapters 13-19. Four pieces were collated with printed texts: I.12 (Bekker 77b1-18 and 77b16-40), II.16 (98a35-b21) and the close of II.17 with the opening of II.19 (99b7-22). Which state of the Latin translation the manuscript carries, James's own version or William of Moerbeke's revision of it, is not settled.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-hmwp) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 36, paper, nine fragment items, 206 x 131 mm (measured on piece 2, 200/206 x 118/131 mm, by Claudia Sojer), online since 18 September 2021. It has the title "x", six images, and no date, description or identification. The nine pieces are as described. Each piece carries a modern pencil label, "Stams, STA, Frg. 36-1r" to "36-9v". The labels do not give the text order: on piece 4 the face labelled "v" is the recto.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence, for the work and loci. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and order with the Latin text on four pieces from both books: the questions proper to each science (I.12), the leaf-shedding and lunar-eclipse examples of cause and effect (II.16), and the closing words of the account of syllogism and demonstration with the opening of II.19 (II.17-19). The pieces are continuous text with separate glosses, not lemmata embedded in a commentary. The version is the Latin translation that goes back to James of Venice (before 1159). It is compared here with two printings: the text Thomas Aquinas commented, as printed in the Leonine edition, and the text printed under Boethius's name in the *Patrologia Latina* (PL), which is James's translation. Where the two prints differ, agreement is mixed. On 36-4r, 36-5r and 36-8r the fragment goes with the Leonine text: "utique erit", "sicut in speculativa" (PL "ut visibilia"), "magis proxima", "quid unumquodque sit". On 36-7r it agrees six times with the Leonine and twice with PL ("aut ex alia arte" without "quae"; "ad [...]re" as in PL "ad invicem ... concurrere"). It also has "ut in dyalecticis", which is in neither print. Other differences are listed under Textual evidence. The critical edition, *Aristoteles Latinus* IV.1-4, could not be consulted, so the recension remains open.
- **Transcription:** source-assisted. The [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-hmwp/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-image-only.json) was made by three readers, each covering a range of images. In the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-hmwp/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-source-assisted.json), a research reader reread on the native images all of 36-4r and 36-5r and the upper nine lines of 36-8r. 36-7r (18 lines) and the tops of 36-4v and 36-6r were read in the independent check. The other pieces rest on the first reading. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader collated 36-4r, 36-5r and 36-8r with page images of the Leonine textus (pp. 219, 389, 392-393) and of PL 64 (cols. 723-724, 757-760).
  Passage checked independently: 36-7r, all 18 lines, canvas 2 (Stams_STA_Frg_036_05r08r), native box 450,3550,2200,6400, compared with the Leonine textus p. 222 ([page image](https://archive.org/details/operaomniaiussui01thom/page/n591/mode/1up)) and PL 64 col. 724 ([page image](https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecursu0064mign/page/n369/mode/1up)). All lines fall in sequence through 77b16-40.
  Gaps: 36-2r, 2v, 3r, 5v, 7v, 8v and 9r are placed from the first reading only, and 36-6r (II.14, c. 98a17-34) only against the Leonine OCR. 36-3v and 36-6v are not placed. The glosses were not read systematically.

Differences besides those named under Identification are mostly spelling and abbreviation. There are also some small departures from both prints: "geometricalibus?" for "geometricis" (uncertain), "se habebit" for "se habet", "Si autem in b est a" for "Sic igitur in B est A", "folia fluunt" for "fluit", and an unexplained "Alia autem" on 36-5r line 2. None of them affects the identification.

The pieces are vertical strips, each holding about half the width of a page: 36-5r, 36-7r and 36-8r keep the right half (line beginnings lost), and 36-4r the left half, whose other face 36-4v is a right half. Labels are wrong for piece 4 and happen to fit piece 7. The pages were not reconstructed.

Not claimed: the recension (James or Moerbeke's revision); the date and origin of the manuscript; its support (the catalogue gives paper; not examined); its use, though the glossing suggests study, it is not shown to be a school copy; whether the marginal division notes follow a known commentary; the novelty of the witness.
