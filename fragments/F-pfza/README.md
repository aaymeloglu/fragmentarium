# F-pfza: Terence, *Andria*, vv. 527-545 and 738-756

Stams, Frg. 28 is a paper bifolium (a folded sheet making two leaves) from a manuscript of Terence's comedy *Andria*, written in one early modern cursive hand with drawn initials at the scene headings. Its four pages carry vv. 527-545, the end of Act III scene 2 and the opening of III.3 (Simo asks Chremes to let the marriage go ahead), and vv. 738-756, the end of IV.3 and IV.4 (Chremes, Mysis and Davus around the child left at the door). The leaves between the two passages, about 190 verses, are lost. The text agrees in wording, speaker order and scene headings with the critical edition, and its variants are those of the medieval manuscripts.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-pfza) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 28, paper, one fragment item, 205 x 154 mm (measured 192/205 x 148/154 mm by Claudia Sojer), online since 18 September 2021. It has the title "x", two images (Frg_028_01r and 01v), and no date, description or identification. The description of the object fits. Pencil notes on the leaf read "aus Einband II/G 36 (a 1534)", that is, taken from the binding of the Stams volume II/G 36, a book of 1534.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence. The basis is sustained agreement with the *Andria* across two separate passages on all four pages, including both scene headings ("Simo. Chremes senex"; "Cremes senex. Myses. Ancilla. Dau[us]") and the speaker sigla (single-letter speaker labels such as S., C., M., D.). The words are Terence's own play, not a quotation of it, so no containing work is in question. The variants follow the medieval (Calliopian) manuscripts against Umpfenbach's editorial choices. Examples are "Per ego te deos oro" (with his manuscripts D E), "ipsus gnatus" (B D1 G), "in ipso tempore" (D G), "Hem, nusquam est" (D E G), "id" absent before "si impetro" (all manuscripts), "aiebant", "ut nuptiae", "aut tu plus vides" and "ab Andria est ancilla haec". Four readings are not in his apparatus: "Per unicam" for "Perque unicam", "video" for "videam", "Dic clare quod rogo" for "Dictura es quod rogo" and "tun adposisti". Spelling differs ("vestram", "Myses", "Cremes").
- **Transcription:** source-assisted. The [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-pfza/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-image-only.json) covered all four pages; its reader reported that the dialogue felt familiar. In the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-pfza/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-source-assisted.json), a research reader reread every line of all four pages letter by letter on the native images and corrected the first reading in several places ("mea" for "intra", "obsecro" for "observo", "Eho Myses" for "Ego [?]ses"). One word before "mihi respondes" and the blotted word before "ipsum obviam" remain unread. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader collated all four pages with page images of Umpfenbach's edition, pp. 45-46 and 61-62, text and apparatus, and checked them against the Latin Library text.
  Passage checked independently: canvas 1 (Stams_STA_Frg_028_01v), right page, vv. 531-535 (the offset-stained lines, scene heading and opening of III.3), native boxes 4050,2350,5450,3000 and 4050,2900,5450,3500, re-read on the image (not blind) and compared with Umpfenbach pp. 45-46 ([page image](https://archive.org/details/comoediaeedidite00tere/page/n140/mode/1up)). Every legible word falls in the edition's sequence. The blotted word fits "eccum", "q(ui) aiebant" agrees with the manuscripts' "aiebant", and line 1 of the page reads "ipsus gnatus".
  Gaps: only Umpfenbach's apparatus was used, not later critical editions or early prints.

Umpfenbach's apparatus on these pages accounts for most differences. "ipsus" is the reading of B D1 G; "in" before "ipso tempore" is omitted by B C E P and kept by D G; "Per ego te" is in D E; "hem nusquam est" is in D E G; "MY. maledicis. DA. unde est" and "est ancilla haec" are in all his manuscripts; "aut tu plus vides" (v. 738) is the manuscripts' reading against Guyet's "ut tu"; "tun" opens v. 742 in all manuscripts ("tun posuisti" or "tun apposuisti"); Umpfenbach prints Ritschl's "tu adposisti". Readings not in the apparatus are "Per unicam", "video" (v. 750), "Dic clare quod rogo" (v. 751, perhaps carried over from "dic clare" in v. 754) and "tun adposisti" as one reading. The others are spelling.

## Leaf order and what is not claimed

The right page of canvas 1 and the left page of canvas 0 are recto and verso of one leaf (vv. 527-545). The right page of canvas 0 and the left page of canvas 1 are recto and verso of the other (vv. 738-756). The sheet is therefore an outer bifolium of a gathering whose middle leaves are lost. Drawn initials (an R with a bearded turbaned head; an I with foliage) open the two scenes. Several words are cancelled in the text. No glosses are visible.

Not claimed: the date of the copy beyond the 1534 binding, its origin and purpose, its exemplar (manuscript or print), or its place among witnesses of Terence. The support was not examined beyond the catalogue's "paper".
