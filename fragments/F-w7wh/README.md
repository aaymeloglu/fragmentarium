# F-w7wh: Nikolaus Herman, *Haußhaltung* (verse rendering of Johannes Mathesius, *Oeconomia*), "Haußsorg das ist ein schwere last" to "Versamlet vnd bstetigt zur erd", abridged; with an unplaced Psalm 128 paraphrase

Two parchment strips at Stams, cut from one calligraphic sheet, carry two German verse texts in a late sixteenth-century Kanzlei hand with Fraktur display lines and cadel strapwork (large looped pen flourishes). The second text is an abridged copy of the rhymed *Haußhaltung* printed as "Haußhaltung Johannis Mathesij, Prediger in Sanct Jochimßthal" (Nürnberg 1561). Mathesius's own preface of 1564 says this verse is Nikolaus Herman's rendering of Mathesius's prose *Oeconomia*. The copy runs from "[Haußsorg is]t zwar ain schwere las[t]" to "vnd bstetigt zur Erd", the end of the closing death-prayer. Four blocks of the printed text are absent: ten verses on the treatment of servants, thirty-two verses on the widower, ten verses in the deathbed passage, and the eight verses on making a will, which are replaced by a short text not found in any print, including the words "Gotes gnad". The first text is a German verse paraphrase of Psalm 128 ("Beati omnes qui timent Dominum", the household psalm), ending in a display "Amen" followed by "denn es war[..]". Its version has not been found.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-w7wh) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 54, one parchment item, 316 x 67 mm (measured on the right strip as 315/316 x 64/67 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), online since 18 September 2021, with the title "x" and two images (01r and 01v). It gives no date, text or description. Nothing in it is wrong; it does not say that the item is two strips of one sheet or name either text.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for section 2; section 1 is not identified. Section 2 agrees with the 1561 print in sustained wording and sequence, collated on page images of that print over more than twenty-five verses by one reader and fifteen further manuscript lines (about two verses each) by a second, from the opening "Haußsorg" stanza through the household rules, the Centurio exemplum and the death passage to "Versamlet vnd bstetigt zur erd" (examples under Textual evidence). Two readings that first looked unmatched are print verses: "Sanct Mar[tins]" ("Wer offt begeht Sanct Mertens tag") and "[vns]er thuen doch als" ("Ist vnser thun doch alls vmb sonst"). The differences are: "zwar ain" for the print's "das ist ein" in the first verse; "Verzer nit" without the print's "mehr"; "hennen" for "henn"; "an" for "on"; Bavarian spelling throughout ("Nachpaurn", "Bluet", "guetem", "Obrigkhait"); and four omissions, each shown by a manuscript line that runs straight on across the gap. (1) Between "Le[tzlich ...]" and "[Mi]t Nachpaurn sein eintrech[tig]" the ten verses from "Nicht gar zu hart die knechte dein" to "wütig" are absent. (2) Between "alls st[eht] im [Euangelio]" and "mag nicht ge[sch]ehen" (print "Dein kindern mag nit gschehen baß") the thirty-two widower verses from "Wenn dir Gott nimbt dein liebes weyb" to "Auff das ers greiff am besten an" are absent. (3) Between "Wandern. Von hinne[n]" and "[..]st morgen sterben" the eight verses on the will ("Soltu Gott vom deim gut was geben ... Wo Christ seins theils darben muß") are replaced by a short text ending "[..]ch. Gotes gnad"; "Gotes gnad" is clear on the image, and the same eight verses stand in every other edition found by full-text search of the Munich digitisation centre (MDZ). (4) Between "[..]finden dich" with the following verse and "wie Simeon" the ten verses from "Vnser fleiß richt sehr wenig auß" to "den ers gan" are absent. The verse is printed under Mathesius's name; the attribution to Herman rests on Mathesius's preface "Zum Leser" of 1564: "Ich Johann Matthesius bekenne mich zu dieser Haushaltung, so Herr Nickel Herman aus meiner Oeconomia in deutsche reim gebracht". The preface was read in the 1796 reprint, not in a sixteenth-century print. Which edition the copyist used is not known: the verses also circulate in other prints, among them an *Ehebüchlein* (bsb11291332) and a *Christliche Gebet* collection (bsb10263284). "Sanct Mar[tins]" agrees with the 1561 "Sanct Mertens" against "S. Mertens" in later prints, which is weak evidence. Section 1 shares two rhyming verses, "Vnd deine kinder vmb de[n tisch]" and "[..]gesunt vnd frisch", with Luther's Psalm 128 hymn "Wol dem der in Gottes furcht steht", but has wording absent from Luther's five stanzas ("du frumer Christ", "auf diser Erden", "der Ewig Got", "[himl]isch Jherusalem"); Michael Vehe's "Die menschen warlich selig synt" (1537) shares only "gesunt vnd frisch" and is excluded.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-w7wh/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-image-only.json) covers both faces; its reader had no proposed identification. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-w7wh/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json) is a research reader's (Claude) reread of every line of both strips on the written face at native resolution, in bands of about ten lines per strip and three full-width bands. A second research reader reread the lower right strip, the abraded foot of the left strip and the lines of section 1 before opening the print at those places, though after seeing the first research reader's tentative readings. Readings revised with the print in view: "eintrech[tig]" (first reading "entwerch"), "steht im", "[f]inden dich", "Bey leib", "[Nüz]t mer", "Sanct Mar[tins]" (first "Saul war"), "muos In", "[ents]chlieff" and "rhue". The lowest four lines of the left strip are abraded and holed and are only partly read. About twenty-five characters of each written line are lost in the gap between the strips. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared section 2 with the 1561 print on BSB page images (bsb10203350, scans 11-15), not OCR. A second research reader compared a further fifteen manuscript lines with the same page images, counted the omitted verses, and found the pairing of the strips: left line n continues on right line n+1 throughout, which holds on every pair compared but is not confirmed physically. The same reader read Mathesius's 1564 preface on a page image of the 1796 reprint (bsb11260410, scan 8). Section 1 was compared with Luther's hymn on a page image of Wolfgang Ammon's *Neuw Gesangbuch* (bsb10592555, scan 270), and with Vehe's hymn in Internet Archive OCR of W. Bäumker's edition of Catholic hymns (bub_gb_oA0DAAAAQAAJ). Not tested for section 1: Hans Sachs's Psalm 128 Meisterlieder, Caspar Ulenberg's psalter (1582) and the printed writing-master books, which may hold section 1 or the whole sheet as a model. No human review.

## Physical notes and what is not claimed

- The two strips are mounted side by side with a gap between them; each has a central fold with sewing holes. The right strip sits about one written line higher than the left, and each written line holds about two verses, divided by commas and points. Each section is headed by cadel flourishes and a Fraktur display line.
- The back, canvas 1 ([image](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-w7wh/Stams_STA_Frg_054_01v.jpg/full/7360,/0/default.jpg)), has no text: only a pen flourish and modern blue notes, "Urbari / Wessobrunn 1594" on the left strip (box 3050,2500-3450,4100) and "Urbari / Wessobrunn ... 1595" on the right strip (box 4750,1400-5250,2400). They suggest the strips were used in the binding of Wessobrunn rent registers (Urbare) of 1594 and 1595; the host volumes were not checked.
- The strip is cut below "vnd bstetigt zur Erd", so whether the copy ended there or continued to the print's "AMEN" is unknown. What follows "Amen ... denn es war[..]" at the end of section 1 is unread.
- Not claimed: the date beyond the script (late sixteenth century, before the reuse if the blue notes record it), the place of writing, the purpose of the sheet (its display script and the wedding-gift character of the *Haußhaltung* suggest a calligraphic show piece, which is not tested), the exemplar edition, the source of the "Gotes gnad" passage, and the identity of the Psalm 128 paraphrase.
