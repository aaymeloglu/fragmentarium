# F-k6l1: *De accentibus*, as transmitted in Johannes Marchesinus, *Mammotrectus*, from the enclitic *cum* to adverbs in -e

This paper leaf carries a continuous stretch of the tract on accent, *De accentibus*, that is transmitted as a section of the *Mammotrectus super Bibliam* of Johannes Marchesinus, which its 1498 print calls a book explaining the Bible and the other books read in church. The text runs from the enclitic *cum* (nobiscum, vobiscum) through the accent of relatives and interrogatives, compounds of *inde*, prepositions and conjunctions, to interjections, barbarous words and adverbs in -e. The same tract also circulated outside the *Mammotrectus*: it is appended as *De accentu* to the printed *Vocabularius breviloquus* and reworked in a 15th-century compilation in Strasbourg. Which of these settings the leaf came from is not established.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-k6l1) describes Gent, Universiteitsbibliotheek, HS.2583/187: one paper leaf, 203 x 143 mm, Cursiva, Latin, dated 1401-1450, with the title "Treatise on Latin grammar" and the tag "Topic: grammar". The subject is right; the record names no author or work. Its date and script are the catalogue's statements and were not examined here.

- **Identification:** identified, confidence high, for the text and its place in the tract. Two passages, one on each face, agree in wording and in sequence with the *De accentibus* of the *Mammotrectus* in the manuscript Paris, BnF, lat. 1803, leaves clxv-clxvi, and the rest of the leaf follows the same order by the first reading. The prose of the tract is not *Doctrinale* text: the leaf quotes one *Doctrinale* verse inside the tract. The Catholicon was searched in poor machine text without a match, which excludes nothing. Two reuses of the tract were compared in machine text only. The *De accentu* of the *Vocabularius breviloquus* (1498 print) is the same tract; the leaf differs from it at three readings, listed under Textual evidence. The compilation in Strasbourg, BNU, Ms. 0.027 is not the leaf's text in the form compared: it adds two passages within the stretch the leaf covers ("ut lacius infra dicetur" after "alio aliquo alias", and a passage on *tenus* citing Isidore and Papias after "et est enclesis"), and the leaf goes straight on at both places. Variants do not settle the leaf's setting either way (see Textual evidence). That the section stands within the *Mammotrectus* is checked in lat. 1803, in the 1498 print (machine text) and in snippets of a 1478 edition; whether Marchesinus wrote it was not.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-k6l1/readings/generic2-2026-11-27-image-only.json) covers both faces; the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-k6l1/readings/generic2-2026-11-27-source-assisted.json) gives the lines relied on. A research reader reread at native size, with lat. 1803 at hand: face 1 lines 1-12; face 2 lines 1-5, about lines 12-19 and the first line after, and the interjection and barbarous-word lines near the foot. Face 1 below line 12 and face 2 lines 6-11 and 25-32 rest on the first reading. The hand is a small, heavily abbreviated cursiva and many single words stay uncertain. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the two passages with page images of BnF lat. 1803 and the readings that bear on the tract's forms with the machine text of the 1498 *Mammotrectus* and *Breviloquus* prints and of Strasbourg 0.027; no page image of either print was opened. The coordinator (Claude) read face 1 from "ipsa inclinatur i(d est) gravatur" to "qualis est Johannes. So[r/c]rates bene legit qualiter legit" (canvas 0, band y 910-1670 across the full width) on the image against [BnF lat. 1803, Gallica f176](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b9068032d/f176): the sequence and the distinctive wording agree. Two fresh readers, given five neutral crops of this record without the transcription or the source, transcribed them. They supported the con-sign of "ꝯ(tra) generales regulas" and the sign "i°" before "ablativorum", but did not expand it. They did not support the incipit's "precedentem ultimam" (both read "ulnaz" for the second word), the name after "qualiter legit" on face 1 line 10 (the coordinator sees a word beginning "cath-", the research reader "[ca]tho?", the blind readers "castz" and "ralpo"), a second "nescio" or the word before the last name in that line, or "dissillaba" on face 2 (both read a con-sign). These are given as uncertain below and none of them carries the identification. Gaps: the unread lines listed above; the word after "servant" on face 2 is unread; no modern edition of the *Mammotrectus* was consulted; no palaeographer has reviewed the reading or the match.

Differences from lat. 1803 in the passages compared: "nisi dicatur" for "dicamus"; "ipsa inclinatur" without "prepositio"; "postponitur" for "postponatur"; "quibuscum quicum" in the reverse order; no "ut" before "in interrogativis"; "Hec prepositio" for "prepositio" (the 1498 print also has "hec prepositio", by its machine text). On face 1 line 10 the name after "qualiter legit" is not "plato" on the image and is not settled. Elsewhere on face 2, by the research reader's reading, the leaf has "allet? allot(is)?" where the prints have "allec allecis".

The readings that bear on the tract's settings point different ways. The leaf has "relative vel infinite retentum" (coordinator-read) with lat. 1803, where the 1498 *Mammotrectus* print has "relatiue sumptum vel infinite retentum" and the *Breviloquus* "relative sumptum vel infinite tentum" (both machine text). The leaf has a sign "i°" before "ablativorum", and lat. 1803 "illorum", where both prints have "ultimam ablativorum"; the blind readers confirm the sign but its expansion is not established. The leaf has "In accentu qui dicitur Prisciani" (research reader) with lat. 1803 and the 1498 *Mammotrectus* print, where the *Breviloquus* has "In libro accentuum qui dicitur Prisciani". So the 1498 *Mammotrectus* print agrees with the *Breviloquus* against the leaf in two of these three places. These variants describe the texts compared; they do not show which kind of book the leaf came from, or any dependence between copies.

The barbarous-word line, "barbara vox no(n) declinata l(a)ti(n)e accentu(m) sup(er) extrema(m) s(er)uabit acutu(m)", quotes the *Doctrinale* of Alexander de Villa Dei ("omnis barbara vox non declinata latine / accentum super extremam servabit acutum", chapter XI, verses 26-27 of Reichling's edition, read in machine text only). The surrounding prose is the tract's.

## Leaf order and limits

One column of about 40 lines a face. Face 1 (canvas 0) ends in the list of relative adverbs and the compounds of *inde*; face 2 (canvas 1) opens "non multu(m) insistat s(ed) po(tius) dissimulet", which follows directly in lat. 1803. So the catalogue's recto and verso order is right. No running head, folio number or neighbouring section survives on the leaf.

Not claimed: a date or origin for the copy beyond the catalogue's; whether the leaf belonged to a complete *Mammotrectus* or to a separate copy of the tract; any relation between this copy and lat. 1803, the prints or the Strasbourg compilation; that Marchesinus composed the section.
