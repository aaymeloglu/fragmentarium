## Status: PARTIAL

Genre and subject are established; the author is not. The text is a Latin scholastic question-commentary (arts-faculty type, first half of the 14th c.) on Aristotle's Parva naturalia, specifically the doctrine of De longitudine et brevitate vitae and De iuventute et senectute, de vita et morte: (1) whether the natural heat / a living body is corrupted by itself (Aristotle's two deaths, marcedo a se ipso and extinctio ab alio, De iuv. 5 = "De morte et vita"), with the maxim "agens naturale in agendo patitur" and the celestial agent; (2) a dubium "utrum magis vivant calida et humida quam temperata", answered from "vita stat per calidum et humidum", the humidum as pabulum of the calidum, the candle/oil analogy and the fire that consumes itself by consuming its wood. Not the Aristotelian text itself, not a lemmatic commentary, not Buridan (checked line by line, see below). The catalogue title "treatise on physics" should be corrected to "Quaestiones/dubia on Aristotle's De longitudine et brevitate vitae (Parva naturalia)".

## What the text is (established)

- Latin question-commentary (dubia/quaestiones with "Dubitatur hic ... utrum", "Ad istam questionem est intelligendum quod", "Si tu dicas", "Adhuc", "¶ Obicitur", "Notandum"), the form of an arts-faculty lecture reportatio, first half of the 14th c. (cursiva, no rubrication). Not a "treatise on physics".
- Subject: the Parva naturalia doctrine of life and death. Fol. [i] treats whether a living body / the natural heat is corrupted by itself: Aristotle's two deaths "marcedo (a se ipso) et extinctio (ab alio)" (Latin De iuventute/De respiratione, cited by the fragment as "libro de morte et vita"; the 1510 Paris print of the Parva naturalia reads "marcedine et extinctione ... eam quidem que a seipso marcedinem, eam que a contrariis extinctionem", Google Books wMF8JMiL5nwC), "calidum naturale agens in humidum radicale consumit ipsum", "agens naturale in agendo patitur", celestial agent ("agente celo et movente"). Authorities cited: 2º De generatione, 2º Physicorum, De morte et vita, De iuventute, De longitudine et brevitate vite, "deus dedit unicuique membro" (De partibus animalium / Galen commonplace).
- Fol. [ii] treats the dubium "utrum magis vivant calida et humida quam temperata" (Aristotle, De longitudine 5, 466a18-b2), answered with "vita stat per calidum et humidum" (Avicenna/Isagoge commonplace), the humidum aereum/unctuosum as "pabulum calidi", candle/oil, fire consuming its wood, and a degrees-and-years arithmetic (3 vs 4 gradus, xx/xxx/xl anni).
- The cross-reference "habes hic quod ... in De longitudine et brevitate vite" and the citations of "libro de morte et vita" and "de iuventute" leave open whether the commented book is De longitudine (most likely: the dubium follows the order of De long. 5) or De iuventute/De morte et vita.
