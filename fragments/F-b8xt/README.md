## Status

**PARTIAL.** Genre and source established, author not. The leaf carries a quaestio-style commentary (question, "Pro declaratione questionis ... sunt duo premittenda", numbered notabilia, conclusiones, corollaria, numbered objections) on Aristotle, *Metaphysics* VII (Zeta), cc. 4–5, on the *quod quid erat esse* / definition: whether definition and the *quod quid erat esse* belong only to substance (only to *species specialissimae* of the category of substance), and whether accidents are definable only *per additamentum* (the "simus = nasus cavus" example). Several runs of the text reproduce Thomas Aquinas, *Sententia libri Metaphysicae* VII, lect. 4, nn. 2–5 almost verbatim, supporting a Thomistic connection in a Metaphysics commentary that embeds Aquinas's expositio inside a question structure. The leaf is not Aquinas's commentary itself (question apparatus, corollaria, numbered contra-arguments are not in the Sententia). One unverified printed candidate, Johannes Versor, *Quaestiones super Metaphysicam Aristotelis* (Cologne, Quentell, 1489/1493/1494), could not be checked: no digitized copy with searchable text was found via Google Books or archive.org.

## Identification (source layer)

Recto col. b 41–50 and verso col. a 1–2 follow Aquinas, *Sententia libri Metaphysicae*, lib. 7, lect. 4, nn. 3–4 (Corpus Thomisticum, https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/cmp07.html, paragraphs [82899]–[82900]):

| Fragment (expanded) | Aquinas, In Metaph. VII l. 4 |
|---|---|
| r b 41–44 si queratur quid est homo et respondetur animal, [ly animal] quia est in genere substantie, non solum dicit quid est homo sed [etiam] absolute significat quid, id est substantiam | n. 3: Cum enim quaero quid est homo, et respondetur, animal; ly animal, quia est in genere substantiae, non solum dicit quid est homo, sed etiam absolute significat quid, id est substantiam. |
| r b 44–47 sed cum queritur quid est albedo et respondetur quod est color, [ly] color, licet significet quid est albedo, tamen non absolute significat quid sed quale, et ideo qualitas non habet quid simpliciter sed secundum quid | n. 3: Sed cum quaeritur quid est albedo, et respondetur, color, licet significet quid est albedo, non tamen absolute significat quid, sed quale. Et ideo qualitas non habet quid simpliciter, sed secundum quid. |
| r b 48–49 ut color est quid alicuius, et hoc quid magis [est] substantiale quam substantia | n. 3: ut cum dicimus quod color est quid albedinis. Et hoc quid, magis est substantiale quam substantia. |
| r b 50 omnia alia predicamenta habent rationem entis a substantia | n. 4: Propter hoc enim quod omnia alia praedicamenta habent rationem entis a substantia |
| v a 1–2 [sicut animal est] quid est homo, ita color est quid est albedinis et numerus est quid est dualitatis | n. 4: sicut animal est quid hominis, ita color albedinis, et numerus dualitatis |
| r a 38–41 ens multipliciter dicitur, non secundum puram equivocationem sed secundum analogiam, que est secundum prius et posterius | cf. lect. 4 n. 5 (ens dicitur multipliciter, non aequivoce sed secundum prius et posterius) — wording not verbatim |

Other content consistent with a question on Metaph. VII cc. 4–5 in the Thomist manner: "ista [tria] sunt idem, scilicet essentia, quiditas seu quod quid erat esse et diffinitio" (r a 10–11); "diffinitio proprie dicta non conveniat accidentibus ... accidens semper indiget diffiniri per aliquid additum extrinsecum, scilicet per subiectum" (r b 10–15; Metaph. VII c. 5, definitio per additamentum); "simum est nasus cavus" (r a 28); "3a conclusio: diffinitio et quod quid erat esse ... proprie solis speciebus specialissimis de genere substantie ... sine addito extrinseco; speciebus autem subalternatis non competit diffinitio ... quid incompletum" (v b 1–11; cf. Aquinas l. 4 nn. 6–7 on species of a genus); "compositum ex anima rationali et corpore organico physico" as the example definition of man (r a 7–8).

The question apparatus (numbered objections 2o–4o, "Pro declaratione questionis ... sunt duo premittenda", "2m notabile", "3a conclusio", "Corollarium", "in littera", "ut dicitur ... philosopho") invites comparison with the fifteenth-century Cologne/Louvain arts-faculty commentaries "iuxta doctrinam Thomae" that paraphrase Aquinas's expositio inside quaestiones. Unverified printed candidate: Johannes Versor, *Quaestiones super Metaphysicam Aristotelis* (Cologne, H. Quentell, 1489, 1493, 1494; GW M50002 ff.), Book VII, the question on whether *quod quid erat esse* / definition belongs to substance alone (or to accidents). Also possible: an unprinted Cologne bursa (Montana/Laurentiana) Metaphysics course, or a Louvain reportatio. These remain leads; the source parallel does not establish school, author, or place of copying.

## Ruled out / checked

- Aquinas, *Sententia libri Metaphysicae* VII itself (Corpus Thomisticum cmp07.html, grepped): the fragment quotes it but adds a question structure absent from Aquinas; not the Sententia.
- Google Books API (17 queries; service healthy): no hit for the connecting prose ("diffinitio proprie dicta non convenit accidentibus", "per additum extrinsecum", "sine addito extrinseco", "speciebus subalternatis non competit diffinitio", "solis speciebus specialissimis" + "quod quid erat esse", "pro declaratione questionis" + "quod quid erat esse", "ex anima rationali et corpore organico" + differentia, Versor Metaphysicam + phrases). Only catalogue entries for Versor's Metaphysics questions (Cologne, s.a.) and modern Aquinas editions came back.
- archive.org full-text (7 queries): only Aquinas editions and Scotus for the Aquinas-derived sentences; nothing for the question prose. archive.org advancedsearch for Versor + metaphysic*: no digitized copy.
- Corpus Corporum (paragraph index): "color est quid albedinis" returns only Aquinas, In Metaph.
- Not an Isagoge/Porphyry commentary (initially considered because of genus/differentia/albedo vocabulary): the *quod quid erat esse* abbreviation, the "simus/nasus cavus" example and the verbatim Aquinas In Metaph. VII passages fix the text on Metaph. VII.
- Buridan, Marsilius of Inghen, and Albert of Saxony were not ruled out by a full comparison here. School labels alone cannot exclude an author or a compilation that incorporates Aquinas. Logic-textbook candidates are less directly suggested by the Metaphysics subject matter.
