# F-m4fu: Breviary, *Proprium de sanctis*, 6 September to 16 October, with a bifolium for May and the Ascension

The thirteen paper leaves of this record come from a breviary. A breviary is the book holding the daily Office: psalms, chants, readings and prayers. These leaves are from its sanctorale, the section of saints' days, for September and October, with Matins in the nine-lesson form of secular (non-monastic) use. The twelve single leaves follow the calendar from St Magnus of Füssen (6 September) to St Gallus (16 October), with at least one leaf lost between leaves 2 and 3. Leaf 13 is a bifolium, probably the centre of a gathering, from a spring section: it has May saints (3-25 May) together with temporale texts, the part of the breviary following the church year, here a common of Eastertide and the Vigil and feast of the Ascension. Its layout and script match leaves 1-12, so it probably comes from the same book; how that book's spring section was arranged is not established.

Three of the readings (lessons) were identified and collated:

- the lessons for **St Magnus** abridge the anonymous *Vita S. Magni* (BHL 5162d);
- the lesson on **St Lambert** is from Stephen of Liège's *Vita S. Lamberti*;
- the lesson on the **Theban Legion** (St Maurice and companions) is from Eucherius of Lyon's *Passio Acaunensium martyrum*;
- a lesson for **St Remigius** (1 October) is from Hincmar of Reims's *Vita Remigii*.

The Office of the **Nativity of the Virgin** (8 September) and an Office of **St Ghislain** (9 October) were also placed.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-m4fu) describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 84 (collection "Stams (Stealth Published)", online since 18 September 2021): 13 paper items, 360 x 293 mm as measured on item 84_13, with the title "x" and no further description. The leaves carry modern pencil notes in their margins giving calendar dates and some saints' names. Examples: "6.IX - S. Magni" on 1r, "S. Mathei apli et ewang. 21.IX" on 5r, "Remigius 1.X" on 9r and "S. Gallus 16.X" on 11v and 12v; leaf 13 has "3.V" to "25.V". These notes fit the content as read. The record itself names neither the book type nor any text.

- **Identification:** identified, high, as a breviary sanctorale for September and October; the lesson sources below are identified with high confidence.
  - The book type rests on the rubrics: Ad invitatorium, In ii/iii nocturno, An(tiphona), Ps(almus), R(esponsorium), V(ersus), Cap(itulum), Ymnus, Oratio, Ad laudes, Ad horas, and numbered lessons ("lc vi"). With three psalms to a nocturn and nine lessons, the Matins is secular rather than monastic.
  - **Magnus lessons (leaf 1r):** they follow the wording and sequence of BHL 5162d, which opens "Diem istum exultabilem reddit nobis grata festivitas". That includes its prologue, the chapter "Beatus vir iste" and the episode of the bear and the apples. The longer Magnus vita printed in the *Acta Sanctorum* (BHL 5163) tells the bear episode in entirely different words and lacks the prologue, so it is not the source of these lessons. The two texts do share the account of the saint's illness and death, which is on leaf 1v.
  - **Abridgement:** the lessons shorten and reword their sources throughout. Examples: Magnus "militem suum" for "militem sanctum"; Lambert "dispensatione" for "dispositione"; Eucherius "et fide nobiliores" for "sed nobiliores fide", with a gospel clause left out.
  - **Use and region:** not determined. Proper lessons for Magnus, Gallus and Ghislain suggest southern Germany or the Lake Constance area, but this was not tested.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-m4fu/readings/stams-2026-10-31-image-only.json) covers leaves 1 and 2 only. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-m4fu/readings/stams-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json) holds the lines relied on here.
  - A research reader reread these lines on the images: leaf 1r column a in full, the lower two-thirds of 1r column b, 1v column a from the sixth lesson onward, the tops of both columns of 3r, the top of 6r column b and the top of 11r column a.
  - Everything said of leaves 2r-2v and of 1v column b rests on the first reading, checked only against the chant texts.
  - Leaf 9r col. b (Remigius) was read by a second research reader on the image before and after consulting the source; its lines are given in the table below, not in the assisted file.
  - The other lessons of leaves 4-5, 7-10, 12 and 13 were not read; those leaves were placed from their headings, rubrics read at overview size, and the pencil notes.
  - Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the manuscript images with sources as follows. Page images were used for every collation except the Magnus comparison with Coens' edition.
  - **Eucherius (leaf 6r):** collated with page images of Krusch's edition (MGH SRM III, pp. 33-34).
  - **Magnus death narrative (leaf 1v):** collated with page images of the *Acta Sanctorum*, September vol. II (1748), p. 755.
  - **Lambert (leaf 3r):** collated with page images of Stephen of Liège in PL 132, col. 645.
  - **Magnus, rest of the comparison:** Coens' edition of BHL 5162d (*Analecta Bollandiana* 81, 1963) was seen **only in Google Books snippets**, and no page image of it was reachable. The comparison of leaf 1r with BHL 5162d therefore rests on snippet text.
  - **Nativity chants:** placed by their standard texts in Cantus Index (a database of liturgical chant), not by comparison with another manuscript.
  - **Remigius (leaf 9r):** a second research reader read 9r col. b on the image, first without the source, and collated it with page images of Hincmar's *Vita Remigii* in Krusch's edition (MGH SRM III, p. 260). The lesson is on canvas 8, native boxes 5350,1800,6700,2500 and 5350,2420,6700,3120 ([page image of p. 260](https://archive.org/details/passionesvitaequesanctorum1/page/260/mode/1up)). It is Hincmar c. 1, abridged at its end, with variants such as "ista conferentibus" and "serenioribus", an added second "dominus", and the psalm quotation brought toward the Vulgate. That reader knew from the report that the leaf is Remigius, but not the source text.
  - **St Ghislain (leaf 11r):** placed from its lesson, which names the saint; the vita it draws on was not identified.

  - Gaps: the lessons of most leaves are unread; leaf 13 is probably from the same book (matching layout and script), but its section and the arrangement of the book are open, the use is not measured, and Coens' text was not seen on the page.

**Differences.** The lessons shorten and adjust their sources, as an Office book commonly does. Material differences against the texts compared:

- **Eucherius:** word order "et fide nobiliores". The gospel clause ("Euangelici ... reddebant") is replaced by "queque". Krusch's apparatus reports no manuscript with either reading.
- **Magnus against Coens:** suum/sanctum; a possible added "sublimare"; "cuiuscunque" for "si cuiquam"; "sectatur in pigro(?)" for "calcavit vel impigro"; "sicque" for "factus"; huius/his; paruit/paret; itaque/ergo; "accersiuitque illum ut" for "et accersivit eum, ut acceleraret atque".
- **Magnus against the *Acta Sanctorum*:** the bishop Tozzo is unnamed; "per dies sedecim laborans" is left out; "conspicis" for "perspicis"; "non desinas auxiliari" for "non desinas opem tuae intercessionis adhibere"; "domino et uenerabili" for "Deo & venerando". At "fidissimum" the manuscript agrees with Coens against the *Acta Sanctorum*'s "fidelissimum".
- **Lambert:** dispensatione/dispositione; docibilissimus/docilissimus. "Docibilissimus" is also the reading of the *Acta Sanctorum* text of the vita, seen in OCR.

The Lambert lesson is preceded by a responsory (a chant sung after a lesson) built from §8 of the same vita: "[...] Estimans pro nichilo omnia preter quod erat eterne salutis gracia. V. Celestibus animo inhiabat sese in holocaustum ... mactabat". Cantus Index lists this text as 602157. Leaf 3r col. a has the Lambert heading "Lamperti epi et mris" and the collect, the prayer of the day: "Deus qui ... in ueneratione beati lamperti martiris tui atque pontificis tribuisti".

**Leaves 1v-2v, Nativity of the Virgin.** These hold the Matins lessons from the Song of Songs (Canticles 4-5) and chants whose standard texts are in Cantus Index. Among them are Regali ex progenie (004591), Dignare me laudare te virgo sacrata (002217), Solem justitiae (007677) and the responsory Beatissimae virginis Mariae ... devotissime celebremus. The leaves also have the oration "Supplicationem servorum tuorum" and the Gospel "Liber generationis" (Matthew 1).

## Leaf order, other items, and limits

The leaf numbers and pencil dates run in calendar order: Magnus and the Nativity on leaves 1-2, Lambert on 3, Matthew on 4-5, Maurice on 5-7, Michael on 8, Remigius on 9 (lesson identified above), Francis to Dionysius on 10, Ghislain on 11, and Gallus on 11-12. The run is not continuous: leaf 2v is still in the Matins of the Nativity of the Virgin (8 September), with the gospel "Liber generationis" and its homily, while 3r begins St Lambert (17 September), so at least one leaf is lost between them. Leaf 1 ends with the start of the Nativity Office and leaf 2 continues its Matins, so leaves 1 and 2 are probably consecutive; this was not checked line by line. Leaves 9v and 10r are continuous: the collect for St Leodegar at the foot of 9v runs on at the top of 10r.

Leaf 13 is a cut-down bifolium; the fold is visible, and 13v is a continuous opening, so it was probably the centre of a gathering. It has collects for May saints (Invention of the Cross, John before the Latin Gate, Gordianus and Epimachus, Nereus and Achilleus, Gangolf, Urban), a rubric for the common of Eastertide ("De communi tempore paschali"), and then, in red, "In vig(ilia) ascensionis d(omi)ni n(ost)ri Iesu xp(ist)i. Sec(undum) Ioh(ann)em" with the gospel "In illo tempore subleuatis Ihesus oculis in celum dixit: Pater venit hora" (John 17:1), an "Omelia Augustini", and the Ascension Office. It therefore mixes the temporale with the saints, unlike leaves 1-12, where no temporale was seen. Its rubrics were read at overview size only. A research reader compared it with leaves 1-12 on the images. The rulers in the photographs show the same scale on both. The text-line spacing is the same, about 5.6 mm (77 px on leaf 13 and 78-79 px on leaves 1 and 6, measured from the ink profile), and the columns are the same width, about 7.6-7.8 cm, with about 38 lines each. Set side by side at full size (leaf 1r col. a, canvas 0, box 4250,1850,5450,2500, against leaf 13v, canvas 13, box 2750,2700,3950,3350), the script is the same kind of gothic hybrid, of the same size and proportions, with the same red rubrics and red-stroked initials; the ink of leaf 13 is darker. The catalogue records all thirteen items as paper; no watermark was examined. This makes it probable that leaf 13 comes from the same book, without showing that it is by the same scribe. Why saints and Eastertide texts stand together on it, whether as a separate section or a different arrangement of the book, is left open.

No claim is made about the date, origin or diocesan use of the breviary, about the text of the unread leaves, or about any value of the lessons as witnesses to the vitae they abridge.
