# F-rqfz: Office lectionary or breviary, summer temporale, Dominica IV (end), V and [IX] post Pentecosten, with homily lessons from Bede, *In Lucae evangelium expositio* II (Lc 5:1-3) and Jerome, *Epistula* 121.6-7

This parchment bifolium (a sheet folded to make two leaves) comes from an Office book, not a missal. It covers the summer Sundays after Pentecost. For each Sunday it gives:
- the gospel opening;
- a patristic homily set out as lessons (readings for Matins);
- the full gospel text;
- the collect (the Sunday prayer);
- the antiphons for the Benedictus and Magnificat.

The leaves hold the following:
- the end of the fourth Sunday: the collect *Protector in te sperantium*, the Benedictus antiphon *Estote ergo misericordes* and *Nolite iudicare*;
- the fifth Sunday ("Dominica v. secundum Lucam", Luke 5:1-11): homily lessons from Bede's commentary on Luke, then Luke 5:1-6 in full;
- a later Sunday on the unjust steward (Luke 16:1-9): homily lessons abridged from Jerome's letter 121 to Algasia, question 6, then Luke 16:1-9 in full, the collect *Largire nobis domine* and the Benedictus antiphon *Dixit dominus villico*. The Sunday is the ninth in the numbering the book follows; its heading is lost.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-rqfz) has these details:
- Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 238, online since 18 September 2021;
- parchment, one fragment item, 380 x 266 mm (measured as 375/380 x 264/266 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), marked as restored;
- title "x", no date and no linked description;
- two images, Stams_STA_Frg_238_01r and _01v.

The record does not name the text. Its physical description fits the images.

- **Identification:** identified, high, as a liturgical placing. The lessons, prayers and chants are assigned to their Sundays and services.
  - **Basis:** the rubrics "Dñ(ic)a v. S(ecundu)m lucam" and "Ad b(enedictus) a(ntiphona)"; the collects; three antiphons confirmed in Cantus Index (002682, 003894, 002291); and two homily lessons collated against page images of their editions.
  - **The homily lessons are abridged.** The Bede lessons leave out most of Bede's sentence on the names of the lake. The Jerome lessons skip §§2-5 and recast Jerome's Greek gloss on *oeconomus* (steward) as "hic autem ethonomus significat qui est dispensator". They also bring the quotation of Luke 15:2 into Vulgate wording ("quia iste peccatores recipit et manducat cum eis" for Jerome's "quare iste peccatores suscipit et comedit cum eis") and omit "fodere non valeo, mendicare erubesco".
  - **Kind of book:** an Office book, not a missal or plenarium. The leaves have no Mass chants, epistles, secrets or postcommunions, and they do have Benedictus antiphons.
  - **Numbering of the Sundays:** the fourth Sunday has *Estote misericordes* (Luke 6) and the fifth has Luke 5. That is the widespread medieval count with Lazarus on the first Sunday, not the Tridentine one.
  - **Use:** not identified. Each homily is followed by the gospel read in full, which the secular Sarum breviary does not do for the ninth Sunday. That suggests monastic Matins (Benedictine or Cistercian), where the gospel is read after the Te Deum. This is an inference only.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-rqfz/readings/stams-2026-10-31-image-only.json) covers all four pages. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-rqfz/readings/stams-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json) is a research reader's reread, at reading size, of:
  - canvas 0: right page lines 1-20 (lines 10-14 with a red-channel enhanced crop) and left page lines 14-20;
  - canvas 1: right page lines 1-20 and left page lines 1-12.

  Other lines rest on the first reading, checked against the overview. The middles of canvas 0 right lines 10-14 and canvas 1 left lines 2-7 are under dense mould spotting; words there are supplied from the source only where marked. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the Bede lessons with Bede's commentary on Luke as printed by Giles, and the Jerome lessons with Hilberg's CSEL edition, both on page images; the antiphons were checked in Cantus Index.
  - **Passage read independently:** canvas 0, left page (leaf 2v), lines 1-20, native boxes 1960,1080,3700,1960, 1960,1880,3700,2780 and 1960,2700,3700,3560, read on the image before the Sarum books were opened. The report had transcribed only lines 14-20 of this page, and the reader had not seen any transcription of lines 1-13.
  - **Sarum check:** a second research reader read leaf 2v lines 1-20 independently and compared them with the Sarum Missal (ed. Dickinson, cols. 485-487, [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/missaleadusumin00cathgoog/page/n251)) and the Sarum Breviary (fasc. 1, cols. mccccxv-xvi, [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/breviariumadusum01cath/page/n765)) on page images.
    - The gospel Luke 16:3-9 agrees apart from spelling, and so do the collect *Largire* and the Benedictus antiphon. Sarum gives all three for the ninth Sunday after Trinity.
    - Sarum, a secular use, reads a Bede homily on this Sunday and gives no full gospel after the lessons. The fragment has Jerome followed by the full gospel. This fits the monastic inference but does not prove it.
  - **Gaps:** the PL 92 and PL 22 page images were not used; the Bede text was also read in the Corpus Corporum transcription of PL. The faint red marginal numerals, probably lesson numbers, and several faded rubrics were not read securely. No printed breviary was compared for the book's own lesson divisions.

**Differences.** Besides the abridgements and the Vulgate-style recasting named above, the lessons have:
- *concursus* for Bede's *conventus*;
- *fide Christi* for *fide*;
- *fluet* for *fluent*;
- *significant* for *figurant*;
- *tue* added after *dispensacionis*, which agrees with the old printed editions (siglum ς in Hilberg's apparatus);
- *neque enim ultra dispensabis* for *neque enim ultra mea poteris dispensare*.

The place of "ergo sive mare" in the Bede lesson is under spotting and was not seen. These are the alterations of a liturgical lesson text. They do not bear on which works are read.

## Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

- Leaf order: canvas 0 is the outer side of the bifolium (left page = leaf 2 verso, right page = leaf 1 recto) and canvas 1 the inner side (left = 1v, right = 2r).
  - 1r runs on to 1v ("de quibus | ysaias"), and 2r runs on to 2v ("Quid faciam | quia dominus meus aufert").
  - 1v does not run on to 2r: it jumps from the fifth Sunday to the steward Sunday. So the bifolium is not the centre of its quire (gathering), and the leaves for the sixth to eighth Sundays are lost between the two leaves.
- One column of 20 lines, formal gothic textualis, red rubrics, red and blue initials. Heavy mould spotting covers parts of 1r and 1v. Faint red numerals in the outer margins sit beside new lessons.
- Not claimed: the date (the hand is consistent with the fifteenth century), the origin, the religious order or use, or any relation to a surviving manuscript.
