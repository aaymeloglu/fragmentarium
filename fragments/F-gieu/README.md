# F-gieu: Breviary, Kings Sundays after Pentecost, Decollation of St John the Baptist (Peter Chrysologus, *Sermo* 127) and Nativity of the Virgin (Ps.-Augustine, *Sermo* 194; Paschasius Radbertus, *Cogitis me*)

Three parchment bifolia at Stams come from a breviary, the book that holds the whole daily Office with its readings and chants. The office has eight lessons in the first two nocturns, as in monastic use.

- The first bifolium has Sunday lessons from the Books of Kings, read in the weeks after Pentecost. One leaf has 1 Kings (1 Samuel) 1:10-17, with the responsories *Montes Gelboe* and *Exaudisti domine*. The other has 3 Kings 1, then the heading "Dominica viii." and 3 Kings 8:1-7.
- The second bifolium is for the Decollation (beheading) of St John the Baptist, 29 August. Matins lesson ii is Peter Chrysologus, Sermon 127 (PL 52, 549). After the lessons come the Gospel Mark 6:22-29 written out, the collect, and the antiphons of Lauds and the day hours: *Herodes enim tenuit*, *Misso Herodes spiculatore* and *Puellae saltanti*.
- The third bifolium has the Nativity of the Virgin, 8 September. Lessons v-viii are the Ps.-Augustinian Sermon 194, *Adest nobis dilectissimi* (PL 39, 2105-2106), with the feast's responsories *Gloriosae virginis Mariae ortum*, *Corde et animo* and *Regali ex progenie*. The leaf joined to it has lessons i-vii from *Cogitis me* §16 (printed as Ps.-Jerome, Epistle 9, PL 30, 139-140), the treatise on the Assumption now given to Paschasius Radbertus. These lessons run on without a break from the recto to the verso.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-gieu) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 70: parchment, three fragment items, 346 x 220 mm (measured on Frg. 70_1 as 338/346 x 216/220 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", online since 18 September 2021, with no date or linked description and four images. The record does not name the text. The leaves carry old blue labels "IV. 44", "IV. 45" and "IV. 46", and later pen trials over two pages. The physical description fits the images.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for the lessons and chants named above. The basis is sustained agreement in wording with three patristic texts read on page images, and with the standard texts of the chants in Cantus Index. Lesson ii of the Decollation agrees with Chrysologus over its six lines. The Nativity leaf agrees with Sermon 194 over lessons v-viii. The joined leaf agrees with *Cogitis me* §16 in about eighteen line-beginnings on the recto, and its verso continues the same section in sequence. The Kings lessons were recognised as the Vulgate text but not collated against an edition. Sermon 194 is abridged for the Office: lesson vii passes straight from the Magnificat verse to *O beata Maria*, leaving out about twenty lines of the printed text. Other variants: *Percussit illa* (PL *Illa percussit*); *Leta maria gestat* (PL *Laeta igitur Maria*); *amplectitur* (PL *amplexatur*); *Audite ergo ... cantauit* (PL *Audite igitur ... cantaverit*); and in Chrysologus, *carcerem* (PL *carcere*). The *Cogitis me* lessons turn the letter's address to Paula and Eustochium into a general one (*dilectissimi*, *nobis*). On the verso the leaf reads *que quasi* where PL has *in qua velut*.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-gieu/readings/stams-2026-10-31-image-only.json) covers all faces. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-gieu/readings/stams-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json) is a research reader's reread at reading size of these pages: canvas 1, both upper pages in full and the last nine lines of the lower right page; canvas 0, the lower right page; canvas 2, both pages; canvas 3, the lower half of the right page and lines 17-25 of the left page. Other lines rest on the first reading. Several pages are faded, stripped, stained or overwritten and were not read: most of canvas 0 upper left, canvas 0 upper right, canvas 0 lower left, canvas 1 lower left, and canvas 3, lines 1-16 of the left page and the upper right page. The chapter after the Decollation Lauds antiphon is unread. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the reread lines with PL 52, cols. 549-550 (page image n276), PL 39, cols. 2105-2106 (page image n310) and PL 30, cols. 139-140 (page image n73), on Internet Archive scans of the Migne volumes. The same reader looked up the chants with Cantus Index. A second research reader read the whole of the canvas 3 (70_3v) left page, native box 640,2250,1800,4600, before opening PL 30 ([page image](https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecursu0030jpmi/page/n73/mode/1up)); lines 18-25 overlap a passage the report had quoted, so they were not read fully blind. That reader matched lines 4-5, 8-12 and 17-25 in sequence to PL 30, 139 D - 140 B, on the same page image; lines 1-3, 6, 13 and 15-16 are too stained to compare. The Kings lessons were not collated against an edition of the Bible. Not consulted: Olivar's CCSL edition of Chrysologus, Ripberger's CCCM edition of *Cogitis me*, and any medieval breviary on the page.

The differences from PL 39 and PL 52 are those listed under Identification, together with ordinary spellings (*penam*, *nuncio*, *pudicicie*). The *Cogitis me* lessons adapt the letter's address. They read *que quasi* for PL *in qua velut*, perhaps *quod* for *quid* before *respuere* (the abbreviation is ambiguous), and *exigit?* for PL *erigitur* (an uncertain reading). None of these bears on the identification. The *Gloriosae virginis* responsory reads *recolamus ... respexit ... nuntiante ... redemptorem*. Cantus Index records that exact form in Bohemian, Moravian, Austrian, Slovak, south German and French books (Prague VI E 13, Rajhrad R 17, Vienna Cod. 1799** and 1890, Spiš, Karlsruhe Aug. LX, Cambrai 38), so it does not point to one region.

## Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

- Single column, red and blue pen-flourished initials, red rubrics, no music. In each bifolium the text fills only the upper part of the page.
- In the Kings bifolium the leaf with 1 Samuel 1 (canvas 1 upper right, then canvas 0 upper left) comes before the leaf with 3 Kings 1 and 8 (canvas 0 upper right, then canvas 1 upper left). In the Decollation bifolium, canvas 0 lower right (lesson ii) has lessons iii-v on its back (canvas 1 lower left). Canvas 1 lower right (Gospel, Lauds, Terce, Sext) has on its back the hours *ad iii*, *ad vi*, *ad ix* and Second Vespers (canvas 0 lower left), which were not read.
- On the Marian bifolium the Nativity leaf runs from canvas 3 right (lessons v-vi) to canvas 2 left (lessons vii-viii). The *Cogitis me* leaf runs from canvas 2 right (lessons i-iv, under a rubric for the first nocturn) to canvas 3 left (lessons v-vii). It cannot be settled which of the two leaves comes first in the quire. So it is open whether the *Cogitis me* lessons belong to the octave of the Assumption, the more usual place for this text, or to that of the Nativity.
- Eight lessons in the first two nocturns, the Gospel written out after the Matins lessons, and Lauds antiphons reused at the day hours fit monastic use. Stams is a Cistercian house, so the book was probably Cistercian, but this is inference and has not been tested against a Cistercian breviary.
- Not claimed: the date, the origin, or any relation to a surviving manuscript.
