# F-h8oz: Bernardus Compostellanus junior, Lectura super primo libro Decretalium, commentary on Innocent IV's constitution *Statuimus* (appeals against elections and provisions)

This parchment leaf in Oudenaarde carries part of the commentary of Bernardus Compostellanus junior (died 1267), papal chaplain, on the first book of the Decretals of Gregory IX. The passage on both sides comments Innocent IV's constitution *Statuimus*, which the commentary treats under the title *De electione* and which later stood in the Liber Sextus in the same title. The constitution regulates appeals to the pope against elections and provisions: the appellant must set out for the curia within a month and the party who arrives waits twenty days for the other, after which the case may proceed with the other party absent. The leaf discusses what each party must prove before the court proceeds in the other's absence, how the appeal is proved (instruments, witnesses, letters of executors, the procurator's letters), and why parties who did not appeal must still come to defend their rights.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-h8oz) is Oudenaarde, Stadsarchief, Perkamenten nr. 56, catalogued as "Unidentified canon law text", 14th century, 292 x 199 mm, two columns.

- **Identification:** identified, high. Sustained agreement in wording and sequence across both sides with two manuscripts of the work, read on their page images, and with the printed text, Venice 1588, fols. 204v-205r, also read on page images. Witnesses: Arras, Médiathèque de l'Abbaye Saint-Vaast, Ms 590 (CGM 508), fol. 66v; Reims, Bibliothèque municipale (Carnegie), Ms 708, fols. 91r-v and 92v. The case of the auditor obtained "per principalem aut per procuratorem", with its model mandate to "magistro B. Hispano", is in both manuscripts and in the fragment but not in the 1588 print, which passes straight from "ff. de reg. iur. cum par." to "Si autem solus appellatus est presens". Durantis, *Speculum iudiciale* II.3, *De appellationibus*, n. 14, has the same case reworded with his own name in the mandate; n. 15 then continues differently, while the fragment continues with Bernardus. The Durantis passage is a parallel, not the fragment's text.
- **Transcription:** source-assisted, selected lines. A research reader (Claude) reread on the native images lines 1-16 and about 25 further lines of the column headed fol. [1]v a, the first six lines of [1]v b, and about 16 lines of [1]r b, with both manuscripts and the print available. The rest of each side, including all of [1]r a, was not transcribed. Several readings remain uncertain and are marked. Accuracy has not been measured. The preserved source-assisted record of September 2026 reads "an" at [1]v a line 3 and expands "ap." at line 12 as *appellans*; the image has "aut", and both manuscripts and the following sentence support *ap(pellatus)*.
- **Verification:** a research reader (Claude) compared the fragment lines on the fragment's images with the Arras and Reims page images and with the 1588 print page images (Google Books, full view). The HTR of CoMMA was used only to locate the passages. Arras was checked on the image for the first passage only; its readings for the other passages come from CoMMA's machine text and are labelled.

The fragment's "ipse" at line 12 is in neither manuscript nor the print. In line 10 the initial after "mag(ist)ro" is a clear "b." followed by "[h]isp(a)no"; Arras has ".b. hyspano", Reims "b(er)nardo hyspano". Innocent IV's registers name a papal chaplain "magistro Bernardo Hispano capellano suo, decano Ulixbonensi" (É. Berger, *Les registres d'Innocent IV*, Google Books snippet). Bernardus junior was a papal chaplain, so the model mandate probably names the author; that identification of the chaplain was not checked further. Durantis gives the same mandate to "Guilielmo Durandi". Whether Durantis took the case from Bernardus was not examined.

### Witness pattern

The fragment sides with Arras in most variants checked ("per .xx. dies partem adversam", "ex causa legitima", "per testes per litteras", "si sufficiat"), with Arras and the print against Reims in "non appellantes", with both manuscripts against the print in carrying the auditor case and the *moris* sentence, and with the print alone against both manuscripts in lacking "tucius tamen ... iuramentum". It has an extra "ipse" at [1]v a line 12. On these few passages it belongs to none of the three witnesses exactly. No recension claim follows.

## Limits

- [1]r column a and most of both columns of [1]r and of [1]v b were not transcribed; [1]r is heavily abraded and its lower edge is torn.
- Arras was read on the image for the [1]v a lines only. Its readings for the other passages are machine HTR, marked [HTR].
- The comparison with the print used Google Books page images. The edition's treatment of the auditor case (absent) was checked on fol. 204v a; other omissions in the print were noticed only where they fell inside the compared lines.
- Kuttner's and Kessler's studies of the Novellae commentaries, Bertram's manuscript lists, and the CGM entries for Arras 590 and Reims 708 were not read; the attribution rests on the print, the Reims catalogue description and Tierney.
- The catalogue's 14th-century date was not examined.
