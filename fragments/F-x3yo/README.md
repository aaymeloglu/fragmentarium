# F-x3yo: Canons regular of St Leonard near Liège, contents leaf of a composite quarto volume (Gregory, *Dialogi*, and nine other pieces), dispersed before 1884

The leaf is the ownership and contents leaf of a composite volume, that is, several separately produced prints and manuscripts bound together (a Sammelband), which belonged to the Augustinian canons regular of St Leonard outside Liège. Five of its ten entries are matched to a work or an edition. Four of the pieces were in the collection of François Vergauwen of Ghent and were sold separately in 1884. The first, Gregory's *Dialogi* in the Louvain edition of Johannes de Westfalia, is now Den Haag, Koninklijke Bibliotheek, KW 169 G 7, and still carries the priory's ownership note. The ninth, a manuscript Life of St Gerard of Brogne, is now Brussels, KBR, ms. II 734. The printed pieces date the list after 20 February 1492, probably not before 1493.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-x3yo) describes a paper leaf, Gent, Bisschoppelijk Archief, fragment nr. 17, 204 x 140 mm, dated 1476-1500, with the title "Table of contents of a lost or unidentified book". The record already says that the table points to a composite volume containing printed material, links the second and sixth entries to incunable editions (books printed before 1501), and notes that none of the 23 surviving St Leonard manuscripts identified by A. Derolez matches the table. That description fits. What is added here is the edition of the third entry, the work of the ninth, the two located pieces, and the route by which the volume was broken up.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for what the leaf is and for the editions of entries 1, 2, 3 and 6 and the work of entry 9. The reconstruction of the volume rests on converging evidence, not on a physical join: entries 1, 2, 6 and 9 all appear as separate lots in the 1884 Vergauwen sale; the manuscript of entry 9 has the leaf's title word for word and the leaf's format; the Gregory has the priory's inscription on its last leaf; lots 119 and 164 share one binding description. The inscription formula was the priory's standard one and occurs in other books, so on its own it would not tie the Hague copy to this list. No one has compared paper, edges or sewing of the pieces with the leaf. Entries 4, 5, 7 and 10 are not matched to any work; entry 8 is matched to a title only.
- **Transcription:** [source-assisted selected reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-x3yo/readings/batch-2026-10-12b-source-assisted.json) of the whole leaf; [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-x3yo/readings/batch-2026-10-12b-image-only.json). A research reader reread all twelve written lines on the image. The first reading's "in deserto" and "in thoro" in entry 4 are corrected to "in refectorio" and "in choro", and its doubtful words in entry 2 are settled from the printed title. The abbreviated words before "generali" in entry 10 and the numeral after "francorum" in entry 3 remain uncertain. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the leaf's entries with catalogue records (ISTC, the Incunabula Short Title Catalogue; GW, the Gesamtkatalog der Wiegendrucke; MEI, the CERL database of copy-specific evidence in incunabula), with page images of the 1884 sale catalogue for lots 119 and 164, with OCR text of that catalogue for lots 128 and 469, with OCR text of the Brussels manuscript catalogue, and with the title-page image of the Munich copy of the Cologne edition of entry 3. The coordinator read the heading and entries 1, 2, 3, 6 and 9 on the image of the leaf and found them as transcribed below; the coordinator did not see the sale catalogue or the catalogue records. The inscription in the Hague copy and the Brussels manuscript are known from catalogue descriptions only; neither book was seen. No human review.

Differences between the leaf and the sources: the leaf describes entry 3 in its own words ("in quibus repudiatur filia regis romanorum per karolum regem francorum") where the title page has "quibus ... datur repudiatio filie Regis Romanorum Maximiliani per Regem Francorum Carolum facta"; the other four editions of this exchange are catalogued by GW and ISTC under the title "Disceptatio", and their title pages were not looked at. For entry 1 the edition is fixed by the surviving copy, not by the wording; six Latin editions of the *Dialogi* before 1501 are quartos.

## Limits and what is not claimed

The house inscription "Liber fratrum Regularium in sancto Leonardo iuxta Leodium" occurs, with small variants, in other books from the priory (for example Oxford, Bodleian Library, Inc. d. N3.1, and Den Haag, Huis van het Boek, 003 B 002, both recorded in MEI), so the Hague Gregory is tied to this list by the combination of circumstances above, not by the inscription alone. The MEI record of the Hague copy mentions a nineteenth-century half-leather binding probably made for Vergauwen and no old foliation or companion pieces. Whether the Cambridge Beissel and the unlocated *Tabulare* are the St Leonard copies is an inference from the sale. The leaf's wording does not say which entries were prints and which manuscripts. An earlier listing of the leaf as a contents list of a St Leonard book is in *Monasticon Windeshemense*, part 1, Belgien (1976), pp. 133-134 (seen in snippet only); Derolez's *Corpus catalogorum Belgii* was not consulted. No claim is made about the hand or about where the leaf sat in the volume.
