# F-5wen: Hugh of Saint-Cher, *Postilla super Ieremiam*, on Jer 49:27-50:16 and Jer 50 end-51:16

Antwerpen, FelixArchief, 2201 # 42 is two bifolia (four leaves) from a copy of the postill, the running commentary, on Jeremiah that is printed under the name of the Dominican cardinal Hugh of Saint-Cher (d. 1263). The leaves expound each verse first in its literal sense and then "mistice", in its spiritual sense, with underlined lemmata (the words of the biblical text being explained) and chains of supporting Bible citations. Fols [1]-[2] carry the exposition of Jer 49:27 to 50:16, including the opening of chapter 50 and its division into eleven parts; fols [3]-[4] carry the end of chapter 50, the division of chapter 51 and the exposition of Jer 51:1-16. Four passages on three of the leaves were compared with the Lyon edition of 1669 and agree with it in wording and order.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-5wen) describes the item as "Commentary on Jeremiah", subtitle "Jeremiah 49-51": two trimmed parchment bifolia from one quire, leaf 309 x 244 mm, two columns of 51 lines, in a Northern Textualis, used as pastedowns in the Antwerp beer-assize register for May to August 1561 (FelixArchief T # 1441). The record dates the leaves 1251-1300; its linked description says 14th century. It assigns fol. [1] to Jeremiah 49, fol. [2] to Jeremiah 50 and fols [3]-[4] to Jeremiah 51. That description fits. It names no author or work.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence. The basis is agreement in the commentary's own prose, not in the Bible text or in shared quotations: the mystical reading of the four winds (Jer 49:36), the opening of chapter 50 with its division into eleven parts, each marked by "ibi" and the opening words of the part, the literal and mystical exposition of Jer 50:6-7, and the mystical exposition of "ululate" (Jer 51:8). The book is fixed by the leaf itself: the lemma "Verbum quod locutus est" (Jer 50:1) stands beside a marginal "l. cap." (chapter 50) and is followed by "expleto uaticinio contra egiptios moabitas amonitas", so a commentary on another prophet is not in question. Against the print the leaves lack a few words ("alios", "et calca", "vel mitigandum"), read "memorans" for "commemorans" and "dominum" for "Deum", and give lemmata in abbreviated form. Whether the leaves carry the postill in the form printed throughout, or a shortened or otherwise variant form of it, is not settled by four passages. An earlier commentary that the postill drew on was not searched for; the comparison shows agreement with the postill as printed and nothing about its sources.

The leaves and the print differ in small ways. The manuscript writes lemmata as catchwords ("cubi. sui") and cites chapters without the print's letter subdivisions. In B it has no "alios" before "predictos", "captiuauerant" for "captiuauerunt", "de destructione" where the print has "destructione", "uastatione" for "vastationem", no "et" seen before "captiuitatem", "memorans" for "commemorans", and "dominum" for "Deum" in the seventh part; the count of parts is written "xi". In C it has no "et calca" after "intra in lutum". In D it has no "vel mitigandum". The order of matter is the same in both: literal exposition, then the mystical, then the division of the next chapter. Row D's print column is corrected from the scan's machine-read text, where the chapter number of the cross-reference is illegible.

The edition is *Hugonis de Sancto Charo ... Tomus quartus, In libros Prophetarum Isaiae, Ieremiae et eiusdem Threnorum, Baruch* (Lyon, 1669), in the Internet Archive scan [bub_gb_78lz_oucS0QC](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_78lz_oucS0QC): [fol. 273r](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_78lz_oucS0QC/page/n542/mode/1up) for A and B, [fol. 273v](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_78lz_oucS0QC/page/n543/mode/1up) for C. The attribution to Hugh rests here on the edition's title page; the postill on the whole Bible has long circulated under his name. The repertory entry for the Jeremiah postill (Stegmuller's *Repertorium biblicum*) and the scholarship on how the postill was compiled were not consulted.

## Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

The text runs on from fol. [1]v to fol. [2]r (Jer 50:4) and, on the first reading, from fol. [3]v to fol. [4]r (Jer 51:7-8), so [1]-[2] and [3]-[4] are consecutive pairs, as the catalogue's foliation has them. Between fol. [2]v, which on the first reading ends at Jer 50:16, and fol. [3]r, the exposition of Jer 50:16-44 is missing, so the two bifolia were not adjacent in the quire. How many bifolia lay between them was not worked out.

The images also show a modern paper slip marked "1561-1", the name of Johannes Maechs written three times on fol. [2]v, and later pen flourishes and checkerboard patterns on fol. [1]. These belong to the leaves' use in the 1561 register and date that use, not the copy.

Not claimed: a date or place of origin for the copy beyond the catalogue's; which form or recension of the postill the leaves carry; any relation to other copies or to the printed edition; that the leaves are an unrecorded witness.
