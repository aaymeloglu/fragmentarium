# F-p8ik: Bible, *Psalterium Gallicanum*, Psalms 36:40 - 37:20

These two parchment strips in Stams are cut from one leaf of a Latin psalter, or of a book with the psalms written out, in the Gallican text (Jerome's revision of the psalter, which became the psalter of the Vulgate). One side of the leaf carries Psalms 36:40 to 37:9 and the other side Psalms 37:12 to 37:20, in Vulgate numbering. The strips are horizontal cuttings, and strip 2 lay directly below strip 1. On the evidence of the text and of a red initial cut through between them, the side of the leaf with the earlier text is made up of the faces labelled 1r and 2v, so the recto and verso labels of strip 2 are the reverse of the leaf's own.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-p8ik) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 167: parchment, two fragment items, 114 x 44 mm (measured on Frg. 167_1 as 113/114 x 42/44 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description, with two images labelled 167_01r02r and 167_01v02v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: two strips of about 11 x 4 cm, each photographed on both faces, one above the other. Each image groups the faces by their pencil labels ("167-1r" over "167-2r" on canvas 0, "167-1v" over "167-2v" on canvas 1), and so shows one strip's recto with the other strip's verso of the leaf.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for the text and its type. The basis is continuous agreement with the Gallican text of Psalms 36:40-37:20 over all 28 lines, and agreement with the Gallican wherever it differs from the Roman psalter (the older Latin version used at Rome) in this stretch: 37:5 "supergresse sunt" (Roman "superposuerunt"), 37:6 "Putruerunt et corrupte sunt" ("Conputruerunt et deterioraverunt"), 37:7 "Miser factus" ("Miseriis afflictus"), 37:8 "lumbi mei impleti sunt illusionibus" ("anima mea completa est inlusionibus"), 37:9 "Afflictus", 37:13 "inquirebant", 37:15 "redargutiones" ("increpationes"), 37:16 "tu exaudies" ("dixi tu exaudies"), 37:17 "nequando" ("ne aliquando") and 37:18 "in flagella ... in conspectu meo semper" ("ad flagella ... ante me est semper"). The Ambrosian psalter, the psalter of the Milanese rite, also reads "anima mea completa est" at 37:8 and is excluded on that verse. Jerome's psalter from the Hebrew was not checked on a source page. The copy differs from the Gallican in omitting "sum" after "Miser factus" at 37:7, where the line is undamaged; whether the second "sum" of 37:9 is written is uncertain. Its "in carne mea" (37:4), "usque in finem" (37:7), "paratus sum" (37:18) and "uiuunt" (37:20), against the readings of Weber's Stuttgart text, are common medieval Gallican readings. Whether the book was a psalter, a breviary or another office book is not established.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-p8ik/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-image-only.json) of 31 October 2026 and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-p8ik/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json). A research reader reread all 28 lines on the native images. An independent checker reread the 14 lines on canvas 1 (the faces labelled 1v and 2v) and corrected 37:7, where "sum" is not written. The faded upright writing in the right margin of the faces labelled 1r and 2v is unread. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared all 28 lines with the Gallican text in an online transcription of the Stuttgart Vulgate, and the Roman readings with the OCR text of the Roman column of the Eadwine Psalter (Harsley's edition) and of Mozley's note on 37:8; no page images of those two editions were used. An independent checker read canvas 1, both strips, native boxes 3100,1350,1600,670 (face labelled 1v) and 3100,2580,1650,660 (face labelled 2v), on the image before opening a source, though it had read the report's transcription, and compared them with the parallel Gallican and Roman psalters of Migne's *Patrologia Latina* 28, cols. 183-186, on page images ([cols. 183-184](https://archive.org/details/patrologiae0000unse_t5x3/page/n91/mode/1up), [cols. 185-186](https://archive.org/details/patrologiae0000unse_t5x3/page/n92/mode/1up)). Psalms 37:5-9 and 37:12-16 agree with the Gallican column throughout, against the Roman wherever the two differ, apart from the omitted "sum" at 37:7. The checker also confirmed on the images that the words broken across the cut run from the face labelled 1r into 2v ("super|gresse", 37:5) and from 1v into 2r ("exau|dies", 37:16), found the tail of the red "Q" of 37:5 on 1r line 7 cut through and continuing at the top edge of 2v, and found the marginal writing running on from 1r into 2v only. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

## Leaf order, other items, and limits

The faces labelled 1r and 2v are one side of the leaf, with 2v directly below 1r, and the faces labelled 1v and 2r are the other side, with 2r below 1v. Three things show it: the words broken across the cut, the tail of the red "Q" on 1r line 7 continuing at the top edge of 2v, and the faded marginal writing that runs on from 1r into 2v. The side with 1r and 2v carries the earlier text and is the recto. Between the foot of 2v and the head of 1v about 6 to 7 lines are lost (Ps 37:9b-12a, "nimis rugiebam ... et qui iuxta"), so the page had at least about 21 lines in a single column. The pencil labels may be face labels for each strip made before the text was known, and not a claim about the leaf; the label on the canvas 1 upper strip is damaged and is read as "1v".

Other items: the faded, upright writing in the right margin of the faces labelled 1r and 2v is not offset from another page; only scraps are legible ("...eorum? / me .. / salu[..]"), and it is unread. It may be marginal notes or the edge of a neighbouring text.

Not claimed: the kind of book (Psalm 37 follows 36:40 with no antiphon, rubric or doxology visible, which fits a psalter, but an office book is not excluded on 28 lines and no liturgical use is claimed), the date and origin of the leaf, its page size, and its place among Gallican psalters. Weber's critical apparatus and his edition of the Roman psalter were not consulted.
