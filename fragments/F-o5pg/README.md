# F-o5pg: Peter Comestor, *Sermones*, end of Sermo XIV (*Laetare*) and the Palm Sunday sermon *Apprehendent septem mulieres*

This parchment strip, Stams Frg. 123, comes from a two-column copy of sermons by Peter Comestor (Petrus Manducator, died about 1178), the Paris master whose surname "Manducator" ("the eater") the manuscript itself uses. One face has the passage on the fourth and sixth temptations from his Sermo XIV for Laetare Sunday (the fourth Sunday of Lent). The other face has the close of that sermon, then a red heading (rubric) *Sermo Manduc(atoris)* / *In ramis palmarum* and the next sermon, for Palm Sunday, on the theme (opening Bible verse) *Apprehendent septem mulieres virum unum* (Isa. 4:1). Sermo XIV is printed in Migne's *Patrologia Latina* (PL) 198, cols. 1763-1764. The Palm Sunday sermon is printed among the works of Hildebert of Le Mans, PL 171, cols. 475-477, and PL 198 lists it as Comestor's Sermo XV, directly after Sermo XIV. At the one place checked closely the manuscript carries a fuller text of the Palm Sunday sermon than the printing.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-o5pg) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 123: parchment, 256 x 87 mm (measured as 208/256 x 6/87 mm by Claudia Sojer), title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description, with two images labelled 01r and 01v. Its reconstruction summary refers to "Reconstruction F-dr4l of Stams, STA, Frg. 123+142", joining this strip with Frg. 142 ([F-vjii](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-vjii)); that reconstruction is not public. The description fits the images. The record does not name the text. A modern pencil note on the second image, "Einband zu ... II/[6] 34 (anno 1586?)", records the host binding; the volume is not identified.

- **Identification:** identified, high, as Peter Comestor, the end of Sermo XIV, *In dominica Laetare* (PL 198:1763-1764), followed by the Palm Sunday sermon *Apprehendent septem mulieres* (PL 171:475-476; Comestor's Sermo XV in PL 198). The basis is sustained agreement in wording and order with the printed text in four columns on two faces: the end of the fourth temptation, the end of the sixth (with Boethius and the Psalmist), the explicit (closing words) of Sermo XIV, and the theme and an early passage of the Palm Sunday sermon. The rubric *Sermo Manduc(atoris)* names Comestor independently of the text match; the manuscript writes *Manduc'* with a general abbreviation stroke, and the expansion *-atoris* is editorial. PL 198 places Sermo XV, *In die Palmarum*, directly after Sermo XIV, the same sequence as on the strip. J. B. Schneyer's *Repertorium der lateinischen Sermones des Mittelalters* IV, under Hildebert, cross-refers the incipit to Petrus Comestor; this was seen only in archive.org OCR text. Hildebert of Le Mans is the alternative author under whose name PL prints the second sermon; the rubric and the PL 198 series favour Comestor. Differences of wording: the manuscript has a closing doxology to Sermo XIV that PL 198 lacks, continues the Palm Sunday theme to "aufer opprobrium nostrum", and has about one and a half lines more than PL 171 before "uigilanter dic(it)" (PL "Unde vigilanter dixit"). It is a fuller text than the printing from an Angers manuscript in PL 171.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-o5pg/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-image-only.json) and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-o5pg/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json). A research reader reread on the native images the two full columns of face A (five lines each), the left and middle columns of face B (seven lines each) and the rubric, with the editions in hand. An independent checker read the rubric at four times magnification, the two narrow outer blocks (five and seven partial lines) before opening any source, and lines 6-7 of the middle column of face B with PL 171 in hand. Line 7 of that column is only partly read, and the outer blocks are fragments of words. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared face A and the end of Sermo XIV with the page images of PL 198, cols. 1761-1764, and the Palm Sunday sermon with the page image of PL 171, cols. 475-476. An independent checker read the rubric on canvas 1, native box 1850,3560,630,260 (the word alone at 2100,3580,270,120), and found *Sermo Manduc'* with an open *c*, not *e* or *t*; it had seen the proposed expansion before looking. It then read lines 6-7 of face B's middle column (canvas 1, native box 2440,3680,1040,310) and compared them with the page image of PL 171, col. 476 ([archive.org, leaf n237](https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_1854_171/page/n237/mode/1up)): the manuscript agrees to "quarum .vii. apprehendunt unam", then has about a line and a half not in PL, mentioning *octaua*, before "uigilan(ter) dic(it)". It read the two outer blocks blind (canvas 0, native box 1100,3260,400,720; canvas 1, native box 3780,3300,420,680) and compared them with PL 171, cols. 475-478 (page images n237-n238 and OCR) and PL 198 Sermo XIV (OCR): neither block matched. On the page image of PL 198 it confirmed that Sermo XV *In die Palmarum*, with a cross-reference to PL 171, col. 476, follows the explicit of Sermo XIV. Gaps: the outer blocks are unmatched; Schneyer's page and the literature on the Hildebert-Comestor attributions were not seen; the history of the PL 198 attribution was not traced. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

Differences: "accendatur" against "accenditur"; "Hoc non" (uncertain) against "non enim"; "audientes" (uncertain) against "auditorem"; a doxology closing Sermo XIV that PL 198 lacks; the Palm Sunday theme continued to "aufer opprobrium nostrum" and "tantum" against "tantummodo"; "in unum plura" against "plura in unum", "Eius est sane" against "Ejus sane est", and "Deus enim" without "noster". Where PL 171 prints "Unde [f. quod]" with the editor's query, the manuscript seems to read *Unum*, which gives the sense "we cannot grasp the One except by proceeding through the unities". After "apprehendunt unam" the manuscript has about one and a half lines with no counterpart in PL 171 before "uigilan(ter) dic(it)", with a present verb where PL has "dixit". In Sermo XIV, the lost text between the foot of A(iii) and the foot of the left column of face B fills one column of the manuscript, but PL 198 has only about 23 lines of its own there against about 48 between A(ii) and A(iii); at that point PL 198 passes from "ad respirandum paululum removetur ab eis in hunc modum" directly to "Tertia est exsultatio", without the first two *exsultationes*. That the manuscript held the fuller text there is an inference from spacing; the passage itself does not survive on the strip. The rubric reads *In ramis palmarum*, where PL 198 heads the sermon *In die Palmarum* and PL 171 *In dominica Palmarum*.

## Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

Face A (canvas 0) is the recto and face B (canvas 1) the verso of one leaf: the text runs from A(ii) to A(iii) and then to the left and middle columns of face B. A narrow block of line-ends at the left of face A (canvas 0, [1100,3260,400,720](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-o5pg/Stams_STA_Frg_123_01r.jpg/1100,3260,400,720/full/0/default.jpg)) and a block of line-beginnings at the right of face B (canvas 1, [3780,3300,420,680](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-o5pg/Stams_STA_Frg_123_01v.jpg/3780,3300,420,680/full/0/default.jpg)) belong to the conjugate leaf across the fold. They read, in part, "[...]caritate co(n)[...]", "[...]sumenta" and "Inq(ui)t" (face A), and "s(c)r(i)pt(ur)a tot[...]", "appellationib(us)", "S(un)t au(tem) .vii. e[...]", "leg(itu)r mul[...]" (face B). Neither block has been matched to the printed text. If the opening of face B ran on across the fold, the right-hand block would fall in PL 171, col. 477 (the eight unities and seven gifts), but the manuscript's fuller text makes a match against PL uncertain. Whether this sheet was the centre of a quire is not settled.

Frg. 123 and Frg. 142 ([F-vjii](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-vjii)) are joined by the catalogue in reconstruction F-dr4l, which is not public. Measured on images at the same photographic scale (about 137 pixels to the centimetre), the two strips have nearly the same line pitch (about 6.0 and 6.3 mm), the same column width (about 6.5 and 6.4 cm), the same small hand, and the same rubric practice: an orange-red title split over two lines to the right of the theme's opening words, with a plain red initial. Both carry sermons with rubrics. That makes one manuscript plausible, not proven; these features are common in sermon books of the period, and the letter forms were not compared systematically. The two strips came from different host bindings, which fits a dispersed codex.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the copy beyond the general character of the script, its place in the textual tradition of Comestor's sermons, the identity of the collection it belonged to, or the history of the attribution of the Palm Sunday sermon beyond the PL headings and Schneyer.
