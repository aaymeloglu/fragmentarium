# F-r30z: Jacobus de Voragine, *Legenda aurea*, c. 168-170 (Elisabeth, Caecilia, Clement), shortened and glossed, with *Collectio epithetorum* lists and model letters

Four faces of these twenty paper pieces carry the legends of Elisabeth of Hungary, Caecilia and Clement of Rome from the *Legenda aurea* of Jacobus de Voragine, chapters 168, 169 and 170 in Graesse's numbering, in that order. The text is much shorter than the *Legenda aurea*. It keeps the narrative sentences, often word for word, leaves out most of the speeches, and adds at least one gloss not found in Graesse's text. Other pieces carry lists of the anonymous *Collectio epithetorum*, a handbook of epithets for letter-writers, which agree item for item over about twenty words with München, Universitätsbibliothek, 4° Cod. ms. 812. A few pieces carry rubricated model letters about a son's schooling, which are not identified.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-r30z) describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv (Stealth Published), Frg. 101: paper, 20 fragment items, 124 x 75 mm (measured on Frg. 100_4), online since 18 September 2021, with the title "x". It gives no description, date or identification. The two catalogue images (Stams_STA_Frg_101_01r20r and _01v20v) show the twenty pieces mounted on one board per face, each with a pencil label "Stams, STA, Frg. 101-n r/v". Several pieces on the first image are upside down.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for the legend pieces. Four faces were collated with Graesse's edition, from three chapters in sequence: 101-10r (c. 168, Elisabeth's death), 101-10v and 101-5r (c. 169, Caecilia), and 101-5v (c. 170, Clement and the lamb that shows the spring). In each, distinctive narrative sentences agree with the *Legenda aurea* in wording and order. Examples: "Angelus autem duas coronas ex rosis et liliis in manu habebat", "ergo totus mundus errat et tu cum fratre tuo verum deum nosti", "vidit agnum stantem qui pede dextro ei locum ostendebat". The differences are of three kinds:
  - Long passages are absent: Urban's prayer and the old man with the book in c. 169, the disputation with Almachius, the Moses comparison in c. 170.
  - Some sentences are reworded.
  - At least one sentence is not in Graesse. On 101-10r the birds over Elisabeth's church are called angels sent to carry her soul to heaven, where the *Legenda aurea* names only the single bird by her bed as her guardian angel.
  Jean de Mailly's *Abbreviatio*, which also tells the Caecilia legend briefly, is further from the fragment's wording than the *Legenda aurea*. Whether the shortened text was made directly from the *Legenda aurea* or comes through an intermediate legendary is not claimed. The *Collectio epithetorum* lists are a related text in a different form from LMU 4° Cod. ms. 812: red headings, a closing formula "Et huiusmodi consimilia", shorter lists. The model letters were searched without result, including in the letter material of LMU 4° Cod. ms. 812 and the printed *Epistolares formulae* of Carolus Virulus.
- **Transcription:** [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-r30z/readings/stams6-2026-11-01-image-only.json), with a supplement reading the first image rotated, and a [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-r30z/readings/stams6-2026-11-01-source-assisted.json) of selected pieces.
  - Reread on the image by research readers: 101-10r, 101-10v, 101-5r, 101-5v, two list pieces (canvas 0 row 2 piece 4; canvas 1 row 2 piece 5) and a third in part (canvas 1 row 1 piece 2). Phrases only on the letter pieces (101-4r, 101-4v, canvas 0 row 1 piece 1) and the faded piece 101-3v.
  - The other pieces rest on the unverified first reading. Several lines of the gloss on 101-10r and many single words are uncertain.
  - Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader collated 101-10v and 101-5r with Graesse's text (OCR of the 1850 second edition, same pagination on the pages used) and the epithet lists with the page images of LMU 4° Cod. ms. 812, fol. 7r-8r. A second research reader read 101-10r, 101-5v and the weaker lines of 101-5r before opening the source, then compared them with page images of Graesse's 1846 edition, pp. 764-765, 774 and 786. The coordinator read 101-10v (canvas 1, native box 4860,1760-5930,3480) on the image, from "Cumque ei omnia uerba cecilie dixisset" through "Tunc ualerianus a sancto urbano baptisma suscepit", the angel with the two crowns and Valerian's request for his brother, to "ambo cum palma martirii ad dominum venietis", and found it in sequence with Graesse p. 773. Not collated: 101-3v, whose red marginal note reads "S' s elizabeth", and the remaining narrative pieces of the second image (row 2 pieces 6 and 8, row 3), all of them faded. Weimar, HAAB, Q 108 (another *Collectio epithetorum*) is known from its catalogue description only.

Besides the omissions and the angel sentence, the readings differ in small ways. 101-10v has "Urbanus episcopus gratias deo egit" where Graesse has Urban's prayer, "mundo(?) corde" for "immaculato corde et mundo corpore", "vobis attuli" for "ad vos attuli", and "consensisti(?)" for "credidisti". 101-5r has "ipsi sepeliebant" for "sepulturae tradebant". On 101-5v the fragment agrees with the reading "pede dextro" that Graesse's apparatus gives from the first printed edition ("Ed. Pr."), against "pede erecto" in his text. One variant does not place the fragment in a branch of the tradition.

The layout shows how short the text was. 101-10v has the line ends of a neighbouring column at its left edge and 101-10r has none, so the piece comes from a two-column leaf: column b of the verso, column a of the recto. Between Elisabeth's death on the recto and Valerianus going to Urban on the verso there is room for two or three columns. Graesse needs about seven printed pages (pp. 765-772) for that stretch.

## The pieces, other texts, and what is not claimed

All twenty pieces are paper in a small cursive book hand with the same red rubrication: struck and underlined headings, red-stroked capitals, paraphs, and a red wavy line-filler. Lists and prose stand on the same face of single pieces. Line spacing measured roughly on three prose pieces is similar (about 4.4-4.6 mm), and a list piece is somewhat tighter (about 3.9 mm). This makes it likely, but not proven, that they come from one book; no two pieces were found to join. On that assumption the book was a compilation joining an epithet handbook and model letters for letter-writing with a shortened, glossed run of *Legenda aurea* legends. The legends could equally belong to a separate legendary bound with the letter-writing material.

The model letters (heads "Responsio filii ad pa[trem]", "... socios esse fideles", "... ut doceat fideliter ... moribus") and the piece 101-3v, with the marginal note "S' s elizabeth" and moralising prose on poverty, are not identified.

No date or origin is claimed for the fragments, and nothing is claimed about the source of the shortened legend text or its relation to other copies of the *Legenda aurea* or the *Collectio epithetorum*.
