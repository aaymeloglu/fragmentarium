# F-wgga: Peter Comestor, *Historia scholastica*, III Regum cap. 29-37

Stams, Frg. 103 is four strips cut from a two-column manuscript of Peter Comestor's *Historia scholastica*, the twelfth-century narrative of biblical history with notes on chronology and secular history. The strips carry the book on 3 Kings (in the Vulgate, 3 Reg = 1 Kings), from Jeroboam and the man of God from Juda (cap. 29) to the call of Elisha and the opening of the first siege of Samaria (cap. 37). This is PL 198, cols. 1373-1381. Each strip shows the right part of one column, the gap between columns and the left part of the next, so every line is cut on one side. The red rubrics include Comestor's "Incidentia", his short notes on contemporary secular history, which the printed edition also carries.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-wgga) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 103, paper, four fragment items, 155 x 95 mm (measured on Frg. 103_1, 153/155 x 92/95 mm, by Claudia Sojer), online since 18 September 2021. It has two images, one for each face of the four strips laid side by side, the title "x", and no date, description or identification. The four strips are as described. Pencil notes on the strips read "Stams, STA, Frg. 103_1r" to "103_4r" (and v), and "Hs 228" on strips 1 and 2, which may refer to the volume the strips came from.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and sequence with Comestor's own prose, not just the biblical text he retells, on three of the four strips and on both faces. The passages are the "Incidentia" under Roboam, citing Herodotus on Sesac; the "Sub Asa fuit" note, citing Josephus on Baasa's death at the hand of Creon; Comestor's reckoning of the reigns of Abia and Asa by synecdoche (counting part of a year as a whole year); and, on strip 2, his note "aposiopesis est" on Elijah's oath. Bare Vulgate text, Peter of Poitiers's *Compendium* or a vernacular history Bible would lack these comments. The differences from PL are scribal: "Susac" for Sesac, "precedebant" for "praeferebantur", "Zela" for Hela, "completi" for "impleti", "his annis" for "annis his", probably "anno v[el] parte anni" for "anno et parte anni", "aliquot" not before "siccatus", and no "enim" after "Non ergo". The manuscript also has the rubric "Incidentia" before "Sub Asa fuit", where PL prints none.
- **Transcription:** source-assisted. The [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-wgga/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-image-only.json) covered all sixteen column pieces. In the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-wgga/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-source-assisted.json), a research reader reread on the native images four column pieces: strip 1 face r right column (14 lines), strip 4 face r right column (15 lines), strip 4 face v left column (14 lines) and strip 1 face v left column (upper lines). Strip 3 rests on the first reading alone, checked against the edition for its place in the text. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the four reread passages with the page images of PL 198, cols. 1373, 1375 and 1377.
  Passage checked independently: canvas 0, strip 2, right column, native boxes 3350,1330,3950,2450 and 3500,2200,3950,3000, read on the image and compared with PL 198 col. 1378 ([page image](https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecursu0198mign/page/n694/mode/1up)), cap. 34, Elijah at Carith and Sarepta. Every line beginning follows PL's sequence, including Comestor's "aposiopesis est". The manuscript has "his annis" for PL "annis his", and PL's "aliquot" is not before "sicca[tus]".
  Gaps: strip 3 was not read on the image beyond the first reading. PL is the only edition compared. The support was not examined.

The differences are the scribal ones listed under Identification. Where the manuscript seems to read "regnare abya", Comestor's argument and PL need "Asa"; the reading is uncertain. The manuscript writes "Incidentia" as a rubric before "Sub Asa fuit", where PL prints the note without a heading. None of these touches the identification.

Edition: Petrus Comestor, *Historia scholastica*, in J.-P. Migne, *Patrologia Latina* 198 (Paris, 1855), Liber III Regum, cap. 29-37. On archive.org patrologiaecursu0198mign: cols. 1373-1374 on [n692](https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecursu0198mign/page/n692/mode/1up), cols. 1375-1376 on [n693](https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecursu0198mign/page/n693/mode/1up), cols. 1377-1378 on [n694](https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecursu0198mign/page/n694/mode/1up) and cols. 1381-1382 on [n696](https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecursu0198mign/page/n696/mode/1up). The collated passages were read on these page images. The running heads name "Petri Comestoris" and "Historia scholastica - Liber III Regum", and the work's authorship is secure.

## Order of the strips and what is not claimed

Both faces of each strip read in sequence, so the two photographs show each strip in the same position:

| Strip | First face | Second face |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | canvas 1: cap. 29-30 | canvas 0: cap. 30-31 |
| 4 | canvas 1: cap. 31-32 | canvas 0: cap. 32-33 |
| 2 | canvas 0: cap. 33-34 | canvas 1: cap. 34-35 |
| 3 | canvas 1: cap. 35 | canvas 0: cap. 36-37 |

The text therefore runs strip 1, strip 4, strip 2, strip 3. The strips come from successive leaves of one manuscript; how they lay on the original leaves was not reconstructed. Parchment binding tabs are pasted on strip 1.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the manuscript, its support (the catalogue gives paper; not examined), the meaning of "Hs 228", and the place of this copy among manuscripts of the *Historia scholastica*. PL is the only edition compared.
