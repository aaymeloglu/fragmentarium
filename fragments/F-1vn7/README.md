# F-1vn7: Rhymed offices of St Kunigunde (end) and of the Canonization of St Henry, *In festo Canonizationis sancti Heinrici imperatoris*, First Vespers to Matins nocturn III

Two small paper leaves at Stams come from an office book, a book of the sung and spoken texts of the daily Office, here without the readings (lessons). They carry two rhymed offices (offices whose chants are in rhymed verse) in direct succession. The first is the end of the office of the empress St Kunigunde: the last three responsories of Matins, the five Lauds antiphons with the Benedictus antiphon, and the Second Vespers responsory *Deus pater misericordiae*, the text edited in Analecta Hymnica 26, no. 77. The second is the office headed on the leaf "Canonisac[io]nis s(an)c(t)i heinrici imp(er)atoris", for the feast of the canonization of the emperor St Henry II, from First Vespers (*Festa pii imperatoris Heinrici*, R. *Converso ad summum studio*, Magnificat antiphon *Chorus novae Ierusalem*, collect *Deus qui beatum Heinricum*) through the invitatory and all three nocturns of Matins as far as the responsory *Aeterna tactus et tractus sapientia*. Its chants, chapter, hymn cue and collect, in this order, are those printed "In festo Canonizationis S. Heinrici imperatoris" in the Bamberg breviary of 1575, pp. 745-750.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-1vn7) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 107 (collection "Stams (Stealth Published)"): paper, two fragment items, 153 x 104 mm (measured on Frg. 107_1 as 148/153 x 79/104 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no date and no linked description, with two images, Stams_STA_Frg_107_01r02r and _01v02v, each showing both leaves. The record does not name the text. Its physical description fits the images.

- **Identification:** identified, high. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and order between the leaves and two printed texts read on page images: for Henry, the Bamberg breviary of 1575 (First Vespers, Matins nocturns I-III, about thirty chants and the collect); for Kunigunde, Analecta Hymnica 26 no. 77 and the Bamberg manuscript Msc. Lit. 25 as inventoried in the Cantus Database. No other office with these texts was found. The leaves write no lessons; where the 1575 breviary interposes lessons, and between the Henry collect and Matins a Kunigunde suffrage and Compline items, the leaves pass straight on. Variants against the Henry print: *que ... exaltat* (1575 *quae ... exaltavit*), *germine nobilitatis* (1575 *gemine*), *expertus* (1575 *experitur*), *in monte cassinum* (1575 *montem Cassinum*), *Ungaria que* (1575 *qui*), *copulat*, *consignat* (1575 *copulavit*, *consignavit*); no versicle after the nocturn II antiphons (1575 *Posuisti Domine*). Against AH 26: *quia Christum* (AH *Quae*), *comprobat*, *Integritatis*, *humilitatis*, *commendat* (AH *comprobavit*, *Integritati*, *humilitati*, *commendavit*), *Nos solve nexu* (AH *a nexu*).
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-1vn7/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-image-only.json) covers all four pages. Other lines rest on the first reading. The first word of the third nocturn-II antiphon (red initial) is uncertain. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the Henry chants of all four Henry pages with the Bamberg breviary of 1575 on its page images (MDZ, scans 813-818) and the Kunigunde chants with AH 26 pp. 226-227 on page images; a second research reader read the Lauds antiphons and the join passage on 1v before seeing a source and compared them with the same page images, with agreement apart from the variants listed. ## Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

- The text runs 107-2v, 107-2r, 107-1v, 107-1r, so the pencilled r/v labels are reversed on both leaves. Canvas 0 left ends with the beginning of the antiphon *Salus altissima sancti huius semper est gloria*, and canvas 1 right begins "que caput eius gratia virtutum exaltat", its continuation; canvas 1 right ends "R. Cum adysset causa religionis" and canvas 0 right continues that responsory. The Kunigunde page (canvas 1 left) ends with a blank lower third, and the Henry office starts on the other side of the same leaf. The Henry office continued on a lost leaf.
- Single column of 25-29 lines, cursive gothic hand, red rubrics, red and faded blue initials, no music. Offset text from another page shows in some margins.
- The psalm scheme is that of the secular (non-monastic) Office: three antiphons per nocturn with Psalms 1-3, 4, 5, 8 and 14, 20, 23, and three responsories per nocturn. The book was therefore not made for monastic or Cistercian choir use. Its diocese is not established: the March feast of Henry's canonization and the sequence Kunigunde-Henry fit Bamberg, but the same Henry office is found in Augsburg, Salzburg and Austrian books, and the Kunigunde office in Passau, St Florian and Würzburg books.
- Not claimed: the date, the origin, how the book reached Stams, or any relation to a surviving manuscript.
