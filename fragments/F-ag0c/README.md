# F-ag0c: Anonymous, *Audi tellus, audi magni maris limbus*, trope to the responsory *Libera me*, Analecta Hymnica 49 no. 779, strophes 1-6

Among the eleven small pieces of binding waste catalogued as Stams, Frg. 7 are parts of a notated music manuscript. Frg. 7-11v and the small piece pasted on it, labelled "Adl.", carry the opening strophes of *Audi tellus, audi magni maris limbus*, an anonymous rhythmic poem on the passing of worldly glory ("Where is Plato, where Alexander, where Paris ...?", a so-called Ubi sunt series) sung as a trope, a set of added verses, to the responsory *Libera me Domine* of the Office of the Dead. G. M. Dreves edited it in Analecta Hymnica 49 (1906) as no. 779, "Super *Libera me Domine*", pp. 378-380. The Stams text runs from strophe 1 through strophe 6, with an expanded and reordered list of names that AH prints from none of its manuscripts. The other pieces of Frg. 7, faded German documentary notes and blank faces, are not identified and are not part of this claim.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-ag0c) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 7, parchment and paper, eleven items, 184 x 138 mm (measured on Frg. 7_9, 180/184 x 136/138 mm, by Claudia Sojer), title "x", no date of origin, photographed four pieces at a time on seven images. Pencil "aus Hs 25" on several pieces gives the host volume, Stams Hs 25. A modern typed slip with a red-pencil note, photographed beside the pieces on canvases 4, 5 and 6, is labelled "Stams, StA, Adl. 1r/1v zu Frg. 7-11r/v" and carries a faint typed year, probably "1935". It gives a typed transcription of music text from these pieces, including "Audi tellus ... ubi Salomon sapientissimus", "... auri congeries Perit enim rerum maneries ... ubi Pompeius" and "Ubi Sampson robustissimus ubi versucia speciosissimus Transierunt leges mortalium ... anima in morte sumus ... adiutorem nisi te domine ... Sancte et misericors sal- ... ne tradas". The slip names no work, and the record has no identification.

- **Identification:** identified, high, scoped to *Audi tellus* (AH 49 no. 779) on Frg. 7-11v and the pasted Adl. piece. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and sequence with AH's text and apparatus across strophes 1-6, read on the fragment's images: the opening "Audi tellus audi magni maris limbus Audi omne [quod vivit sub sole]", strophes 2-4 ("huius mundi decus et gloria ... Contestantur ... temporalia non in uno statu manentia", "enim regalis dignitas", "[aur]i congeries"), and the Ubi strophes 5-6 with Plato, Pompeius, Darius, Alexander, Paris with Helen, Achilles, Caesar and Hector of Troy. AH says the examples in strophes 5 and 6 change "in almost all sources" and prints several arrangements; the Stams list matches none exactly. It shares "Aristoteles" with manuscript D ("Aristoteles ubi doctissimus") and FG ("Vel egregius Aristoteles"), and "Ubi Salomon sapientissimus" with D ("Ubi Salomon, gloria sapientiae") and FG ("Ubi Salomon prudentissimus"), but has "Aristoteles uir doctissimus" and adds "Ubi fortes", "Domocritus" (Democritus) and "tu[llius]" (FG has "Ubi Tullius et Virgilius" in another line); it reads "Perit enim rerum maneries et transit rerum materies", a line doubled against AH's single "Transit enim rerum materies" (str. 4b), and "maneries" is not in AH's apparatus. AH no. 778 shares only the opening line and is not the text here. "Ubi Sampson robustissimus" and "ubi Absalon speciosissimus" are not claimed: they are not read on any image, but come only from the typed slip, which reads "versucia", and "Absalon" is a conjecture for that word from AH's FG ("Ubi Absalon, vir pulcherrimus"). The antiphon *Media vita in morte sumus* (Cantus 003732) is also not part of the claim: it rests on a mirror-image offset on 7-8v, the trace of an adjoining page that is not in the images, and is reported below separately.
- **Transcription:** source-assisted. The [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-ag0c/readings/stams-2026-10-30-image-only.json) is not blind: its reader saw the typed slip on the overview images before reading the leaves, transcribed the Sampson to *Media vita* part of it, and marked its readings of the two offsets (canvas 3 and the upper right of canvas 5) as contaminated. The slip also carries text of 7-11v and the Adl. piece, so the first reading of those pieces cannot be treated as independent of it either. That reading also noted the pencil labels "Stams, STA, Frg. 7-1 ... 7-12" and read the music pieces from unmirrored images. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-ag0c/readings/stams-2026-10-30-source-assisted.json) is a research reader's (Claude) reread, with AH available, of both text blocks of 7-11v (six lines each), the Adl. piece (five lines), the mirrored and contrast-stretched offset on 7-8v (six lines) and fragments of the offset on 7-10v. The top lines of the pieces are abraded or cut and several names remain uncertain. The documentary notes were not read; those on 7-7v were examined with enhancement without result. The notation was not transcribed. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader (Claude) compared the Stams lines with the OCR of AH 49. An independent research reader then read the left block of 7-11v, *Ubi Alexander* to *Ubi Salomon*, on canvas 5, native boxes 1200,250,1550,1000 and 1200,1150,1550,1050, without the transcription, and compared it with AH 49 no. 779, pp. 379-380, on the archive.org page images (leaves n382-n383), not the OCR. The blind reading agreed with the research reading line for line, and the AH citations held on the page images. The same reader could not read Sampson on the 7-10v offset, where only "leges mortali[um]" and "spacium Sed" are legible. The right block, the Adl. piece and the offsets were not independently checked. No manuscript of the poem was compared, and no Cistercian or Tyrolean book was searched for the expanded list.

## Other items in the images and what is not claimed

- **Offsets.** 7-8v, 7-9v and 7-10v carry mirror-image offsets of music pages from the same presumed book, pages that are not in the images and not catalogued. On 7-8v, canvas 3 [2980,392,1031,1916, mirrored](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-ag0c/Stams_STA_Frg_007_05v08v.jpg/2980,392,1031,1916/full/!0/default.jpg), the research reader read "[Medi]a vita in morte sumus que[m] [quaerimus] adiutorem n(isi) te domine [qui pro peccatis] nostris iuste irasce(ris) [Sancte] ... deus [Sancte] ... et misericors sal[vator] amare morti ne tradas", which agrees as far as legible with the standard text of the antiphon *Media vita* (Cantus 003732). On 7-10v, canvas 5 [4268,343,1031,1720, mirrored](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-ag0c/Stams_STA_Frg_007_09v11v_Adl_01v_1_.jpg/4268,343,1031,1720/full/!0/default.jpg), only "[transi]erunt leges mortali[um]" and "[spa]cium Sed [tu] deus" are legible, AH str. 7a-b; the rest is mixed with show-through. At the lower right of canvas 5 the first reader saw a paler copy agreeing line for line with the two blocks of 7-11v. These offsets are evidence of the lost pages, not text of Frg. 7. The slip gives *Audi tellus* followed by *Media vita*; that sequence is not confirmed on the leaves, so whether *Audi tellus* led here into *Media vita* or into the *Libera* is open.
- **Documentary pieces.** Faint German cursive notes on 7-1v (top edge: "leyb vn(d) gut ... ellen vn(d) wassen?"), 7-4v and 7-7v remain unread and unidentified; they need UV or multispectral imaging. Pen trials are on 7-2r; the other faces are blank apart from ruling, compass arcs and pencil labels.
- **Not claimed:** the date of the music manuscript (its gothic hand looks 15th century, not examined palaeographically), the kind of book (processional, gradual or cantionale), the recension of the expanded name list, and any Stams or Tyrolean origin.
