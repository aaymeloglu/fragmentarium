# F-3wa4: Thomas Aquinas, *Expositio libri Posteriorum*, book I, lectiones 7-13

These two parchment strips are from a copy of Thomas Aquinas's *Expositio libri Posteriorum*, his literal commentary on Aristotle's *Posterior Analytics*. They carry parts of book I, lectiones (lecture sections) 7 to 13 in the numbering of the 1882 Leonine edition, the text reproduced by Corpus Thomisticum. The two strips are the upper and lower parts of one bifolium (a folded sheet giving two leaves), which on the evidence of the text sequence was the central sheet of its gathering.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-3wa4) describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 85: parchment, two items, 319 x 119 mm (measured on item 85_2), no catalogue date, title "x", no linked description, with a reconstruction summary by Claudia Sojer. It names no text. The description fits the images: two horizontal strips, each running across an opened bifolium and showing part of four columns, two to a page, in a small, heavily abbreviated gothic book hand, with the lemmata (the quoted words of Aristotle) underlined and introduced by "Deinde cum dicit" or by paraphs. Canvas 0 shows the faces pencilled 85-2r (upper strip on the image) and 85-1r (lower); canvas 1 shows 85-2v and 85-1v. The faces on canvas 0 are well preserved; those on canvas 1 are abraded and coated with brown paste residue, and only their edge bands read well. Pencil notes on the strips include "4° Ms 12" and "c. 1280"(?); they were not interpreted.

- **Identification:** identified, high. The strips follow Thomas's exposition sentence by sentence, with his divisions of the text ("Postquam ... hic ... Et circa hoc duo facit"), his glosses and his examples, in four passages collated on both faces of both strips (listed under Textual evidence). The other legible columns follow the same sequence of lectiones in the first reading. The text is the commentary itself, running continuously across several lectiones, not a later work quoting it. No other commentary on the *Posterior Analytics* (Grosseteste, Albert, Kilwardby) was compared, since none follows Thomas's wording. The differences from the printed text are those of a copy: two omissions by eye-skip, a few small omissions and word variants, and lemmata in a different wording of the Latin Aristotle ("Circulo autem quoniam impossibile" where the edition has "circulo quoque"). At four points in the passage checked against the printed edition's page, the fragment agrees with readings the edition's apparatus reports from manuscripts, against its printed text. Nothing suggests a different or reworked commentary.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-3wa4/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-image-only.json) and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-3wa4/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json). A research reader reread three passages on the native images with the edition text at hand: 85-2r col. c (16 lines), 85-1r col. b (15 lines) and the bottom band of 85-1v col. c (10 lines). An independent checker read 85-2r col. a (13 lines) on the image before opening the source. The other columns rest on the image-only first reading, and most of canvas 1 is unread under the paste. Several abbreviated groups are unresolved and are marked in the reading. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the three passages with the electronic text of the 1882 Leonine edition on Corpus Thomisticum, not with page images. An independent checker read 85-2r col. a (canvas 0, native box 2180,1060,3240,1680, 13 lines from the lemma "Utrum autem secundum quod est triangulus" to "universale primum") on the image before opening the source, then compared it with the page image of the 1882 Leonine edition, t. I p. 186 col. b, lect. 12 n. 11 ([archive.org, operaomniaiussui01thom, leaf n555](https://archive.org/details/operaomniaiussui01thom/page/n555)), and with the edition's side apparatus. It agrees sentence by sentence, with small differences of wording; at four points ("quomodo", "quod est", "comprehenditur", and probably the omission of "tres angulos scilicet") the fragment follows manuscripts named in the apparatus against the printed text. That checker had already seen the research reader's transcriptions of the other columns and the lectio assigned to col. a, but not any transcription of col. a. The same checker worked out the leaf layout from the positions of the columns in the edition text. Gaps: R.-A. Gauthier's critical edition (Leonine t. I*2, 1989) and its list of manuscripts were not consulted, so whether this witness is recorded there, and whether its lectio divisions differ, are not known; most of canvas 1 is unread; script and date were not assessed. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

**Differences.** Two omissions are by eye-skip and leave sentences that do not construe, which points to copying, not abridgement: A11 lacks "est principium quoddam totius scientiae. Nam ex" (from *cognitio* to *cognitione*), and B9 lacks "sicut cum dicitur quod corpus vivit per animam. Quandoque autem habitudinem causae materialis". Smaller omissions: B16 lacks "in eo, quod quid est"; A16 may lack "ostendens"; C may lack "et ita". Word variants include "Dicat igitur" for "Dicit ergo", "assumatur" for "assumantur", "est eorum" for "eorum est", "procedent" for "proceditur", "id" for "illud" and "ipsius" for "illius" (D). At A8 an omitted "infinita" is supplied between the lines and a wrong "duo" struck through. In passage D the fragment agrees with manuscript readings recorded in the Leonine apparatus against the printed text at "quomodo", "quod est" and "comprehenditur", and its unresolved short group at D6 probably reflects the manuscripts' omission of "tres angulos scilicet". The lemmata follow a different wording of the Latin Aristotle from the edition's; which translation was not checked against the *Aristoteles Latinus*.

**Edition.** *S. Thomae Aquinatis Opera omnia* iussu Leonis XIII, t. I (Rome 1882), lect. XII on p. 186, lect. XIII from p. 187 ([archive.org, operaomniaiussui01thom, leaf n555](https://archive.org/details/operaomniaiussui01thom/page/n555)), read as a page image for passage D. Passages A to C were compared with the same text as reproduced by Corpus Thomisticum ([Expositio Posteriorum lib. 1](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/cpa1.html), headed "Textum Leoninum Romae 1882 editum"). Lectio and paragraph numbers are those of the 1882 edition and Corpus Thomisticum; the critical edition by R.-A. Gauthier (Leonine t. I*2, 1989) was not consulted. The *Expositio libri Posteriorum* is an authentic work of Thomas Aquinas, and the attribution of this text to him is the Leonine edition's.

## Leaf layout and limits

The layout below was worked out by an independent checker from where each column's text falls in the Corpus Thomisticum text, at about 6,600 characters (about 75 lines) of edition text to a column. It is an inference from text positions, good to about five lines, not a physical reconstruction.

- On 85-2r (canvas 0) the left page runs exactly three pages of text after the right page: the outer side of a central bifolium, with the right page the recto of its first leaf and the left page the verso of its second.
- On 85-1r (canvas 0) the text runs on from the left page to the right page: the inner side, the central opening of the same bifolium.
- The 85-1 columns stand about 40 lines earlier in the text than the matching columns of 85-2. 85-2 has a blank lower margin and 85-1 a narrow upper margin, so 85-1 is the upper part and 85-2 the lower part of one bifolium, with about 10 lines lost between them and perhaps a few lost above 85-1.
- Canvas 1 bears this out: lect. 7 n. 1-3 stands in the lowest lines of 85-1v col. c, the first column of the first leaf's recto, and lect. 11 n. 1 at the top of 85-2v col. c, the first column of the second leaf's recto, both where the arithmetic puts them.
- By inference the bifolium began in the last part of lect. 6 and ended in lect. 13 (about n. 6-7). The legible text runs from lect. 7 to lect. 13. The head of the first leaf (end of lect. 6) is under the paste and was not seen.
- The written area works out at about 28 x 15.3 cm, a large two-column page; that is arithmetic, not a measurement.

Text placed in the other columns from the first reading: 85-2r col. b, lect. 13 n. 5-7; 85-2r col. d and 85-1r col. a, lect. 8 (circular demonstration, "animal rationale mortale ... risibile"); 85-1r col. c, lect. 10 (modes of *per se*, "isto ambulante coruscavit"); 85-1r col. d, end of lect. 11 and lect. 12 n. 1-3; 85-2v col. c, lect. 11 n. 1. These were not collated.

Not claimed: the date or origin of the manuscript (the pencil "c. 1280"(?) was not assessed), whether the witness is known to the Leonine editors, which Latin translation of Aristotle the lemmata follow, or any reading of the paste-covered parts of canvas 1.
