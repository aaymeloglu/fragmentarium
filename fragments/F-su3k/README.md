# F-su3k: *Pharetra doctorum et philosophorum*, "De amore inimicorum"

The leaf is identified with high confidence as part of the *Pharetra doctorum et philosophorum*, an anonymous florilegium of excerpts from the Fathers and other authorities arranged by subject, in its section on the love of enemies. The surviving entries, with their source labels, their internal cross-reference and their note of a parallel in the Decretum, are found together in the first printed edition. This is not the four-book *Pharetra* printed among Bonaventure's works, which shares a few excerpts but not the arrangement.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-su3k) describes a parchment leaf, Gent, Universiteitsbibliotheek, HS.2582/177, 336 × 239 mm, dated 1301–1350, catalogued as an unidentified theological text. Those catalogue details have not been reassessed.

- **Identification:** identified, high. About twenty entries on the clearer face correspond to the print, each with the same source label and opening, and three features belong to the compiler, not to any quoted source: "Idem in quadam omelia. Perfectus amor et perfectum odium etc. quere supra de amore proximi"; "... et transsumitur et habetur in decretis xxiii q. iiii"; and paraphrased entries such as "Precipua laus Romanorum fuit ...". Material alternatives examined: Thomas of Ireland's *Manipulus florum* (its *Inimicicia* has fourteen short entries in another order) and the pseudo-Bonaventure *Pharetra* (II.9 *De ira* shares three Chrysostom excerpts, with Gregory first).
- **Transcription:** [source-assisted selected reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-su3k/readings/recheck-2026-10-11-source-assisted.json) of entry labels and openings on the clearer face, read by a model partly from a reduced view; two image-only readings are preserved ([first](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-su3k/readings/batch-2026-10-11-image-only.json), [native retry](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-su3k/readings/batch-2026-10-11-native-retry-image-only.json)). Accuracy has not been measured. The faded face was read by a second model only.
- **Verification:** one model compared the manuscript image with the page images of the Munich copy of the print, fols. 39v–40v, directly. The comparison covers the entries listed below, not every word.

The print and the leaf do not arrange the section identically. On the leaf the Chrysostom entries are followed by a "Crisostomus super Matheum" entry, a group from Gregory, and then the Augustine group; in the print the Augustine group (fol. 39v) precedes the Chrysostom group (fols. 39v–40v), "Non sane convenit christiano" stands earlier within it, and Ambrose follows. Within the Augustine group the *Enchiridion* entry stands before "Si quis inimicis" on the leaf and after the *De conflictu* entry in the print. The leaf's *seipsum*/*semetipsum* and *respondit*/*respondet* are small verbal variants. The leaf is therefore a witness to the same compilation in a different order of authorities, not a copy of the text as printed. The Munich catalogue describes the Mentelin text as an alphabetically arranged and enlarged version, which is consistent with rearrangement between manuscript and print, but the relation between the two forms has not been investigated here.

The faded face, as read by a second model, carries the preceding part of the section (a Gregory entry; "Crisostomus de compunctione cordis libro i. Si tantummodo non ledas ..."; "Idem in libro de laudibus sancti Pauli ... nemo sic dilexit inimicos ..."; "Idem super Iohannem ... ploremus ..."), ending "Idem super illud", which the clearer face continues. Those entries stand in the print on fols. 39v–40r in that order. That face was not rechecked against the image.

## Edition and attribution

The edition is recorded as GW M32924, ISTC ip00571000, [BSB-Ink P-420](https://inkunabeln.digitale-sammlungen.de/Ausgabe_P-420.html) ("Pharetra doctorum. Alph. geordnete und erweiterte Fassung"), which also notes the unresolved question of authorship. The work is treated here as anonymous. A different printed book with a similar title, [Nuremberg: Creussner, about 1473](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb11303129) (GW M32923), has the four-book arrangement by persons, vices and virtues and is not the text of this leaf.

No claim is made about the date or origin of the leaf, about the folio number that may stand at its head, or about its place among the manuscripts of the work.
