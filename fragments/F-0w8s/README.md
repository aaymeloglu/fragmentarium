# F-0w8s: Guillelmus Durantis, *Repertorium aureum iuris canonici*, book I, De electione to De temporibus ordinationum

This parchment leaf in Stams is from a copy of the *Repertorium aureum iuris canonici* (also called *Breviarium aureum*) of Guillelmus Durantis (d. 1296), known as the Speculator after his *Speculum iudiciale*. The work goes through the titles of the Decretals of Gregory IX in order and, under each title, lists legal questions ("An ...", "Quid iuris ...", "Qualiter ...") with a reference to where each is treated, often adding "secundum papam" for the commentary of Innocent IV. The leaf has the end of the title De electione and then De translatione, De auctoritate et usu pallii, De renunciatione, De supplenda negligentia prelatorum and the opening of De temporibus ordinationum, which in the Paris edition of 1513 are fol. xvi verso to xix recto.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-0w8s) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 71: parchment, one fragment item, 287 x 195 mm (measured as 282/287 x 184/195 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description, with two images labelled 071_01r and 071_01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: one leaf written in two columns, photographed on both sides. Modern pencil notes on the parchment read "Aus VIII/25" and "anno 1616".

- **Identification:** identified, high. The basis is agreement in wording and order with the 1513 edition in five passages, three on the recto and two on the verso, together with the leaf's five red title headings and the large initial under each, which agree with the edition's sequence of titles and with the first word of each title (Quid, Quare, An, Quando, Quando). The heading of the last title on the leaf adds "de etate et qualitate" to "De temporibus ordinationum", as the edition's combined heading does and the Decretal title itself does not. The leaf also has the cross-reference "vide in nostro speculo", the compiler's reference to his own *Speculum*. Works that treat the same titles in continuous prose are excluded by the form of the text: the *Summa* of Goffredus de Trano opens these titles with other words (read in New Haven, Beinecke Library, Marston MS 36, fol. 10r-11r) and is several times longer, and the Decretal text itself and its ordinary gloss could not fit five titles on one leaf. The *Summa aurea* of Hostiensis, the *Summa* of Henricus de Merseburg and the works of Bernard of Parma were not read; they are set aside because the leaf agrees with Durantis's question list and not because their texts were compared. An abridgement or reworking of the Repertorium is not excluded by the passages read. No difference of wording from the edition was seen in the lines that could be read, apart from spelling; most of the leaf is too abraded to say more.

A few phrases were read before the source was known ("possit eligi ... eligatur", "valet electio pauco[rum]", "consentire [..] dissentire electioni", "secundum omnes", "An soli pape", "significasti", "que non possunt"); the other words in the table below were read with the edition open beside the image, which on so abraded a leaf shows that the edition fits the traces and is not an independent reading. The recto's left column, the lower third of both sides and the body of the verso's right column were not reread and rest on the first reading, which is itself very incomplete there. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the passages listed below with page images of the 1513 edition in the Munich copy (scans 36-41). The coordinator read canvas 0, region 3330,2240,1230,380 (recto col. b, "An soli pape ... compellere aliquem dimittere", "An habens plures", "Qualiter formetur", "in nostro speculo", "De hac materia tractatur lx ... lxi"), on the image and against the 1513 page image https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb10188391?page=37 , and found the same sequence. Gaps: only one printed edition was used and no manuscript of the work; roughly half the leaf was not compared; the edition's colophon was not read.

Differences: in the lines read, only spelling (-cio for -tio, "sollempnitatibus" for "solennitatibus"). In passage 2 the small word between "consentire" and "dissentire" is an abbreviation that could be "nec", as printed, or "vel". The edition's heading "De translatione episcoporum vel clericorum" cannot be checked beyond its first two words. Because so much of the leaf is illegible, the absence of observed variants says little about how close this copy is to the printed text.

## Leaf order and what is not claimed

The text runs from the recto's left column to the verso's right column, so the catalogue's 01r and 01v are in the right order. Brown-ink notes in the left margin of the verso were not read.

No date or place of origin is claimed beyond the general appearance of a 14th-century hand, which was not studied. The pencilled "anno 1616" is a modern note about the volume the leaf came from and does not date the leaf. Nothing is claimed about the relation of this copy to the printed text or to other manuscripts, or about whether it is the full work or an abridged form of it. Whether the fragment is already recorded in the literature on Durantis was not checked.
