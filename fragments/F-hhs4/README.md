# F-hhs4: Gregory the Great, *Moralia in Iob*, XIX.xxvi.47-xxvii.49 (on Job 29:17-18)

Stams, Frg. 141 is a parchment leaf cut down to a vertical strip of one column of Gregory the Great's *Moralia in Iob* (his moral commentary on the Book of Job), book XIX, from the end of chapter 26 into chapter 27, on Job 29:17-18 ("Conterebam molas iniqui ... Dicebamque: In nidulo meo moriar, et sicut palma multiplicabo dies"). The recto keeps the beginnings of about 25 lines, from "[aper]to lapsu revocare. Huius iniqui molas" to "crebris gemitibus utitur et velut nidu[m]". The verso continues after about three or four lost lines and keeps the line ends, from "[...]mpore se ad superna migrare" to "Omnis [namque arbo]r in suo robore iuxta terram vasta". The leaf is written in a protogothic book hand, with red touches on some capitals.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-hhs4) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 141, parchment, one fragment item, 199 x 135 mm (measured 198/199 x 133/135 mm by Claudia Sojer), online since 18 September 2021. It has two images (01r, 01v), the title "x", and no date, description or identification. The leaf carries a pencil shelfmark, "Stams, STA, Frg. 141r" in the recto's left margin and "Frg. 141v" in the verso's right margin. A modern ruled paper slip, photographed beside the cut edge of the recto, has pencil notes lined up with the manuscript lines. They supply the lost ends of two Bible quotations, "tamquam leo rugiens" and "[re]sistite fortes" (1 Peter 5:8-9), and "[t]urtur nidum sibi ubi ponat" (Psalm 83:4). Whoever wrote them had recognised the scriptural quotations but did not name the work. The slip on the verso is ruled but blank.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and sequence with the Maurist text in PL 76 across both faces. The recto carries Gregory's prose around 1 Peter 5:8 ("Contra hunc leonem sancta ecclesia ... fidei caulas munit ... quotiens hereticorum argumenta destruit") and on the nest ("Quid hoc loco per nidi nomen exprimitur ... nutrimentorum suorum dies quasi in nido"). The verso has the comparison of the just to the palm tree ("Palma enim tarde proficit ... quasi aridis corticibus obvoluta ... Habet quippe aliud palma"). Odo of Cluny's abridgement of the *Moralia* (PL 133) shares some phrases but lacks "per nidi nomen", "nisi tranquilla quies fidei" and the "Nam nisi sancta ecclesia ... nutriret" clause, all present on the leaf, so it is not the containing work. The leaf differs from PL in several readings, listed under Textual evidence, among them "Habet quippe" for "Habet quidem" and "qui se ab ecclesiastice pacis tempore de hoc [mundo exi]turos" for "qui se ecclesiasticae pacis tempore ab hoc mundo exituros".
- **Transcription:** source-assisted. The [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-hhs4/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-image-only.json) was made with the paper slip and the pencil shelfmark masked out; it read the shelfmark as "144". The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-hhs4/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-source-assisted.json) is a research reader's reread, on the native images, of every legible line on both faces. The top and bottom lines of each face are cropped and only partly read. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared four passages, two from each face, with the PL 76 page image for cols. 129-130.
  Checked again by the coordinator on the image: read on canvas 1 (about 16 lines from the top of the text, "...crediderunt. Eorum vox beati iob voce preve[nitur]" through "Dicebamque in nidulo meo moriar ... Palma enim tarde proficit ... Hec immerito uita iustorum", native box 2100,2380,4100,3900), compared with the PL 76 cols. 129-130 page image ([archive.org patrologiaecursu76mign, n64](https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecursu76mign/page/n64/mode/1up)); differences promittebat/promittebant and "Hec immerito uita iustorum"/"Nec immerito justorum vita".
  The variants were not checked against the critical edition (CCSL 143A). No human review.

Differences from PL where the line survives: "[pro]mittebat" for "promittebant"; "Hec inmerito uita iustorum" for "Nec immerito justorum vita"; "Habet quippe" for "Habet quidem"; "subito temptationis" for "subitae tentationis"; "stare diutius" for "diutius stare"; "nutriret" for "enutriret"; "[...]is istis quasi multis corticibus" for "in imo ista quasi multis corticibus"; and "qui se ab ecclesiastice pacis tempore de hoc [mundo exi]turos" for "qui se ecclesiasticae pacis tempore ab hoc mundo exituros". At recto ll. 9-10 the leaf probably has "quotiens p[redicando quem]piam ab errore convertit", a transposition against PL's "ab errore quempiam praedicando", but the words at the cut are lost. The rest is spelling (hereticorum, cicius, pulcre, inmerito). None of these affects the identification, and they were not compared with the apparatus of the critical edition.

Edition: *Moralia in Iob*, lib. XIX, cap. xxvi-xxvii, in J.-P. Migne, *Patrologia Latina* 76 (Paris, 1857; Maurist text), cols. 129-130, [archive.org patrologiaecursu76mign, n64](https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecursu76mign/page/n64/mode/1up). The page image was read; the running head "Moralium lib. XIX" and the chapter heads on Job 29:17-18 give the locus. The modern critical edition is M. Adriaen, CCSL 143A (1979), which was not consulted. The work is securely Gregory's.

## Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

The verso text follows the recto's after about three or four lost lines, so the strip is cut from one leaf trimmed at top and bottom as well as down the column. Whether the page had one column or two cannot be told from the strip. The modern slip and pencil notes are described above; there are a large pen-drawn S-like sign and faint later scribbles in the recto's left margin, and "hoc loco" (recto) and "obuolu" (verso) are underlined. Not claimed: the date and origin of the manuscript (the hand is protogothic by appearance, not examined palaeographically), its place in the textual tradition of the *Moralia*, the host volume, and whether any other Stams fragment belongs to the same book.
