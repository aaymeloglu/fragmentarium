# F-qqhf: Deed of sale of the Baurecht of a holding at Ögsten (Östen) in the Ötztal, held of the abbey of Frauenchiemsee, St Genevieve's day (3 January) 152[x]

This parchment sheet in the archive of the Cistercian abbey of Stams carries one German deed of sale. It was later cut down for use as a binding wrapper, with chamfered corners and a long slot cut through the middle. The text face was photographed upside down. A man of Ögsten, whose first name is lost, son of the late Thoman Sch[...], and his wife Katherina, daughter of the late Lienhar[t](?), both described as "zu Ogsten im Eztall" in the Gericht (court district) of St. Petersberg, with a reference to Roppen, declare "mit dem offen brief" that they have sold. The buyers are "[...] erbern Ewirt[leuten]", an honourable married couple, whose names are lost. The object is the Baurecht (a hereditary tenant's right to a farm held of a landlord) of a Hof (farm holding) or part of one, "zu Ogsten gelegen", held "von dem Gotshaus und Closter zu Chiemse". This is the Benedictine nunnery of Frauenchiemsee in Bavaria, the main landlord in that part of the Ötztal. The sellers convey "aus derselben" with the landlord's rent and lordship reserved, and acknowledge payment ("bezalt und ausgericht"). The dating clause reads "Geschechen an sand Geno[feen tag]" (St Genevieve, 3 January), and its last line ends "[...]zwayntzigisten Jare". The units of the year are lost at the cut corner, so the year is 152[x]. Ögsten is the old form of Östen, a hamlet of Umhausen in the Ötztal, not the village of Oetz.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-qqhf) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 48, one parchment item, 325 x 240 mm (measured 174/325 x 105/240 mm), online since 18 September 2021, with the title "x" and no date, type or description. The image labelled 01r (with an attached paper sheet, "Adl. 1r") is the outside of the wrapper. The image labelled 01v ("Adl. 1v") is the text face. Twentieth-century archivists left a typed partial transcription, a pencilled transcription of part of the disposition, and pencilled dates on a paper slip and label photographed with canvas 0 (see below). The record itself repeats none of this.

- **Identification:** partial, medium. The legal action (sale of a Baurecht), the sellers (a man of Ögsten and his wife Katherina, with their fathers), the place (Ögsten in the Ötztal, Gericht St. Petersberg), the landlord (Frauenchiemsee) and the feast (St Genevieve) are read on the image. The result is partial because the buyers are not established. "von Erbern Ewirt[...]" is the formula "von den erbern Ewirtleuten" (from the honourable married couple), not a personal name, and their names are lost. The pencilled transcription on the slip names the buyer's wife Madalena and calls the object two pieces ("zwey Stuck") of a Hof. Neither was read on the image, and neither is part of the regest. The year is 152[x]: the units digit fell in the chamfered lower-left corner or the cut slot. The pencilled "152(5?)" and "152X" on the labels are modern guesses and are not used. Place and landlord agree with the printed inventory *Die Gemeindearchive des Bezirkes Imst* (Tiroler Landesarchiv, 1995), which gives "Östen (Ögsten) im Ötztal" and Baurechte on "chiemseeische Lehen". They also agree with *Tiroler Familiennamenkunde* (1990), which notes Frauenchiemsee's spelling "Awsten" for Östen in its rent register of 1400. Both were seen as Google Books snippets only. The price, the boundary neighbours and the right halves of most lines are not read.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-qqhf/readings/stams-2026-10-31-image-only.json) found the text face, rotated it, and read scattered words ("Kauff", "verkaufft", "bezalt", "gelegen der"). It did not see the typed slip, which was masked. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-qqhf/readings/stams-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json) is a research reader's (Claude) reread of the rotated text face at native resolution in a grid of crops, with enhanced detail crops of the opening lines, the object, the landlord and the dating clause. That reading was made before the slip and pencil notes were compared. The middle band of the sheet is badly rubbed and was not read. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** there is no outside text of the act. A fresh reader who had not seen the reading was given neutral crops of the opening names, the place lines, the landlord line, the dating clause and the price. On the faded text it could read almost nothing, so the independent check is inconclusive. The names, place and feast rest on the research reader's reading. They agree with the typed slip, an earlier human reading made by a twentieth-century archivist, which gives "Ich ...n Ogsten weylenden Thoman schuebh... ...en zu Roppen Im gericht sandtpetersperg ... und ich Katherina weylenden Lienhar(?)... zu Ogsten im Eztall ... von dem Gotshauss und Closter zu Chiemse ... Geschechen an sand Genofeentag ... nach Cristus gepurdt funfze.. ...zwayntzigisten Jare". A second research reader could read "Ogsten weylenden Thoman sch[..] / vnd Ich Katherina weylenden [..] / [..] sein eliche hausfraw bek[ennen] / von Erbern Ewirt[..]" on a crop of the opening lines (canvas 1, Stams_STA_Frg_048_01v_Adl_01v, box 2462,5710-3612,6210, [image (rotated)](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-qqhf/Stams_STA_Frg_048_01v_Adl_01v.jpg/2462,5710,1150,500/full/180/default.jpg)), but had already seen the slip, so this is not a blind confirmation. Not checked: the Umhausen village archive entry the pencil label cites ("cf. Umhausen B VI"), the Frauenchiemsee fief and rent registers in the Bavarian State Archives, monasterium.net, and the host volume. No human review.

No edition or regest of this act was found, so there is no source column. The typed slip is the only other reading of the text, and it agrees in every point the image shows. It is part of the same object and is not an independent record. The pencilled transcription on the same slip goes further. It names Madalena as the buyer's wife (not read on the image), calls the object "die hienach benenten Baurecht der zwey Stuck ... Hoff ... daselbs zu Ogsten gelegen", and gives boundaries "ainhalb an der Margretten Witschijn(?) ... Fischmann ... Oberhalb an Clementen Walsers Baurecht". Those boundary names lie in the rubbed middle band and were not read independently. They are not part of the regest. St Genevieve's day is a fixed feast on 3 January. With the units of the year lost, the weekday is unknown (in the Julian calendar it falls on a Tuesday in 1520 and 1525, a Sunday in 1524 and 1529).

## Physical notes and limits

- One document in a German cursive chancery hand. Clause openings "Und", "Und", "Und", "Also" are enlarged in the left margin, and there is a decorated strapwork initial at the head of the left column. A flourished mark near the end of the text may be a scribe's paraph. The seal edge is cut away, so whether the sheet was sealed cannot be checked. Nothing marks it as a copy.
- Canvas 0 ([full image](https://fragmentarium.ms:443/loris/F-qqhf/Stams_STA_Frg_048_01r_Adl_01r.jpg/full/4912,/0/default.jpg)) shows the blank outside of the wrapper and the attached paper sheet. The sheet carries the typed slip and pencil transcription (box 780,450-3700,2450) and a pencil label (box 150,1500-1100,5300, written sideways). The label says the sheet served "bis 1966 als Einband" (as a binding until 1966) for a manuscript of an *Explicatio in Danielem prophetam* ("Hs. L V"), with the cross-references "cf. Umhausen B VI" and "Östen - Roppen" and the date "152(5?), 3. [Jänner]". A small label at the top edge (box 1750,2800-2350,3300) reads "3. Jänner / 152X".
- Not claimed: the units digit of the year, the seller's first name, the buyers' names, the price, the boundaries, and whether the sheet is the sealed original.
