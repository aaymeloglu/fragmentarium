# F-03xq: John of Abbeville, sermons for saints' days

The leaf is identified with high confidence as part of the sermons for feast days of John of Abbeville (Johannes Halgrinus de Abbatisvilla, d. 1237). Its four columns are one continuous passage: the second half of the sermon "Sicut exaltavit Moyses serpentem", on the cross, and the opening of the sermon "Ego quasi vitis fructificavi" for the Nativity of the Virgin. They agree, sentence for sentence, with a thirteenth-century manuscript of the collection.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-03xq) describes a trimmed parchment leaf used as the lower flyleaf of a volume in Oudenaarde, Stadsarchief, dated 1251–1300, catalogued as an unidentified biblical commentary with references to John 3:16 and the Book of Wisdom. The text is a sermon collection: John 3:14–16 is the theme of the first sermon and Ecclesiasticus 24 that of the second. The catalogue date has not been reassessed.

- **Identification:** identified, high. About 120 manuscript lines in three stretches correspond to the witness, including the end of one sermon ("ad quam nos perducere dignetur qui vivit"), the rubric for the Nativity of the Virgin, and the next sermon's opening, in that order. The cross-reference "illud scutum de quo dictum est in precedenti sermone" in the witness shows the passage belongs to an ordered collection. The work is not printed, so the comparison is with a manuscript, not an edition. A later collection that copies these two sermons whole was not excluded.
- **Transcription:** [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-03xq/readings/batch-2026-10-12-image-only.json) by an isolated reader, covering most of both sides, and a [source-assisted selected reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-03xq/readings/batch-2026-10-12-source-assisted.json). The middle of one column on the verso is rubbed and unread; line ends on the recto are lost under a turn-in. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader found the candidate through a sermon repertory, then read ff. 38r–40r of the Charleville manuscript from its page images and compared them with the first reading. The coordinator compared two places on the fragment image with the Charleville page image (below). The witness is a bitonal microfilm scan, legible but small. No human review.

The research reader's comparison also covers the rest of the left column on r (the four dimensions of the cross from Ephesians 3, the adamant that draws iron, from Amos 7 in the Septuagint reading) and about fifty lines of the Nativity sermon in the right column ("mater pulchre dilectionis, id est caritatis, ad differentiam dilectionis mundane vel carnalis ... Qui edunt me adhuc esurient"). The variants found are small: *sic habemus* for *sicut habetur*, *adimplens* for *implens*, *perducere* for *ducere*, *et exponitur* for *que tota exponitur*, *figura crucis domini* for *significat mysterium crucis Christi*. The comparison corrected several words of the first reading, for example "fractis portis inferni".

## The work and its attribution

The witness is Charleville-Mézières, Bibliothèque municipale, Ms. 218, thirteenth century, digitised from microfilm by the IRHT ([ARCA](https://arca.irht.cnrs.fr/ark:/63955/md19s1786r4p)) and catalogued there as John of Abbeville's sermons *de sanctis*. J. B. Schneyer, *Repertorium der lateinischen Sermones des Mittelalters*, vol. 3, p. 529, lists the two sermons under Johannes Halgrinus de Abbatisvilla as nos. 271 and 272, for the Exaltation of the Cross and the Nativity of the Virgin, with the same opening and closing words. The attribution rests on Schneyer and the IRHT catalogue; no ascription was looked for in the manuscript, and Schneyer was seen in search snippets only.

No claim is made about the fragment's date or origin, or about its relation to other manuscripts of the collection.
