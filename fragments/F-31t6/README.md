# F-31t6: Anonymous, *Tractatus passionis* "Scitis quia post biduum" (items 81_1-2), and Antonius Rampegolus, *Figurae Bibliae*, chapters *Diabolus*, *Fides*, *Humilitas*, *Perseverantia* and *Religiosi* (items 81_7r, 81_8r, 81_9, 81_10r, 81_11v)

This record holds pieces of two different manuscripts, which the catalogue already separates by hand. The pieces in the first hand, items 81_1 and 81_2, are from an anonymous Latin commentary on the Passion narrative of the four gospels, beginning *Scitis quia post biduum Pascha fiet*, edited by M. Reinhildis Ferber (1935) as the Latin source of the German *Kreuziger* of Johannes von Frankenstein. They cover the arrest of Christ, the bloody sweat, the crucifixion outside the gate, the age of Christ at his death, and the vinegar and gall. Five of the pieces in the second hand are from the *Figurae Bibliae* of Antonius Rampegolus (died 1423), an alphabetical handbook of moral topics for preachers: item 81_7r has part of the chapter *Humilitas*, item 81_8r part of *Fides*, item 81_9 part of *Religiosi*, item 81_10r part of *Perseverantia* and item 81_11v part of *Diabolus*. The other pieces in the second hand, items 81_4-6 and the faces 81_7v, 81_10v and 81_11r, are not placed.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-31t6) describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 81: paper, eleven items, 205 x 168 mm, title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description, with a reconstruction summary by Claudia Sojer assigning items 81_1-3 to one hand and 81_4-11 to another. It names no text. The six images each show several pieces mounted together: canvases 0 and 1 items 1-6 (fronts and backs), canvases 2 and 3 items 7-8, canvases 4 and 5 items 9-11. The division of hands fits the images. Items 81_1 and 81_2 are wide strips from a two-column book in gothic textura, each showing the outer column of one page, the fold, and the two columns of the facing page, so each is probably part of a bifolium (a folded sheet); item 81_3 is a small holed scrap. Items 81_4-11 are in a small, fast, heavily abbreviated cursive with red initials. The catalogue says paper; the first reading took the pieces for parchment; the material was not checked.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for items 81_1-2 (hand 1) and 81_7r, 81_8r, 81_9, 81_10r and 81_11v (hand 2). Items 81_3 and 81_4-6, and the faces 81_7v, 81_10v and 81_11r, are not identified.
  - Hand 1: five passages, on both faces of item 81_1 and both faces of item 81_2, agree with the commentary in Munich, BSB, Clm 2941 in argument, order of questions, authorities (Hebrews, Chrysostom, Isaiah 53:7, "dominus Albertus", Theophilus) and transitions, between red-framed lemmata (quoted gospel phrases) such as "Sequitur. Dormite" and "Sequitur. In littera". The fragment carries the commentary itself, not a quotation from it. The Stams copy differs from Clm 2941 in wording ("intimus" for "intrinsecus", "naturalem" for "supernaturalem", "universo mundo" for "toti et universo orbi") and twice in content: on 81_2v a boxed quotation of Bernard (*O Maria cur filius ...*) follows the bloody sweat where Clm passes to *Adhuc eo loquente*, and on 81_2r a note on "Quem queritis" follows the Isaiah quotation where Clm has Augustine on the falling back. This tract circulates in several versions; these are differences of redaction or copy, not of work. Thomas Aquinas, *Summa theologiae* III q. 46 a. 10, shares the phrase "ut veritas responderet figurae", but the surrounding text is not his.
  - Hand 2: on item 81_9 the end of section A of *Religiosi* (the three strong men at the cistern of Bethlehem, 2 Sam 23) and section B (*Expoliavi me tunica mea*, Song of Songs 5, with the *figura*, the Old Testament type, of Jonathan giving his tunic to David, 1 Sam 18) agree with the Paris 1510 print in sequence of lemma, moralisation and *figura*. On item 81_10 section B of *Perseverantia* (the *figura* of Apocalypse 1, the Son of Man *vestitum podere*) runs into the opening of the next section, *Exibit homo ad opus suum*, as in the print. Differences are of wording: "veste propria se exuens" for "vestem propriam extrahens", "attingens" for "pertingens", "tui certaminis obtinere" for "certaminis tui habere". On item 81_8r the end of section A of *Fides*, all of section B (lemma *Docet manus meas ad prelium*, Ps 143, on the training of soldiers, with Vegetius) and the opening of section C (*Notas mihi fecisti vias vite*, Ps 15) follow the print, scans 160-161, in order. On item 81_11v section D of *Diabolus* (*Sinite parvulos venire ad me*, Mt 19, on plants harmed in flower) follows scan 129, and on item 81_7r the *figura* of Joseph and Benjamin that closes section A of *Humilitas* follows scan 189.
  - A manuscript of the work, Munich, BSB, Clm 14376, was compared for 81_8r and 81_11v. The fragment agrees with it against the print in some readings: "insuetus" (print "inconsuetus"), "artis bellice" ("in artibus bellicis"), "clamorem hostium" ("hostium clamores"), "Satis bene facit" ("Satis ... facit"), "eos" ("eis"), and "dum leduntur in floribus totus perditur fructus" ("si ledantur in floribus totus eius fructus sequens leditur"). It agrees with the print against Clm 14376 in others: "in pugna" (Clm "in bello"), "Jo(b). In arte" (Clm "in marte", without Job), "eos vias" (Clm without "vias"). The fragment's text therefore follows neither witness throughout; these are differences of copy, not of work.
  - Not identified: items 81_4-6 and the faces 81_7v, 81_10v and 81_11r are in the same hand, with lemmata under red initials and "Fig(ura)" sections, but were not placed in the print. A search of the CoMMA corpus (machine-read text of about 32,000 digitised manuscripts) found nothing above chance for the passages of 81_4r, 81_4v and 81_6v, which are short and mostly from the first reading; 81_5 has only line fragments of one to three words and was not searched; 81_7v was not located in the print. They may come from the same work, but that is not shown.

## Other items and limits

Items 81_4-6 and the faces 81_7v, 81_10v and 81_11r (hand 2) are not placed. Lemmata read on them, mostly in the first reading: 81_6v Psalm 106:26 *Ascendunt usque ad celos*; 81_7v a section on kin ("cognatio") under a red C; 81_10v *Sagitte potentis acute*; 81_11r a numbered list ("6o vestit vasa ad sacrificium ...", "palleum"). Searches of the 1510 print's full text did not find these as lemmata, but its OCR is unreliable (it misses the lemma of 81_8r), and the CoMMA misses on short passages are not negative results either. The *Figura Exodi* at the foot of 81_8r section C was read only with the print in hand and is not in the tables. Item 81_3 is assigned to hand 1 by the catalogue only and is unread. Item 81_2r beyond the checker's lines, with Papias and a *figura* of Saul, was not collated.
