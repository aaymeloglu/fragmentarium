# F-r5u2: Guido de Baysio, *Rosarium super Decreto*, De consecratione D.2 (end) to D.3 "Ieiunia"

The leaf is from the *Rosarium* of Guido de Baysio, a commentary on Gratian's Decretum built as a supplement to the ordinary gloss (the standard marginal commentary), whose entries typically begin "In glo. … adde". It covers the third part of the Decretum, De consecratione: the last chapters of distinction 2, on the Eucharist (the closing glosses of the chapter before "Si credis", then "Si credis", "Nec Moyses", "Accipite" and those that follow), and distinction 3 from its first chapter "Pronuntiandum" to the chapter "Ieiunia", on feast days, fasting, the Rogation litanies, Lent and abstinence from meat.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-r5u2) describes Leuven, Archief van de Abdij van Park, IIB3h.1b/3: one folded parchment leaf, 242 x 194 mm, Southern Textualis, Italy, dated 1301-1350, with the title "Canon law commentary". That description fits. The record names no author, work or passage. The date, origin and script are the catalogue's statements and were not examined here.

- **Identification:** identified, confidence high. Four passages, one from each column, agree almost word for word with the Venice 1481 edition of the *Rosarium*, with the same abbreviated names of authorities (hu., lau., io. de fan., tho.) and the same legal citations, and the order of chapters and of "In glo. … adde" entries is the same across the leaf. Material from Thomas Aquinas on the fasting of children, from Huguccio and from Laurentius is quoted inside the text under those names; these are sources of the work, not the work. The later commentary on the Decretum by Aegidius Bellemera (Lyon 1550) repeats the same sentences. It is rejected as the containing work from search snippets only, the page not having been read: at the corresponding place the snippets show matter of his own ("scripsi post Henricum …", a citation of the Liber Sextus, headings "Tertia pars" and "Quarta pars tertiae distinctionis" with a summary) which is absent from the leaf where it was read continuously. Small differences of wording from the 1481 print are listed under Textual evidence; no claim about version is made.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-r5u2/readings/generic1-2026-11-24-image-only.json) and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-r5u2/readings/generic1-2026-11-24-source-assisted.json). A research reader reread on the images, with the edition at hand: recto column a lines 6-8; recto column b lines 1-12; verso column a, the last fourteen lines; verso column b lines 1-10. The rest of the leaf rests on the first reading, used only to follow the order of chapters against the print. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared crops of the manuscript images with page images of the Venice 1481 edition in the Munich copy, reading both sides of each comparison; the opening of recto column a was compared on a reduced view of the print page. COORDINATOR CHECK: the coordinator (Claude) read the "De esu" passage (canvas 1, native box 320,3560,1500,4290) on the image against [the 1481 Venice edition, Munich scan 808](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00093258?page=808): from "De esu. hic incipit" through "licet canon non observetur" the leaf follows the print with the same sigla (hu., lau., io. de fan.) and the same order, with only small variants (dicit/dic., consuetudine/consuetudo). Gaps: most of recto column a and the middle of the other columns were not reread; chapter numbers were not checked against Friedberg's edition of the Decretum; Bellemera's commentary and manuscripts of the *Rosarium* were not opened; no palaeographer has reviewed the reading or the match.

Differences. The manuscript has "uiri boni" for "boni viri", "anno et s." for "an. supra tamen", "de ieiuniis" for "de ieiunio", "cum integris noctibus precedentibus" for "cum suis noctibus integris preceden.", "peccatur" for "peccant" and "secundum lau." for "secundum h." after "sabbato"; it lacks the print's "exaudi", "lau." after "de nocte" and "secundum hu." after "non liceat"; where the print has "concilium" the manuscript has "consilium". Four readings are uncertain and are given as read, not as established variants: "indicuntur" and "dicitur" where the print has "inducuntur" and "legitur"; "duas" in "duas noctes"; and "io. de." without the "fan." of the print, in two places. The first reading's "in parisius scolares" is "alias sunt condempnandi scolares" on rereading. The leaf's coloured initials, as given by the first reading, do not all match the print's chapter openings (for example "[D]e quibus" where the print has "Ne quis tumore"); those were not reread.

Edition compared: Guido de Baysio, *Rosarium decretorum*, edited by Franciscus Moneliensis, Venice, Johann Herbort for Johann of Cologne, Nicolas Jenson and partners, 3 April 1481 (Gesamtkatalog der Wiegendrucke 03747), Munich, Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, digital copy bsb00093258, scans [806](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00093258?page=806), [807](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00093258?page=807) and [808](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00093258?page=808), page images. The attribution rests on the Gesamtkatalog entry and on the Munich record for this copy, which gives the author, editor and the colophon date; the colophon page itself was not opened.

## Leaf order and limits

Recto precedes verso, as the catalogue has it: the recto runs from the end of distinction 2 to the litanies, the verso from servile work on feast days to "Ieiunia". A running head in red and blue letters stands at the top of each face; it was not reread. Modern shelfmarks are written on the recto.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the leaf, which are the catalogue's; any relation of this copy to the printed text, to Bellemera's commentary or to other manuscripts of the *Rosarium*; chapter numbers within distinctions 2 and 3.
