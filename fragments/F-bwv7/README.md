# F-bwv7: Jacobus de Voragine, *Legenda aurea*, end of the Elizabeth chapter and part of the Cecilia chapter

The parchment bifolium Stams Frg. 222 comes from a copy of the *Legenda aurea* of Jacobus de Voragine, a collection of saints' lives arranged by the church year. Its visible face carries, on the right page, the end of the chapter on Elizabeth of Hungary (her posthumous miracles), then the rubric "De nomine sancte cecilie" and the explanation of Cecilia's name that opens the next chapter; on the left page it carries the middle of the Cecilia legend, from Valerian's answer to the prefect Almachius to Cecilia's interrogation. In the chapter numbering of Graesse's edition (2nd ed., 1850) these are the end of cap. 168 and part of cap. 169, Graesse pp. 770-771 and 774-776.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-bwv7) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 222: parchment and paper, one fragment item, 427 x 314 mm (measured as 423/427 x 310/314 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no date and no linked description, with two images labelled 222_01r and 222_01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: one parchment bifolium photographed open on canvas 0, and its other face, covered by a paper lining, on canvas 1.

- **Identification:** identified, high. The basis is sustained agreement in wording and sequence with Graesse's text over two separate passages, one on each page: the run of Elizabeth's miracles (Gertrud, Henricus, Mechtildis, the woman of Trier, Theodericus) followed by the Cecilia etymology, and the dialogue of Valerian, Almachius and Maximus in the Cecilia legend. The chapter order and the layout, with the explanation of the saint's name before the legend, are the *Legenda aurea*'s own. Abridgements and sermon collections built on the *Legenda aurea* were not tested separately; the continuous agreement of the Cecilia passage and the presence of the etymology favour the work itself. The copy differs from Graesse in a number of readings. The miracle of Scintrudis (Graesse's no. 15) is absent, the text running straight from Gertrud's cure to Henricus in the same line. The woman of Trier is not named (Graesse "Helibingis"). The Mechtildis miracle ends differently: her parents carry her to the tomb and she is healed at once, where Graesse has them vow her to Elizabeth and receive her healed. The Theodericus sentence lacks "a tumulo". In the Cecilia passage the copy reads "eternum inuenietis interitum" for Graesse's "invenietis aeternum luctum", "si pennis uolaueris" for "etiamsi pennis volaveris", "cotidie" without "vero", and "persi[..] uilissimi" for "personae vilissimae". These are variants within the work; which redaction of the *Legenda aurea* they belong to is not determined.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-bwv7/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-image-only.json) and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-bwv7/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-source-assisted.json). A research reader reread on the native image lines 9-31 of the right page and lines 1-23 of the left page; the remaining lines of the left page (24-45) and lines 1-8 of the right page rest on the first reading. The first one to three words of most right-page lines are faded and partly unread. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the two passages above with Graesse on page images of the archive.org scan (pp. 770, 771, 774, 775 and 776), and checked Graesse's Mechtildis sentence against Maggioni's critical text as given in Corpus Corporum, which agrees with Graesse there. Checked again by the coordinator on the image: canvas 0, left page, 14 lines from "recipiemus gloriam" through "interitum" and "O iuuentutis flos purpureus" to "Cui ualerianus ait. Quod", read on the image against the [page image of Graesse p. 775](https://archive.org/details/legendaaureavulg00jacouoft/page/775): agreement, with "interitum" for "luctum", "si pennis" for "etiamsi pennis", "cotidie" without "vero", and "persi[..] uilissimi" for "personae vilissimae". Coverage: the other face of the bifolium is hidden under paper, and the lower half of the left page was compared only through the first reading. Maggioni's apparatus was not consulted.

Elsewhere the copy agrees with Graesse in wording and order through the rest of both passages: the cure of Mechtildis's sight and hearing, the vision at the altar for the woman of Trier, Theodericus's four weeks at the tomb, the Cecilia etymology ("Cecilia quasi celi lilia uel cecis uia ... ardens per karitatem suc[censam]"), and the prefect's "Ergo iupiter nomen dei non est" with Valerian's reply. The differences are of three kinds: an absent miracle and an absent name in the Elizabeth chapter; a differently worded ending to the Mechtildis miracle, where the copy also differs from Maggioni's text; and small changes of word and order ("interitum" for "luctum", "cito" for "citius", "conterens" for "conficiens"). The first reading also gives "reddet duplum" where Graesse (p. 776) has "reddet centuplum", "Quarto autem miliario ab urbe roma" for "Quarto igitur milliario ab urbe", and "cum sacramento" for "cum jurejurando"; these lines were not reread on the image.

## Leaf order, other items, and limits

The right page (Elizabeth, Cecilia etymology) comes before the left (Cecilia legend) in the text, so the visible face is the outside of the bifolium: the right page is the recto of the first leaf, the left page the verso of the second. The text missing between them, about 129 lines of Graesse, fills about two pages at this copy's density (the left page holds about 64 Graesse lines), so the bifolium was probably the central one of its gathering. This is an estimate from text quantity, not a collation.

Relation to other Stams fragments. Stams Frg. 122 ([F-rdns](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-rdns)), also from the *Legenda aurea* (the Elizabeth and Clement chapters, caps. 168 and 170), is a candidate for the same manuscript. On images photographed at the same scale (about 137 pixels per centimetre on the rulers), both have a line spacing of 71-72 pixels (about 5.2 mm) and a written column about 149 mm wide, and both use the same red paragraph marks in a similar small textualis. This rests on measurement and appearance only; no hand comparison has been made by a palaeographer, and the text quantities do not fit the two bifolia standing in one gathering. Stams Frg. 219 ([F-n2rx](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-n2rx)), a *Legenda aurea* with chapters from Dominic to Martin, is not shown to share a manuscript with this bifolium: it is ruled for about 36 lines at about 6.4 mm, against 45 lines at about 5.2 mm here, and it places the chapter rubric differently.

Other items in the images: an upside-down calligraphic inscription in the lower margin of the left page from the bifolium's reuse in a binding (not read); an oval label "IV / 56"; a later marginal note beside the Cecilia rubric, read in part as "Defectu[s] ... in fine lib(ri)"; small marginal corrections, such as "a[p]paritoribus" beside "apitonib(us)"; and the pencil shelfmark. Canvas 1 shows the paper lining with a strip of later music manuscript at the spine and no medieval text.

Not claimed: date, origin, script dating, which redaction or family of the *Legenda aurea* this copy belongs to, or whether it shares a manuscript with Frg. 122.
