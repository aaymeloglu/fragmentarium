# F-gh8b: Breviary, sanctorale, offices for 1-14 September and 1-8 November

The six pieces of Stams Frg. 106 come from a Latin breviary, the book holding the texts of the daily Office. They hold its sanctorale, the part with the offices of saints' days, and they cover two stretches of the year.

- **September:** Verena and Giles (1 September), the octave of Augustine (4 September), the Nativity of Mary (8 September) and probably its octave (15 September), Protus and Hyacinthus (11 September), the Exaltation of the Cross and Cornelius and Cyprian (14 September).
- **November:** All Saints (1 November), All Souls with Eustachius and companions (2 November), Leonhard (6 November), Willibrord (7 November) and the Quattuor Coronati (8 November).

The Matins office for All Saints has three nocturns of three lessons each, the secular arrangement rather than the monastic one with twelve lessons. Its third nocturn carries the standard antiphons *Timete Dominum*, *Domine spes sanctorum* and *Beati quos elegisti*. These are followed by the Gospel Matthew 5:1-3 and the homily of Augustine, *De sermone Domini in monte* I.1, "Sermonem quem locutus est Dominus ...". The use, meaning the diocese or religious order whose form of the Office this was, is not established.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-gh8b) describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 106 (collection "Stams (Stealth Published)", online since 18 September 2021). It has six fragment items and a size of 100 x 94 mm, measured on piece 106_1 by Claudia Sojer. The title is "x", and the record gives no date or script. It describes the material as paper, which the images bear out: the blank margins show laid lines about 1.1 mm apart. Modern pencil notes on the pieces number them 1 to 6 with r and v, and say that they were taken "aus Urbar IV/42 v. J. 1625", that is, from the binding of an estate register of 1625. Other pencil notes give feast dates: 4.IX, 11.IX, 14.IX, 1.XI, 2.XI, 6.XI, 7.XI and 8.XI. The dates show that the calendar was recognised, but the record names no text and no kind of book.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence, as a breviary sanctorale for the feasts listed above.
  - The diagnostic basis is the combination in one book of Office chants, chapters, collects, Matins lessons with Gospel homilies, and rubrics for Lauds, Vespers, Compline and the Office of the Dead. Specific texts were located:
    - Three All Saints antiphons in sequence, Cantus Index 5151, 2390 and 1594. All three occur together in 60 of the 95 chant inventories that have any of them, so this is the common series and does not point to a use.
    - The Augustine homily.
    - The All Saints collects *Omnipotens sempiterne Deus, qui nos omnium sanctorum tuorum merita sub una tribuisti celebritate venerari* and *Da quaesumus ... fidelibus populis omnium sanctorum semper veneratione laetari*, from the Gregorian sacramentary.
    - The capitulum Apocalypse 7:9 with 7:16-17.
    - The Cornelius and Cyprian collect *Infirmitatem nostram respice*.
    - The Quattuor Coronati collect *Praesta quaesumus ... ut qui gloriosos martyres*, in the form that adds Severus, Severianus, Carpophorus and Victorinus to the five Pannonian names.
    - The Exaltation chant beginning "Salue crux" and continuing in the wording of *O crux gloriosa ... lignum pretiosum et admirabile signum per quod ... diabolus est victus et mundus Christi sanguine redemptus* (Cantus Index 4018).
    - The lessons for Giles, taken from the prose *Vita S. Aegidii* (BHL 93) §§3-4 in abridged form, with the second lesson beginning "Die vero dominica".
  - Differences from the standard texts:
    - The homily lacks "sicut in Evangelio secundum Matthaeum legimus" and probably "noster".
    - The second All Saints collect reads "omnipotens Deus" for "Domine".
    - A third All Saints prayer joins the opening "Omnipotens sempiterne Deus qui nos omnium sanctorum tuorum multiplici solemnitate gaudere ..." to the close of *Da quaesumus ... temporali gratulamur officio, ita perpetuo laetemur aspectu*. This combined form was not found in print.
  - The proper Leonhard collect *Deus qui largiflua bonitatis tuae auxilio ... beatum Leonhardum ... virtutum meritis decorasti* was read on the image but not found in print.
  - A monastic breviary is ruled out for the All Saints and Leonhard offices by the three-lesson nocturns.
- **Transcription:** see the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-gh8b/readings/stams-2026-10-31-image-only.json) and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-gh8b/readings/stams-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json).
  - A research reader reread every piece and face on the native images. That included contrast-stretched enlargements of piece 3, both faces, and of piece 5 on the face pencilled r. Pieces 2 and 3 are mounted upside down on canvas 0 and were read rotated.
  - Not read: the cut top lines of most pieces, the abraded middle of lines 8-16 on piece 3 (face r), most of the red rubric on piece 2 (face r) lines 5-6, and the faint traces below line 2 on piece 6 (face v).
  - Several rubric words remain uncertain, among them the rubric for the octave of the Nativity of Mary on piece 5 (face r).
  - Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the manuscript with these sources.
  - The opening of the Augustine homily (piece 3, face v, canvas 1, native box 3270,3960,4400,4330) against a page image of Migne, *Patrologia Latina* 34, col. 1229.
  - The All Saints collects and the Cornelius and Cyprian collect against the Gregorian sacramentary in the Corpus Corporum transcription, not page images.
  - The chants against the Cantus Index and Cantus Database inventories.
  - The Quattuor Coronati collect against a text snippet of *Acta Sanctorum*, November III.

  An independent checker compared the Giles lessons on piece 4, face r, lines 1-17 (canvas 0, native box 3480,320,4060,1450), with the *Vita S. Aegidii* (BHL 93) §§3-4 on the [page image of Jones 1914, p. 100](https://archive.org/details/saintgillesessai00joneuoft/page/n111/mode/1up). The line openings agree in sequence with the vita in abridged form: it omits "Mirabili quoque ... Interea", reads "oratione sua" for "oratione fusa", and starts lesson ii at "Die vero dominica". Line 17 is unresolved. The same check confirmed the feast rubrics on the images and the paper material, and it narrowed the gap between pieces 2 and 1.

  Gaps in coverage:
  - The second-nocturn lesson for All Saints on piece 3 (face r) and both faces of piece 6 are unidentified.
  - The homily on *Liber generationis* on piece 5 (face v) is unidentified; it is not Jerome on Matthew nor Augustine, *De consensu evangelistarum* II.1.
  - The Leonhard collect and one Exaltation prayer were not found in any source.
  - No other person has reviewed the reading or the match.

Differences:

- The homily omits the clause naming Matthew's Gospel, which fits the shortening usual in breviary lessons. The surviving letters cannot show whether it also omits "noster".
- The first All Saints collect has "tuorum" and "intercessoribus". The Gregorian text in Migne has "intercessionibus" and lacks "tuorum", though other witnesses (the Golden Legend's quotation among them) have "intercessoribus".
- The second All Saints collect has "omnipotens deus" for "Domine".
- The Quattuor Coronati collect names nine martyrs where the Roman form names five. *Acta Sanctorum* explains that the two groups, five Pannonian sculptors and four Roman soldiers, were joined under one feast.
- The Giles lessons abridge the vita: they omit "Mirabili quoque ... Interea" and probably "forte", read "oratione sua" for "oratione fusa", and lack or transpose "recepisse".
- The Exaltation chant opens "Salue crux" where the standard text opens "O crux". No other witness of this opening was found.

## The pieces and what is not claimed

**Order of the faces.** On the two tall strips (pieces 4 and 5) the face pencilled v carries the earlier text. Piece 5 runs on from the face pencilled v, which ends with the first Exaltation item "... sancte crucis", to the face pencilled r, which continues "Salue crux ...". The pencil r and v therefore do not give the order of reading.

**Joins.** No two pieces join edge to edge.

- Piece 2 stood above piece 1 in the same column. The narrow tongue of piece 2 is the upper part of that column. About one or two lines are lost between them. On the v faces piece 2 ends inside the Leonhard collect ("...decorasti ... concede quesumus") and piece 1 begins with its end ("[infor]mari exemplis ... protegamur adversis"), followed by the rubric "Historia de uno confessore non pontifice". On the r faces piece 2 ends a capitulum at Apocalypse 7:9 and piece 1 begins a new short reading at about Apocalypse 7:16.
- Piece 3 carries an upper margin and piece 6 a lower margin.
- The two tall strips are separate columns.

**Use.** What follows is inference and has not been tested.

- The three-lesson nocturns point to a secular breviary, not a monastic one.
- An octave of Augustine is a feature of breviaries of Augustinian canons, though not only of theirs.
- Verena belongs chiefly to the Alemannic and Swabian dioceses.
- Leonhard, with a proper collect, and Willibrord were kept widely in southern Germany and Austria.
- Together these suggest a south German or Alpine secular book, perhaps of Augustinian canons. A comparison of the Leonhard and Verena collects and the September sanctorale with breviaries of the candidate uses would be needed to settle it.

**Not claimed:**

- the date and the script, since the record gives neither;
- the origin and the use;
- whether all six pieces come from one leaf;
- any relation to other Stams fragments.
