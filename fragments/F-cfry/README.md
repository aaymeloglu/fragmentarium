Fifteen anchor points across both columns, in the edition's order, with the intervening text (the divisio textus, the Augustine *De civ. Dei* XIX,19 quotation, the Ps 109 gloss) following Lyra's sequence. The fragment's small variants (aut for nec; "in pontifice" for "ad pontificem"; "igitur" for "consequenter") are ordinary copyist substitutions.

Catalogue implications: title should read "Nicolaus de Lyra, Postilla litteralis super Epistolam ad Hebraeos, cap. 5" (genre: biblical postill, not homily). 15th-century date and Austrian/German hybrida are entirely consistent (the Postilla was completed c. 1331 and copied everywhere in Central Europe). The Farbkeil canvas shows the same side; the other side of the leaf (which should carry Heb 4:14–5:1 or 5:9–6) is not photographed.

## Ruled out

- Thomas Aquinas, *Super Epistolam ad Hebraeos* cap. 5 lect. 1–2 (Corpus Thomisticum che01.html): same pericope and similar divisio, but wording differs throughout; the Augustine "otium sanctum" citation is absent from Aquinas's Hebrews lecture (it is in *ST* II-II q.185 a.2 s.c.).
- Corpus Corporum (index p): "otium sanctum quaerit caritas" NEAR pontifex, "debitus modus" NEAR pontifical*, "non violenter extorta", "vacandum est" NEAR "nec quisquam sumit" — no hits (Lyra is not in that corpus).
- Google Books API queries on eleven connecting phrases: only Lyra (1588 ed.), the Lyra-derived *Postilla Guillermi* (1492, for the epistle "Omnis pontifex" of the Commune confessoris pontificis), Aquinas *Summa* (for the Augustine quote) and the Luther-lecture apparatus surfaced.
- Not checked because not needed once Lyra verified: Hugh of St Cher (gloss-e), Nicolaus Gorran, Peter of Tarentaise, Vienna-school sermon collections (Dinkelsbühl, Nider, Herolt).
