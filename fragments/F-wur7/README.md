# F-wur7: Guido de Baysio, *Apparatus ad Librum Sextum*, on Liber Sextus book 1 (De aetate et qualitate, c. *Permittimus*; De filiis presbyterorum, c. *Is qui*)

The legible side of this leaf is from the *Apparatus* on the Liber Sextus of the Decretals by Guido de Baysio, known as the Archidiaconus (died 1313). Its left text block runs from the glosses at the end of the title before *De aetate et qualitate* into the gloss on the chapter *Permittimus*, on the age required of those raised to dignities and personatus (offices of rank in a church); its right block is in the gloss on the chapter *Is qui*, under *De filiis presbyterorum*, on the sons of priests. The other side of the leaf is almost effaced and was not read.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-wur7) describes Leuven, KU Leuven Bibliotheken Bijzondere Collecties, Ms. 1378: one parchment leaf (parts), 170 x 238 mm, Southern Textualis, Latin, dated 1351-1400, tagged Canon Law, with the title "Commentary on the Liber sextum decretalium" and the subtitle "(De etate et qualitate; De filiis presbiterorum)". That description fits: the record already had the genre and the two titles of the Liber Sextus, but no author or work. Its date and script are the catalogue's statements and were not examined here.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence. Six passages from the two text blocks of the legible side agree with the Milan 1490 edition of Guido's *Apparatus* in wording, in the legal allegations (the cited canons and laws) and in sequence, across the chain of lemmata (the words of the text that each gloss takes up) from the end of the preceding title to *Permittimus*, and again in the gloss on *Is qui*. The author refers back to his own earlier gloss in the first person ("et dixi s(upra) e(odem) li(bro) de ele(ctione)"), as in the print. The *Novella* of Johannes Andreae, which reuses Guido extensively, and the ordinary gloss of the Liber Sextus were not compared at these places, so they are not excluded by collation; the agreement in sequence and in the first-person cross-reference is with Guido's printed text. At one place in the left block the first reading has "allegans q(uod) s(ecundu)m ar(c)do", which would make the text a commentary citing the Archidiaconus; the print there reads "alle. quod verum concedo", the coordinator read "al[le]" but not the words after it, and the leaf's wording at that point is left open. Differences from the print, listed under Textual evidence, are small: "permissio iuris noui" for "permissio istius iuris noui", a few changes of word order, and, in the *Is qui* gloss, no "et hoc verum credo" where the print has it.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-wur7/readings/generic2-2026-11-27-image-only.json) and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-wur7/readings/generic2-2026-11-27-source-assisted.json). A research reader reread on the native images, with the edition at hand, about twenty lines of the legible side: the first three lines of the left block, its lines from "[debito]res sumus" to the lemma *Permittimus* and the following gloss, four lines of the age passage lower down, and five lines of the right block. The rest of the side was only scanned, and the worn side was not read. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the passages in the table with page images of the 1490 edition, reading both sides of each comparison. The coordinator (Claude) read the passage from "c. placuit et c. subdyaconus" to "lxxviij di. c. i. ib[i]" on canvas 4 (native box 150,2020,2100,2660) against the page image of [fol. 40r col. b of the 1490 edition, BSB scan 83](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00065588?page=83): the sequence agrees, and a passage lower in the same block shows the age provisions ("de ele. cum in cunctis", "minor xxv annis"). Two fresh readers, given four neutral crops of this record without transcription or source, confirmed that nothing stands between the word read "ubicumq(ue)" and "quia" in the *Is qui* gloss, where the print has "et hoc verum credo". They did not confirm the opening words of the left block ("promoveri habita uxoris licentia", read after seeing the print), the start of the line read "[pun]iatur ad huius declarationem", the words read "quod prohibetur pro" after "quia" in the right block (both read "annos pl-" there), or the words read on the worn side; those readings are withdrawn and none is used below. Gaps: most of the right block and the lines between the collated passages of the left block were not collated; the worn side is unread; the *Novella*, the ordinary gloss, other editions and manuscripts of the *Apparatus* were not opened. No palaeographer or canonist has reviewed the reading or the match.

Differences. The manuscript has "permissio iuris noui" for "permissio istius iuris noui", "reg(u)las(?) sacr(as)(?)" for "sacras regulas" (the endings are not clear), "locum habet" for "habet locum", "assumet(ur)" for "assumeretur", and, in the *Is qui* gloss, "non ambigendum" without "est" and nothing between "ubicumq(ue)(?)" and "quia" where the print has "et hoc verum credo". In the coordinator's passage the leaf has "vel n(ecessi)tas(?)" after "utilitas", which the print does not have there; the word is uncertain. Several numerals and allegation sigla differ or are unsure ("lxxxiiij(?)" for "lxxvij(?)", "l. i(?)" for "l. ii(?)"). The word after "ho." in the age passage looks like "ad r[..]"; an abbreviation of the print's "adriani" is not excluded. Whether the leaf has the print's cross-reference "in fi. et hic adde et videas quod dicam ... ar. contra etc." after "dicunt quidam etc." is not settled, because the start of the following line was not securely read. These differences show that the leaf and the 1490 print differ at these places; they do not show which wording is earlier.

Edition compared: Guido de Baysio, *Apparatus Libri Sexti*, Milan, Jacobus de Sancto Nazario and Bernardinus de Castelliono, 23 December 1490 (Hain/Copinger 2719, IGI 4552, BMC VI 783, Goff B284), Munich, Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, digital copy bsb00065588, fol. 40r col. b to 40v col. a, [scan 83](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00065588?page=83) and [scan 84](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00065588?page=84), page images. The attribution rests on the Munich catalogue entry for this copy and its bibliographical references; the colophon page was not opened.

## Leaf, other side and limits

The legible side carries two text blocks of about 44 lines each, side by side, with a later running head "de etate (et) qualitate" in a cursive hand on the upper strip of canvas 4, a red and blue penwork initial **P** for *Permittimus* in the left margin, and a red marginal rubric beside it. In the print the right block's text begins about seventy lines after the left block's first line, so the two blocks may be the upper parts of columns a and b of a taller two-column page whose lower part is lost; that rests on the quantity of text only. The leaf was reused as a wrapper.

The other side (canvas 0) is almost effaced; only letter traces remain in the body, and the few words read on its turned-in lower strip were not confirmed by the two blind readers. It is not identified. The canvas labels call the effaced side fol. [1]r and the legible side fol. [1]v, while the image file names say the reverse; which side is the recto is left open.

Not claimed: the date, script and origin of the leaf, which are the catalogue's; any relation of this copy to the printed text or to other copies of the *Apparatus*, or whether its differences reflect a different state of the work; chapter numbers within Liber Sextus book 1; the text of the effaced side.
