# F-reyc: Dinus de Mugello, *Commentaria in regulas iuris pontificii*, regulae 20-21 and 30-32

This bifolium carries the commentary of Dinus de Mugello (Dinus Mugellanus) on the title *De regulis iuris* of the Liber Sextus, the collection of legal maxims that closes Boniface VIII's law book of 1298. The first leaf has the end of regula 20 (*Nullus pluribus*) and regula 21 (*Quod semel placuit*); the second has regula 30 (*In obscuris*), regula 31 (*Eum qui certus est*) and the opening of regula 32 (*Non licet actori*).

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-reyc) describes Brugge, Bisschoppelijk Archief, C516, 2: one parchment bifolium, 205 x 151 mm (leaf), Latin, Northern Textualis, dated 1301-1350, with the title "Commentary on the Regule iuris canonici". That description fits. The record names the kind of work but no author and no place in the text. The date and script are the catalogue's statements and were not examined here.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence. Four passages on three of the four faces agree with the printed commentary of Dinus in wording, in the order of examples and in the chains of legal citations (allegations), and the lemmata follow the Sext order of the regulae. A Roman-law commentary was considered, because nearly every citation is to the Digest and Code; it is not the containing work, since the headings are regulae of the Liber Sextus and the text cites "de electionibus c. nulli liceat li. vi". Citing Roman law throughout is how this commentary works. Other commentaries on the same title, and the ordinary gloss of Johannes Andreae, were not collated, so a later work reproducing Dinus at length is excluded only by the sustained agreement, not by a direct test. The manuscript differs from the 1563 print in a few places, the most material being "actione venditi vel quanto minoris" where the print has "actione redhibitoria, vel quanto minoris".
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-reyc/readings/generic1-2026-11-24-image-only.json) covers all four faces; the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-reyc/readings/generic1-2026-11-24-source-assisted.json) gives the lines relied on. All other lines rest on the first reading. Two further readers, given the image only, read the catchword. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the four passages with the Lyon 1563 edition: two against page images (pp. 129 and 169) and two against the uncorrected machine text of the same scan (pp. 127 and 171). The remaining columns were matched to the edition by sequence, from the first reading against the machine text, and were not collated.

Differences. In regula 31 the manuscript names the remedy for undisclosed defects of slaves as "actione venditi vel quanto minoris", the print as "actione redhibitoria, vel quanto minoris"; this is a difference of substance and is not explained here. The manuscript's lemma has "quis" where the print has "qui", and its red initial could be C or E. The manuscript adds "et § fi." to the first allegation and has "morbis" where the print has "moribus". The words between "actione" and "ad interesse" are washed out; "[...]to" survives, compatible with the print's "ex empto". In regula 21 the manuscript has a longer chain of Digest allegations after "l. Paulus" than the print, and reverses "aequa sive iniqua". On fol. [1]v the scribe wrote one line twice ("non licet regulariter penitere licet fallat in casibus qui", lines 3-6). Smaller differences are of number and word order ("vendit" for "vendunt", "huius" for "istius"). The printed additions of later jurists that surround the 1563 text are absent.

The catchword at the foot of fol. [2]v is uncertain in its first word. The catalogue-stage reading "impetere prohibetur ex" is not supported by the image. What can be said is that the leaf ends "et reo multo pl(us)" and the catchword ends "in ex", and that the print continues "ut patet in exceptionibus opponendis".

Edition used: *Dyni Muxellani ... Commentarius in regulas iuris pontificii*, Lyon, heirs of Jacopo Giunta, 1563, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_3qa8GbrPNXYC). The attribution rests on its title page and running heads ("Dyni Muxellani in"), and on the same attribution in the other sixteenth-century editions listed there. Ehrenreich Pirhing's *Jus canonicum* (1674) cites "Dynus n. 11" on regula 21 for the example of the elector who has named a candidate in the scrutiny, which is the passage on fol. [1]v. Modern scholarship on the attribution was not consulted.

## Leaf order and limits

The two leaves are not consecutive: nine regulae lie between regula 21 and regula 30. A catchword at the foot of fol. [2]v shows that a gathering ended there, which fits an outer bifolium whose inner leaves are lost; the structure of the gathering was not otherwise examined. Later cursive notes with tally strokes stand in the outer margin of fol. [1]r and are not part of the text.

By the first reading set against the edition's machine text, fol. [1]r column a holds the end of regula 20 (on joining actions and the plea of a matter already judged), fol. [1]v column a regula 21 on confession and payment of what is not owed, fol. [2]r regula 30 on interpreting against the seller, fol. [2]v column a regula 30 on offences and regula 31, column b regula 31 on notice. These placements were not collated.

Not claimed: a date or origin for the copy beyond the catalogue's; any relation between this copy and the printed text or other manuscripts; that the manuscript's fuller allegations or its "actione venditi" represent an earlier or better state of the text; that the fragment is unrecorded in the literature on Dinus.
