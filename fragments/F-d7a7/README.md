# F-d7a7: *Tabula super Summam confessorum*, the index printed with Johannes de Friburgo, *Summa confessorum*: Impedimentum to Interdictum

These four parchment strips in Stams are cut from one leaf of the alphabetical subject index (*tabula*) that is printed with the *Summa confessorum* of Johannes de Friburgo, a Dominican handbook for confessors. Each entry of the index states a point of law or a question and refers to book, title and question of the *Summa*, or to its additions drawn from the Liber Sextus ("in addicionibus"). The strips carry the end of the entry Impedimentum, the entries Inquisitio, Insidie and Institutio, and two stretches of the long entry Interdictum. In the Lyon edition of 1518 this is fol. xv, recto and verso, of the index. Who compiled the index was not examined.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-d7a7) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 150: parchment, one fragment item, 127 x 25 mm (the left standalone strip, measured as 125/127 x 24/25 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description, with two images labelled 150_01r and 150_01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: one standalone strip and three adjoining strips, mounted under bands of brown paper and photographed on both sides.

- **Identification:** identified, high, as the index printed with the *Summa confessorum*. The basis is agreement entry for entry, in wording, order and question numbers, with the index in the 1518 edition across all four strips on both sides: Inquisitio from "tam in capite quam in membris q. vi" to its last entry, then "Insidie. Quere bellum", "Institutio. Capitulum vacante sede", and the opening of Interdictum on one side; Interdictum with the question numbers 232 to 234 and 241 to 249 on the other. The single letter "e" that follows most question numbers is explained by the index's own prologue: it means that the question begins with the same words as the entry. The entries refer to the *Summa*'s book III, title 31 (inquisitio) and title 33 (interdict); the text of those questions was not reread, since the index itself was matched. A different index to the same *Summa* is excluded for these columns by the sustained agreement of wording; whether other recensions of this index exist was not examined. Differences from the print are small: "audiri possint" for "possunt", "appellat" for "appellaret", "vel aliorum" for "aut aliorum", an added "Nota" before one reference, and in one line "qui excludu[..]" where the print has "quos excludere".
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-d7a7/readings/stams7-2026-11-01-image-only.json) of 1 November 2026 and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-d7a7/readings/stams7-2026-11-01-source-assisted.json). A research reader reread all four strips on both sides on the native images. On the side labelled 01r the question numbers, "e . t . q", "qui causam dede-", "interdictum", "audiri possint", "in aggrauacionem cessacionis", "beate virginis", "vrticis et spinis" and "Suspensio ab officio" were read before the source was known. The side labelled 01v and the full line-by-line text were read with the printed index open; where the ink is clear each expansion can be checked letter by letter on the image, but these are not independent readings. The middle of the narrow strip on 01v and the faded right half of the leftmost adjoining strip on 01r were read only in part. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the three adjoining strips of both sides and the standalone strip of 01r with page images of the index in the 1518 edition (archive.org scans n580 and n581), and the standalone strip of 01v with the top of the same printed page. The coordinator read the middle band of canvas 0, region 3640,2670,1160,520 ("q . ccxxxij . e", "suspendi possit idem et q. se[quenti]", "in addicionibus e. q. De forma", "capella nec in cimiterio", "sepeliri in addicionibus idem"), on the image and against the page image https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_pIDxHkKLkbkC/page/n580/mode/1up , col. b, and found the same entries in the same order. A separate reader given only crops of three bands, with no source, read scattered words that agree with the print, among them "Suspensio ab officio", "suspendi possit", "addicionibus e. q. De forma", "capella" and "inquisicionis ... duo noua", and read the numeral as ccxxxij, although the example in its instructions was that same numeral, so its agreement there carries little weight. Gaps: only one printed edition was used and no manuscript of the index; the attribution of the index was not checked in the scholarship.

Differences: "audiri possint" for the print's "audiri possunt"; "appellat" for "appellaret"; "vl' alio[..]" for "aut aliorum"; "No(ta)" before "in addicionibus e. q. in principio", absent from the print; "z qui excludu[..]", with a stroke over "qui" and the word cut after "excludu", where the print has "et quos excludere debeant"; "Sub q(..) form[..]", cut at the strip's edge, which may be the print's "Sub qua forma" or a plural. The rest is spelling (-cio for -tio, nichil, id(em) for ibidem). These show that the copy and the print differ in small ways; they do not show how the two are related.

Edition used: *Summa confessorum reuerendi patris Ioannis de Friburgo*, Lyon 1518, in the archive.org scan [bub_gb_pIDxHkKLkbkC](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_pIDxHkKLkbkC), read on the page images: the index's prologue at [n552](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_pIDxHkKLkbkC/page/n552/mode/1up), fol. xv recto at [n580](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_pIDxHkKLkbkC/page/n580/mode/1up) and verso at [n581](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_pIDxHkKLkbkC/page/n581/mode/1up). The index is headed there "Tabula summe confessorum. Prologus tabule ... Incipit tabula super summam confessorum". Its prologue says: "ubi post numerum non ponitur principium questionis ad quam fit remissio sed tantum .e. intelligitur eadem esse questionis inceptio sicut hic in tabula ponitur", and "cum dicitur .e. q. intelligitur in ti. eo. et de his que ponuntur sub numero eiusdem questionis". The edition's title page attributes the *Summa* to Johannes de Friburgo; it does not settle who made the index, and that was not looked into.

## Leaf order and what is not claimed

The text shows that the side labelled 01v comes first. On 01v the standalone strip (right) is from the first column, with the end of Impedimentum, and the three adjoining strips (left) are the second column, with Inquisitio to the start of Interdictum. On 01r the three adjoining strips (right) are the first column of the other side, with Interdictum, questions 232 to 234, and the standalone strip (left) is from its second column, with Interdictum, questions 241 to 249.

No date or place of origin is claimed; the hand looks 14th-century but was not studied. It is not claimed that Johannes de Friburgo compiled the index, that this copy stood in a volume with the *Summa* itself, or that it has any particular relation to the printed text. Whether the strips are already recorded in the literature was not checked.
