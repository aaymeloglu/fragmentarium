# F-l7tu: Missal (probably Cistercian), proper prefaces of Christmas and Epiphany and the end of the Ordo missae

This parchment bifolium at Stams comes from a missal. One leaf holds the proper prefaces of Christmas and Epiphany, each followed by its proper Communicantes (the variable paragraph of the Canon for a feast). The other leaf holds the end of the Ordo missae (the fixed prayers of the Mass) after communion, and on its back the end of a Sanctus and a full threefold Agnus Dei:

- **Christmas:** the end of the preface "Quia per incarnati verbi mysterium" ("... lux tue claritatis infulsit ... sine fine dicentes"), then the Communicantes "Communicantes et diem sacratissimum celebrantes quo beate Marie intemerata virginitas huic [mundo edidit salvatorem]".
- **Epiphany:** the rubric "In epiphania", the preface "Quia cum unigenitus tuus in substantia nostre mortalitatis apparuit, in nova nos immortalitatis sue luce reparavit", and the Communicantes "quo unigenitus tuus in tua tecum gloria coeternus".
- **End of the Ordo:** "Corpus domini nostri Ihesu Christi custodiat me in vitam eternam. Amen", "Sanguis domini nostri Ihesu Christi custodiat me in vitam eternam. Amen", the prayer "Placeat tibi sancta trinitas", and the opening of the blessing "Meritis et precibus istorum et omnium [sanctorum ...]".
- **The other face of that leaf:** the end of a Sanctus, the Agnus Dei three times in full, and a faint prayer beginning "[O]mnipotens sempiterne deus", not identified.

The texts were matched on page images with a Cistercian manuscript missal (Fribourg, BCU, Ms. L 305) and with the Roman Missal printed at Milan in 1474. The use of the book is probably Cistercian, but that is an inference of medium confidence.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-l7tu) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 212: parchment, one fragment item, 387 x 213 mm (measured 335/387 x 201/213 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), no date, with the title "x", no linked description, and two images labelled 01r and 01v. The record does not name the text. Its description fits the images: one bifolium, one column to a page, about ten or eleven surviving lines on each page, the tops trimmed.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for the content. The basis is every page read on the images and collated with the two missals.
  - **Prefaces and Communicantes:** they agree with L 305, ff. 138r-138v, and with the 1474 Roman Missal, p. 202, apart from small readings. The Epiphany preface reads "in noua nos", as L 305 does, where the Roman print has "noua nos".
  - **End of the Ordo:** the leaf runs Corpus "custodiat me" → Sanguis "custodiat me" → Placeat → "Meritis et precibus", with nothing between them. L 305, f. 144v, has exactly that sequence. The 1474 Roman Missal (pp. 210-211) has "custodiat animam meam", puts Quid retribuam, Quod ore sumpsimus and Corpus tuum before the Placeat, and has no Meritis prayer.
  - **Differences:** the leaf omits "nostre" before "visibiliter", which both books have; it reads "genitricis eiusdem dei" (with the Roman print; L 305 "genitricis dei"), "Placeat tibi sancta trinitas" without "domine" (with the Roman print), "sit tibi acceptabile" (with L 305), "tuum sacrificium", "Amen" after the Corpus and Sanguis formulas, and "Sect" for "Sed". Its text differs from L 305 in these points; whether it depends on L 305 is not established.
  - **Use (inference, medium):** probably Cistercian. A blessing of the "Meritis et intercessionibus istorum et omnium sanctorum" type after the Placeat, and the formula "custodiat me", also occur in diocesan books of the Salzburg province and elsewhere in Germany: Seckau, and a German codex printed by Brenner, which has "Sanguis ... custodiat me". So neither feature is Cistercian by itself. The diagnostic points are the wording "precibus", every attestation of which that was found is Cistercian, and the run from Corpus and Sanguis straight to the Placeat, with no ablution prayers (the prayers said while the vessels are cleansed). The Augsburg order, read on page images of its printed missals of 1510 and 1555, has those ablution prayers and no Meritis, so Augsburg use is excluded. No missal of Brixen (the diocese of Stams), Salzburg or Freising and no Benedictine missal was checked, and the Carthusian order of Mass, which may share the short ending, was not tested.
- **Transcription:** source-assisted. The [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-l7tu/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-image-only.json) covers all four pages. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-l7tu/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json) is a research reader's reading of every page in close crops: all lines of the two preface pages and of the Ordo page, and on the Sanctus/Agnus page lines 1-7 in full and lines 8-10 (the faint prayer) only in fragments. An independent checker reread lines 1-10 of that page on the image. Uncertain points are "hec tuum sacrificium" (edition "hoc"), the last word of "Meritis et precibus istorum et omnium [sanctorum]" at a tear, and the whole of the faint prayer. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the prefaces, Communicantes and post-communion section with page images of L 305 (ff. 138r, 138v, 144v) and of the 1474 Roman Missal (pp. 202, 210-211), and the Placeat and Meritis with page images of the Cistercian Ordo printed in *Der Katholik* 1882. The use claim was tested against Seckau, Brenner's codex and the Augsburg printed order on page images of nineteenth-century editions or descriptions; Dijon 114 and Pontarlier 8 (Cistercian) and some Rhenish, Westphalian and Italian books were seen only in OCR text of printed descriptions.
  An independent checker read lines 1-10 of the Sanctus/Agnus page (canvas 0, left page, native boxes 1750,1150,3700,2000 and 1750,1900,3700,3050, with a blue-channel crop of 1750,2400,3700,3050) and compared the Agnus Dei with L 305, f. 144r ([page image](https://www.e-codices.unifr.ch/en/bcuf/L0305/144r)). The text agrees; the leaf writes the Agnus in full each time, where L 305 abbreviates. Because the Agnus is invariant, this confirms the reading but not the use. The checker could confirm only "-potens sempiterne deus" and "complacacion-" in lines 8-10 and could not identify the prayer either. The checker had seen the research transcription of the page first.
  Gaps: no person has reviewed the reading. No Brixen, Salzburg, Freising, Benedictine or Carthusian missal was compared, and no second Cistercian missal from the German-speaking area.

The Christmas preface on 1r (canvas 0, 4150,1150,6100,2250) agrees with both books apart from spelling ("amore" with an ur-sign over "rapiam", "dnacionibus"); in the Christmas Communicantes the leaf reads "diem sacratissimum ... quo", where L 305 has "diem sacratissimam ... qua" and the Roman print "noctem uel diem sacratissimam ... qua uel quo". The *Der Katholik* Ordo has "Placeat tibi sancta trinitas unus Deus", "hoc sacrificium" and "tibi sit acceptabile", against the leaf's wording. The small differences listed under Identification are the kind found between any two copies; the post-communion sequence is a structural feature.

## Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

- **Rectos and versos:** right-hand pages are rectos on both canvases. The Christmas preface (canvas 0, right) continues on canvas 1, left, so the preface leaf's recto and verso are fixed, and so are those of the Ordo leaf (Corpus/Placeat on the recto, Sanctus/Agnus on the verso).
- **Leaf order:** which leaf comes first cannot be read off the images. Prefaces before the end of the Ordo is the natural liturgical order, and the leaves are numbered 1 and 2 on that inference. If so, the remaining prefaces and the Canon stood between them.
- **The second Sanctus and Agnus:** on 2v a Sanctus end and the threefold Agnus come after the Meritis blessing, followed by the unidentified prayer. In L 305 the Agnus stands inside the Ordo, before the commixtio. This arrangement is unexplained; 2v may begin another section, but that is not shown.
- **Lines lost:** the tops of all pages are trimmed through the first line; the lower parts are blank margin.
- **Other marks:** modern pencil "Stams, StA, Frag. 212r" and "212v"; pencil "1684"(?); an ink note "O V XXII ... n. 169" on 1r; pencil numerals on 2r.
- **Not claimed:** the date, the origin, the use of the book (Cistercian is probable, not established), that it was made or used at Stams, and any relation to F-v82x (Frg. 69), which shares the Meritis ending but has a smaller script (line pitch about 113 against about 155 pixels).
