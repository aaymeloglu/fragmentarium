# F-t6yk: Bishop Georg of Brixen, annual circular to the parish clergy with the calendar of movable feasts for 1530, and a Lenten confession faculty, [1529]

This paper leaf in the archive of the Cistercian abbey of Stams holds two documents. Both are issued by Georg ("Georgius dei gratia Episcopus Brixinensis"), that is Georg of Austria, bishop of Brixen 1525-1539. Both are addressed to the rectors, perpetual vicars and deputies of the parish churches of the city and diocese of Brixen. The first is an annual circular. It orders the clergy to pay the cathedraticum, the yearly due owed to the bishop's see, at the usual rate to the bishop's messenger. Newly appointed vicars, curates and assistants must present themselves to the vicar general within a month for examination and admission, on pain of excommunication. Outside priests and monks are not to be admitted to celebrate without proof of orders, and questors and alms collectors are not to be proclaimed without a written licence. Reserved cases and marriage cases (marriage within forbidden degrees, couples living together within them, weddings in the closed season without licence) are to be sent to the bishop's consistory. The circular ends with the calendar for "Anno Tricesimo proximo venturo", the coming thirtieth year. It gives the "regula" of the breviary table as 27, the dominical letter (the letter marking the Sundays of the year) as B, Septuagesima on 13 February, Easter on 17 April, Ascension on 26 May, Pentecost on 5 June and Corpus Christi on 16 June, followed by the transfers of feasts that clash. It closes "Alia omnia sunt plana & clara". The second document grants the same clergy the faculty to hear their parishioners' confessions "hoc futuro tempore Quadragesimali" (this coming Lent) and to absolve them, also in cases reserved to the bishop, with a list of exceptions (heretics, the excommunicated, violators of clergy, offenders of parents, enchanters and others). It breaks off at the torn lower edge. Neither document keeps a dating clause. The calendar is for 1530, so the circular was issued in [1529], and the Lenten faculty belongs with it to Lent [1530].

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-t6yk) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 177, one paper item, 330 x 212 mm (measured 327/330 x 205/212 mm), with the title "x" and no date, type or description. Modern pencil notes on the leaf read "(Dat. 1529)", "(1525-1539)", "als Einband zum II/633 (vom Jahre 1535)" and the shelfmarks "Stams, STA, Frg. 177r/v". The record has nothing to confirm or correct beyond the missing regest.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for both documents. The leaf holds two documents, each opening with its own intitulatio "Georgius dei gratia Episcopus Brixinensis". The regests are read on the image: issuer, addressees, and the substance of both acts. The year of the circular is read in the text ("Anno Tricesimo proximo venturo") and checked against the calendar it gives. Easter on 17 April, Septuagesima on 13 February and dominical letter B fit 1530 in the Julian calendar. Between 1460 and 1545 Easter fell on 17 April only in 1468, 1530 and 1541. 1468 was a leap year (letters CB, Septuagesima 14 February). 1541 fits the calendar but not the written "Tricesimo", and by then Georg had left Brixen. Georg was elected at Bruneck in 1525 and moved to the archbishopric of Valencia in November 1538 (*Die Brixner Bischöfe in der Geschichte Tirols*, 1984; Nuntiaturberichte index, "Bischof Georg von Österreich, 1525-1539"; *Innsbrucker historische Studien*, 2000; Google Books snippets). The earlier bishops named Georg (Georg von Stubai, 1437-1443; Georg Golser, 1464-1488) cannot issue a calendar for a "thirtieth" year. The supplied elements are the year of issue [1529], deduced from the coming year 1530, and the Lent of the second document, [1530], deduced from its place on the same leaf. The place and day of issue are not preserved. Without seal, address or signature, written by one hand on both faces with struck words, the leaf is a copy or a chancery exemplar, not a sealed original. No independent record of either circular was found.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-t6yk/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-image-only.json) covers every written line of both faces. Its reader had no proposed identification in context. The modern pencil notes were masked from that reader. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-t6yk/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-source-assisted.json) is a research reader's (Claude) reread of both faces at native resolution in half-width bands of five to six lines, with detail crops of the first line and of the pencil notes. It agrees with the first reading in substance and makes small corrections (for example "persolvistis"). Uncertain readings remain at the opening of canvas 0 ("Licet(?) d[...]tis"), in the taped centre strip and along the torn lower edges. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** there is no outside text of the acts to compare. A fresh reader who had not seen the reading was given neutral crops of the words the regests depend on and asked what each says. The crops were both openings, the year line, the line with the dominical letter and Septuagesima, the line with the Easter date, and the line naming the season of the second document. The fresh reader confirmed "Georgius dei gratia Episcopus Brixinensis" in both openings, the letter B, Septuagesima "tertiadecima Februarii", Easter "decima septima Aprilis" and "Quade[...]" (Quadragesima, Lent). It read the year as "Anno Tricesimo p(ri)mo" (thirty-first). The coordinator reread that crop as "Anno Tricesimo p(ro)ximo uenturo", with the x clear. The calendar figures (letter B, 13 February, 17 April) fit 1530 and not 1531, so the reading 1530 stands. The research reader checked the bishop's dates in book snippets, not page images. Not checked: Sinnacher's *Beyträge zur Geschichte der bischöflichen Kirche Säben und Brixen* for Georg's episcopate, the Brixen consistorial records where such circulars may be registered, and Stams archive item II/633 of 1535, which the leaf once covered. No human review.

No edition or regest of either act was found, so there is no source column. The calendar was checked by computing the movable feasts of 1530 (Julian): Septuagesima 13 February, Easter 17 April, Pentecost 5 June and Corpus Christi 16 June, all as written. The transfers fit the same year: St Vitus (15 June) falls on the Wednesday after the octave of Pentecost, and St Leonard (6 November) on a Sunday.

## Physical notes and limits

- One paper leaf in landscape, folded vertically in the middle, with modern tape repairs on the fold, a hole in the centre of both faces and a torn lower edge that loses a line or two on each face. One fast Latin chancery cursive throughout.
- Canvas 1 opens with the circular's intitulatio; canvas 0 begins mid-sentence with the calendar and ends the circular before the second document. The leaf is therefore probably read canvas 1 then canvas 0, with little lost between them. A lost leaf in between cannot be excluded.
- The pencil note "als Einband zum II/633 (vom Jahre 1535)" (canvas 0, box 4300,1180-6350,1500) records that the leaf served as the cover of Stams archive item II/633, of 1535. That item was not identified.
- Not claimed: the place and day of issue, whether the leaf is a chancery minute, a circulated exemplar or a copy made for the abbey's parishes, and the exact year of the second document.
