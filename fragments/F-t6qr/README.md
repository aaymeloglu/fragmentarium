# F-t6qr: Justinian, *Codex* with the Accursian Glossa ordinaria, C. 4.13 (Auth. *Habita*) and C. 4.21.21-4.22.1

Stams, Frg. 254 consists of two parchment binding strips from two different leaves of a glossed manuscript of Justinian's *Codex*. Each strip preserves only the left-hand edge of a column, so every line is cut after its first 8 to 15 characters.

- **Strip 254-1:** the main text of C. 4.13 with the *Authentica Habita* of Frederick I. This is the privilege for students placed after C. 4.13.5 *Ex patroni*, here under the rubric "l. Ex patroni. Nou[a constitutio]" and enclosed in a red and blue ruled frame. Below it is the ordinary gloss of Accursius on *Habita*.
- **Strip 254-2:** Accursius' gloss on C. 4.21.21 (*Cum quidam instrumentum*), the rubric and title summary of C. 4.22 (*Plus valere quod agitur quam quod simulate concipitur*), and the opening gloss on C. 4.22.1 (*In contractibus*).

The catalogue record is at [fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-t6qr](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-t6qr): Stams, Stams (Stealth Published), Frg. 254, parchment, two fragment items, 449 x 45 mm (item 254_1 444/449 x 40/45 mm), online since 2021-09-18. Its title is "x" and it links no description. The catalogue therefore identified no text. Its count of two items and its dimensions fit the two strips in the photographs.

- **Identification:** identified, confidence high.
  - **Strip 254-1.** The line openings of the framed main text follow the *Habita* text in sequence over some twenty lines, from "[H]abita sup(er)" to "[...] ca(usa) iustissima". The rubric names the Codex law after which the constitution stands.
  - **Strip 254-2.** The gloss lemmata, which are the quoted words a gloss comments on, run *Dolo*, *Reuerentia*, *Ei*, *Per solitam*, *Sopita*. They are followed by the *Plus valere* rubric. The allegation chain of the *Sopita* gloss (its string of references to other laws) agrees with the Accursian gloss as printed: "fratris ... inst. de p[oe.] ... j. de ag[ri.] ... post sententiam ... instru. l. i. ... iudex. ibi sic ... fuit de hoc q[uestio]".
  - **Alternatives.** I considered the *Libri feudorum* and the tenth *collatio* of the *Volumen*, where constitutions of Frederick also circulate. They are not the containing work: strip 254-2 glosses C. 4.21.21 of the Codex itself, and the gloss on strip 254-1 matches the Codex gloss on *Habita*. A canon-law gloss, suggested by a first impression of the strips, is not supported.
  - **Differences from the 1574 print.** Lemmata are shorter in two places ("Sopita" against "Et sopita", "Reuerentia" against "Subtili reuerentia"). The *Pagina* gloss appears to end after about five lines with "s. e(o). l. ii.", but only line openings could be compared. One *Optione* allegation cites the same Digest law by another incipit: "l. si q(ui)s a[...]" against "l. si quis intentione".
  - **Which version of the Accursian gloss** the manuscript carries is not established.
- **Transcription:** I made the source-assisted reading, rereading the rotated recto of both strips in bands of 10 to 15 lines. The *Habita* main text, the *Speciali* and *Optione* glosses, and the left-strip glosses from *Pagina* to *Plus valere* were reread on the image. The first reading, made from the images alone, had taken the framed main text for gloss; it was corrected against the source. Accuracy has not been measured. Readings:
  - [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-t6qr/readings/stams5-2026-11-01-image-only.json)
  - [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-t6qr/readings/stams5-2026-11-01-source-assisted.json)
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the strips with page images of the 1574 Accursian Codex: *Habita* in cols. 763-765 and C. 4.21.21-4.22 in cols. 831-832. Because only line openings survive, **the match rests on short line openings in sequence**, not on complete lines.

  An independent checker read a passage this research had not transcribed: the eighteen lines of strip 254-2 directly below the *Plus valere* rubric (canvas 0, native box 1690,3050-2020,4200). The checker read them first from the image and then compared them with the 1574 page image of col. 831-832. Findings:
  - The openings of the italic title summary "[Q]uia per scriptura(m) d[e qua ...]" fit.
  - So do the decorated initial of "In contractibus" and the gloss opening "P(re)mittit [aphorismum]".
  - The lines after them are allegation strings, too short to compare securely.

  The checker also confirmed the rubric and opening lines of the framed *Habita* on a separate crop.

  Not covered:
  - the block of gloss on strip 254-1 between the frame and the *Habita* gloss (probably gloss on C. 4.13.1-5);
  - the versos of both strips, which are abraded and carry later notes.

  The coordinator compared the left strip, canvas 0, below the *Plus valere* rubric (native box 1690,3050,2020,3450, read rotated 180 degrees; line openings "...cau. l. ii", "[per im]pressionem", "...ad Macedo[nianum]", "l. si fi[liusfamilias]") with the 1574 page image, col. 832 ([archive.org n542](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_Ol5_1dLT8CEC/page/n542)): the openings fall in the print's order; only line openings survive.

Differences:

- The manuscript writes "Hac/Hec igitur", "irrog[ata]" and "carea(n)t" where the 1574 text has "irroganda" and "se carituros".
- The frame closes at "iustissima". Whether "a tali conamine cadat" followed outside the frame or is lost cannot be seen.
- In the *Habita* block an opening read "[...]torib(us) aut ep(iscop)is" is unexplained.
- Gloss lemmata are shorter in the manuscript, as noted above. Its gloss signes restart at "a" for *Sopita*, where 1574 has "g".
- The *Pagina* gloss and an *Ei* gloss reading "p(re)sumit(?)" do not follow the printed wording. These lines are uncertain and were compared only by their openings.
- The manuscript carries the title summary of C. 4.22 ("Quia per scripturam ..."), which the 1574 edition sets in italic as a separate item.

Edition compared: *Codicis DN. Iustiniani ... repetitae praelectionis, libri 12. Accursii commentariis ... illustrati*, 1574. I read page images, not OCR:
- [cols. 763-764](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_4tFKnx1BKAYC/page/n497)
- [col. 765](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_Ol5_1dLT8CEC/page/n509)
- [cols. 831-832](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_Ol5_1dLT8CEC/page/n542)

The gloss is attributed to Accursius by the title page of that edition. The wording of *Habita* and the lemmata of C. 4.21.21 were also checked against Krüger's text of the Codex at [droitromain, CJ 4](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Corpus/CJ4.htm).

## Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

The two strips come from two different leaves. In the 1574 print their passages are about 68 columns apart, so they are not parts of one leaf. Their order and distance in the manuscript are not known.

The versos (canvas 1) are heavily abraded. The left verso strip has a red and blue frame with faint line ends, and the right verso strip has a later cursive note of about eighteen lines; neither was read. Faint later marginal notes on the recto were not read either. The block of gloss on strip 254-1 between the frame and the *Habita* gloss was not collated; its two-colour initials and phrases such as "Non posse" and "Exceptioni" are probably gloss on C. 4.13.1-5.

Not claimed:
- the date and origin of the manuscript;
- which redaction of the Accursian gloss it carries;
- any relation between this copy and the 1574 print or other witnesses.

The date 1574 is that of the edition compared, not of the fragment.
