# F-zms8: Hostiensis (Henricus de Segusio), *Summa aurea*, book III, De decimis (end) and De regularibus et transeuntibus ad religionem (beginning)

The leaf carries part of the *Summa aurea* of Hostiensis, a summa (systematic treatise) on the titles of the Decretals of Gregory IX. It holds the end of the title on tithes, first-fruits and offerings, legible from the twelfth numbered question ("Duodecimo quaero", on tithes of newly cultivated land) to the end of the seventeenth (on first-fruits), and then the rubric and opening of the next title, on members of religious orders and those entering religion: the linking sentence "Tractatum est supra de clericis secularibus", the list of questions, "Quis sit regularis", and the question whether peasants who found hospitals count as religious.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-zms8) describes Gent, Universiteitsbibliotheek, HS.2582/186: one trimmed parchment leaf, 321 x 180 mm, Southern Textualis, possibly Italy, dated 1301-1400, with the title "Treatise on canon law". That description fits. The record names no author, work or passage. The date, origin and script are the catalogue's statements and were not examined here.

- **Identification:** identified, confidence high. Five passages from all four columns agree with the Turin 1579 edition of the *Summa aurea*, fol. 213v-214r, in wording, order and legal citations, and the change of title falls at the same point with the same linking sentence and list of questions. The agreement runs through the body of one title and across the boundary into the next, with the work's own cross-references ("ut no. infra eodem § et utrum", "supra eodem sub § septimo"), so what matched is the summa itself and not a text quoting it. The Summa of Goffredus de Trano, which follows the same order of titles, was not opened; the sustained agreement with Hostiensis through the numbered questions leaves no room for it, and the text cites "Gof." as another writer. The manuscript's wording differs from the print in small points listed under Textual evidence; no claim about version is made.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-zms8/readings/generic1-2026-11-24-image-only.json) and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-zms8/readings/generic1-2026-11-24-source-assisted.json). A research reader reread on the images, with the edition at hand: recto right column lines 1-27 and the four lines of the hospital question; recto left column, twelve lines around "septimodecimo"; verso right column, about thirty lines from "Terciodecimo" to the fifteenth question; verso left column, the last twelve lines. Everything else rests on the first reading. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared crops of the manuscript images with page images of the Turin 1579 edition, reading both sides of each comparison. The edition's machine-read text was used only to find the pages. COORDINATOR CHECK: the coordinator (Claude) read the Terciodecimo-Quartodecimo passage (canvas 1, native box 4400,4400,6100,6000) on the image against [the 1579 Turin edition, fol. 213v](https://archive.org/details/ARes01409/page/n425): from "[empt]ori sic si bona fide emit" through "Quartodecimo queritur si quis p[astor] ... una parrochia et caulam" the leaf agrees with the print in wording and order; the cut line ends are the only gaps. The opening of De regularibus was not checked by the coordinator. Gaps: the upper two thirds of the verso left column are abraded and unread; no comparison was made with a critical text or another manuscript; no palaeographer has reviewed the reading or the match.

Differences. The manuscript has "nunc subicitur" where the print has "subiicit", and "uideamus ergo" where the print has "videamus". "Professionis" stands in the margin beside the line, not in it. The manuscript has "et ex quo professus est" where the print has "ex quo". Two readings are uncertain and are given as read, not as established variants: "esse iunctus … seruit" where the print has "seiunctus, inservit", and "absit enim" where the print has "absit etiam". Whether the citation "ff. de reg. iur. bona fides" stood in the manuscript cannot be told, because the line end where it would begin is cut away. The first reading's "de illis" and "signum crucium" in the hospital question are "de rusticis" and "signum certum" on rereading, and its "octavodecimo" and "nonodecimo" are "Terciodecimo" and "Quartodecimo". The print's later *Additiones* and summaries are not in the manuscript.

[213v](https://archive.org/details/ARes01409/page/n425) and [214r](https://archive.org/details/ARes01409/page/n426), page images. The attribution rests on the edition's running head, "Liber III. Summe Hostiensis", read on the page, and on the library record's transcription of its title; the title page was not opened. The text names Raymond of Penyafort ("Ray.") and Goffredus ("Gof.") as others.

## Leaf order and limits

The catalogue's faces are in reverse order of the text. The text runs from the verso left column (its legible foot is the twelfth question) to the verso right column (thirteenth to fifteenth), then the recto left column (end of the sixteenth, the seventeenth) and the recto right column (the new title). The catalogue's verso is therefore the original recto, which agrees with the cut column lying at the outer edge on both faces. About two fifths of each line of the recto left column, and the line ends of the verso right column, are lost to trimming. The verso carries a stamp of the Ghent University Library.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the leaf, which are the catalogue's; any relation of this copy to the printed text or to other manuscripts; the content of the unread upper verso left column, which by the length of the print should hold the tenth and eleventh questions and the start of the twelfth, an estimate and not a reading.
