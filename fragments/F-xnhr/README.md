# F-xnhr: Laurentius de Aquileia, *Tractatus de variis modis componendi epistolam*, opening tables to the formulas *ad rescribendum*, with the end of the *Tabula narrandi petendi et concludendi*

These four paper pieces are from a copy of the *Tractatus de variis modis componendi epistolam* ("treatise on the various ways of composing a letter") of Laurentius de Aquileia, a work of *ars dictandi*, the medieval art of letter writing. The tractatus, which begins "Sciendum est quod narratio potest incipere ...", is set out as tables: columns of interchangeable words and phrases, joined by braces and ladders, from which a letter's narration, petition and conclusion can be assembled. The pieces carry the end of the preceding *tabula narrandi petendi et concludendi*, the red incipit rubric of the tractatus, which names its author as "... de Aquil(egia)", the opening narratio tables, a run of formulas about studies and promotion, the petitio tables, the conclusiones (affirmative and negative), answers to petitions headed "super eodem", and the beginning of the formulas "ad rescribendum". The comparison witness is Paris, BnF, lat. 14174, fols. 19v-22r, where the same text is unattributed. The Stams copy is a variant school version of it.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-xnhr) describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 35: paper, four items, 149 x 126 mm (measured on item 35_2), no catalogue date, title "x", no linked description, with a reconstruction summary by Claudia Sojer. It names no text. The description fits the images: four larger paper pieces (35_1 to 35_4) and two tiny scraps, imaged together, canvas 0 with the faces labelled r and canvas 1 with those labelled v. The hand is a gothic bookhand with cursive features, with rubrics, paraphs and initials in red and red braces, ladders and star-shaped connectors linking the columns of alternatives. Old pencil signatures read "IVa/M 25" (canvas 0) and "IVa/N 29" (canvas 1).

- **Identification:** identified, high. Four passages, on 35_1r, 35_1v, 35_2v and 35_4r, agree with BnF lat. 14174 in wording, sequence and table layout, read on page images; the tables on 35_3v, 35_3r, 35_4v and 35_2r follow the Paris order where they were compared at overview and crop scale. The work was found through a search of the CoMMA manuscript text corpus for the 35_1v list "de mesto hylarem / novum de veteri / de infirmo sanum ...", which returned lat. 14174. The attribution rests on two grounds: the Stams copy's own red rubric on 35_3r, which reads "... d(e) Aquil(egia) s(upe)r div(er)sis mo(dis) [..] epistolas" with the forename lost at the cut edge; and other copies recorded in E. J. Polak's repertory, whose rubrics name "magister Laurencius" (seen as Google Books snippets only). The Paris copy's rubric on fol. 19v names no author. The companion work, Laurentius's *Practica sive usus dictaminis* (printed by Rockinger), shares one conclusio formula and the tabular method but not the Stams lists, so it is not the text of these pieces. The Stams text differs from Paris in many small wordings, casts the "efficiar" list as accusatives governed by "efficietis me" where Paris has nominatives, gives its rubrics as imperatives ("Hic pone conclusionem") where Paris has "hic ponit conclusiones", and has one eye-skip omission. These are the variants of a working school text, not of a different work. The grammatical "Exposicio" notes in the left column of 35_2r were not located in Paris and may be an added layer.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-xnhr/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-image-only.json) and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-xnhr/readings/stams4-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json). A research reader reread on the native images, with the Paris page images at hand, the middle column of 35_2v, the middle of 35_1v and the upper part of 35_1r. An independent checker read the main block of 35_4r on the image before opening the Paris page at that place, and read the red rubric at the upper left of 35_3r. Everything else, including 35_3v, the rest of 35_3r, 35_4v and 35_2r, rests on the image-only first reading, checked only against the Paris overview. Many words in the formulas are uncertain and are marked. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the three passages on 35_2v, 35_1v and 35_1r with page images of BnF lat. 14174, fol. 22r, which are grayscale scans from microfilm; no edition of the tractatus was reached or used. An independent checker read 35_4r (canvas 0, native box 1610,3420,2680,4110, the main text block) on the image before opening the Paris page at that place, then compared it with lat. 14174, fol. 20r, right column ([Gallica, btv1b10034659b](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b10034659b)): it is the same run of formulas about studies, a canonry of Paris and ordination ("ad sacros ordines sum promotus"), in the same order, with variant wording ("doctrinam" for "documenta", the canonry formula in inverted word order). That checker knew from the research reader's notes that the face should fall on fol. 20r. The same checker confirmed on fol. 19v that the Paris rubric is unattributed, read "d(e) Aquil(egia) s(upe)r div(er)sis mo(dis)" in the Stams rubric on 35_3r (canvas 0, native box about 280,2960,960,3100), identified 35_3v as the end of the preceding *tabula* (Paris fol. 19v), and established the reading order of pieces 35_3 and 35_4 against Paris. Gaps: 35_2r (both columns), 35_3v and 35_4v were compared at overview or crop scale only; the forename in the Stams rubric is lost; Polak and the other copies were seen as snippets, not page images; no other copy of the tractatus was compared, including the Central European ones. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

**Differences.** Besides the accusative construction of the "efficiar" list and the imperative rubrics, Stams adds "et primo de affirmativa" to the first conclusio rubric; lacks Paris's "de labore ad requiem pervenisse. Duxeritis ad effectum me", an eye-skip from one "me constabit" to the next; reads "clientulus" where Paris seems to read "clericulus"; and differs in many small wordings within the formulas ("Cognoscetis" for "Cognoscentes", "doctrinam" for "documenta"). The repeated red group on 35_1r is "super eodem", as in Paris. A red line at the foot of 35_4r has no counterpart visible on the Paris scan, where faded rubrics barely register.

**Comparison witness and attribution.** Paris, BnF, lat. 14174 (thirteenth century, second half, per Polak), fols. 19v-22r, read on the Gallica page images ([btv1b10034659b](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b10034659b)); its rubric on fol. 19v reads "Explicit tabula narrandi petendi et concludendi. Sequitur(?) / Incipit tractatus de variis modis componendi epistolam", with no author. Polak, *Medieval and Renaissance Letter Treatises and Form Letters*, describes the Paris volume (vol. 2, 2015, p. 264), listing after Laurentius's *Summa dictaminis* and his *ars narrandi, petendi et concludendi* the "<Laurentius de Aquileia.> Tractatus super diversis modis componendi epistolam", inc. "Sciendum est quod narratio potest incipere"; other copies carry the name in their rubrics: "Incipit tractatus magistri Laurencii super diversis modis componendi epistolam" (vol. 2, p. 257), "magistri Laurencii Lombardi de modis variis componendi epystolam" (vol. 2, p. 274) and "Magistri Laurencii de civitate Austrie Aquilegensis diocesis" (vol. 3, 2023, p. 282). Polak notes that the work usually travels with Laurentius's *Practica sive usus dictaminis*. Snippets of the *Journal des savants* (1899), of a description of a Barcelona or Tarragona copy and of H. Kopičková, *Listy na Husovu obranu* (1999), which quotes the incipit from a Bohemian copy, give the same title with Laurentius's name. All of these were seen as Google Books snippets only. No printed edition of the tractatus was reached. The Stams rubric on 35_3r, "... d(e) Aquil(egia) s(upe)r div(er)sis mo(dis) [componendi?] epistolas", is secure for "d' aquil" and "s(upe)r div(er)sis mo(dis)"; the forename and the first line of the rubric are lost or unresolved.

Not claimed: the date, script or origin of the Stams copy (the catalogue's paper and a later medieval hand were not examined further); the author's forename from the Stams rubric itself; which version of the tractatus the Stams copy represents, which would need a Central European copy for comparison; or the source of the "Exposicio" notes on 35_2r.
