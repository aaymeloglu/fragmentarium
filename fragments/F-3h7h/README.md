# F-3h7h: Missal, sanctorale from Barbara (4 December) to Placidus (5 October), Augsburg saints and Benedictine features (use by inference only), owned by Benediktbeuern

Six paper and parchment pieces at Stams come from the sanctorale of a fifteenth-century manuscript missal. The sanctorale is the part of a missal with the Masses for saints' days. Here the collect, secret and postcommunion (the three prayers of each Mass) are written out, and the chants and readings are given by their opening words. The pieces run in calendar order:
- Barbara, Nicholas, Ambrose and the Conception of the Virgin, with the sequence *Conceptio Marie virginis que nos lavit a labe criminis*;
- January and February saints, then March to early October;
- the end comes after Placidus (5 October) with the rubric "Reliqua vide in fine libri et deinceps in tali signo": the rest is at the end of the book.

A blank page carries the ownership note "P(rese)ns Missale p(er)tinet ad Monasterium Benedict(en)pewren" (the syllable after *Benedict* is abbreviated with a stroke), i.e. the Benedictine abbey of Benediktbeuern in the diocese of Augsburg.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-3h7h) has these details:
- Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 98, online since 18 September 2021;
- "Parchment and Paper", eight fragment items, 235 x 180 mm (measured on Frg. 98_3 as 133/235 x 135/180 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck). The record describes Frg. 98_1-3 as parchment, with a paper strip on 98_3r, and 98_3-8 as paper;
- title "x", no date and no linked description;
- six images.

The record does not name the text.

- **Identification:** identified, high, as a liturgical placing: the Masses are assigned to their feasts, and the book is a missal.
  - **The Ulrich collect** (4 July) was read on the page. It agrees word for word with the collect that the Augsburg *Proprium* of 1857 prints for use "Augustae in ipsa S. Udalrici ecclesia". The gradual, offertory, Creed and Preface of the Apostles also agree. The epistle, alleluia, gospel, communion, secret and postcommunion differ.
  - **Missale Augustanum of 1491:** the Ulrich collect is also the collect of this printed Augsburg missal. The only difference: the leaf reads *confessoris tui atque pontificis*, the print *gloriosi confessoris tui*.
  - **The Hilaria secret** (12 August) agrees with the 1491 print and with the Augsburg proper of about 1765. The words struck through after it, "domine confisi suffragiis", are the opening of the 1491 postcommunion.
  - **Against the 1491 print** most other items differ:
    - Ulrich: introit, epistle, gradual, gospel, offertory, secret, communion and postcommunion;
    - Afra: introit and collect;
    - Hilaria: introit and collect;
    - Petronilla: all three prayers, where the leaf uses common prayers for a virgin.
  - So the leaves are not a copy of the Augsburg diocesan missal. They share some Augsburg proper prayers.
  - **The calendar** contains saints of the diocese of Augsburg and the region around it (as calendar evidence; the use is not established):
    - Augsburg saints with full propers: Ulrich, Afra and Hilaria;
    - Willibald (Eichstätt), Magnus of Füssen, Emmeram (as a commemoration), Castulus, Florian, Godehard, Walburga, Kunigunde, Henry, Odilia, and the Division of the Apostles (15 July).
  - **Monastic features:** the abbots Maurus, Romanus and Placidus, and rubrics for commemorations of St Benedict in the days after his Translation (11 July), including "De s. Benedicto non fit commemoratio" on the Division of the Apostles. This points to Benedictine use, in keeping with the owner.
  - **What remains inference:** any Augsburg or Benedictine use. A monastic sanctorale that took some Augsburg proper prayers would fit the evidence, but this is not shown.

**Differences.**
- Ulrich: the collect agrees apart from spelling. The rest of the Mass differs as listed: the 1857 book is a post-Tridentine revision.
- Afra (page 17): the lesson *Confitebor*, gradual *Dilexisti* and alleluia *Specie tua* agree with the 1765 book, but the collect (*Deus cuius caritatis ardore beata Afra edaces incendii flammas*, adapted from the collect of St Laurence), the offertory and the secret differ.
- Against the 1491 print:
  - Ulrich: the collect is the same apart from *confessoris tui atque pontificis* (1491 *gloriosi confessoris tui*), and the Alleluia *Iuravit* agrees; everything else differs.
  - Afra and Hilaria: the introits and collects differ.
  - Hilaria: the secret agrees.
  - Petronilla: all three prayers differ.
- These comparisons show that some Augsburg proper prayers are present, but the manuscript does not follow the printed Augsburg missal.

## Leaf order, other items and what is not claimed

- **Frg. 98_3:** a bifolium whose first page is blank except for the ownership note (a paper strip with part of the Mass of St Antony is pasted on it). Its other pages are 1, 2 and 3. Pages 1 and 2 face each other and are continuous (Ambrose).
- **Frg. 98_4 (pages 4-5):** Odilia (13 December) to Antony (17 January).
- **Frg. 98_5 (pages 6-7):** Agnes(?) to Kunigunde (3 March). It is probably not continuous with page 5: Fabian and Sebastian are missing.
- **Frg. 98_6 to 98_8:** three nested bifolia, pages 8 and 19 outermost. Pages 8-19 run continuously from March to October.
- **Hand and layout:** one cursive gothic hand, two columns, red rubrics and initials. Several passages are struck through in red, with corrections in the margins.
- **Frg. 98_1 and 98_2:** narrow parchment strips that still carry sewn headband cores from a binding. They show only faint, abraded traces of a gothic hand with some red, not legible in these images. They may come from a different manuscript.
- **Not claimed:** the date beyond the general fifteenth-century character of the hand; the date of the ownership note; that the book was written at Benediktbeuern; any relation to a printed missal or a surviving manuscript.
