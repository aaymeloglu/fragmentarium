# F-tnqw: Stams Abbey, *Necrologium Stamsense* (mortuary), 9 and 13-15 January, 9-13 and 24 February

The calendar strips of Stams, STA, Frg. 168 come from the lost mortuary of the Cistercian abbey of Stams. A mortuary (necrology) is a calendar in which the names of the dead are entered under their anniversaries so that they are remembered on those days. Its older entries are printed in *Monumenta Germaniae Historica, Necrologia Germaniae* III (1905), pp. 47-59, from an eighteenth-century copy. The strips also carry many later entries for monks and canons of other houses, which the edition does not print. The Stams archivist P. M. Grebenc already identified the strips, cited the edition and transcribed them in 1964-1965. His envelope and typed slips are photographed with the record. What this report adds is a new comparison of the strips with the printed edition.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-tnqw) (online since 18 September 2021) describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 168 as 12 items of parchment and paper. It gives the dimensions as 227 x 148 mm, measured on item 168_4. Its title field reads only "x" and its text names no work. The record's images include the archivist's material. On canvas 8 an envelope bears the typed label "Fragmente [des] Nekrologs von Stams entnommen wurden von den Inkunabeln 26. Oktober 1964 P. M. Grebenc" and, in red pencil, "Fragment Necrologium Stamsense 'antiquum' (ca. J. 1532?)". On canvas 5 a typed slip for 168-1 is annotated "Mon. Germ. Necrol. III 47-57, Necrologium Stamsense" and refers to Primisser's "Addit. II fol. 41 ff.". Canvases 10-15 hold typed day-by-day transcriptions of the strips, headed "Fragmente Original-Nekrologs von Stams". The identification, the edition and the dates of the entries were therefore all known before this research.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence, already known from the archivist's slips (P. M. Grebenc, 1964-1965, unpublished, photographed with the record).
  - **Basis:** the older entries on the strips follow the printed text in wording and order on three faces, at 14-15 January, 9-10 and 13 February and 24 February. The calendar letters of the strips (f at 13 January, A at 15 January, e at 9 February, f at 10 February) fit those days.
  - **Differences:** besides spelling variants, the strips mark Masses with red crosses or a red marginal "Missa +" where the edition prints "missa". The strips also carry a later layer of entries in several cursive hands, absent from the edition. They name religious of Neustift (Novacella), Kremsmünster ("Cremiphanensis"), St. Mang in Füssen, Gries, Wilten ("in Wiltau"), Ettal, St. Emmeram in Regensburg, Buxheim and Steingaden, and one entry for a professed canon of Neustift carries the year 1717.
  - **Alternatives:** no other necrology was considered. The obits are those of the Stams benefactors printed in the edition.
- **Transcription:** see the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-tnqw/readings/stams9-2026-11-01-image-only.json) and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-tnqw/readings/stams9-2026-11-01-source-assisted.json).
  - A research reader reread these faces on contrast-enhanced crops at full size: the January block of 168-1r, the whole of 168-11r and 168-11v, and the top of 168-7v. A second reader reread 168-11v without the edition in view, and also the additions on 168-1v.
  - The other faces rest on the first reading.
  - Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:**
  - A research reader compared the passages in the table below with page images of the edition.
  - A second reader checked the 13 February passage on 168-11v against the page image of p. 49, after reading it independently.
  - COORDINATOR CHECK: 168-11r, 9 February, "Herman(nu)s Sutor d(ictu)s ... Krapp[el]", canvas 6 (Stams_STA_Frg_168_08v10v_11r_12v), native box 2428,2014-4011,3266, against MGH Necrologia III p. 49 ([archive.org page n62](https://archive.org/details/monumentagenecro03geseuoft/page/n62/mode/1up))
  - Gaps: the base layer of 168-1v is under tape and illegible. The later entries cannot be checked against any printed source. The eighteenth-century copies by Primisser and Kuprian, on which the edition rests, were not consulted.

**Differences.** Most are spelling: "pietantiam" for "pietanciam", and "Leibolvungen" (u with a diacritic) for "Leibolvingen". The strip's red cross corresponds to the edition's "missa". Some words of the edition fall in losses at the edges of the strips, among them "Wens", "Schnals" and "Kirchperg". At 13 January the edition's "Karolus m. et sac." is not legible on the strip, and the legible text of that day consists of later additions. At the foot of 168-11v the second reader saw the start of the 14 February entry, "[...] monachus et sacerdos. R[...]". This fits the edition's "Georgius de Pregnez m. et sac., refectorarius", but the name was not read without the edition in view. The later entries interleaved with these lines are not in the edition, and the comparison does not show whether the editor left them out or his copy lacked them.

**The edition.** *Monumenta Germaniae Historica, Necrologia Germaniae* III: *Dioeceses Brixinensis, Frisingensis, Ratisbonensis*, ed. S. Herzberg-Fränkel (Berlin 1905), pp. 47-59 ([archive.org](https://archive.org/details/monumentagenecro03geseuoft)). Pp. 48-50 are archive.org pages n61-n63; the preface on p. 47 is page n60. The archivist's slip gives the range as pp. 47-57.

The preface says that Stams has no necrology written in the Middle Ages. It says there were two copies of a Stams mortuary begun in the fifteenth century and continued to 1749, which by 1905 was lost: Antonius Kuprian's of 1749 and Cassian Primisser's, made after 1750 in the second volume (fol. 41-62) of his *Additiones ad Annales Stamsenses*. The edition prints Primisser's copy. The strips carry the Stams anniversaries in the same order and wording. This, together with their place in the Stams archive, is the basis for treating them as remnants of the lost mortuary.

## Other items, leaf order and what is not claimed

The strips found so far cover 9 January (168-1v, later additions only), 13-15 January (168-1r), 9-10 February (168-11r), 13 February running into 14 February (168-11v) and 24 February (168-7v). Item 168-2r, otherwise ruled and blank, has one later entry, "Balthasar Ober[er?] Monach[us]". On the typed slips this name appears under 12 January.

The other items are not identified:
- **168-4v:** a strip with two lines of large liturgical script, mostly worn away. Its mirror image shows through on 168-4r.
- **Two scraps in early-modern cursive:** a brown, cracked piece and a stained strip.
- **Labels from the books the strips bound,** written on reused parchment and naming:
  - a Bible of 1514 "cum Explicatione nom[inum] hebr." (168-3r)
  - three parts of Vincent of Beauvais, *Speculum historiale*, 1474 (168-7r, 168-9r, 168-12r)
  - a *Quadragesimale* of the Franciscan Johannes Gritsch (168-10r)

  These dates belong to the host books, not to the necrology.

**Not claimed:**
- **The date of the hands.** The envelope's "ca. 1532" rests on a note of Primisser (*Additiones* VI, fol. 174v), known here only as quoted on the archivist's typed sheet. The note says that Johannes Fuscanes, a Benedictine of Reichenbach, copied the old necrology in 1532. Neither that note nor any attribution of the base hand to Fuscanes was checked. The latest dated entry seen, 1717, shows only that additions continued into the eighteenth century.
- **When the book was cut up.** The envelope says the strips were taken from incunabula, which records their reported provenance; when the strips were reused is not established.
- **Publication.** No publication of the strips themselves was found, but recent Stams literature was seen only in search snippets.
