# F-nbxz: Jacobus de Voragine, *Legenda aurea*, end of Simon and Jude and opening of All Saints

The four parchment strips of Stams Frg. 64 come from one leaf of a two-column copy of the *Legenda aurea* of Jacobus de Voragine. The *Legenda aurea* is a 13th-century collection of saints' lives and feasts arranged by the church year. The recto has part of the chapter on the apostles Simon and Jude (28 October): the dispute with the magi Zaroes and Arfaxat, and the accused deacon. The verso has the closing notice on Simon, citing Eusebius, Bede and Johannes Beleth, then the rubric and opening of the chapter on All Saints. In the chapter numbering of Graesse's edition (2nd ed., 1850) these are caps. 159 and 162, pp. 709-711 and 718. In this copy All Saints follows Simon and Jude directly.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-nbxz) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 64. It gives parchment, one fragment item, 84 x 62 mm (measured as 34/84 x 58/62 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no date and no linked description, with two images labelled 064_01r and 064_01v. The description fits the images, which show the four strips held together at the foot in a plastic sleeve. The first image also shows a typed archival slip. The slip records that the strips came from the spine of volume III/61, bound in 1661/66, and names the feast, "28. Okt: ss. Simonis et Jude". It then transcribes the second column of the verso, from "historia ecclesiastica Beda super" to "Omnium sanctorum festivitas ... propter cuiusdam". The slip names the feast but not the work. It does not read the recto or the line-ends of the verso's first column.

- **Identification:** identified, high, as the *Legenda aurea*, caps. 159 (Simon and Jude) and 162 (All Saints). The basis is sustained agreement in wording and order with Graesse's text in two passages, one on each face: Simon's answer to the advocates and the sign of the cross (recto, first column), and the closing notice on Simon (verso, second column). The leaf also has the following rubric and the *Legenda aurea*'s own opening of All Saints ("Omnium sanctorum festiuitas iiij de causis instituta fuisse ..."). Juan Gil de Zamora's *Legende sanctorum* copies the Simon notice almost word for word, so that notice alone cannot separate the two works. But the All Saints opening that follows is the *Legenda aurea*'s: a Corpus Corporum search for it returned only Jacobus. The copy differs from Graesse in version, not in work:
  - It reads "testatur cum beatus Symon apostolus in Egypto predicasset" where Graesse has "testantur. Cum enim, ut ajunt, in Aegypto praedicasset".
  - It lacks "unanimiter".
  - It closes Simon and Jude with a short clause ending "[..]artiu[m]? adeptus est" in place of the Trajan and Atticus passage and the "Nonnulli vero dicunt" correction.
  - It has no chapters on Quintin and Eustace (Graesse caps. 160-161) between Simon and Jude and All Saints.
  - It reads "fuisse no[..]" where Graesse has "fuisse videtur".
  - It reads "potestis" where Graesse has "poteritis".
  - In the episode at Samir it largely omits the apostles' reply to the angel ("Cui apostoli: adoranda est Dei misericordia ... perducat"). Two consecutive lines end "[a]ut ues[trum]" and "[si]lencio a[postoli]", where Graesse has about 125 characters between those words, roughly three lines of this copy.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-nbxz/readings/stams-2026-10-31-image-only.json) of 31 October 2026 and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-nbxz/readings/stams-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json). The first reader did not see the typed slip, which was masked. It saw the pencil shelfmark labels on the parchment. A research reader reread on native images the second column of the verso (14 lines) and the middles of 14 lines of the recto's first column. That reader also checked the first reading of the recto's second-column line-beginnings and the verso's first-column line-ends. The two narrow, crumpled strips (verso strip 2, recto strip 3) are largely unread. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the verso's second column with the page image of Graesse p. 711, and the recto's first column with the page image of Graesse p. 709. The line-beginnings and line-ends of the other two columns were compared with Graesse's archive.org OCR text for pp. 709-710, and the All Saints opening with the OCR text of p. 718. Short passages were also checked against Maggioni's critical text in Corpus Corporum, as search snippets only. An independent research reader then read the recto's second-column line-beginnings and the verso's first-column line-ends and compared them with page images of Graesse pp. 709-710. That check confirmed the sequence and the leaf order, corrected one reading ("[f]aciendi") and found the shortened reply at Samir. Those strips are canvas 1, native box 3040,2620,3320,3540 (recto, second column), and canvas 0, native box 2740,2640,2960,3560 (verso, first column), compared with Graesse p. 709 ([page image](https://archive.org/details/legendaaureavulg00jacouoft/page/709/mode/1up)) and p. 710 ([page image](https://archive.org/details/legendaaureavulg00jacouoft/page/710/mode/1up)). That reader had seen a summary list of the first-column line-ends before reading, so that half was not fully blind. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

Regions are given as x,y,width,height in native pixels. Abbreviations are expanded in parentheses. Square brackets mark lost, damaged or uncertain letters, and "|" marks a cut between strips.

The script is a small gothic textualis of the 14th century, with a red rubric and a red initial on the verso and red-touched capitals on the recto. Canvas 1 (labelled 01v) is the recto: its first column (strips 1-3, region 2455,2647,616,862) and second column (strip 4, line-beginnings, region 3078,2647,206,862) come before the text on canvas 0. The line-beginnings of the recto's second column follow Graesse's sequence on pp. 709-710: "sani et", "[f]acie(n)di", "p(er)dura[ntes]", "eos to[tam]" (against Babylon), "i(n)fama[uit]" (the deacon), "concepit", "[dya]conu(m) o[ccidere]", "et quando", "et dyac[onum]", "[per]ducite?". So do the line-ends of the verso's first column, for the episode at Samir: "[sacri]ficare", "ad p(re)dic[tam]", "p(re)dic[ti]", "De[mones]", "[ap(osto)]li dei", "[eli]gite", "[a]ut ues[trum]", "[si]lencio a[postoli]", "ydola d[emonibus]". The third line-end of that column is unresolved. In the verso's line on the thirty raised dead, the copy seems to bring "fluctibus mersos" into the narrative sentence before giving the chant. That reading rests on a crumpled strip.

The other differences are those listed under Identification above. They are differences of version. Which branch of the *Legenda aurea* tradition the copy belongs to is not determined. Graesse notes that the editio princeps also lacks the "Nonnulli" passage.

The edition compared is Th. Graesse, *Jacobi a Voragine Legenda aurea vulgo historia lombardica dicta*, 2nd ed. (Dresden and Leipzig, 1850), the standard 19th-century edition of the work under Jacobus's name. It is read in the University of Toronto copy on archive.org ([p. 709](https://archive.org/details/legendaaureavulg00jacouoft/page/709), [p. 711](https://archive.org/details/legendaaureavulg00jacouoft/page/711)). G. P. Maggioni's critical text, which has the same chapters, was consulted in Corpus Corporum.

## Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

Leaf order: the catalogue's labels are reversed. The text on 064_01v comes before the text on 064_01r. The typed slip on canvas 0 is a modern archival note and not part of the fragment. This leaf is not from the same manuscript as the other Stams *Legenda aurea* fragment Frg. 219 ([F-n2rx](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-n2rx)), whose leaves have one column, place Eustace immediately before All Saints and give the rubric "De festiuitate omnium sanctorum". Nor is it from the same manuscript as Stams Frg. 242 ([F-c7me](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-c7me)), another *Legenda aurea* fragment, which has one column at about 7.2 mm line pitch against two columns at about 4.8 mm here. No claim is made about date beyond the script, origin, the version of the text, or whether other strips of this manuscript survive at Stams.
