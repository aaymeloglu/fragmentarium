# F-z56d: Petrus Hispanus (non-papa), *Summa "Absoluta cuiuslibet"*

One leaf of the bifolium is identified with high confidence as the grammatical *Summa "Absoluta cuiuslibet"* attributed to Petrus Hispanus, a treatise on syntax in the tradition of Priscian's *Institutiones* XVII–XVIII (not the logician Peter of Spain, author of the *Tractatus*). The lower recto and the whole verso of that leaf follow pages 8–10 of the printed edition in order: the definition of *devocatio*, then the question whether *ille* answering *quis* is demonstrative or relative, then the person of the verb after *ille qui*. The other leaf is too faded to read.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-z56d) describes a parchment bifolium fragment, Leuven, Archief van de Abdij van Park, VIIIB20/89, 220 × 223 mm, dated 1300–1350, catalogued as an unidentified treatise on logic. The text is grammar. The catalogue date has not been reassessed; the work itself is of the late twelfth century.

- **Identification:** identified, high. About forty lines of the verso and eight of the recto were matched to the edition at seven points, in the edition's order, with each stretch of manuscript continuing where the previous one stopped. The wording is continuous prose with its own sequence of examples (Georgics and Aeneid, *sic Iupiter ille monebat*, *semini tuo qui est Christus*, *tu es ille Eneas quem genuit Venus*), not one shared example. Material alternative: a later work that copies these pages verbatim; none was found.
- **Transcription:** two source-assisted selected readings, [six lines of the verso](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-z56d/readings/recheck-2026-10-11-source-assisted.json) and [both faces of the leaf](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-z56d/readings/recheck-2026-10-11-leaf-against-edition.json), from contrast-stretched crops. The [image-only reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-z56d/readings/batch-2026-10-11-image-only.json) recovered no connected text. Accuracy has not been measured, and some lines within the matched stretches were not read.
- **Verification:** the manuscript images were compared with Google Books search-inside results for the edition, which give the printed page number and about forty words per hit. The [responses are saved](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/audits/recheck-2026-10-11-F-z56d-edition-snippets.json). The edition is in copyright and its pages were not seen, so line numbers, apparatus and the editor's notes on manuscripts are unknown.

The manuscript is not a letter-for-letter copy of the edited text. It has small differences of word order (*demonstrationem facit* against *facit demonstrationem*; *ille ibi* against *ibi ille*; *facit notitiam* against *notitiam facit*) and of wording (*de isto* against *pro isto*, *sibi eam adiungit* against *adiungit eam sibi*, *de se vero* against *per se tamen*, *qui genitus est* against *qui est genitus*). These are the differences one witness shows against another, and none changes the argument. Whether they agree with a manuscript in the edition's apparatus was not checked.

The edition is C. H. Kneepkens, *Het Iudicium constructionis: het leerstuk van de constructio in de 2de helft van de 12de eeuw*, vol. IV: *Werkuitgave van Petrus Hispanus' Summa "Absoluta cuiuslibet"* (Nijmegen, 1987), pp. 8–10. The text there carries the editor's marks for his base manuscript, f. 51vb on p. 9 and f. 52ra on p. 10. ## Attribution and limits

Author and title are those of Kneepkens's edition, which is the scholarly basis for calling the work the *Summa "Absoluta cuiuslibet"* of Petrus Hispanus; the name is the edition's and the identity of this Petrus is a separate question not addressed here. Nothing is claimed about which recension the fragment carries, about the upper recto, or about the bifolium's other leaf, which should hold another part of the same work but could not be read. A later "Registrum" inscription on the fragment belongs to its reuse.
