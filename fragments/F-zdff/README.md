# F-zdff: Draft deed of sale, Natters in the Landgericht Sonnenburg, sealed by the abbot of Wilten, Conversion of St Paul (25 January, probable) 15[x]8

This parchment sheet in the archive of the Cistercian abbey of Stams carries the lower part of a draft (Konzept, a working version written before the fair copy) of a German deed of sale. The opening words of every line are cut away at the left edge. A group of vendors ("wir obbemelte verkauffer") give the usual warranty (Währschaft, the seller's promise to defend the buyer's title) "an aller stat und gegen menigklich" and renounce further claims. Their wives are named, among them two women called Agnes whose surnames are uncertain (read as K/A(i)rcherin and K/A(i)tzorin or similar). The same line names a Cristan Holtzna[...], probably one of the vendors. At their request the abbot of Wilten ("Herrn Abbt zu Welthau"), the Premonstratensian abbey by Innsbruck, hangs his seal on the letter. The sealing clause shows only that the abbot was asked to seal; it does not establish that Wilten was the landlord of what was sold. The next line names "Hanns Wiesen(?) zu Natters", whose role is lost with the start of the line; he may be the buyer. The witnesses are "[...]pein, Oswald Spirgl und Pölt Cammerlannder zu Natters". The act was done "im Lanndgericht Sonnenburg", the court district that included Natters. The dating clause gives a feast read as the Conversion of St Paul (25 January) and a year that breaks off unfinished: "tausent fünffhundert und In dem Achtund", followed by a closing mark and blank parchment. The year is therefore 15[x]8, with the decade never written. The object sold and the price are lost.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-zdff) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 52, one parchment item, 174 x 258 mm (measured 172/174 x 60/258 mm), online since 18 September 2021, with the title "x" and no date, type or description. The image labelled 01r is the text face and 01v the back. The record has nothing to confirm or correct beyond the missing regest (a regest is the short summary of a document: issuer, action, parties, place and date).

- **Identification:** partial, medium. Read on the image are the form (a draft deed of sale with warranty), the sealer (the abbot of Wilten), the vendors' wives and Cristan Holtzna[...] (surnames uncertain), the witnesses of Natters, the court district (Sonnenburg) and the dating clause. The decade of the year was never written. The corrections and the unfinished year suggest that the sheet is a draft rather than a copy or an original; this is an inference. A struck group of words is replaced by "Herrn Abbt" written above it. Another deletion near the sealing clause is replaced by an interlinear phrase read as "sein und seins aignen Convent(?)". An "und" is added above the line. There are no seal slits and no seal. The status is partial because the object sold, the price and the vendors' and buyer's full names are lost at the cut left edge, and Hans Wieser's role is not established. The year cannot be narrowed: the dating clause names no weekday, and the abbot of Wilten is not named. An old endorsement on the back reads "[B]rief Stambs" with a year below it that was read in two ways, "15[..]58(?)" and "15?2"/"1552". It does not settle the decade, and the regest does not use it.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-zdff/readings/stams-2026-10-31-image-only.json) covers all 18 line positions of the text face, the joined strip and the back. Its reader had no proposed identification or reading in context. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-zdff/readings/stams-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json) is a research reader's (Claude) reread of the whole text face at native resolution, in bands of about seven lines and two halves, with detail crops of the corrections, the witness lines and the dating line. It reads "zu Welthau" (Wilten), "Herrn Abbt", "Natters" and "Cammerlannder", which the first reading had as uncertain. The first lines (2 to 6) of the warranty formula and the joined strip at the top rest largely on the reread without independent check. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** there is no outside text of the act to compare. A fresh reader who had not seen the reading was given neutral crops of the words the regest depends on and asked what each says. These were the dating line, the sealer's line, the names line, the witness lines and the back endorsement. The fresh reader confirmed "Oswald Spi(r)gl", "Pölt Cammerlannder zu Natr(e)s", "Landgericht Sonnenburg", "Tausennt Funffhundert vnd In dem Achtund[..]" and "herrn Abbt zu Welthan". For the feast it read "Sannd Peiers Ackerer?". A second research reader reread the same crop (the dating clause, canvas 0, Stams_STA_Frg_052_01r, box 2000,2840-5200,3140, [image](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-zdff/Stams_STA_Frg_052_01r.jpg/2000,2840,3200,300/full/0/default.jpg)) as "an Sannct Pauls Bekerr[ung]", so the Conversion of St Paul is probable, not certain. For the wives the fresh reader read "Agnetz Kirchern ... Agnetz Kirchnerin", and the initial may be K or A. Not checked: the Wilten abbey archive, the court books (Verfachbücher) of the Landgericht Sonnenburg in the Tiroler Landesarchiv, monasterium.net, and the host volume Hs. 125. No human review.

No edition or regest of this act was found, so there is no source column. This is a private sale, the kind of act that is rarely printed. The sources with a specific reason to hold it are Wilten's own archive and rent registers and the Sonnenburg court books, none of which was checked. Google Books full text was searched for "Cammerlander" with Natters, "Spirgl" with Natters, and Wilten with Natters and Wieser, without a matching record. The feast conversion: the Conversion of St Paul falls on 25 January in every year. With the decade unknown, the weekday cannot be used.

## Physical notes and limits

- The sheet has one document in a German cursive hand of the sixteenth century, ending with a blank lower margin. That margin carries fold creases, paired slits and three vertical ink rules (box about 2600,3150-3300,3500). A narrow strip joined to the top with film (box 3420,480-4350,1400) carries seven line ends in the same hand. It probably comes from the upper part of the same draft, but the join is not proven.
- The back is canvas 1, Stams_STA_Frg_052_01v. It carries, upside down, the stamp "Stiftsarchiv Stams", a blue "IV/7", a pencil "Hs. 125" naming the host volume, and an older endorsement "[B]rief Stambs" with a year below it whose reading is disputed (box 4250,3380-5250,3750, [image](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-zdff/Stams_STA_Frg_052_01v.jpg/4250,3380,1000,370/full/180/default.jpg)).
- Not claimed: the decade of the year, the object sold, the price, the buyer, the vendors' full names, the identity of the abbot of Wilten, and whether a fair copy of the deed was issued.
