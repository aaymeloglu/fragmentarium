# F-xoto: Nicholas of Lyra, *Postilla litteralis*, Luke 17:5-18:5 and John 1:1-4

Stams, Frg. 218 is a bifolium from a manuscript of Nicholas of Lyra's *Postilla litteralis*, his literal commentary on the whole Bible. One leaf carries the commentary on Luke 17:5 to 18:5, from the mustard seed and the mulberry tree to the unjust judge. The other leaf carries Lyra's opening exposition of John 1:1-4, beginning *Advertendum tamen quod in nobis est duplex mentis conceptus*. Passages from both leaves and both sides agree in wording and sequence with the Lyra sections of the 1603 Glossa ordinaria and Lyra print.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-xoto) (Stiftsarchiv Stams, Frg. 218, online since 18 September 2021) gives the title "x", parchment, 341 x 240 mm (297/341 x 164/240 mm in the reconstruction summary by Claudia Sojer), and two images, Stams_STA_Frg_218_01r and _01v. It identifies no text and links no description. The images show one bifolium, so the record's single fragment item fits.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence. The diagnostic basis is Lyra's own connected exposition, not the Gospel lemmata (the quoted words of the text being explained): the division of Luke 18 (*Postquam salvator ostendit quod peccatores penitentes sunt benigne recipiendi et caute nutriendi ... ad martirii tollerantiam*), the explanation of Luke 17:6-8 (*Duo mira coniungit ...*), and the account of the inner word on John 1:1 (*duplex mentis conceptus ... diffinicio temperancie*), each checked against the print. The agreement runs across both leaves and both sides, through the commentator's divisions and cross-references, so the text is Lyra's *Postilla* itself rather than the Glossa ordinaria, the *Additiones* of Paul of Burgos or the *Moralitates* printed beside it, none of which has this wording. Works that reuse Lyra were not compared. The manuscript differs from the print in single words: *in devocione nutriendi* for *devote instruendi*, *ad eum* for *ad Deum*, *Et quia* for *Sed quia*, no *principaliter* in Luke 17:7, no *de agro* in the lemma of Luke 17:7, *accedendum* (or *attendendum*) for *procedendum*, *in actu perfecto* for *in perfecto*, an added *beatus* before *Iohannes*, and *tangentes solum* for *tangendo solummodo*.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-xoto/readings/stams5-2026-11-01-image-only.json) was made before any source was consulted; the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-xoto/readings/stams5-2026-11-01-source-assisted.json) gives the lines relied on. A research reader reread on the images the head of the left leaf, column b, on canvas 1 (10 lines), the head of the right leaf, column a, on canvas 1 (11 lines), and the middle of the right leaf, column a, on canvas 0 (10 lines). An independent checker read the right leaf, column b, on canvas 1 (about 25 lines) before seeing the source, and corrected that reading against the image afterwards. All other lines rest on the first reading. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the three passages named above with page images (not OCR) of the 1603 print, columns 927, 935 and 1017. An independent checker then compared a passage the research reader had not used, canvas 1, right leaf, column b, from *secundum rationem similitudinis* to *In principio erat verbum, idest in ipso Deo qui ab omnibus supponitur primum*, with the page image of column 1017 of the same print, and found it in the same order and wording apart from the single-word variants listed above. The same check found the John leaf's verso (canvas 0, left leaf, column a) partly legible (box 1540,1980,2520,2480), with the red-underlined lemma *Hoc erat in principio* (John 1:2) followed by *coeternitas patris et filii*. Gaps: canvas 0, left leaf, column b, lies mostly under later overwriting and was not compared; only one edition was used. No person has reviewed the reading or the match.

The coordinator compared canvas 1, right leaf, col. b, lines 1-8 (native box 5280,1180,6260,1760, "secundum rationem similitudinis quia de ratione verbi ... verbum in divinis et filius idem sunt ... Aug. vi de Trinitate") with the 1603 page image, col. 1017 ([archive.org scan n509](https://archive.org/details/bibliorumsacroru05strauoft/page/n509/mode/1up)): the wording agrees in sequence.

The differences are of single words and word forms, listed in the Identification bullet. The print's *interior* in passage B was not seen in the manuscript, where the word is worn; its absence is not certain. In passage C the manuscript abbreviation read *non* is uncertain, and the print's gutter clips a few letters at the left edge (supplied in brackets). These differences show that the manuscript and the print differ; they say nothing about how the two are related.

Edition: *Bibliorum sacrorum cum Glossa ordinaria ... et Postilla Nicolai Lyrani*, volume 5 (Venice: Giunta, 1603), Internet Archive `bibliorumsacroru05strauoft`: Luke 17 at [scan n464](https://archive.org/details/bibliorumsacroru05strauoft/page/n464/mode/1up), column 927; Luke 18 at [scan n468](https://archive.org/details/bibliorumsacroru05strauoft/page/n468/mode/1up), column 935; John 1 at [scan n509](https://archive.org/details/bibliorumsacroru05strauoft/page/n509/mode/1up), column 1017. The matched passages stand in the sections headed *Nicolaus de Lyra*, and the title page names the *Postilla Nicolai Lyrani* beside the Glossa ordinaria, the *Additiones* of Paul of Burgos and the *Replicae* of Matthias Doring. The attribution of the *Postilla litteralis* to Nicholas of Lyra is standard.

## Leaf order, other items and limits

Canvas 0 shows the John leaf's verso on the left and the Luke leaf's recto on the right; canvas 1 shows the Luke leaf's verso on the left and the John leaf's recto on the right. Canvas 0 is therefore the outer side of the bifolium, the Luke leaf comes first and the John leaf last. Lyra's commentary on Luke 18-24 falls between them, so this was not the centre bifolium of its quire. Each page has two columns of about 35 lines, with red underlining of the lemmata, a red rubric *Cap. xviii* and a penwork initial on canvas 1.

On canvas 0 the left leaf is darkened and overwritten upside down by a later hand: a column of years 1641-1649 and lines of large display script, unread. They belong to the later reuse of the bifolium as a wrapper and date only that writing.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the manuscript, its version or textual affiliation, its relation to other copies, and whether other witnesses are known. Column b of the left leaf on canvas 0 was not read.
