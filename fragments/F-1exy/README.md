## Reading and comparison

The [preserved image-only response](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-1exy/readings/review-2026-09-22-image-only.json) precedes source comparison. Its physical lines 3–5 read, with uncertainty, *In generacōe … virtutes? activa et passiva* and *enim hominis virtus activa est in viro Passiva vero*. The abbreviation and singular/plural construction must not be settled solely from a fluent expansion.

Selected source-assisted comparison text, corresponding to the larger crop's lines 3–8. This is normalized text with expanded abbreviations; it does not record every visible letter or abbreviation boundary:

```text
3 In generacione enim requiritur(?) virtus activa et passiva
4 correspondens active quasi contra eam divisa. In generacione
5 enim hominis virtus activa est in viro, passiva vero
6 in muliere. …
7 [line omitted from this selection]
8 … vir et mulier sunt eiusdem speciei …
```

The numbered rows preserve physical divisions; ellipses omit unverified words; `?` marks an uncertain reading or expansion. The normalized singular *requiritur … virtus* remains uncertain against the isolated reader's *requiruntur? virtutes?*; it is not presented as a settled manuscript variant. The preserved notation *generacōe* with proposed expansion *(generacione)* is retained in the linked image-only response. Other abbreviation boundaries have not been systematically resolved, so the normalized comparison must not be reused as a diplomatic transcription. The isolated reader flagged crowded strokes around the subsequent *adiutorium et adiutor* passage. This review does not assert a definite scribal correction there.

The [1507 edition, n15](https://archive.org/details/OEXV848RES/page/n15/mode/1up), lower right column, has *In generatione enim requiritur virtus activa et passiva correspondens active quasi contra eam divisa*. It continues at the [top left of n16](https://archive.org/details/OEXV848RES/page/n16/mode/1up) through the woman/man comparison and shared species. The connecting explanation supports the identification; Genesis 2:18 alone would not.

An additional check in the larger manuscript crop reaches *Tercio capitulo … Tu autem insidiaberis calcaneo eius*, agreeing with the transition at n16. This checks local sequence, not the full claimed span through Genesis 6. The comparison was candidate-aware, not held out.
