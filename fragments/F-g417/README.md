# F-g417: Noted monastic antiphoner, Ascension Day (end of Matins to Vespers II), the Ascensiontide Gospel antiphons, and the Common of Apostles

This large parchment leaf at Stams comes from a noted antiphoner (a choir book of the Office chants with their melodies) for the monastic cursus, the order of Office used by monks. In text order it carries, first, Ascension Day from the end of Matins through Lauds and a Day Hours antiphon to Vespers II: the verse *Pater sancte serva eos* (007360a), the responsories *Eduxit dominus* (006623) and *Ite in orbem* (007028), the five Lauds antiphons *Videntibus illis*, *Cumque intuerentur*, *Viri Galilaei*, *Exaltate regem*, *Sic veniet* (005392, 002063, 005458, 002764, 004925), the short responsory *Ascendens Christus* (006120), the Benedictus antiphon *Ascendo ad patrem* (001493), *Hodie secreta caeli* (003120), the responsory *Ascendo ad patrem* (006125) and the Magnificat antiphon *O rex gloriae* (004079), marked "ad ii ves(peras)". Second, the series of Gospel antiphons for Ascensiontide from the end of *Vado parare* (005308) through *Nisi ego abiero*, *Rogabo patrem*, *Euntes in mundum*, *Domine si in tempore*, *Dominus Iesus postquam*, *Illi autem profecti*, *Pacem meam* and *Non turbetur* (003882, 004662, 002739, 002384, 002419, 003176, 004204, 003937), then the Benedictus antiphon of the Sunday after Ascension, *Cum venerit paraclitus ... qui a patre procedit* (002043). Third, under a red "Ap(osto)l(orum)", the Common of Apostles: the antiphons *Isti sunt viri sancti* (003449) and *Ecce ego mitto vos* (002512), the invitatory *Regem apostolorum dominum* (001125) and the nocturn-I antiphons *In omnem terram*, *Haec est generatio*, *Exultate iusti*, *Clamaverunt iusti*, *Constitues eos* (003262, 002999, 002815, 001823, 001902), with the faint opening of a sixth, *[A]nnuntiaverunt* (probably 001429). A later hand added at the foot, without notation, the Magnificat antiphon of the Sunday after Ascension, *Cum venerit paraclitus quem ego mittam vobis a patre* (as 002477). The texts agree, with recorded differences, with the Zwiefalten antiphoner, Karlsruhe, Badische Landesbibliothek, Aug. LX.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-g417) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 135, parchment, 412 x 328 mm (measured 404/412 x 318/328 mm by Claudia Sojer), dated 1450-1499, title "x", with two images labelled 01r and 01v. The record has no description or identification.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for the chant texts and feasts. The basis is the sustained run of Ascension chants in a fixed order, the Gospel antiphon series and the apostles' chants with their psalm cues, read on both faces and compared on page images with Zwiefalten, Aug. LX, ff. 111v-112v and 166v-167r. The Ascension run (*Eduxit*, *Ite in orbem*, the five Lauds antiphons in this order, *Ascendens Christus*, *Ascendo* at the Benedictus, *Hodie secreta*, R. *Ascendo*, *O rex*) is shared exactly by Zwiefalten and Vienna, A-Wn Cod. 1890 (both southern German or Austrian, 12th century). A Cantus Database sequence search on eight of these chants found 19 inventories with all eight and 6 with them in the leaf's order (A-Wn 1890, St Gallen 388 and 391, Prague VI E 13 and XIII E 14d, Zwiefalten); St Gallen 388 orders the responsories and Lauds antiphons differently. The Cistercian books opened (Morimondo, Rein, Lubiąż) have a much shorter Ascension office, so the leaf is not in the Cistercian order and stands closest to the South German monastic tradition; this placement is an inference from chant order, not part of the identification. No inventory checked has the whole leaf. Differences from Zwiefalten, all seen on both books' images: the leaf lacks Zwiefalten's hour versicles after *Hodie secreta*, its short cues after *O rex*, its three responsories *Exaltare domine*, *Exaltare super caelos* and *Nimis exaltatus* before *Cum venerit*, and *quidem* in *Dominus [quidem] Iesus*; it reads "ad dextris" for "a dextris", "domino cooperante" for "deo cooperante", "Cum venerit" for "Dum venerit", and has *Annuntiaverunt* where Zwiefalten's sixth apostles' antiphon is *Principes populorum*. Zwiefalten has *Ecce ego mitto* as the first responsory of the Common (f. 167r), not as an antiphon. On the readings that separate Zwiefalten from A-Wn 1890 (the long *Euntes*, *sedet*, *ego vado*) the leaf sides with Zwiefalten.
- **Transcription:** source-assisted. The [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-g417/readings/stams-2026-10-30-image-only.json) covers every text line of both faces; its reader saw no proposed identification, and noted on the images the modern pencil labels "Stams, STA, Frg. 135r/v", the ink "1570" and blue "(Westbrunnen?)" in the left margin of canvas 0, none of which names the text. It left the face order open. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-g417/readings/stams-2026-10-30-source-assisted.json) is a research reader's (Claude) reread of every text line of both faces in four bands per face, with blue-channel enhanced crops of the rubbed apostles' lines, made with Zwiefalten available. It corrects the red "L(au)d(e)s?" before *Hodie secreta* to "Bndcs", the Benedictus cue, and reads the margin beside *O rex* as "ad ii ves". Unresolved: the short group "nu chy slo io" after *Pater sancte*, the marginal rubrics ("sext"?, "ad ix"?), the cue after *In nomine patris* ("Qui c(rediderit)?"), and the faint sixth apostles' antiphon. The added foot lines are pale. The neumes were not transcribed. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader (Claude) compared the leaf's lines on its images with Zwiefalten, Aug. LX, on BLB page images (2000 pixels wide), ff. 112r-v and 166v-167r. An independent research reader then read canvas 0 (labelled 01r), native boxes 500,1900,4400,800 and 500,2650,4400,850, the antiphons from the end of *Domine si in tempore* to the opening of *Cum venerit*, without the transcription, and compared them with Zwiefalten f. 112v on the BLB page image (id 1253474): the wording agrees apart from the variants listed above, including *domino* against *deo*. The same reader checked the junction of the faces (end of canvas 1 and top of canvas 0, below). A-Wn 1890 and St Gallen were seen only as Cantus Database transcriptions. Regional antiphoners closer to Stams (Brixen, Augsburg, Tyrolean houses) were not checked. No human review.

## Textual evidence

Fragment images (manifest order): canvas 0, labelled 01r, and canvas 1, labelled 01v, each 4912 x 7360 pixels. Boxes are native pixels, x,y,width,height. The leaf thus has the same chant texts as Zwiefalten in the same order within each section, but a shorter selection between them and a few word variants. The Common of Apostles follows the Sunday after Ascension with no Pentecost material; this arrangement of the book is unexplained. An apostles' feast in this season borrowing the Common (Philip and James, 1 May) was considered and not tested; the invitatory and antiphons without alleluia argue against it.

## Leaf, other items, and what is not claimed

- One large leaf, a single column of 16 (canvas 0) or 14 (canvas 1) text lines, each under a red four-line staff with Gothic neumes and C and F clefs; red or red-touched initials, a large red I for *Isti sunt* running down about nine lines, red differentiae (the "Euouae" endings of psalm tones, struck through in red) and red marginal sigla. The top margin is trimmed on both faces.
- The two cursive lines at the foot of canvas 0 are an old addition to the leaf, not a modern note. The "1570", "(Westbrunnen?)" and pencil labels are later binding or archival notes and were not used as evidence; an interlinear "d(omi)n(u)m"? with "14" above *patrem* in *Non turbetur* is a later mark.
- Not claimed: the date (the catalogue's 1450-1499 was not tested), the house or diocese of use, or why the Common of Apostles stands where it does.
