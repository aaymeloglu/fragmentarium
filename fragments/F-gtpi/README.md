# F-gtpi: Abbot Paul and the convent of Stams grant the Walthauserin, Johann's son and daughter and other Stiftsfreie at Tartsch acres and a meadow at Mals as a hereditary tenancy (Erblehen), [15]43

This parchment sheet in the archive of the Cistercian abbey of Stams carries one tenancy grant (Lehenbrief). Paul, abbot, and the convent of Stams, as lords of the land ("Recht grunt unnd Lehenherren"), grant in perpetual hereditary tenure ("Recht bestannden Erblehen", "erb unnd Paurecht") to the Walthauserin and to the son and daughter left by Johann [...], for themselves and for other Stiftsfreie (free tenants of the abbey), now at Tartsch in the Vinschgau, a set of plots at and around Mals: three Mattmal (a measure of land) of field "hindter Jann[...]", one and a half Mattmal of field below St Martin's church, land "under Prauradunt, auch Mallser pirt gelegen", and a meadow of two old Mannmad (another land measure) "in Stelnig(?)". The holding had partly fallen back to the abbey. The tenants must offer the Baurecht to the abbey first if they sell, with a month for the abbey to buy, may not sell to powerful clergy or similar people, must keep the land in good repair, and owe a yearly rent of grain, one and a half Mut of rye and one Mut of barley by the Clausen measure, to the abbey's Zinskasten at Mals, with mention of its grain store at Mais outside the town of Meran and a money rent of one pound Berner. The abbot announces his own seal. Only "Unnd Im Dreiundvierzigisten Jare" survives of the date; the century is supplied below. The sheet was later cut down to serve as the cover of a Stams register.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-gtpi) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 198, one parchment item, 368 x 283 mm (measured 335/368 x 246/283 mm), with the title "x" and no date, type or description. Modern additions on the sheet are a pencil "Hs. 119", "IV/13" in ink, the stamp "Stiftsarchiv Stams" and the pencil shelfmarks "Frg. 198r/v". The record has nothing to confirm or correct beyond the missing regest.

- **Identification:** identified, high. The regest is read on the image: issuer (Abbot Paul and the convent of Stams), beneficiaries (the Walthauserin and Johann's son and daughter, with other Stiftsfreie at Tartsch; the endorsement names the children Johannes and Petronella and also names Wilhalm; a further co-recipient, probably Anthoni, may be named in line 3, see below), legal action (grant in Erblehen, Erb- und Baurecht), object (fields and a meadow at Mals) and rent. Lost in the cut right edge: the place of issue, the day and month, the saint's day of the rent, and the thousands and hundreds of the year, so no dating clause can be converted. The year is given as **[15]43**: "Dreiundvierzigisten" (forty-third) is read, the century is supplied, not read. Stams had two abbots named Paul, Paul I Käsinger in the 1540s and Paul II Gay (1631-1638); only Paul I can issue a deed in a forty-third year. The Stams abbey archive's own charter regests, on monasterium.net, have grants by Abbot Paul on 5 October 1541 and in April or May 1543 ([1543](https://www.monasterium.net/mom/AT-StiAStams/Urkunden/L_LXVI_24/charter): "Verleihung Herrn Abt Pauls", Nassereith) and the first by Abbot Simon on 24 April 1544 ([1544](https://www.monasterium.net/mom/AT-StiAStams/Urkunden/L_LXXII_22/charter)). With the Kaisheim local history (Paul I, 1540-1544), they place a forty-third year of Abbot Paul in 1543; Siebmacher's list (Paul I to 1542, Simon from 1542) is contradicted by them. It is consistent with the host volume, a register titled for 1578, though that does not show when the sheet was cut to cover it. The "n." in "Wir paulus Abbt unnd n. das Conuennt", a blank for a name, and the absence of any surviving plica, seal slit or tag leave open whether the sheet is a sealed original, an unsealed engrossment or draft, or a chancery copy. No independent record of the act was found.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-gtpi/readings/stams-2026-10-30-image-only.json) covers the whole text face, the dorsal title and the endorsement. Its reader had no proposed identification or reading of the document in context; it reports that its background context mentioned a "Stams pilot" and that the sheet itself shows the shelfmark "Frg. 198r/198v", the stamp "Stiftsarchiv Stams" and the dorsal title "Ad Annum 1578", none of which names the act. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-gtpi/readings/stams-2026-10-30-source-assisted.json) is a research reader's (Claude) reread of the whole text face at native resolution in eight half-width crops, with the endorsement and the dorsal title. It corrects the first reading in "zu Mayss ausser der Stat" (not "Maysshauser der Stat"), "Tartsch" (not "Tautsch") and "Mitt[er?]kellner". An independent research reader (Claude) read lines 7-11, the intitulatio and the sealing and dating lines afresh on the image. The preserved readings, and the independent reader, have "Dreiunddreissigisten" for the year word; on the image it is "Dreiundvierzigisten", with "-tzig-" and no long s (box 1100,4780-2550,4930). Personal and place names are read letter by letter and some remain uncertain. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** there is no outside text of the act to compare. An independent research reader examined on the native image of canvas 1 the land description, lines 7-11 (boxes 1050,1580-2800,2100 and 2700,1580-4420,2100), the intitulatio (box 1000,1020-4420,1240) and the sealing and dating lines (box 1050,4700-4420,4900), with the report available. That check supported the land description and intitulatio but repeated the mistaken year reading "Dreiunddreissigisten". The reader added the first name in "Jacoben Mitterhouers erben" and read "Mittag" (south) before "Claus Ph[...]" as a direction in the bounds, not part of a name. A separate review of the date crop (box 1100,4780-2550,4930) read "Dreiundvierzigisten"; two fresh readers given no proposed date independently favored that reading, retaining some uncertainty in the internal letterforms. The original checker also consulted [Austria-Forum, Stift Stams](https://austria-forum.org/af/AustriaWiki/Stift_Stams) and the [Oberdeutsche Personendatenbank, Stams](https://oberdeutsche-personendatenbank.digitale-sammlungen.de/Datenbank/Ort:Stams) for Pelagius Bauer and Paul II Gay. Those chronology checks concerned the rejected [16]33 inference, not the corrected [15]43. The supplied century rests on the Stams abbey archive's own charter regests on monasterium.net (Abbot Paul granting in 1541 and 1543, Abbot Simon from 24 April 1544) and on the [Kaisheim local history](https://www.kaisheim.de/de/marktgemeinde-kaisheim/geschichte/kloster/tochter-stams) (Paul I Käsinger, 1540-1544); Siebmacher's snippet (1540-1542) is contradicted by the regests. The host volume, Hs. 119, was not examined, and monasterium.net, the Stiftsarchiv Stams inventory and the Südtiroler Landesarchiv were not searched. No human review.

The display first line has a large "W" with cadels. Johann's surname is lost at the right edge. The word given above as "anndern" (other), box 2550,1450-3300,1560 ([image](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-gtpi/Stams_STA_Frg_198_01v.jpg/2550,1450,750,110/full/0/default.jpg)), has a "th" ascender and is probably a name, "Anthonien" (Anthoni); two fresh readers given the crop without any transcription both read a forename Anthoni there, followed by a surname they read "Stirckh..." or "Stirkhsperg(?)", where the next word may instead be "Stifftfrey[en]". The line may therefore name a further co-recipient, Anthoni, now living at Tartsch ("yezo zu Tartsch hauswonhafft"); this is unresolved. The same readers read a surname after "Wilhalmen" in the endorsement ("Obersperger(?)", "Ottenprugt(?)") and a further "Anthony Stirckh...(?)" there. "Tartsch" is identified as Tartsch in the Vinschgau, near Mals; that is an identification of the place name, not a reading.

## Physical notes

- One document of about 38 lines in a German chancery hand, with flourished line fillers. The right edge is cut away, losing a word or more in each line, and the lower right corner is cut off; the lower third of the text face is blank.
- No plica, seal slits or seal tag survive in the part that remains.
- The sheet was cut down to wrap a register. The back carries its title in large script, box 4784,1129-6440,2087 on canvas 0 ([image](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-gtpi/Stams_STA_Frg_198_01r.jpg/4784,1129,1656,958/full/0/default.jpg)): "Des Gottshaus / Stambs Mitt[er?]kellner / Ambts urbar / Ad Annum 1578", the last digit written heavily. The first reader noted that the 8 may be written over another digit; this was not examined further.
- A three-line endorsement in a sixteenth- or seventeenth-century hand, box 2440,560-2820,2050 on canvas 0, read rotated ([image](https://fragmentarium.ms/loris/F-gtpi/Stams_STA_Frg_198_01r.jpg/2440,560,380,1490/full/270/default.jpg)): "W[...] ... Johannes und Petronella / der Walth[...] und Wilhalmen Stifftsrecht auch / [...] Stifftsfreyen Lehenbrief". It names the grantees independently of the text.
