## Status: IDENTIFIED

Richard of Middleton (Richardus de Mediavilla OFM), *Quaestio disputata de gradu formarum* (the disputed question on the plurality of substantial forms, c. 1286), second principal argument section: the "leprosus generat leprosum" proof and the "tria impossibilia" that follow it. Ed. R. Zavalloni, *Richard de Mediavilla et la controverse sur la pluralité des formes* (Philosophes médiévaux 2), Louvain 1951, pp. 35-169 (edition not accessible to me; see below). Verbatim excerpts of the same passage from Paris, Arsenal 516, fol. 151-152 are printed in E. Hocedez, *Richard de Middleton* (Spicilegium sacrum Lovaniense 7), Louvain 1925, pp. 391-392, and those match the fragment line by line.

Proposed leaf order (not independently recollated in the second review): the text runs [2]r -> [2]v -> [1]r -> [1]v (the end of [2]v col. b, "Restat ergo quod ratio quare leprosus generat leprosum semper vel ut in pluribus", continues without gap into [1]r col. a). So the bifolium is the central bifolium of its quire and the catalogue's provisional leaf numbers are reversed.

Arguments that the form induced by the semen is not corrupted by the "influencia" of the heavens nor by the generant ("...ab influencia contraria ...; influencia ... ad educendum illius forme de potentia materie ...; in illo instanti in quo anima intellectiva a deo infunditur corpori ..."), then the "frustra" argument ("natura ... si posset ... per viam breviorem ... frustra ... per viam longiorem; ... forme medie ... frustra"), then (col. b) the leprosus proof. Structure agrees with Hocedez's summary of the Quaestio (Arsenal 516, fol. 145-153): first principal argument = permanence of accidents/figure (our [2]r), second = the form educed from matter is not corrupted by the intellective soul, minor proved by "influencia"/"frustra" arguments plus the leprosus proof "confirmee par trois impossibilites" (our [2]v-[1]v). Hocedez explicitly says the leprosus proof is "absente de son Commentaire", so this is the Quaestio disputata, not Sent. II d. 17-18.

Secondary parallel (not the source): Hervaeus Natalis, *De unitate formae substantialis in eodem supposito*, §§ 84-93 (ed. De Rijk, Studia Artistarum 30, 2011, pp. 173-177) reports the same argument series (influentia celi, frustra/via brevior, leprosus, forma asini, esse genitum, patre meo/porco) in reworded form as the pluralists' rationes; wording differs throughout (e.g. "Preterea ad idem sic. Perfecte leprosus generat leprosum ... Sed hoc non esset verum, si fieret resolutio usque ad materiam primam in adventu anime rationalis"), so Hervaeus is a witness to the argument's circulation, not the text of the fragment.
