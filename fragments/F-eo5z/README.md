# F-eo5z — Richard of Middleton, De gradu formarum

Re-adjudicated 22 September 2026. The two-fragment pilot is included in this full-set review.

- **Identification: identified; high confidence.** Richard of Middleton, *Quaestio disputata de gradu formarum*. The leprosy argument followed by the ass/intellective-soul argument agrees in distinctive wording and sequence with the excerpts in Hocedez, *Richard de Middleton* (1925), p. 392. The familiar leprosy maxim alone does not establish this attribution. This review retains the identification; it does not independently establish authorship of the underlying work.
- **Transcription: selected, source-assisted readings; accuracy unmeasured.** Two fresh isolated readers transcribed separate crops, and CATMuS recognized the first crop. All raw outputs were preserved before comparison. They contain substantial errors and gaps. The selected readings below are the coordinator's subsequent image review with the old report and Hocedez available, not a corrected blind transcription. No specialist has checked these new readings.
- **Verification: manuscript crops compared with a scanned printed source.** The coordinator inspected native crops of fol. [2]v column b and fol. [1]r column a, and the image of Hocedez p. 392. Hocedez supplies selected Latin quotations and French discussion, not a full edition of the fragment. Zavalloni's complete edition and apparatus remain unchecked. The remaining columns, proposed leaf order, and witness novelty were not re-adjudicated.

Catalogue: Gent, Universiteitsbibliotheek, HS.2582/290. [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-eo5z). [Hocedez p. 392](https://archive.org/details/richarddemiddlet0000edga/page/n405/mode/1up).

## Selected manuscript evidence

Physical line numbers refer to the pilot crops, including clipped edge lines, not the old report's numbering. Parentheses mark proposed abbreviation expansions; square brackets describe uncertainty or interventions. This is a selected expanded reading, not a diplomatic edition.

**Crop A:** manifest canvas 0, fol. [2]v column b; native region `2000,1550,2000,1800`, no rotation. Lines 4–8:

```text
4 … Item hoc idem pro-
5 bo p(er) viam aliam. Certum est q(uod) [word cancelled in red, unresolved] leprosus
6 g(ener)at leprosum semp(er) vel frequen(ter). H(oc) a(utem) non esset
7 verum si in adventu intellective fieret resolutio usq(ue)
8 ad essentiam m(ater)ie prime sicut probabo. Ergo cum intellectiva …
```

A marginal abbreviation beside line 5 is provisionally expanded **p(er)f(ec)te(?)**. Its role as the replacement is plausible from the placement and printed parallel; the cancelled word is not reconstructed. The old report silently presented *perfecte* as ordinary running text. The image-only reader independently noticed both the cancellation and the marginal writing but could not resolve the latter.

**Crop D:** manifest canvas 0, fol. [1]r column a; native region `4650,1450,1950,1500`, no rotation. Selected lines 2–8:

```text
2 introduceretur forma asini [abbreviated words unresolved here]
3 anima intellectiva infunditur a deo m(ater)ie ipsius hominis
4 in illo eodem [instanti?] m(ater)ia ipsius asini equaliter esset
5 apta ad recipiendum animam intellectivam sicut
6 m(ater)ia ipsius hominis. Quia dispositio ipsius m(ater)ie ad hoc
7 q(uod) sit nata recipere formam est denudatio ab omni alia
8 forma. …
```

These connected clauses provide better evidence than the common leprosy maxim. They agree with Hocedez's quoted argument about an ass's matter being equally ready to receive an intellective soul once stripped of every other form. A material difference remains: the manuscript appears to have **equaliter**, where Hocedez prints **sequeretur**. This must stay a reported variant, not be silently harmonized. The isolated reader's *denudata* on line 7 is revised to *denudatio* with image and source assistance.

The crop also shows the transition to another *impossibile* at lines 16–18, consistent with Hocedez's sequence, but its last line is clipped. No complete transcription of that next argument is claimed. This is an additional passage check with an isolated reader; the coordinator already knew the passage from earlier work, so it is not held-out evidence.

## What the new tools contributed

The raw first reader recognized the physical correction but produced a very incomplete reading. The second crop's reader recovered more useful connecting text. CATMuS produced 24 detections for approximately 22 visible physical lines, including stray detections and a fragment of marginal writing placed last. Neither its line numbering nor its text can be adopted as a verified transcription.

In the two-text retrieval trial, one of three selected raw-reading anchors located Hocedez; after explicitly source-assisted expansion, all three selected anchors occurred in order there, with none in the Durandus control excerpt. The assisted matches required normalization but no edit correction. This is a small, candidate-aware localization check, not evidence of improved discovery accuracy or unique authorship.

The next substantive source check is the complete Zavalloni edition and apparatus. Until then, retain the identification, publish only scoped readings, and leave leaf reconstruction and witness novelty unresolved.
