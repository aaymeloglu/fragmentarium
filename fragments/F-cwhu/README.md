# F-cwhu: Breviary, probably Cistercian, Office of the Sunday within the octave of Christmas, with Fulgentius of Ruspe, *Sermo 2*, as Matins lessons 5-6

Stams Frg. 209 is a bifolium from a monastic breviary. On its better-preserved side it carries the
Office of the Sunday within the octave of Christmas.

- **One page** has Matins lessons 5 and 6, taken from Fulgentius of Ruspe, *Sermo 2 de duplici
  nativitate Christi* (PL 65, col. 727B-C). Between the two lessons stands the responsory *O regem
  caeli*.
- **The other page** has:
  - the end of Matins, with the responsories *Propter nimiam caritatem* (followed by *Ecce iam venit
    plenitudo temporis*, its verse in Morimondo) and *Verbum caro factum est*;
  - Lauds, followed by a commemoration (the red rubric "Comm(emoratio) de ..." with an abraded third
    word) with the antiphon *Dum medium silentium* and the collect *Omnipotens sempiterne Deus dirige
    actus nostros*;
  - the chapters for Terce and Sext.
- **The other side** is abraded. Its red rubrics include "In ii noc." and "In vigilia circumcisionis".

The order of the chants matches the Sunday after Christmas in Cistercian antiphoners. The responsory
*Propter nimiam* is recorded almost only in Cistercian books, so the use is probably
Cistercian.

The [catalogue record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-cwhu) is Stams, collection "Stams (Stealth
Published)", Frg. 209: parchment, 362 x 257 mm, online since 18 September 2021, title "x",
reconstruction summary by Claudia Sojer. The record has no description and does not name the text.
Its measurements fit the opened bifolium on the two images (Stams_STA_Frg_209_01r and _01v).

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence, for the office and the loci.
  - **The lessons.** Two Matins lessons agree line by line with Fulgentius, *Sermo 2* §4.
  - **The chants.** The responsories, Lauds items and Hours chapters read on the second page follow
    the order that three Cistercian antiphoners give for the Sunday after Christmas: Rein (Vienna,
    ÖNB, Cod. 1799\*\*), Morimondo (Paris, BnF, NAL 1411) and Lubiąż (Wrocław, BU, I F 401).
    *O regem caeli* stands after lesson 5 as the Sunday's fifth responsory, and *Propter nimiam* and
    *Verbum caro* are its eleventh and twelfth. The weekday office of Christmas week in the same
    books has only six responsories, so it does not fit.
  - **Use.** The run *O regem caeli*, *Propter nimiam* and *Dum medium silentium* is held complete by
    six inventories in the Cantus Index network, and all six are Cistercian antiphoners of the
    monastic cursus. 101 others have the other two chants but not *Propter nimiam*. The only
    non-Cistercian book recorded with *Propter nimiam* is Toledo AT1, which was not examined.
  - **Caveat.** The separating chant has a recent Cantus number used almost only in Cistercian
    inventories, so its absence elsewhere may partly reflect indexing. The use is therefore given as
    probably Cistercian, not as established by an Order-specific feature or rubric.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-cwhu/readings/stams5-2026-11-01-image-only.json)
  and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-cwhu/readings/stams5-2026-11-01-source-assisted.json).
  - A research reader reread on the images:
    - every line of both pages of canvas 1;
    - the top twelve lines of canvas 0, left page;
    - the top eight lines of canvas 0, right page.
  - An independent checker reread the weakest lines of canvas 1 and the rubrics of canvas 0, right
    page.
  - Much of the dark ink on canvas 0 is show-through from the other side: a mirrored crop reads as the
    Fulgentius lesson. Its own black text is faded; only its red rubrics and a few chant incipits
    are read.
  - Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:**
  - **Lessons.** A research reader compared them with PL 65 in Internet Archive OCR.
  - **Chants.** A research reader checked them in Cantus Index and fixed the day against the
    Cantus Database order data of the Rein, Morimondo and Lubiąż antiphoners (database rows, not
    folio images).
  - **Independent check.** Canvas 1 has no text the research had not transcribed. An independent
    checker therefore reread its weakest lines and compared lesson 6 (canvas 1, left page, lines
    16-19) with the PL 65 page image, which the research had used only as OCR. The checker also read
    the "Lectio quarta" and "In ii noc." rubrics on canvas 0, which the research had not relied on,
    and re-ran the chant-run count.
  - The coordinator read fol. 1v (canvas 1, left page), lesson text "[Deus] ... unigenitus in forma dei manens ... formam servi accipiens ... manens ut conderet" on the image; the comparison with the PL 65 page image rests on the research reader and the independent checker.
  - **Gaps in coverage.**
    - No Cistercian breviary or lectionary was compared, so the Fulgentius lessons are placed by
      their position among the chants.
    - The homily ending the third nocturn is unidentified.
    - The Lauds hymn and the antiphon for the Hours are not read.
    - Canvas 0 is placed by its rubrics and by leaf order only.

*O regem caeli* (Cantus 007297) with its verse *Natus est nobis* stands after lesson 5 on the other
page. In these antiphoners it is the Sunday's R 2.1, the first responsory of the second nocturn. The
collect *Omnipotens sempiterne Deus dirige actus nostros* and the chapters *Multipharie* (Hebrews 1:1)
and *Parvulus* (Isaiah 9:6) are the familiar texts for the Christmas season. They were not compared
with a printed Cistercian breviary.

**Attribution.** *Sermo 2* is printed as Fulgentius of Ruspe's in PL 65 (Migne, after Mansi). The
Sarum breviary has the same passage as "Sermo beati Fulgentii episcopi" (lesson iv on St Silvester;
ed. Procter and Wordsworth, fasc. 1, col. cclxxiv). The modern edition (CCSL 91A) was not checked.

## Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

**Leaf order.** On canvas 1 the two pages are not continuous: lessons 7-11 and their responsories are
missing between them. So canvas 1 is the inner side of a bifolium that is not the centre of its
gathering. That gives:
- **fol. 1r** (canvas 0, right page): rubrics "Lectio quarta" and "In ii noc.", the end of the first
  nocturn;
- **fol. 1v** (canvas 1, left page): lessons 5-6;
- **fol. 2r** (canvas 1, right page): the end of Matins, Lauds, Terce and Sext;
- **fol. 2v** (canvas 0, left page): red rubrics for the later Hours and Vespers ("Ad iii cap.",
  "Ad v(e)s(peras) a.", "R. Verbum?", "Ad m(agnificat)? a."), then "In vigilia circumcisionis [d(omi)ni?]. Ad
  v(e)s(peras) sup(er) ps(almos) a(ntiphona)" and "(con)f(essoris) pont(ificis). Cap(itu)l(u)m".

Physical details:
- A drawn shield of arms (ink and wash) is in the upper margin of canvas 1, left page. It was not
  identified.
- Binding strips run along the fold.
- Pencil shelfmarks "Stams, STA, Frg. 209r/209v" are modern.

**What is not claimed:**
- **Use.** Cistercian use is probable, not established.
- **Date and origin.** No date or origin is claimed; the formal textualis was not dated
  palaeographically.
- **Unread texts.** The homily ending at the head of fol. 2r ("...mus pudica p[ermansit]? in
  templu(m) ... nesciens uiru(m) ... [.]cepit filiu(m)"), the Lauds hymn and the Hours antiphon are unidentified.
- **Relation to other copies.** No relation to any other copy of the office is claimed.
