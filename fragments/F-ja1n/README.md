# F-ja1n: Johannes Faventinus, *Summa super Decretum Gratiani*, pars I, D. 86

The four spine strips carry part of the commentary of Johannes Faventinus (John of Faenza, about 1171) on distinction 86 of the first part of Gratian's Decretum, from the comment on chapter 8 (*Qui venatoribus*) to that on chapter 24 (*Tanta nequitia*). The work is a summa, that is a continuous commentary that quotes a few words of the law text (a lemma, here underlined in red) and then explains them. It is not the standard marginal gloss on the Decretum.

Catalogue record: [Fragmentarium F-ja1n](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-ja1n), Orselina-Locarno, Biblioteca Madonna del Sasso, MdS 16 Ga 8/F1-4; four parchment spine strips, 70 x 99 mm, Italy, dated 1301-1400, Gothic script, titled "Unidentified glosses on Decretum Gratiani", with no description. The catalogue had the subject right: this is commentary on the Decretum. It did not name the work, the author or the place in the Decretum. The script looks earlier than the fourteenth century to a non-specialist eye (a small rounded glossing hand of a kind usual around 1200); this is recorded as a tension with the catalogue date, and no new date is proposed.

- **Identification:** identified, confidence high. Four separate passages on the strips agree in wording and in order with the Summa as it stands in two manuscripts: Vatican City, Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana, Borgh. lat. 71, f. 57ra-57va, and Reims, Bibliothèque municipale, Ms. 684, the openings the library labels f. 35v-36 and f. 36v-37. The Summa is unprinted. Johannes Faventinus compiled it largely from two earlier summae, by Rufinus and by Stephen of Tournai, and the fragment shows exactly that mixture in one running text:
  - the passage on hunting in the arena is Stephen's (ed. Schulte, p. 107) and is not in Rufinus, whose treatment of hunting (ed. Singer, p. 176) is worded differently;
  - the passage on the publicans, *Misericordia plenitudo virtutum*, *Morte luitur* and *Insolentem infrenat iactantiam* is Rufinus's (Singer, p. 177); Stephen (Schulte, p. 108) words the same lemmata differently ("Publicani dicuntur qui publico officio fruuntur ...", "plenitudo ..., i. e. sine misericordia non potest quis plenus esse virtutibus") and has no comment on *Insolentem infrenat*;
  - the story of Elisha is in both sources but in neither edition: Singer prints "Tangit illam ystoriam, in qua legitur ....." with a note that Rufinus gives it after Paucapalea, and Schulte prints only "[historia secundum Paucapaleam et Rufinum]". It therefore does not separate the three works;
  - the scheme of dispensation at *Tanta nequitia* is in neither source as printed at distinction 86. Singer's text and apparatus pass from chapter 22 to Gratian's dictum *Similiter*; Schulte's text passes from chapter 21 to the same dictum (Schulte abridges, so this is weaker for Stephen). Singer prints a related but differently worded scheme only in his apparatus at C. 1 q. 7, as an insertion in one Rufinus manuscript (Paris, BnF lat. 4378).

  So the fragment has a Stephen passage and a Rufinus passage that Stephen words otherwise, joined in one text, followed by a passage neither edition has, all as in the two Faventinus manuscripts. One later work is a real rival for the last passage only: the Summa "Omnis qui iuste iudicat" (Summa Lipsiensis, ed. Weigand, Landau and Kozur, 2007, pp. 365-366) copies the dispensation scheme almost word for word; but its hunting and publican passages at distinction 86 are worded differently (search results inside the edition: "Hii omnes sunt infames, si ad id locant operas suas; secus, si uirtutis ostendende causa id fecerunt"; "Publicani dicebantur qui publica uectigalia exigebant"), and it has no hit for *Morte luitur*, *infrenat*, *dentatis* or *locet*. The Summa Parisiensis (ed. McLaughlin, pp. 67-68) and the Summa Coloniensis differ in the hunting and Elisha passages, on the evidence of search snippets. Not tested: Simon of Bisignano, Sicardus of Cremona, the Summa "Tractaturus magister" and Huguccio. The printed ordinary gloss (Rome 1582) gave no hit for the distinctive words in a search of its scanned text and was not read page by page.

  Wording. Most places where the fragment differs from the Vatican copy are shared with the Reims copy and are therefore variants inside the tradition of the Summa: *appellatur* or *appellantur* (Vatican *dicitur*), *et apparere*, the clause "qui ex laico factus est episcopus" after the first reference to C. 1 q. 7, *Sic et triplex est causa*, *scilicet*, *et extrinseca*, "sociam multitudinem", and "[venation]em mortale pec[catum]" without *non solum* before it. Both copies and the fragment read *potestatis*. Left unmatched in either copy: the fragment's "negocia seculi exigunt ... sectantur" with *autem* in the publican passage (both copies have the one clause "negotia seculi sectantur"), and a verb after *dispensationem* that looks like *movet* or *movent* (both copies *adducit*).

Against the sources. Stephen's printed text of passage A has "nisi in arena se passus sit honorari" and "sed etiam aspicere culpandum est"; the fragment has "in arena pas[...]" without *se*, with the two manuscripts. Rufinus's printed text of passage C has "publica seculi negotia vel lucra sectantur: in euangelio qui publice peccat publicanus appellatur" and "nullus potest etiam apparere ... misericordiam facit" (*faciat* in some of Singer's manuscripts); the fragment's *faciat* is that of both Faventinus copies.

## Order, other items, and limits

In both manuscripts the four passages follow one another as A, B, C, D within about one and a third columns, so the strips come from one leaf or one opening of the same book; their original arrangement on the leaf was not reconstructed. Image 1 also shows two further panels of the same lining, out of focus and unread. The third image is a colour chart.

Not claimed: a date or origin for the fragment; that its two unmatched readings represent a distinct version of the Summa; any closer relation to the Reims or the Vatican manuscript; that the fragment is a new or independent witness. A later summa that copies Johannes Faventinus through the whole of distinction 86 would be indistinguishable on this evidence; none was found among those tested.
