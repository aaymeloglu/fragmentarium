# F-ja1n: Johannes Faventinus, *Summa super Decretum Gratiani*, pars I, D. 86

The four spine strips carry part of the commentary of Johannes Faventinus (John of Faenza, about 1171) on distinction 86 of the first part of Gratian's Decretum, from the comment on chapter 8 (*Qui venatoribus*) to that on chapter 24 (*Tanta nequitia*). The work is a summa, that is a continuous commentary that quotes a few words of the law text (a lemma, here underlined in red) and then explains them. It is not the standard marginal gloss on the Decretum.

Catalogue record: [Fragmentarium F-ja1n](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-ja1n), Orselina-Locarno, Biblioteca Madonna del Sasso, MdS 16 Ga 8/F1-4; four parchment spine strips, 70 x 99 mm, Italy, dated 1301-1400, Gothic script, titled "Unidentified glosses on Decretum Gratiani", with no description. The catalogue had the subject right: this is commentary on the Decretum. It did not name the work, the author or the place in the Decretum. The script looks earlier than the fourteenth century to a non-specialist eye (a small rounded glossing hand of a kind usual around 1200); this is recorded as a tension with the catalogue date, and no new date is proposed.

- **Identification:** identified, confidence high. Four separate passages on the strips agree in wording and in order with the text of the Summa in Vatican City, Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana, Borgh. lat. 71, f. 57ra-57va. The Summa is unprinted, and this one manuscript was the only witness compared. Johannes Faventinus compiled his Summa largely from two earlier ones, by Rufinus and by Stephen of Tournai, so single passages of the fragment also stand in those works: the passage on the publicans and on *Misericordia plenitudo virtutum* is in Rufinus (ed. Singer, p. 177), and the passage on hunting in the arena is in Stephen (ed. Schulte, p. 107). Neither is the containing work: Rufinus's distinction 86 lacks the arena passage, the Elisha narrative and the passage on dispensation, and Stephen words the *plenitudo* comment differently ("sine misericordia non potest quis plenus esse virtutibus"). The identification rests on the combination of the Rufinus passage, the Stephen passage and the dispensation passage in the order of the Vatican copy. The fragment differs from the Vatican copy in several readings, and some of these agree with Rufinus's printed text: *appellantur* (Rufinus *appellatur*, Vatican *dicitur*); *et apparere* (Rufinus *etiam apparere*, Vatican *apparere*); a longer clause on the publicans ending *… sectantur*, where Rufinus has "negotia vel lucra sectantur" and the Vatican copy only "negotia seculi sectantur"; and *potestatis* where the Vatican copy has *etatis* (a parallel scheme printed in Singer's apparatus has *potestatis*). Other summae that draw on Rufinus were not collated, so a later compilation reusing the same material is not excluded by direct comparison.

Differences. In C the fragment has "publice negocia seculi exigunt" followed by a second clause ending "sectantur", *autem*, the plural *publicani appellantur* and *et apparere*, where the Vatican copy has a single clause, *publicanus dicitur* and no *et*. In D the fragment has a clause "que ex placito? fiunt a .e. episcopo" in the text after the first reference to C. 1 q. 7; the Vatican copy has a marginal addition at that point, which was not read in full, so whether the two are the same addition is open. Also in D: *Sic et triplex est causa que dispensationem movet* against *Sic triplex causa que dispensationem adducit*, *potestatis* twice against *etatis*, and *tollatur* (or *toleratur*) against *tollitur*. In D two lines after *principis* are too uncertain to quote. The readings shared with Rufinus's printed text are listed in the Identification bullet above.

## Order, other items, and limits

In the Vatican copy the four passages follow one another as A, B, C, D within about one and a third columns, so the strips come from one leaf or one opening of the same book; their original arrangement on the leaf was not reconstructed. Image 1 also shows two further panels of the same lining, out of focus and unread. The third image is a colour chart.

Not claimed: a date or origin for the fragment; that the fragment's variants represent a distinct version of the Summa or a derivative work; any relation to the Vatican manuscript beyond shared text; that the fragment is a new or independent witness. A second manuscript of the Summa at distinction 86 would show whether the variants are ordinary within its tradition.
