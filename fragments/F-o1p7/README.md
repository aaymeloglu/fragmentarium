# F-o1p7 — Oudenaarde, Stadsarchief, Boekbanden, BB 0027

**Second-model review, 20 September 2026:** Distinctive extended prose, including the De novo passage on possession of pension/election rights, matches the [1516 edition OCR](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_3RhpO70ZNiwC), with manuscript-image samples rechecked. The print page images were not inspected in this second review; fols.55v-56v and ambiguous readings still need facsimile verification.

- Catalogue: "Unidentified Roman law text", 14th c., one trimmed leaf, 266 x 158 mm, 1 image
  ([Fragmentarium overview](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-o1p7)).
- Layout: two columns. Column A (left) is complete at both margins (c. 69 lines). Column B (right)
  is cut on the right, losing roughly a third of every line. A few lines are lost at the foot.
  Only one side is imaged, so recto/verso cannot be settled; the wide margin is on the left.
- Decoration: red and blue initials with pen flourishing (D, Q, D, S in col. A; P, I in col. B),
  red pilcrows inside the text, red bracket lines in the margin marking sections.

**Status: IDENTIFIED.** Bernardus Compostellanus iunior (d. c. 1267), *Lectura in primum librum
Decretalium*, on X 1.6.23 *Bonae memoriae* (end of the gloss *Duximus confirmandam*) and X 1.6.24
*Querelam* (glosses *Querelam*, *De novo*, *Petebat*, *Subtractam*), i.e. the title *De electione
et electi potestate*. The text is canonistic, not Roman law: the "ff." citations are the
commentator's allegations of the Digest.

## Notes and queries

- The leaf's lemma at A1 is read as *Duximus confirmandam*, matching the print's lemma
  "Duximus confirmandam.]". Friedberg's X 1.6.23 ends "electionem memorati archiepiscopi
  confirmantes"; the lemma reflects the Vulgate wording, not an error.
- A20 "ut s. ostendi" (= ut supra ostendi), not "ut Host.". No Hostiensis citation was found, so
  the text is consistent with a 1260s author.
- Marginal red bracket lines and pilcrows correspond to the section breaks in the print
  ("¶ Sed quia", "¶ De novo", "¶ Dicitur autem", "¶ Item si capitulum").
- The blue initial at B41 is read as P (*Petebat*, the narratio of X 1.6.24); the print gives the
  equivalent gloss under the lemma *Subtractum*. The lemma differs, the gloss text is the same.
- Bernardus Compostellanus iunior's Lectura covers book 1 only and survives in few manuscripts;
  a 14th-century copy in a Flemish binding is a notable witness. Catalogue title "Roman law" should
  be corrected to canon law (Decretals commentary).

## Ruled out

- Corpus iuris civilis text and Accursian gloss: the "ff." references are allegations inside a
  canonistic argument (postulatio, legatus, papa, capitulum, electio), not Digest text.
- Decretals Glossa ordinaria (Bernardus Parmensis), Innocent IV, Hostiensis, Johannes Andreae
  Novella, Antonius de Butrio, Panormitanus: the phrase search hit these only for the topic
  (possessio iuris eligendi); none has the wording "pone quod unum annum vel plures semel vel bis
  omisi petere pensionem", which the Compostellanus print does.
- Corpus Corporum and Corpus Thomisticum: not applicable (canonistic lectura, not PL/Aquinas).
