# F-o1p7 — Oudenaarde, Stadsarchief, Boekbanden, BB 0027

- **Identification:** **Identified; high identification confidence.** The fragment transmits the text printed as Bernardus Compostellanus's *Lectura aurea in primum librum Decretalium*, on X 1.6.23 *Bonae memoriae* and X 1.6.24 *Querelam*. Sustained commentary prose agrees with Paris 1516, fols. 55v–56v, including the opening question about confirmation of the legate's proceedings and the distinctive *De novo* question about pension and election rights. The attribution is to the text of that edition, conventionally assigned to Bernardus Compostellanus iunior; historical authorship and textual dependence were not separately established.

Neither is a certified full-leaf transcription. Identification confidence does not measure transcription accuracy.

Significant differences and unresolved readings are not silently harmonized. At the first *ab honore* occurrence, the tentative manuscript reading *p(ro)hibeatur?* is supported by the print's *prohibeat(ur)*. Later, the manuscript appears to have *remoueri ... sine accusatore* and an abbreviated negative expression, whereas the print gives *ut removeatur ... sine accusatione*. The exact manuscript expression still needs palaeographic review.

The large Q belongs to *Querelam* on crop row 3, not the first word of row 4. The red row-3-right mark is pilcrow-like, but its following word remains unread. No correction, deletion, or marginal addition is securely identified in this span.

## Result and remaining limits

The sequence and extended distinctive prose identify the text in the 1516 edition. No competing compilation with this complete sequence was established; wholesale borrowing is not excluded merely by identifying the printed text. The shared canonical lemmata and Roman-law allegations alone would not settle the attribution. Phrase-search failures against other canonists are not treated as exclusions.

Much of the leaf, exact legal-reference abbreviations, the foot of column A, and most of column B remain uncollated. The number of missing connecting lines, the presence or absence of a Hostiensis citation, manuscript rarity, and witness novelty are not established by these checks.

The useful next work is a palaeographic check of the unresolved *Querelam* wording and column B lemma, or a complete manuscript-to-print collation if full transcription is wanted.
