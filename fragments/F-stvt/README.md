# F-stvt: Jean de Blanot (Johannes de Blanasco), *Libellus super titulo Institutionum de actionibus*, chapters on the actio negotiorum gestorum and the actio tutelae

The three parchment strips of this record carry Jean de Blanot's commentary on the title *De actionibus* of Justinian's Institutes (Institutes 4.6), a thirteenth-century civil-law work that sets out, action by action, a model complaint (a *libellus*) and notes on how it is framed. Strip 1 is identified with high confidence. One face has the end of the chapter on the actio negotiorum gestorum (the action between someone who looks after another person's affairs unasked and the owner of those affairs): the passage on the causes that bar the action, Azo's *Summa* on the Code, and the distinction between a person who manages "sponte" (of his own accord) and one who acts "necessitatis suspicione" (believing it necessary). The other face has the second section of the following chapter, on the actio utilis negotiorum gestorum against a curator, up to the red rubric "Libellus" that introduces its model complaint. Strips 2 and 3 are from the same work a little further on. Strip 3 has the discussion of several guardians in the chapter on the actio tutelae (the action on guardianship), and strip 2 has the end of the chapter on the contraria actio tutelae. These two placements rest on a run of matching words read on stained surfaces, not on a full collation.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-stvt) describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 256, in the collection "Stams (Stealth Published)", online since 18 September 2021. It lists three parchment fragment items, measured on Frg. 256_1 at 73/75 x 49/50 mm. The title is "x" and the record names no text, script or date. Two images each show all three strips, one with the faces labelled 1r-3r and one with the faces labelled 1v-3v. The record does not contradict anything found here, but it says nothing about the content.

- **Identification:** identified, high, for strip 1. On the face labelled 1v, ten consecutive line ends agree in wording and order with Blanot's text in the 1568 print, fol. 276r col. b to fol. 276v col. a. The run starts with "l. si pupilli § sed si ego" and goes through "[que] impediunt actionem neg. g. dic ut", "[A]zo in summa C. de neg. g. § cessat", "[locum] habent quando quis sponte administrat", "autem quis non sponte administrat", "[nece]ssitatis suspicione habebit locum neg.", "l. atqui natura § si libero. Et scias quod aliquando", and "[nece]ssitatis non tamen est necessitas ut si" to "[...] sit. Et idem iuris est in isto". On the face labelled 1r, lines 6-9 agree with fol. 276v col. b: "negotiationis vel", "negotii in quo male se habuerit", "[for]matur libellus : Libellus" with "Libellus" in red, and then probably "[de] Bla. minor ... quod". These lines come from the model complaint in which Blanot names himself as plaintiff. The work's own structure (a commentary on the action, a model libellus, then notes on the libellus) and the self-naming in the complaint place the text in Blanot's commentary. It is not a source he quotes: the shared allegations (citations of Roman-law texts) and Azo's *Summa* could also stand in other civilian works, but the surrounding argument and the formula do not. Strips 2 and 3 (faces labelled 2v and 3v) are placed in the same work, at fol. 278r col. b and fol. 277v col. b. Several readings there rest on weak letters, and the rectos of these strips and column a of strip 2 are not placed. Difference noted: 1v line 9 reads "non tamen est necessitas" where the print has "non tamen necessitas est". The 1542 edition was seen only in snippets.

Differences. On 1v line 9 the manuscript has "non tamen est necessitas" against the print's "non tamen necessitas est". On 1v, "a(utem)" in line 3 and "l. iii § hac" in line 7 are uncertain readings that fit the print but are not shown by it. On 1r line 9 the form of Blanot's name is uncertain. The readings on strips 2 and 3 are too weak to support any variant. On strip 1 the lines run at about 45-55 printed characters each, and the printed wording fits the lost part of each line. No relation between the manuscript and the print is inferred from these differences.

The attribution rests on the 1568 running heads "Ioan. de Blanasco" and the section heading "Super tit. de actio. instit.", on the title of the 1542 edition, and on the medieval incipit "Incipit libellus compositus per Johannem de Blanasco, Burgundionem, Masticonensis diocesis, super titulo institutionum de actionibus", which Delisle reports (seen as a snippet). Within the text, the author names himself in the model complaints ("ego Ioan. de Bla.").

## Leaves, layout and limits

Strip 1 is one column of a two-column page. The face labelled 1v has the line ends and the outer margin, and the face labelled 1r has the line starts with its margin. By text order, 1v comes before 1r: the gap is about 80 printed lines, roughly one manuscript column. The line pitch is about 3.6 mm. The visible part of each line is about 6.3 cm and holds about three fifths of the text, so the full column was about 10.5 cm wide. This was a large book with two such columns, not a small-format one. Strip 3 (faces 3r, 3v) and strip 2 (faces 2r, 2v, which show the end of one column, the intercolumn space and the start of the next) come from a passage about two printed leaves later. The faces 3v and 2v column b lie about one printed column apart, which fits adjacent columns at the same height of one page. This is not established, because the line ends of 2v column a could not be aligned line by line with 3v. Which leaf or leaves the three strips came from, and the text of the faded rectos 2r and 3r, remain open. Red ink is used for the rubric "Libellus", for paraph signs and for touches on letters.

No claim is made about the date or origin of the manuscript, its version of the text, or its relation to the printed editions or to other copies.
