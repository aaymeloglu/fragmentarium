# F-fpb6: Rufinus of Aquileia (after Eusebius of Caesarea), *Historia ecclesiastica*, II 6,6 - 10,2

This leaf in Stams is from a copy of the *Historia ecclesiastica* (Church History) of Eusebius of Caesarea in the Latin translation of Rufinus of Aquileia. It carries book II, chapters 6 to 10 of the Latin text: the riot over Pilate's aqueduct, Pilate's suicide (ch. 7), the famine under Claudius (ch. 8), the death of James and Clement of Alexandria's story of his accuser (ch. 9), and the death of Herod Agrippa (ch. 10), in Theodor Mommsen's edition pp. 123,4 to 127,14. The identification is not new: P. Bruno Griesser of Mehrerau identified the leaf and collated it against Mommsen's edition in January 1928, and his notes and letters are photographed with the leaf on the Fragmentarium record. The present work confirms his identification and his readings on the images.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-fpb6) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 184: parchment, one fragment item, 270 x 154 mm (measured as 245/270 x 128/154 mm by Claudia Sojer, Innsbruck), title "x", no catalogue date and no linked description. Its six images are the two faces of the leaf (184_01r, 184_01v) and four photographs of added papers (184_Adl_01r to 184_Adl_02v04v). The record's metadata do not name the text, but the added papers do. The first, headed "Stiftsarchiv Stams No. 305", describes a single leaf of 26.8 x 15.3 cm, trimmed at left, right and top, with 23 lines and three more lost at the top, in a script of the tenth century; identifies it as "Eusebius-Rufinus, hist. eccl. II 6,6 (Mommsen 123,4) incidit perturbatio - II 10,2 (Mommsen 127,14) consonantiam divinarum"; cites the edition of Schwartz and Mommsen (Leipzig 1903); and lists the leaf's readings against Mommsen's text and apparatus, against the Mantua 1479 edition, and its errors of word division. It is signed P. Bruno Griesser. Two letters from Griesser, dated Mehrerau, 8 and 15 January 1928, report the identification and the collation to the abbot. A card numbered 305 describes the leaf as parchment of the end of the tenth or beginning of the eleventh century, 27 x 15.5 cm, written on both sides, lifted from a binding, containing Eusebius's Church History book II, chapters 6-10, with some departures from the copy in Stams Cod. VIII D 20.

- **Identification:** identified, high, already known from Griesser's notes of January 1928 on the record itself. The basis is continuous agreement in wording and order with Mommsen's text over two passages on each face, with the Latin chapter numbers VII and VIIII in red where Mommsen's edition places chapters VII and IX. The agreement is with Rufinus's Latin wording, sentence by sentence, so the leaf is a copy of his translation and not a work quoting it: the passages run continuously across chapter breaks marked as in the edition. The leaf differs from Mommsen's text in small readings, several of which Mommsen's apparatus records for single manuscripts: "in fugam confe[rti]" with manuscript N for "conversi", "ex admissa sacrilegii tempore" for "admissi", "sic in historiis" for "sicut", "desscribunt" for "(Olympia)das scribunt", "habitantibus hierosolima" without "sanctis", "cum fames erat designans" with N for "quo fames", "obtulit" for "obtulerat", "hac de sublimi" for "ac", "dei uox" for "dei voces", "dederat" for "dederit", and two false word divisions, "dum nouissem p(er) reb(us)" for "dum novis semper rebus" and "tantam consonant iam" for "tantam consonantiam". Griesser's lists record the same readings.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-fpb6/readings/stams-2026-10-31-image-only.json) of 31 October 2026 and the [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-fpb6/readings/stams-2026-10-31-source-assisted.json). A research reader reread on the native images lines 1-8, 11-16 and 17-23 of the recto and lines 1-9 and 17-23 of the verso, 37 lines in all. Lines 9-10 of the recto and the stained lines 10-16 of the verso rest on the first reading. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the lines listed above with Mommsen's edition on page images of pp. 123, 125 and 127 from archive.org, and checked Griesser's lists against the same lines; every reading checked agrees with his. A second research reader then read canvas 1 (verso), lines 9-17, native box 2850,1800,5100,2950, which the report had left to the first reading and Griesser, on the image before opening the edition, and compared them with Mommsen pp. 125 and 127 ([page image](https://archive.org/details/p1eusebiuswerke02euse/page/n136/mode/1up), [page image](https://archive.org/details/p1eusebiuswerke02euse/page/n138/mode/1up)). The lines follow Mommsen 125,21 to 127,6 continuously, with the red chapter number X where Mommsen has [X], and no substantive variant; the reader's divergent first readings on the stained surface resolved to the edition's words on a second look. That reader knew the identification from the report. Griesser's full collation of 1928, not repeated here, covers the whole leaf against Mommsen's apparatus.

The edition prints the Latin as Rufinus's translation facing Eusebius's Greek; the attribution is standard.

## Leaf order, other items, and limits

The recto (184_01r) begins in II 6,6 and the verso (184_01v) continues after a loss of about three lines at the top of the page, as Griesser noted. Canvases 2 to 5 show Griesser's description and collation (184_Adl_01r, 184_Adl_01v), his two letters (on 184_Adl_02r04r and 184_Adl_02v04v) and the card numbered 305 with a later note dated 26 August 1975. Other marks are the Stiftsarchiv stamp and pencil shelfmarks.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the leaf (the 1928 notes and the card give the tenth or early eleventh century), its place among the manuscripts of Rufinus's translation, and whether other leaves of the same book survive in Stams.
