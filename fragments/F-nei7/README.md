## Content and attribution hypotheses

1. **Sentences I commentary, one continuous question**, confirming the unpublished catalogue note. The question runs: (a) Word and Love as principles of God's operation ad extra, (b) corollary that the emanation of creatures presupposes the emanation of the divine persons and that "the philosophers" erred for ignoring it, (c) answers to two objections about "primitas et fecunditas" in the Holy Spirit, (d) thesis that innascibilitas in the Father is "ratio generationis et primitatis" and a notion distinct from paternity and active spiration, adding only a negation, (e) a summary of 3 properties / 4 relations / 5 notions deferred to d. 26, (f) "Circa secundum principale" begins and the copy stops.
2. **Position**: after d. 18 and before d. 26 (references above), i.e. d. 19–25 of Lombard; d. 20 (potentia generandi / producing in the Son and Spirit) is the natural home for a discussion of primitas and fecunditas.
3. **Possible school**: Franciscan/Bonaventurian, not established by the vocabulary alone. "Primitas", "fecunditas naturae et voluntatis", "emanatio", "innascibilitas = ratio primitatis" are Bonaventure's (I Sent. d. 7, d. 27 p. 1 q. 2, d. 28). The "ovis non signata" example for a negation that "notifies" is in Bonaventure I Sent. d. 28 ("sicut ovis non signata a signata"), Alexander of Hales, Summa I n. 431, Albert; William of Auvergne calls it a "vulgatum exemplum". But the author holds that innascibilitas says "solam negationem, non aliquam positionem" (against Bonaventure's fontalis plenitudo reading, and against the Florence-manuscript Franciscan quoted by Friedman, Medieval Trinitarian Thought, who says it has "positio"). He also says "nihil omnino intendo asserere temerarie", an Olivi-school habit.
4. **The corollary's exact wording is a known question title**: "Utrum emanatio creaturarum a Deo praesupponat emanationem personarum divinarum" is q. 15 of Francis of Meyronnes' Quodlibet (16 questions; Venice 1520 print col. 260P–261; listed in Histoire littéraire de la France 36, p. 321, and in Schabel's Companion to Theological Quodlibeta; Franziskanische Studien 53 (1971) and 54 (1972) also note a manuscript question "Q. 2, f. 115va: Quaeritur utrum emanatio creaturarum a Deo praesupponat emanationem personarum" in an unnamed collection). This provides a candidate comparison but does not date the fragment or the origin of its topic: the fragment is a Sentences commentary, not a quodlibet, and no wording was compared.
5. **Not a cut-down leaf**: text is complete on the recto; the verso stops after 10 lines. Whatever the codex was, this leaf ends the copied stretch.
