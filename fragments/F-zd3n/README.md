# F-zd3n: Judgment of Hanns Rott, judge in a fief case, declaring Balthasar Mitterhofer's holding at Nauders fallen back to Stams abbey (16th century, date not established)

This parchment bifolium is part of a judgment written as a small booklet, a form used for court letters too long for a single sheet. The text is German, in one sixteenth-century chancery cursive. Hanns Rott writes as judge in this case under fief law ("der sachen Lehenrichter"), sitting with the "Lehennsgenossen vnnd mannen", the fellow vassals who give the verdict. He rules for the procurator, or legal agent, of the abbot and convent of Stams ("des herrn Abbts zu Stambs vnnd Conuents") against Balthasar Mitterhofer ("Mitterhouer"). The text calls the abbot and convent "Lehen grundt vnnd zinssherrn", meaning the feudal lords, landlords and rent-holders of the plot. Mitterhofer held a plot from the abbey, "es sy garten oder Plaz", at Nauders in front of his own house. He had dealt with it with a Clas Paur without the knowledge and consent of the abbot and convent. Earlier proceedings had taken place before the court at Nauders, and Mitterhofer had there freely given up his tenant's right ("Paurecht") and hereditary right ("Erbrecht"). By unanimous verdict, under the Tyrolean Landesordnung ("vnser Tyrolischer Lanndsordnung, die Lehenrecht betreffend"), the plot is declared to have fallen back freely and wholly ("fur frey vollig haimgefallen") to the procurator for Stams. Mitterhofer keeps his claim for his improvements ("mit verbehallt seiner Beserungen"). He must hand over all deeds concerning the plot within a set number of days after the judgment. The procurator's costs and damages are not remitted. Rott issues the judgment at the procurator's request under his own pendant seal. The opening of the document, with the protocol (the issuer's opening and titles), is on lost leaves. The date is not established: it was not found on the leaves read, and it may stand in the unread block on fol. 2v or on the lost opening leaves.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-zd3n) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 190, parchment, 482 x 343 mm, two images (01r, 01v). The record has the title "x" and gives no date, type or content, so it identifies nothing. The description of a large parchment bifolium fits.

- **Identification:** identified as an archival document, confidence high. For a document, the identification is its regest: issuer, parties, legal action, place and date. Here the regest is read on the image: judge Hanns Rott; the procurator of Stams, Peter N(?)üben; the defendant Balthasar Mitterhofer; the action, forfeiture of a tenancy at Nauders; and Rott's sealing. The date is not established: it was not read, and whether it was on the lost opening leaves or in the unread block on fol. 2v is open. Under the repository's rule for archival documents, a single lost or unread element, stated in the regest, does not make a fully read document partial; the action and all parties are read on the image, so the identification is recorded at high confidence. The procurator's surname is uncertain: the initial may be N or C, and the vowel carries a u-hook. An outside record concerns the same plot: the abbey's grant of 1534 to Mitterhofer, described in section "Textual evidence". It records a related act, not this judgment. No record of the judgment itself was found.

## Leaf order, date and what is not claimed

- **Leaf order:** fol. 1r (canvas 0 right) begins mid-sentence and continues on fol. 1v (canvas 1 left) and fol. 2r (canvas 1 right). Fol. 2r ends with the sealing clause and "Dist(?) Recht(s):". At least one leaf before fol. 1r is lost, carrying the opening of the judgment. Fol. 2v (canvas 0 left) has a faded block of seven short lines at the top: "fertigung seins dr[...] / gedings vnnd Lehen[...] / vnnser hanns T[...] / [...] zu Heng[...](?) / Ambrosy Co[...] / Vlrich / Anno(?)". Faint traces run across the rest of the page. Whether this block lists the judges announced by "Dist Recht(s):" and holds the date is not established. It is unread and might yield to ultraviolet or multispectral imaging.
- **Date:** not established; the dating clause was not read on the surviving leaves. The text refers to it twice ("an heut Dato", "nach dato diser vrtl"). The judgment presupposes that Mitterhofer held the plot from Stams, so it is probably later than the 1534 grant. That is an inference from the outside record, not a reading. A modern pencil note on the outer face (canvas 0, about x 1700-2650, y 1700-1970) reads "1534 2[6/8] Juni", with an uncertain day digit. That date falls a few weeks before the grant. The note's relation to this act, to the grant or to the host volume is unknown, and it was not used. The appeal to the Tyrolean Landesordnung and the hand are consistent with the sixteenth century; no closer date is claimed.
- **Original or copy:** the clean layout and the sealing clause with a pendant seal suggest the sealed original booklet rather than a draft or register copy. No seal or seal cord survives on this sheet, and this is not claimed as established.
- **Other items:** on fol. 2v there is a later shelfmark-like "QXIII n 1", written mirror-wise, and there are modern pencil numbers on the sheet. A band of faint writing in another hand runs along the gutter of canvas 1 and is unread.
- **Not claimed:** the place where Rott sat for this judgment, which the surviving text does not state; Rott's office beyond his role as judge in this case; the full name of the procurator; and the identity of the Clas Paur named on fol. 1r.
