# F-1m6r: Boniface VIII, *Liber Sextus* 1.3.10-11, with the *Glossa ordinaria* of Johannes Andreae

Stams, Frg. 78 is a parchment leaf from a glossed copy of the *Liber Sextus*, the collection of papal decretals (legal rulings) issued by Boniface VIII in 1298. The main text, in two columns in a large gothic hand, is title 1.3, De rescriptis: the end of chapter 10, "Si propter tua debita", and chapter 11, "Statutum, quod circa iudices", as far as §3 (Friedberg II, cols. 941-942). A pasted strip adds line ends of chapters 8-9. Around the text runs a frame gloss (a commentary written around the text) in a small hand. It is the *Glossa ordinaria*, the standard commentary, of Johannes Andreae (completed about 1301), here on chapters 8, 10 and 11, and the leaf itself signs one gloss "Jo. And.".

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-1m6r) is Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 78, parchment, one fragment item, 294 x 184 mm (measured 212/294 x 97/184 mm by Claudia Sojer), online since 18 September 2021. It has two images (01r, 01v), the title "x", and no date, description or identification. The leaf was reused in a binding: it is cut to a wide upper part and a narrower lower tongue with clipped corners, with turned-in strips at the sides. Face v carries the offset of a printed book in roman type. Besides the modern "Stams, STA, Frag. 78r" and "78v", the only later writing is a pencil note on face r above the main column, "V d/M,9 (1548)", which reads as a shelf or host-volume reference with a date, not a title. No title of the text by a later hand was found on either face.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence, as two separate claims.
  - Main text: sustained agreement in wording and sequence with Friedberg's text of VI 1.3.10-11 across both faces, and the text runs from the foot of face r column b ("ut nullis nisi dignitate preditis") straight into face v column a ("aut personatum optinentibus"). The leaf differs from Friedberg's text in four readings, none recorded in his manuscripts: it omits "in ea" after "caveatur", has "sicut docuit experiencia" for "sicut experientia docuit", "dinoscantur" for "dignoscantur", and "litteris commissionis" for "literis commissionibus".
  - Apparatus: sustained agreement in wording, citations and order of glosses with the ordinary gloss as printed in the Turin edition of 1588. On face v the glosses on chapter 10 ("Vacabunt", "Impediri", "Praeiudicium aliquod", including "Unde prima lectura magis mihi placet") and on chapter 11 ("Statutum", with its division of the chapter into four parts) were collated. On face r the upper and right-margin gloss is the gloss on chapter 8 ("Sed contrarium est verum ... ex transmissa ... q. dominicalis ... Goffredus"). The gloss differs from the print in small points: it omits "translatione" in "Vacabunt", cites "xlv. di. c. ij." where the print has "55. dist. c. 2.", adds the author's siglum "Jo. And." at that point, and has "statuit" (uncertain) for "statuitur". The siglum names Johannes Andreae inside the leaf. Johannes Monachus's commentary on the *Sextus*, which divides chapter 11 into six parts, does not match the leaf's four-part division and was not compared further.
- **Transcription:** source-assisted. The [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-1m6r/readings/stams-2026-10-30b-image-only.json) covered the main text and sample gloss lines on both faces. The rest of the face r gloss rests on the first reading, and four lines of face v column a are unread under the print offset. Accuracy has not been measured.

The differences are those given under Identification, with spelling apart (dyocesis, experiencia, preiudicium). Friedberg's critical notes on these lines record variants only for "posset" (possit AG), "concedimus" (concedamus ABCG) and "Sed et" (et omitted in BCD), and the leaf agrees with his text in all three. In the gloss the leaf cites a chapter as "c. una" where the print has "capitulo unico", and omits "translatione".

## Leaf layout, other items, and what is not claimed

The main text on face r is the second column of a page, and it continues at the head of the first column of face v, so face r is a recto. The pasted strip at the left of face r carries line ends of the first column of that recto (chapters 8-9: "[cu]ius gratia p[er quam ma]ndabatur ... per nos fu[erat] ... ei cui po[st] ... restitucione(m)"). A lighter piece beside it, across a seam, shows a few line beginnings ("[I]dem", "incerta", "revo" three times) that were not placed. Words "Johannes an[...]" at the top of that strip are in the gloss hand and belong to the gloss.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the manuscript, which book "V d/M,9 (1548)" refers to, whether this copy carries an early state of Johannes Andreae's apparatus, and the textual place of the leaf among manuscripts of the *Sextus*.
