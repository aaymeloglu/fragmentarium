# F-utlg: Alexander de Villa Dei, *Doctrinale*, chapters 8-9 (vv. 1234-1295 and 1397-c. 1450), with the Glosa "Admirantes"

The two bifolia at Innsbruck come from a glossed copy of the *Doctrinale* of Alexander de Villa Dei, a Latin grammar in verse. The large script carries verses 1234-1295 of chapter 8 (on government, *de regimine*) and verses 1397 to about 1450 of chapter 9 (on construction, *de constructione*), in Reichling's numbering, with interlinear glosses. The small script around them is a commentary which, in the three passages collated, is the anonymous thirteenth-century gloss known from its opening words as the Glosa "Admirantes" ("Admirantes quondam philosophi ..."): those passages agree clause by clause with the commentary in Paris, BnF, lat. 8422, and two of them with Charles Thurot's printed excerpts of that gloss.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-utlg) describes Innsbruck, Universitäts- und Landesbibliothek Tirol, Frg. 54, as two parchment pieces, each a bifolium with one leaf nearly whole and the other trimmed, 312/314 x 218/220 mm (Frg. 54_1) and 310/312 x 216/218 mm (Frg. 54_2), Latin, dated to the fifteenth century, with the title "Grammatical text" ("Grammatikalischer Text"). The description (Claudia Sojer and Viviana Kleinlercher, 2020) notes a larger script for the main text and a smaller one for the commentary, and blue paint stains that are not original. The typed index card linked from it says only "2 Fragm. einer grammatikal. Handschrift, 15. Jh., 18.5 x 22, Pg." The title fits as far as it goes; neither the record nor the card names the *Doctrinale* or its gloss, and no host volume is given.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence, in two layers. The verses are the *Doctrinale*: the first and last verses of each page, and two longer runs (vv. 1426-1435 in the first reading, and vv. 1436-1447 read on a reduced image), match Reichling's edition, compared in its machine-read text. The commentary: three passages on two different bifolia and in both chapters (A and B on the same column of leaf IV recto, C at the foot of leaf II verso) follow the commentary of BnF lat. 8422, compared on page images of ff. 48r and 39v, clause by clause, with the same school examples ("magister cuius generis", "primus amor Phebi", "hac Arethusa tenus", "sillogizantem ponendum est terminos ... clericum vel logicum", "Sor est maior Platone uno pede") and the same objections and replies in the same order. Passages B and C also correspond to Thurot's excerpts of the Glosa "Admirantes" from an Orléans manuscript (1869, pp. 349-350 and 312). The name rests on Thurot, who describes the gloss, its incipit and its copies, among them a Paris manuscript written in 1276; that this Paris manuscript is lat. 8422 is inferred, since the shelfmark is garbled in the machine-read text of his book, and the incipit and colophon of lat. 8422 were not checked on the page. Only three passages were collated with Paris, so a later commentary that takes over the Admirantes text is not excluded for the rest of the fragment. A fourth passage (D, on "Cum quia dum", v. 1427) agrees with Thurot's excerpt in its second half and not in its first, and was not found on the Paris pages fetched. Differences from Paris are small changes of wording and order, listed below. In the verses, v. 1440 appears to end "equiperatur" where Reichling prints "assimilatur"; this was read only on a reduced image and remains to be checked.
- **Transcription:** the [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-utlg/readings/generic2-2026-11-27-image-only.json) was made before any source was known and covers canvases 0 and 2 only. The [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-utlg/readings/generic2-2026-11-27-source-assisted.json) was made by a research reader who reread passages A-D at native size and read the verse lines of canvases 1 and 3 from reduced overview images. The rest of the commentary on canvases 0 and 2 rests on the first reading alone; the commentary on canvases 1 and 3 was not read. Unresolved or doubtful words are marked: three short words after "ex defectu" in passage B, "reperimus(?)", "actor(is/es)(?)", "necessitas(?)", and in passage D "implicatam(?)" and the word read as "relativis", now left uncertain. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared passages A-C with BnF's scans of the microfilm of lat. 8422, and passages B-D with Thurot's excerpts and the verses with Reichling, both in machine-read text only. The coordinator read passage B, from "Verbum multotiens. Hactenus manifestavit actor constructionem impropriam" to "magister cuius generis, et item primus amor Phebi", on canvas 2 (Innsbruck_ULBT_Frg_054_02_r), native box 2644,2050,3777,2560, against the [page image of BnF lat. 8422, f. 48r](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b10035258q/f51.item), column b: the passage follows Paris in sequence and with the same examples, where Paris has "nominis vel verbi" and "si ex parte" for the fragment's "verbi vel ex parte nominis" and "si a parte". Two fresh readers transcribed three neutral crops of this record, all from canvas 2, without any transcription or source. They gave "faciat contra ipsos" at the end of passage A, and one of them "necessitas"; both read "actores" where the research reading had "actoris", "implicatam" where it had "implicantibus", and an abbreviated "r'lois" (perhaps "relationis") where it had "relativis", and those readings are withdrawn or marked uncertain; the three short words after "ex defectu" remain unresolved, though both readers saw the same letters. Gaps: the commentary of canvases 1 and 3 is unread; the commentary was collated with Paris at three places only; the Orléans manuscript (Médiathèque, MS 299, Thurot's base) is not online at the pages needed and was not collated; the verses of canvases 1 and 3 were read on reduced images. No human palaeographic review is claimed.

Differences from Paris. In A: habitum / tactum, diligentissime / diligentius, aliud exemplum / exemplum, "iam" not in Paris, Solebat quidam / Quidam solebant, and the last sentence worded differently. In B: verbi vel ... nominis / nominis vel verbi, a parte / ex parte, Paris without "substantivum" before *est*, Hunc defectum / Defectum istum, ubi / cum, the word order of "defectus est aliquando nominis substantivi", and Paris adds *sillogizantem*; Paris also has words at "ex defectu, et hoc ... aut" where the fragment's three short words are unresolved. In C: in capitulo de participio / in participio, quia non videtur / Ergo videtur non, fit / est, Dicendum / Ad quod dicendum est, unum potest / unus poterit, scilicet / construitur. The three passages lie on two leaves of lat. 8422 nine folios apart, as they lie on two different bifolia of the fragment. These are differences of wording and order within the same sequence of argument; they do not show how the fragment's copy relates to lat. 8422.

Thurot's excerpts (machine-read text of the 1869 volume, from the Orléans manuscript). For v. 1433 (pp. 349-350) Thurot prints the same text as passage B, from "Hactenus manifestavit actor constructionem impropriam" to "clericum vel logicum". At two places where his Orléans text differs from Paris ("que provenit" against "provenientem"; "Namque per naturam illius adiectivi ... datur intelligi" against "in qua per naturam illius adiectivi ... potest intelligi"), the fragment has the Paris reading. For v. 1294 (p. 312) he prints "Unum comparativum potest construi cum diversis ablativis, sed diversis respectibus et habitibus. Cum uno construitur ex vi comparationis, et cum alio ex vi excessus quantitatis", the same sentence as in C. For v. 1427 (p. 349) he prints "Sicut relativa, ratione copulationis quam includunt, habent preponi suis verbis, sic et illa, cum naturam habeant ligativam orationis ad aliam orationem, ratione illius ligationis vel copule suis verbis in constructionis ordinatione preponuntur". The fragment's passage D reads: "¶ *Cum quia dum.* Dicto de dictionibus principaliter interrogativis et relativis, hic declarat de quibusdam non principaliter naturam relativam implicatam(?), ut patet ordo. Dicit ergo actor quod iste dictiones *cum*, *quia* et alia que sequuntur suis verbis in construendo preponuntur. Cum naturam [one word uncertain] habeant ligativam orationis ad aliam orationem, ratione illius ligationis seu copule verbis suis in constructionis ordine preponuntur". Its second half is close to Thurot's; its first half is different. Thurot says that the Paris text departs markedly from the Orléans text; whether passage D stands in lat. 8422 as it does here was not established.

Attribution. The *Doctrinale* is Alexander de Villa Dei's, by the title and introduction of Reichling's edition. The gloss is anonymous; its name and description are Thurot's. He gives the incipit "Admirantes quondam philosophi cum viderent rerum mirabiles bonitates", notes that later glosses cite it as "glosa Admirantes" or simply "glosa" and that its latest authority is John of Garland, and names a complete copy at Orléans (dated 1284; the IRHT lists the glossed *Doctrinale* there as MS 299, which is taken to be the same book) and a Paris copy written in 1276 by Galterus de Frelesyo. Identifying that Paris copy with lat. 8422 rests on the garbled shelfmark, on lat. 8422 being a glossed *Doctrinale* of about that date, and on its text agreeing with Thurot's excerpts at these verses. The gloss has no edition.

## Leaf order, other items in the images, and what is not claimed

The verses give the order of the leaves. The two bifolia were probably nested, 54_2 outside 54_1: leaf I recto (canvas 3, right half) has vv. 1234 ("Accusativi regimen subiungitur istis", with a flourished red initial) to 1258; leaf I verso (canvas 2, left half) vv. 1259-1270; leaf II recto (canvas 1, right half) vv. 1271-1282; leaf II verso (canvas 0, left half) vv. 1283-1295; then vv. 1296-1396 are missing, a gap of 101 verses; the surviving leaves carry about 54-62 verses per pair (62 on leaves I-II, about 54 on leaves III-IV), so the number of lost leaves is open; leaf III recto (canvas 0, right half) vv. 1397-1417; leaf III verso (canvas 1, left half) vv. 1418-1425; leaf IV recto (canvas 2, right half) vv. 1426-1435; leaf IV verso (canvas 3, left half) vv. 1436 to about 1450, the last lines cut and under paint. The verse limits on canvases 1 and 3 were read on reduced images and may be out by a line or two. Blue paint, ink blots and brown stains affect all four faces. Canvas 4 shows a colour chart and no text.

Not claimed: the date and origin of the copy, which are the catalogue's (fifteenth century) and were not examined; the date of lat. 8422 and the gloss's circulation in the thirteenth century date that manuscript and that text, not this copy; that the whole commentary on the fragment, including the unread commentary of canvases 1 and 3, is the Glosa "Admirantes"; which form of the gloss the fragment follows beyond the two readings noted above; the host volume; and whether this copy is otherwise known to scholarship.
