# F-mrl7: Anonymous, *Lumen anime* (C-type text), Marian chapters, §§474-475 and 480-481

Stams, Frg. 108 is a small torn scrap in a Gothic cursive book hand, with about eleven lines of one column on each face, from the *Lumen anime*. The *Lumen anime* is a late-medieval preachers' handbook that sets quotations from natural philosophers and Church authorities side by side, each followed by a moral application. The fragment comes from its chapters on the Virgin Mary. One face (canvas 0) carries the comparison of Mary to the firmament that holds up the "machina" of heaven and earth, with an authority "Athanasius in sermone", and then the rubric "Alkabicius in theorica planetarum: [Cu]ncti quidem planete motu ...". In Nigel Harris's edition of version C these are §474 and §475. The other face (canvas 1) has "Anshelmus in sermone: Tu auctor salutis mee et turris fortitudinis contra demonem ...", then "Gilbertus de secretis mulierum: Mulier debilis et infirma sepe speculum quod inspicit visibus suis ipsius frangit", and then "Constantinus". These are the end of §480 and §481 in Harris. The canvas 1 face agrees with Harris's C text. At §474 the fragment does not agree with that text as Harris indexes it. It has a quotation and authority that the edition's index does not list there.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-mrl7) describes Stams, Stiftsarchiv, Frg. 108 as paper, 130 x 55 mm (measured 18/130 x 15/55 mm), with two images (01r, 01v). It gives the title "x", no date, no linked description and no identification. The record therefore says nothing about the text. The size fits the scrap in the images. The writing material was not examined.

- **Identification:** identified, high confidence for the work and for the canvas 1 locus. The basis is canvas 1. There the Anselm quotation, the rubric "Gilbertus de secretis mulierum" and the simile of the sick woman whose gaze breaks the mirror run in the same order and largely the same words as Harris §§480-481, with differences listed under Textual evidence. Canvas 0 has the next rubric after §474 in Harris's C text, "Alkabicius in theorica planetarum: [Cu]ncti ... planete", and the theme of Harris's §474, the firmament whose swift motion keeps the earth from falling. Its quotation, "Unde Athanasius in sermone: Dudum in inuicem tam celi quam terre [r]uisset machina si non sustentass[es] ... o tu mater dulcissimaque media(?)", does not match §474 as Harris indexes it. Harris's index of authorities lists Athanasius only at §§32 and 77, and lists Ignacius at §474. The same sentence stands in a print of version A (Augsburg 1518), where it is given to "Orosius super cant." and speaks of Mary in the third person. The canvas 0 face is therefore a variant application, with a different authority, at the place of §474. It does not agree with the edited C text there. Alternatives considered:
  - The 1518 print of version A has the Athanasius/Orosius sentence. Its Marian chapter has nothing corresponding to the canvas 1 passages and has Centobius, not Alkabicius, after it. This rests on MDZ OCR of scans 40-51.
  - The Augsburg 1477 *Liber moralitatum ... Lumen anime dictus* (ed. Matthias Farinator) has the same comparison of Mary to the sky in different words. A full-text search found no "Mulier debilis", "Anshelmus in sermone" or "Gillibertus" in it.
  - Version B was not compared.
  So "C-type" means that the sequence and the canvas 1 text agree with Harris's C. It does not place the fragment within the C tradition, which Harris describes as transmitted in very varied forms.
- **Transcription:** an [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-mrl7/readings/stams7-2026-11-01-image-only.json) and a [source-assisted reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-mrl7/readings/stams7-2026-11-01-source-assisted.json). Research readers reread both faces on the native images: canvas 1 lines 1-8 and canvas 0 lines 1-11. The source-assisted reading corrects, among others, the first reading's "deserit intrum" to "de secretis mulierum", "Dudum i[.] munita" to "Dudum in inuicem", "Dulassaq3" to "dulcissimaque" and "ysagoica" to "theorica". The weakest readings are on canvas 0: the opening words of lines 1-2, the word after "dulcissimaque" in line 5 ("media[trix]" or possibly "maria"), the word after "machinam" in line 3 ("Quia" or "huius"), and the rubricated authority in line 7 ("Ph-" or "Pl-"). The lower lines of both faces are damaged and only partly read. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared canvas 1 lines 1-5 and canvas 0 line 6 with Harris's Latin text of §§480-481 and §475. This comparison used Google Books snippets only. Harris's page images were not seen. Canvas 0 lines 1-5 were compared with the 1518 print of version A, using its page image on MDZ (scan 47), and with Harris's German parallel to §474 and his index of authorities, again from snippets. The Latin of Harris §474 could not be obtained, so the absence of the Athanasius quotation there rests on the index and on failed snippet searches, not on the page.
  - The coordinator read canvas 1 (Stams_STA_Frg_108_01v, native box 3100,2450 to 5000,3000) on the image (not blind: the research reading of this passage was known): the Anselm quotation (*Tu auctor salutis mee et turris fortitudinis contra demonem, omnem eius in nobis tu perimis pravitatem*), the rubric *Gilbertus de secretis mulierum* and *Mulier debilis et infirma sepe speculum quod inspicit visibus suis ipsius frangit*, followed by a passage citing *Constantinus de medicina*. The sequence agrees with Harris's §§480-481 as far as the Google Books snippets reach; no page image of the edition was compared.
  Gaps in coverage:
  - The rest of the §475 quotation and the end of §481 could not be compared, because the snippets break off.
  - Canvas 0 lines 7-11 and canvas 1 lines 6-8 were not compared with any text.
  - No manuscript of version C was consulted.

Differences on canvas 1 against Harris:
- "Tu auctor ... tu perimis" against "occidis et perimis". Only the end of Harris's Anselm quotation appears in the snippet, so its opening was not compared.
- "Gilbertus de secretis" against "Gillibertus in de secretis".
- "ipsius frangit" against "ipsa frangit".
- "Nam" added after the "Racio est" rubric, and "quedam infectiua exalacio visus" against "quedam exalacio visus infectiua".
- In line 5 the fragment goes on with "comestiu(m)(?) puer(orum)(?) corp(or)is ..." where Harris has "ex mensili corrupcione proueniens menstrui sanguinisque immundi". The fragment's line is uncertain and was not collated further.

On canvas 0 the fragment differs from the 1518 print of version A in the authority (Athanasius in sermone against Orosius super Cantica) and in person: it addresses Mary as "tu", with "sustentasses ... o tu mater", where the print has "sustentasset illa". The words leading into the quotation are worded differently from the print's "Celum quidem velocitate sui motus ...". They share its theme, "sustentas totam machinam [huius?] mundi" against the print's "sustentat totam machinam huius mundi". Harris's German §474 gives the same theme ("Das firmament helt vnd behabt mit snellheit seiner wegung das ertrich in der mitt vor vallen"), under the authority "Phylosophus in der durchsichtigen kunst". These differences show that the texts differ at this point. They do not show how the fragment's text relates to A or to the edited C.

## Leaf order, other items, and what is not claimed

Canvas 0 comes before canvas 1, because §§474-475 precede §§480-481 in Harris's numbering. This agrees with the catalogue's 01r/01v labels. The two faces are mirror images of one torn shape. Each holds about eleven lines, which leaves the rest of §475 and §§476-480 for the lost part of the page. That fits a single column of about this line length, but the amount of lost text was estimated, not measured.

Other items: the modern pencil shelfmark "Stams, STA, Frg. 108r" or "108v" in the top margin of each face.

Not claimed:
- the date and origin of the copy. The hand was not examined palaeographically.
- the writing material, which the catalogue gives as paper.
- the fragment's place among the manuscripts of version C, or any relation of dependence between its text and versions A or C.
- the authority and wording of the edited C text at §474.
- whether other Stams fragments come from the same book.
