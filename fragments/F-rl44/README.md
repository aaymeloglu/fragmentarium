# F-rl44 — Johannes Serapion, *Breviarium medicinae*

The **upper-cover leaf** is identified at high confidence as Johannes Serapion the Elder's *Breviarium medicinae* (also printed as *Practica Io. Serapionis dicta breviarium*), tract VII, chapter XX, on electuaries. The distinctive discussion of cumin and three kinds of pepper, its patient anecdote, and the surrounding recipe sequence agree with the Venice 1497 edition, fols. 71v–72r. Avicenna's *Canon* contains a substantial parallel, but does not preserve the same surrounding sequence.

The [preserved image-only reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-rl44/readings/batch-2026-10-11-image-only.json) is unverified machine text. The selected readings below are [preserved source-assisted corrections](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-rl44/readings/batch-2026-10-11-assisted.json), made by a Codex reader through direct manuscript and edition image inspection. A second Codex coordinator inspected the inside manuscript image against edition fol. 71v and confirmed the sequence. Neither check constitutes expert palaeographic review or certification of a full transcription. The lower-cover leaf has not been compared.

## Manuscript and edition

[Fragmentarium F-rl44](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-rl44): Hasselt, Rijksarchief te Hasselt, Archief Schepenbank Mechelen aan de Maas, nr. 2. The catalogue describes two parchment leaves, 290 × 180 mm, in Northern Textualis, dated 1301–1400. The inspected upper-cover leaf has two columns with red rubrics and alternating decorated red and blue initials. A modern slip obscures part of its outside face.

The comparison edition is *Practica Io. Serapionis dicta breviarium*, Venice, Bonetus Locatellus for Octavianus Scotus, 16 December 1497, BSB 2 Inc.c.a. 3538. Its [title page](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00061068?page=5) names the *Practica* and several other works in the volume. The [BSB bibliographic metadata](https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/presentation/v2/bsb00061068/manifest) attributes the *Breviarium* to Serapio Senior. This distinguishes the matched work from the younger Serapion's *De simplici medicina*, also named on that title page. No claim about a particular translator or manuscript recension is made here.

## Alternative and scope

The cumin commentary also appears in Avicenna, *Canon*, book V, summa I, tract III: [Lyon 1522, printed fol. 410r, scan 302](https://archive.org/details/1835057.med.yale.edu/page/n302/mode/1up). That edition and [Milan 1473, scan 453](https://dl.ub.uni-freiburg.de/diglit/avicenna1473a/0453) were inspected as images. Their surrounding recipe sequence differs from the manuscript. Serapion agrees both before and after the shared prose, including the intervening Galenic *diapipereon* discussion and the following *diapipereon usitata* preparation. This establishes the containing text of the inspected leaf; it does not determine the direction of literary borrowing or exclude local variants and abridgements.
