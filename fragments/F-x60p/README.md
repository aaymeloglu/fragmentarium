# F-x60p: Justinian, *Digestum vetus* with the Accursian gloss, Digest 4.8.42-49 and 4.8.52-4.9.3

Five of the six strips are identified with high confidence as pieces of a single leaf of the *Digestum vetus* (the first of the three volumes in which the Middle Ages copied Justinian's Digest), with the *Glossa ordinaria* of Accursius, the standard thirteenth-century commentary, in the margins. One page of the leaf carries Digest book 4, title 8 (*De receptis*, on arbitrators), laws 42 to 49; the other carries the last law of that title (4.8.52), the red rubric of title 9 (*Nautae caupones stabularii ut recepta restituant*, on the liability of shipmasters, innkeepers and stable-keepers) and its laws 1, 2 and the opening of 3. This is a standard law book: the catalogue already had the kind of text right, and what is added here is the book, titles and laws, the reconstruction of the six strips into one leaf, and the identity of the gloss. The sixth, strip 3, has only a few words in the gloss hand and is not placed; that it belongs to the same leaf is likely from the hand and the shared binding, not shown.

The [Fragmentarium record](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-x60p) describes Leuven, KU Leuven Bibliotheken Bijzondere Collecties, Ms. 1032: six parchment strips, 80 x 75 mm, in Northern Textualis, dated 1301-1350, with red pen initials, catalogued as "Unidentified Roman law text, with gloss". Its reconstruction summary says the strips were used as a spine lining, were probably recovered from the same binding as Ms. 1033 ([F-18n8](https://fragmentarium.ms/overview/F-18n8)), and that strips 2 and 6 carry the offset of a twelfth-century liturgical manuscript. The description fits; it names no work, book or title. The catalogue date has not been reassessed.

- **Identification:** identified, high. On strips 2, 4, 5 and 6 the surviving line ends and line beginnings of the main text agree with the Digest in wording and sequence across two titles, including the rubric of title 9, the praetor's edict "in eos iudicium dabo", and three successive red initials V, P, I standing at the inscriptions (the headings naming the jurist excerpted) of Ulpian, Paulus and Julian at D. 4.8.45-47. A first candidate, D. 42.8 (*Quae in fraudem creditorum facta sunt ut restituantur*), fits the rubric ending and "dabo" on strip 4 but not the words that follow; the Code, Institutes and Authenticum are excluded by the jurists' inscriptions. In the margins, five glosses to D. 4.9.1 (on *mesonautam*, *vel per navis magistrum*, *nobis magis*, *si ante* and *vectorum*) agree with the printed Accursian gloss in wording and in order, and one ends with the siglum "ac." in the manuscript; the marginal text is therefore the ordinary gloss itself, not an earlier or independent apparatus. Small differences of wording are given below. Strip 3 and one face of strip 1 carry only scraps in the gloss hand and are not placed.
- **Transcription:** [source-assisted selected reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-x60p/readings/batch-2026-10-12c-source-assisted.json) of the lines collated. The [image-only first reading](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/fragmentarium/blob/main/fragments/F-x60p/readings/batch-2026-10-12c-image-only.json) recorded many of the letter groups correctly (for example "fraudib(us)", "tam accep", "corrigit(ur). ali(a)s hic loq(uitur)") without connecting them. A research reader reread all the quoted lines on the images with the Digest and the printed gloss at hand and expanded the abbreviations from them; the reading of strip 5 and of the gloss on strip 1 does not rest on the first reading, which gave only isolated words there. Every line is cut at one or both ends. Accuracy has not been measured.
- **Verification:** a research reader compared the manuscript images with a transcription of the Mommsen-Krueger text of the Digest (not a page image of that edition) for the main text, and with page images of the glossed Paris edition of 1566 for the gloss and for the layout of the passage. The coordinating model read the main column of strip 2 "verso" on the manuscript image (canvas 3, native box 2450,200,3570,3000: "uersus eos q / ne nunc q / fraudibus / tam accip / exercet quam / qui nauis n / sint sed de ex / tit nec enim") against D. 4.9.1.1-2, and the upper lines of strip 6 (canvas 10, "habemus", "nobis magis") against D. 4.9.1.6-7; it did not check the gloss. Gaps: strip 3 and the "recto" of strip 1 are unplaced; the column on strips 5 "recto" and 6 "verso" is abraded and was read only for a few words or not at all; the gloss on strip 2 "recto" was not collated; the gloss was compared in one sixteenth-century print only, with no manuscript or incunable of the gloss.

Differences. In the main text no difference of wording was seen in the letters that survive, with two reservations. The first rubric line appears to end "-nes (et)", read as *caupones et*, where neither the Digest edition nor the 1566 print has "et"; only the last letters of the two rubric lines survive. Between "quia necesse" and "committere" on strip 4 the line is too short for the full wording and a small cursive addition stands beneath it, so some words were probably omitted and supplied; the addition is not legible. In the gloss the manuscript seems to read "per hoc corrigitur" for the print's "quod hoc corrigitur" (the first sign is uncertain) and "de ea(?) actione dicit" for "de illa actione dixit". Beside strip 5 "verso" the gloss words "id est principium l[egis]" and "sufficit enim" correspond to the printed glosses on *Et ita* and *Recepit* at D. 4.9.3 pr.; these were not collated further.

## The leaf, other items in the images, and limits

All the placed pieces belong to one leaf with two columns of main text of about 31 to 37 letters a line and gloss in both side margins. On the page with the later text, strip 2 gives the gloss and the line beginnings of the first column and strip 4 its line ends, the two overlapping by two lines; strips 6 and 5 follow one another in the second column, and strip 1 gives the line ends of the gloss beside it. The catalogue's "recto" and "verso" are labels of convenience: the earlier page (D. 4.8.42-49) is the "verso" of strip 4 but the "recto" of strips 2, 5 and 6. From the Julian initial on strip 4 to D. 4.8.52 at the same height on its other face is about 1,600 letters, one column of roughly fifty lines, which fixes the order of the two pages; which of them was the recto of the original book is not determined. On strip 5 "recto" a red initial D with "e reb(us)"(?) probably opens D. 4.8.43, with the end of 4.8.42 above it; that column is abraded and the reading is tentative.

Strip 3 has a few words in the gloss hand on one face and nothing visible on the other; strip 1 "recto" has the beginnings of gloss lines with marginal signs. Neither is placed. The offset of the twelfth-century liturgical book noted by the catalogue is visible on strip 6 "verso" as large letters at right angles to the legal text, and red rules and a red-framed box on strip 2 "recto" may belong to it; it was not read.

Ms. 1033 (F-18n8) was not examined, so nothing is claimed about whether it comes from the same leaf or the same manuscript. No claim is made about the date or origin of the leaf, about the form of the Digest text or of the gloss it carried beyond the passages collated, or about any value of the fragment as a witness.
