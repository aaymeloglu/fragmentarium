# F-ahiy — Brugge, Archief Grootseminarie Brugge, Ms. 95/130

**Identification: partial, medium confidence.** An alphabetical collection of *auctoritates*, with source passages identified in the *Moralium dogma philosophorum*, Ambrose, Jerome and Augustine. The compiling work and compiler remain unidentified. Shared quotations do not distinguish an original compilation from an adaptation of another florilegium.

**Transcription:** selected source-assisted excerpts, reviewed against manuscript images by a Codex model. Both full sides were inspected for layout; the detailed comparisons cover the upper *Modestia* band, two adjacent Jerome excerpts under *Monachus*, and one *Mors* item. No complete transcription, accuracy measurement, or human palaeographic review is claimed.

**Verification:** manuscript images were compared with electronic Latin texts; the *Moralium dogma* comparison used the extracted text of Holmberg's edition. A separate Augustine passage on side B provides a further source-layer check, not confirmation of a proposed compiler. The standard *Manipulus florum* index and its *Monachus* entry were also checked. Their shared wording is evidence of circulation, not an attribution of this leaf.

## Selected manuscript readings and comparisons

Parentheses mark proposed abbreviation expansions; `?` marks uncertainty. These are selected excerpts, with physical line breaks retained. Crop names identify the cached derivatives recorded in the assisted reading, not additional witnesses. The crops overlap the full-side images.

### Side A, left column: Modestia

`aL0.jpg` is the same band as `image-001.png` in the frozen packet. Its clipped first row counts as line 1. Crop lines 4–7 below correspond to the sixth through ninth extant physical lines of side A's left column, counted from the top of the full rotated image. The opening `ia` continues `Modest-` at the end of crop line 3.

```text
aL0.jpg:04 ia tocius vite ornatu(m) tollit. id(em) e. Et de off(ici)o ei(us)? et utilita-
aL0.jpg:05 te. lib(ro) de dog(ma)te ph(ilosoph)or(um) [reference uncertain]. Modestia cultu(m) et motu(m) et om(n)em
aL0.jpg:06 n(ost)r(a)m occupat(i)onem ult(ra) defectu(m)? et cit(ra) excessu(m) sistit. unde
aL0.jpg:07 Modestia est in a(n)i(m)o (con)tine(n)s mod(er)atio cupiditatu(m). tullio? 2?
```

The *cultum et motum* definition has a direct parallel in [*Moralium dogma philosophorum*, ed. John Holmberg (1929), Latin p. 42, “De modestia”, lines 1–2](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_mor004mora02_01/_mor004mora02_01.pdf#page=102). The edition reads “Modestia est cultum et motum et omnem nostram occupationem ultra defectum et citra excessum sistere.” The manuscript apparently omits *est* and ends with *sistit*. Its earlier reference visibly names a *liber de dogmate philosophorum*, although the numeral and its exact attachment remain uncertain. The source assists the proposed *defectum* and *citra*, but the former remains uncertain on the image. The comparison used extracted edition text; the attempted PDF screenshot was unavailable.

The preceding *tocius vite ornatum tollit* corresponds to Ambrose's wording about modesty at [*De officiis* I.45, §218](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/De_officiis_ministrorum): “eam quae totius vitae ornatum attollit modestiam”. The manuscript's visible *tollit* has not been replaced with *attollit*. The following definition, beginning *Modestia est in animo*, and its “Tullius” attribution remain unresolved here; the apparent *modestia/temperantia* issue has not been settled by inspecting Cicero's original text.

### Side A, right column: the final Monachus quotation before Mons

In `aR1.jpg`, count the clipped top row as line 1. Lines 5–7 lie in the upper third of side A's right column and end immediately above the *Mons* heading. Line 5 begins by completing the previous quotation; the new one starts at *prima*.

```text
aR1.jpg:05 mu(n)dus ante me(n)dicos. J(e)r(onimus)? ad heliodor(um) x. ep(istola)? xii? g. prima v(ir)tus mo-
aR1.jpg:06 nachi est (con)te(m)pne(re) ho(m)i(nu)m iudicia. et s(em)p(er) ap(osto)li recordari dice(n)tis
aR1.jpg:07 Si ad(huc) ho(m)i(n)ib(us) placere(m) x(rist)i s(er)vus no(n) esse(m). J(e)r(onimus)? ad pa(m)machiu(m).
```

A boxed reference beside the following *Mons* line is read separately as `xi? ep(istola)? vi? a.`; those uncertain numerals should not be normalized to modern letter numbering.

The quotation was compared with [Jerome, Ep. 66.6, *ad Pammachium*, in the electronic Latin transcription](https://patrologia-lib.ru/patrolog/hieronym/epist/epist03.htm). The manuscript's *prima virtus monachi est* changes the source's order *Prima virtus est Monachi*. Its *placerem* agrees with the main reading displayed there, which also records *placere vellem* as an alternative. The agreement extends through the judgment-of-men clause and the apostolic quotation; the visible addressee supports the source connection.

The preceding paragraph in the same crop, about wealthy monks under a poor Christ, was also compared with Jerome Ep. 60.11 on that page. The sequence concerning the poor Christ, rich devil, wealthy church members and their earlier poverty agrees, but the manuscript compresses and alters the wording: *sunt seculares* and *possident omnia* differ from the displayed *fuerant saeculares* and *possideant opes*. This second connection confirms the use of Jerome material, without identifying the surrounding compilation or certifying every letter of the preceding paragraph.

### Side B, right column: a summary of Augustine on death

`bR0.jpg` covers the top of side B's right column. Its first main-text row counts as line 1; the quoted item occupies lines 2–3. The cached crop script records the rectangle (1260, 180)–(2420, 900) in the 2884 × 4000 rotated full image.

```text
bR0.jpg:02 Q(uo)d mala mors no(n) e(st) q(uam) vita bona p(re)cessit. na(m) mala(m) mortem
bR0.jpg:03 facit q(uo)d ea(m) sequitur. no(n) q(uo)d p(re)cedit. i de civit(ate) x? d. g. [next item omitted]
```

The source is [Augustine, *De civitate Dei* I.11, Latin Library text](https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/augustine/civ1.shtml), with the same passage checked in [the Logic Museum's Latin text](https://www.logicmuseum.com/wiki/Authors/Augustine/City_of_God/City_of_God_Book_I). Augustine begins “Mala mors putanda non est, quam bona vita praecesserit” and explains that what follows death makes it bad. The manuscript makes a *Quod* summary, omits *putanda*, changes word order, and adds *non quod precedit*. Its uncertain chapter numeral is retained as `x?`, not silently corrected to XI. This additional passage on the other side was checked with source assistance.

## What this establishes about the compilation

The alphabetical lemmata, multiple named authorities, short quotations, *Quod* summaries and internal references support describing the leaf as an alphabetical tabula or florilegium of authorities. They do not establish a particular compiler, order, institutional affiliation, or direct use of the original authors.

A concrete comparison with the [Electronic Manipulus florum Project's index](https://manipulus-project.wlu.ca/browse.html) shows *Modestia*, *Monachus* and *Mors* consecutively in that part of its standard edition. It lacks the intervening lemmata visible here. Nevertheless, [*Monachus* f](https://manipulus-project.wlu.ca/lemmata/Monachus.html) contains the same *prima virtus monachi* saying and attribution to Jerome. Its surrounding extract arrangement differs from the inspected manuscript sequence. This supports rejecting a straightforward copy of the standard edited sequence; it does **not** exclude adaptation, rearrangement, shared sources or dependence. The *Modestia* entry did not load and was not compared.

No actual candidate entry by Jean de Fayt, from *Flores paradisi*, or from a Kilwardby-related tabula was examined for this comparison. Likewise, no comprehensive comparison against *Catholicon*, Papias, Hugutio, Brito, biblical *Distinctiones*, Vincent or Bersuire was made. Genre differences may guide a search, but they are not checked exclusions.

The compilation remains unresolved. A useful next comparison would seek the consecutive excerpts and the manuscript's distinctive reference system, particularly the Jerome addressees and boxed numerals, in an actual candidate witness. Additional matches to Jerome or Augustine alone would not resolve that question. Most of both sides, especially the natural-philosophical entries and the lower *Mors* material, still lacks a word-by-word source comparison. No claim of unprinted status, recension, novelty, or full transcription accuracy follows from this result.
