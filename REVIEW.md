# Re-adjudication of all 22 fragments — 22 September 2026

The results remain **8 identified, 12 partial, and 2 not identified**. All 22 reports now distinguish the identification claim, selected readings, and the checks actually performed. This run supports the eight existing identifications in the passages checked. It does not certify every transcribed word, complete fragment endpoints, dating, physical reconstruction, or witness novelty.

Two complete saved sets are available: [before re-adjudication](https://aaymeloglu.github.io/fragmentarium/runs/before-2026-09-22/index.html) and [after re-adjudication](https://aaymeloglu.github.io/fragmentarium/runs/after-2026-09-22/index.html). Each contains the same 22 fragment reports, its own index and review, and versioned links to repository evidence. The before set preserves the previously published results at commit `9373f57407028528b147bb06883608ce5f1f4c8e`; it is not a rerun of the old method. The after set records this complete re-adjudication. Both retain their content when the current site is rebuilt. External library images and edition websites can still change.

## What was done

Fresh isolated subagents received neutral image packets for all 22 fragments. Their complete responses, including unsuccessful readings, were preserved and committed before the corresponding candidate comparison. Most fresh packets contain only a few lines; they are an independent starting measurement, not independent identification of a whole fragment. The coordinator knew the earlier proposed answers, and all subsequent comparison, additional image reading and quality review was source-assisted. References to an isolated worker in the assisted-record reader descriptions concern separate execution, not blindness to the candidate during that later review.

The two pilot fragments, F-eo5z and F-h8oz, retain the substantive manuscript-to-edition work from the same day's pilot, with its records and limits. New shorter image-only samples for them were preserved but did not improve recognition. The other twenty reports received fresh image inspection and scoped source comparison or renewed identification searches. Separate workers performed several full adjudications while the coordinator handled the rest and the cross-report review.

The revised workflow was applied to identification questions: selected diagnostic passages, further available passages where useful, source comparison, and explicit unresolved results. It was not a complete transcription of every leaf. Additional passages previously discussed in the old reports are not held-out evidence. No accuracy score or blind end-to-end performance comparison is claimed.

CATMuS remains an optional measurement. The pilot's saved tests did not yield a reliable additional transcription, including a segmentation failure for F-h8oz; this run did not repeat HTR across the other twenty fragments without a specific reason. The isolated readers were useful for exposing uncertainty and possible answer-conditioned completion, but their readings also contained mistakes and sometimes almost no usable text. Their agreement does not substitute for image and source checks.

## Material findings

- **Actual print comparison expanded:** F-o1p7 was checked against printed fols.55v–56v, replacing the earlier OCR-only limitation. Other supported identifications retain scoped manuscript-to-print comparisons and visible variants.
- **Variants remain visible:** examples include F-lmfj's moved commentary clause, F-cfry's different wording for access to the pontiff, and F-8bfm's several local departures from Holcot's print. Identification confidence does not imply exact transcription agreement.
- **Exclusions were narrowed:** F-072h's earlier alleged Galen check used a second translation of the Hippocratic lemmata, which does not establish exclusion of Galen's commentary. Several named-author exclusions elsewhere rested only on format or failed search and are not retained.
- **Sources were audited:** F-8bin's supposed *Summula* OCR was a manuscript catalogue; F-szxp's presumed article PDFs were access-block HTML; F-ss0e's usable edition evidence was a snippet, not a full printed page. Those limits are explicit.
- **New comparisons and leads:** a digitized Versor witness was located for F-b8xt but could not be collated. The Gregory pair could be compared with the *Acta Sanctorum* electronic primary text, including the anonymous BHL3664 version. Narrative connections are supported; the exact Latin recension remains unresolved.
- **Confidence changes concern source connections:** F-ahiy and F-hcpv move from low to medium for their now-documented source parallels, while remaining partial. Neither compiler nor exact Latin recension is thereby identified.

## Preservation and limits

Each current report links its preserved image-only and assisted records; the pilots also link their saved HTR results. The old reports remain readable in the before set. Both saved sets include file hashes and commit references in `snapshot.json`, and automated checks cover complete membership, hashes and local navigation.

Most manuscript images were reused from earlier cached downloads. Where original crop coordinates or scale could not be recovered, the packet records and reports say so; “viewed at stored resolution” is not a claim to a native-scale acquisition. F-ahiy's crop-position description and F-2a37's probable front/back label error are corrected in later notes without rewriting the immutable initial records. Checksums establish preservation, not correct provenance, reader isolation or textual truth.

No human palaeographer reviewed the new readings. The LLM-generated expanded comparison passages are labelled as such where abbreviation boundaries were not reconstructed; they are not diplomatic editions. All catalogue date/place statements remain attributed unless separately supported. A no-hit search or inaccessible source cannot establish unprinted status, a new witness or exclusion of an author. Remaining questions are research results, not silently completed by plausible Latin.
